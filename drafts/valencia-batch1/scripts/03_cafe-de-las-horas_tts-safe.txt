Kaffay deh las Oras

A minute from the cathedral, behind a modest doorway, is a room that looks as if a Baroque opera set wandered offstage and decided to open a bar.

Karreh del Komteh dalmodovar is the narrow lane at your feet, running just off the Plaza deh la Veerhen, and the cafe sits at number one, so if the door is open, step just inside and let your eyes adjust to the plush reds and blues, the portraits crowding the walls, and a ceiling scattered with stars.

The cafe opened in nineteen ninety four, borrowing its mood from the literary cafes of old Europe, and it keeps long hours, serving coffee from the morning and shifting, as the light goes, into a cocktail bar that stays busy until the small hours.

What most people come for, though, is a jug of Agwa deh Valencia. In nineteen fifty nine a barman called Konstanteh Heel was running the Kaffay Madrid, a few streets from here, when a group of Bask travelling salesmen who always ordered the house fizz, which they'd nicknamed Agwa deh Bilbao, challenged him to invent something new. He mixed fresh Valencian orange juice with cava, gin and vodka, sweetened it a little, and named it after the city.

Heel tried to patent the name and was turned down because it was too general, so the cocktail drifted into every bar in town while he never quite got the credit.

That's the charm of this room, where the decoration is borrowed from half the cafes of Europe but what arrives at the table is pure Valencia, made from the fruit that grew in the fields around the city and shaken into a local legend by a Galician.

Order a small jug to share, take a seat by the window if one's free, and afterwards wander back into the Plaza deh la Veerhen, where the reclining bronze figure in the fountain represents the river Tooria surrounded by the irrigation channels that fed those orange groves.
