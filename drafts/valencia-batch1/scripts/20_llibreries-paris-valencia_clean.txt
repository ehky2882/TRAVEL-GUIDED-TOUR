Llibreries París València

This bookshop began with one man's overflowing collection of books and a pitch in the Plaza Redonda.

With the Estación del Norte just along the street, the bookshop at number seven Calle de Pelayo sits before you, and the way it fills its shelves tells you most of what you need to know about how this business has always worked.

The founder was a collector who had simply accumulated more books than he could keep, so he began selling them at the Plaza Redonda, the circular market square in the old town. From there he moved to Calle Sevilla, and then here to Pelayo, and the business is now run by his son, Carlos Olivert, who has kept to the same idea his father started with.

The family's early trade was in comics, sold wholesale, including much-loved Spanish titles like Jaimito and Pumby, and that taught them the lesson the shop still lives by: buy the books that publishers have stopped selling, the remainders and discontinued editions, and pass them on at prices people can afford.

It makes for a shop of discoveries, where alongside discounted new books you'll find out-of-print volumes and a remarkable range of facsimile editions, exact copies of illuminated manuscripts and rare old books.

There are four París València shops in the city now, and one of them, on Calle Navellos, has a mural on its wall painted in 1956 by the Valencian artist Luis Arcas Brauner, a small, little-known treasure of the city's art history.

Its quiet distinction is loyalty to the books other people gave up on. It's a bookshop built on second chances, and in an age of algorithms deciding what we read next, that kind of browsing feels rather valuable.

[beat]

Give yourself twenty minutes to browse, and then, if you'd like to complete the family story, head into the old town to see the Plaza Redonda, where the whole business began on a market stall.
