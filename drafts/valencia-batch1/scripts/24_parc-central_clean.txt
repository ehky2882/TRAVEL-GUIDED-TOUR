Parc Central València

For more than a century, railway tracks cut this part of Valencia in two, and the park around you is the first stitch in sewing it back together.

Look around the open square at the centre of the park, the Plaza de Demetrio Ribes, lined with low industrial buildings that were once railway warehouses, while beyond them stretch lawns, water channels and young trees.

The park opened on the seventeenth of December 2018, designed by the American landscape architect Kathryn Gustafson and her practice, Gustafson Porter and Bowman, after an international competition. The first phase covers about eleven hectares, which is only around forty per cent of what's planned, because the rest depends on the railway lines next door being moved underground.

The square is named after Demetrio Ribes, the architect of the Estación del Norte, and these warehouses were part of the same railway world. Four of them have been given new lives: one as a sports centre, one as a popular university, one as a cultural space and one as a social services centre, so the buildings that once handled freight now handle people.

Around the square, the park is divided into gardens with different characters, including a garden inspired by the huerta, the irrigated farmland around Valencia, a children's garden with climbing walls, and a romantic garden of flowers. Water runs through the whole design, and the planting includes around a thousand trees.

The thing to understand is that this park is unfinished, and openly so. It's a promise half kept, waiting on trains to go underground, and in a city that turned its old river into a garden, it's another bet that infrastructure, once moved, can give a city back a place to breathe.

From here, walk north to the Estación del Norte, Ribes's station, and look at the facade and the ticket hall, where oranges, blossom and Valencian motifs are worked into the decoration in a way that makes catching a train feel like a celebration.
