Lyeesa Negra

Lyeesa negra is the Valencian name for the black mullet, a humble fish of the lagoons, and it's a telling choice for a chef with three Michelin stars to his name.

At the corner where Karreh deh Paskwal ee Heneess meets Karreh deh Korray oos, a couple of minutes from the Plaza del Ayoontameeyento, is where the restaurant has its home, and the entrance here leads into a dining room built around an open fire.

The chef is Keekeh Dakosta, whose restaurant in Daynia, down the coast, is one of the most celebrated in Spain, and he opened Lyeesa Negra here on the fifteenth of November twenty eighteen. Where his flagship is famous for invention, this place goes back to basics, with embers, a grill and the smell of wood smoke, and the kitchen is set up so that diners can watch the cooking from their tables.

The Michelin Guide calls it Dakosta's most Mediterranean creation, and the menu bears that out. There are prawns poached in seawater, grilled grey shrimp, octopus from Daynia and seasonal vegetables charred over the coals, and rice holds a special place, cooked to order in proper paella pans, with a set menu that changes every three weeks to follow the market.

The architect Seelvia Belyot designed the rooms with simple, natural materials, so the space stays uncluttered and the fire takes centre stage.

There's a quiet confidence in the way a chef who has spent his career at the cutting edge of Spanish cooking has opened a restaurant whose main tool is a fire and named it after a fish that most people overlook, which says a good deal about where Valencian cooking finds its pride: not in fuss, but in getting simple things exactly right.

Book directly with the restaurant, and after your meal, walk round the corner into the Plaza del Ayoontameeyento, where the old post office building has a glass dome that rewards a look inside.
