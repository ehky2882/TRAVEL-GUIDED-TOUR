Restaurante Hōchō

Hōchō is the Japanese word for a kitchen knife, and here the knives are very much on display.

Inside the SH Valencia Palace hotel on Paseo de la Alameda, near the Palau de la Música and the river gardens, the dining room is built around a long Japanese-style counter facing a fully open kitchen.

Hōchō opened in March 2021, but the team behind it has a longer history. The Honrubia family, who also run the Valencian restaurants La Principal and Aragón 58, spent the best part of a decade running another Japanese restaurant called Kōmori, which earned two Repsol suns, Spain's own restaurant guide awards. When that arrangement ended, nearly all of the staff moved here with them.

The interior was designed by Julio Guixeres, and it follows a calm, zen-inspired idea of simplicity, with neutral colours and natural textures. The open kitchen projects out from a wide counter, so if you sit there, you can watch the chefs slicing fish only a few feet away, which is where the restaurant's name comes into its own.

The food brings together Japanese cooking and Valencian tradition, so alongside nigiri and sashimi you'll find dishes that borrow from local produce and Mediterranean flavours, with sake and wine pairings to match.

The connection makes more sense than it might seem. Japan and Valencia are both rice cultures and both seafood cultures, and both take ingredients very seriously, so fusion here feels less like a gimmick and more like two cuisines discovering they have a good deal in common.

Ask for a seat at the counter if one's free, since that's where the action is, and after your meal, cross the road into the Turia gardens and walk to the Palau de la Música, whose great glass hall looks lovely lit up after dark.
