Hotel Ospes Palow deh la Mar

Two nineteenth century town houses stand side by side here, and at some point they agreed to become a single hotel.

Kahyeh Navarro Reverter runs past the door here at number fourteen, a minute's walk from the old Porta deh la Mar and the river gardens, and the twin facades before you are Valencian town houses of the eighteen hundreds, built for families who had done very well indeed.

This stretch of the city grew up as Valencia expanded beyond its old walls, and these two palaces, as Valencians tend to call such grand houses, were part of that new prosperity. Today they're joined as the Ospes Palow deh la Mar, with sixty seven rooms and suites spread across the original buildings.

The interior work was done by the Spanish designer Tomas Aleea, who played dark woods against bright white walls. If you step into the entrance hall, look for the grand staircase, with its intricately carved wooden banister rising through a loggia, and above it a stained glass skylight that pours coloured light down the stairwell.

Out of sight there's a pool and a spa called Bodeena, which is a long way from the original purpose of the place, though perhaps not so far from the idea of rest and comfort that the families who built it had in mind.

What the hotel shows, quite elegantly, is a particular Valencian habit of keeping the face and changing the body. Up and down this part of the city, nineteenth century facades have been carefully preserved while whole new buildings have grown up behind them, and here the join has been handled with real tact.

From the hotel door, turn towards the Porta deh la Mar and walk down into the Tooria gardens, where the old riverbed stretches away in both directions and makes a very pleasant start to an evening stroll.
