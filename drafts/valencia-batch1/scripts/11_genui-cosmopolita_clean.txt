Genuí Cosmopolita Restaurant

A restored farmhouse in the middle of a golf course is not the obvious place to find one of the most personal kitchens around Valencia.

The old farmhouse here stands inside the Club de Golf Escorpión, out in the countryside near Bétera, some twenty-odd kilometres north-west of the city, and its tall windows look straight out over the greens and the Mediterranean scrub beyond, with tables arranged so that nearly everyone gets a view.

Genuí is the Valencian word for genuine, and the chef, Paco Aviñó, seems to take the name as a brief. After more than thirty years in kitchens, he describes his cooking as a conversation in his head, trying to guess how to surprise the person he's feeding.

Inside, the farmhouse keeps its high ceilings, old wooden cabinets and chandeliers, livened up with bright contemporary paintings, so the room manages to feel both rural and slightly theatrical, and you don't need to be a member of the club to eat here.

The menu follows the market and the seasons, with rice and fideuà, the Valencian noodle cousin of paella, taking pride of place. The beef fideuà has become something of a signature, and you'll also find dishes like wild scallops with a leek teriyaki, which explains the cosmopolitan part of the name: local produce, cooked with an eye on the wider world.

What's surprising here is the balance of power. Clubhouse restaurants are usually an afterthought to the golf, but at Genuí the arrangement runs the other way round, and plenty of the people parking outside have never picked up a club in their lives.

[beat]

It's a lunchtime place, open from Wednesday to Sunday, so book ahead, and afterwards drive the few minutes into Bétera itself, where the old castle at the heart of the town is a reminder that this quiet farmland was fought over long before anyone thought of putting a golf course on it.
