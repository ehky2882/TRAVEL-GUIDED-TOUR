Graffika Showroom

Valencia takes design rather seriously, and this small shop is where the city's best known design magazine finally gave itself a front door.

Tucked behind the cathedral on the short Kahyeh del Almoodeen, the showroom at number sixteen is the one in front of you, and if you glance along the street, you'll catch the long, plain brick flank of the Almoodeen itself, the medieval grain store that gave the street its name.

Graffika began online and in print around fifteen years ago, covering graphic design, typography and illustration in Spanish from its base here, and it has become a reference point for designers across the Spanish speaking world.

The showroom opened on the twenty third of April twenty twenty five, which was World Book Day, a date chosen with some care. Inside you'll find every issue of the magazine, the publisher's own books, a careful selection from houses like Tashen, Fydon and Gustavo Heelee, and the small tools of the trade: Pantone swatches, pencils, rulers and paper samples.

There's also a small auditorium for talks, screenings and book launches, and the team made a point of holding events in the morning on weekdays, an idea borrowed from London and Berlin, so that working designers can drop in before the day gets going. The opening programme included a round table on the future of design in Spain with Pepeh Heemeno, one of the country's most respected graphic designers.

The real point of the place is that it's a shop built to be a meeting room for a profession, and in a city that was named World Design Capital in twenty twenty two, it gives all that design talk a permanent table to gather around.

When you leave, walk two minutes to the Plaza deh lalmoyna, beside the cathedral, where a shallow pool of water laid over glass lets you look straight down into the excavated remains of the Roman city beneath your feet.
