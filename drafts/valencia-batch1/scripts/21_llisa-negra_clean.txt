Llisa Negra

Llisa negra is the Valencian name for the black mullet, a humble fish of the lagoons, and it's a telling choice for a chef with three Michelin stars to his name.

At the corner where Carrer de Pascual i Genís meets Carrer de Correus, a couple of minutes from the Plaza del Ayuntamiento, is where the restaurant has its home, and the entrance here leads into a dining room built around an open fire.

The chef is Quique Dacosta, whose restaurant in Dénia, down the coast, is one of the most celebrated in Spain, and he opened Llisa Negra here on the fifteenth of November 2018. Where his flagship is famous for invention, this place goes back to basics, with embers, a grill and the smell of wood smoke, and the kitchen is set up so that diners can watch the cooking from their tables.

The Michelin Guide calls it Dacosta's most Mediterranean creation, and the menu bears that out. There are prawns poached in seawater, grilled grey shrimp, octopus from Dénia and seasonal vegetables charred over the coals, and rice holds a special place, cooked to order in proper paella pans, with a set menu that changes every three weeks to follow the market.

The architect Silvia Bellot designed the rooms with simple, natural materials, so the space stays uncluttered and the fire takes centre stage.

There's a quiet confidence in the way a chef who has spent his career at the cutting edge of Spanish cooking has opened a restaurant whose main tool is a fire and named it after a fish that most people overlook, which says a good deal about where Valencian cooking finds its pride: not in fuss, but in getting simple things exactly right.

[beat]

Book directly with the restaurant, and after your meal, walk round the corner into the Plaza del Ayuntamiento, where the old post office building has a glass dome that rewards a look inside.
