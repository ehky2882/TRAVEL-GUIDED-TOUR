Shappo Valencia

Shop windows usually exist to show you the clothes, but here the whole front of the shop has been turned into a stage.

A broad black steel canopy frames the glass frontage here, on Karreh dairnan Kortess in the Ayshampleh, close to the Merkado deh Kolon, and behind it the shop splits into two entrances, one leading into menswear and the other into womenswear, each window lit like a little theatre set.

Shappo is a multi brand fashion shop, and a serious one, stocking the big European houses alongside streetwear labels, from Gucci, Dior and Balenciaga to Stone Island and Golden Goose. What sets it apart in a city full of shops is the building work behind the clothes, which was done by the studio of the Valencian architect Ramon Estayveh.

The interior runs back for some eight hundred square metres, and it's planned like a journey. A line of white mirrored boxes, which turn out to be the changing rooms, divides the space along its length, black mirrors and polished stone throw reflections back at you, and screens play video art by Havyair Santa ayya alongside footage from the catwalks.

At the very back the ceiling tilts up into a huge skylight of angled glass, so the deeper you go, the brighter it gets, which is the opposite of how most shops treat their far corners.

What's going on here is a sort of quiet argument about the Ayshampleh itself. This neighbourhood was laid out in the nineteenth century as the city's smart new expansion, and its shops have always been about display, but Shappo treats shopping less like browsing rails and more like walking through an installation, where the architecture is half the point.

Even if you're not buying, wander through to the skylight and see how the light changes as you go, and then walk the few steps round to the Merkado deh Kolon, a market of the same city ambitions but a hundred years older.
