Hi fi Valencia

In Japan in the nineteen fifties, bars began to appear where the main attraction wasn't the drink but the speakers.

Kahyeh deh Hesoos has a long pedigree for late nights, having been home over the years to clubs like Hwaneeta Club and Dub Club, and number ninety one is now Hi fi, a small room built entirely around the sound of records.

The people behind it are the Basement, a Valencian collective founded in twenty twelve that spent a decade putting on electronic music nights in other people's venues before opening a place of their own at the end of twenty twenty three. Hi fi, they say, is a different idea, borrowed from the Japanese listening bars of the post war years, with a dash of speakeasy.

The speakers are the stars here, and they've even been given names. A custom built pair called Chip and Chop does the heavy lifting, another pair at the bar answers to Starsky and Hutch, and two subwoofers handle the bass, all of it playing vinyl through high end equipment from the audio firm Admire Audio.

The music wanders well beyond the house sound the Basement made its name with, taking in jazz, reggae, dub, soul, funk and disco, and when the records demand it, there's a small dance floor for anyone who can't keep still. Now and again the decks fall silent for talks and discussions about music.

The idea underneath all of this is surprisingly old fashioned. At a time when most music is heard through earbuds on the move, Hi fi asks people to sit in one room, together, and actually listen to an album played properly, and it turns out a lot of people have been missing exactly that.

Check the programme before you come, since the nights vary, and arrive early in the evening, when the room is quieter and you can ask whoever's behind the turntables what's playing and why it sounds so good.
