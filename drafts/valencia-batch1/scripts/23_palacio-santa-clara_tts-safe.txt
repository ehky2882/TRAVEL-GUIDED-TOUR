Palathio Santa Clara, Autograph Collection

In nineteen sixteen this building went up for a family of silk merchants, with the business on the ground floor and the household above.

Close to the Plaza del Ayoontameeyento, on Karreh deh Santa Clara, the building facing you is a modernist town house, its facade softened with the curving lines and natural motifs that Valencia's architects loved in the early twentieth century.

The architect was Franthisko Havyair Gerlik, one of the leading figures of his generation in Valencia, and the family were the Neederlightners, who ran a silk business from the ground floor. According to the hotel's own history, it was the woman of the house who managed the company, and the present day hotel has made her its guiding spirit, a portrait of the independent modernist woman of her day.

It was converted into a boutique hotel as the first in Valencia to join Marriott's Autograph Collection, and the restoration leaned into the building's original style, with curving lines, organic decoration and artisan detail running through its rooms.

Two touches stand out: guests are welcomed with a glass of meestela, a sweet Valencian dessert wine made from grape must and spirit, and up on the roof there's a pool and a bar called El Torrayon, which looks out across the rooftops of the city.

What connects this building to the city is silk. Valencia grew rich on the silk trade in the fifteenth century, which is why it built the Silk Exchange, and the Neederlightner shop was part of the long tail of that history, still selling fine cloth almost five hundred years after the merchants first gathered there.

Go up to the roof terrace in the early evening if you're staying here, to watch the light fade over the old town, and then walk ten minutes to the Silk Exchange, where the story of Valencia's silk began.
