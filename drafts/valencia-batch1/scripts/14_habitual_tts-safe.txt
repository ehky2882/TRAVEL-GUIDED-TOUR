Habitual by Reekard Kamarena

One of Valencia's most celebrated chefs chose to open a restaurant underneath a market, and it turned out to be an inspired idea.

Facing you on Kahyeh deh Horheh Hwan is the tiled and brick Art Nouveau front of the Merkado deh Kolon, and that's your way in, so once inside, head down to the lower floor, where Habitual sits tucked beneath the market hall.

The market came first, designed by the Valencian architect Franthisko Mora Berengair between nineteen fourteen and nineteen sixteen, at the height of the city's love affair with Art Nouveau, and it's now a national monument, with glazed tiles and ceramic decoration celebrating the produce of the region's farmland.

Reekard Kamarena opened Habitual here on the first of September twenty fifteen. His flagship restaurant holds Michelin stars, but this was meant to be the opposite kind of place: comfortable cooking, rooted in the western Mediterranean, using the same care with ingredients as his tasting menus without asking diners to sit through one.

He works directly with growers in the wairta, the market gardens around the city, and the menu leans on what they bring in, alongside house made charcuterie, fresh pasta and bread. The Michelin Guide also notes that several dishes from the chef's own back catalogue turn up here, a rare chance to try them without the formality of his flagship.

What's appealing is the honesty of the name. Habitual means the place you go out of habit, and that's precisely the ambition: a restaurant you could eat in every week, not just on special occasions, sitting under a market that was built for exactly that sort of everyday use.

Before you go down for lunch, step back out into the street and look up at the facade, where the ceramic panels and ironwork are some of the finest Art Nouveau decoration in Valencia.
