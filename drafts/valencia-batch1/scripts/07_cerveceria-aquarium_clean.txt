Cervecería Aquarium

Since 1957, the waiters here have worn jackets and ties with their names printed on them, and the dry martini still follows the original recipe.

Gran Vía del Marqués del Turia is the broad, tree-lined avenue with the promenade running down its centre, and Aquarium occupies a ground floor at number fifty-seven, where through the glass you can make out dark wood, leather armchairs and a long bar that gives the whole room the feel of a ship's saloon.

Three partners opened it in 1957, and legend has it that the place very nearly flooded on its first day, which is rather a lot to ask of a bar called Aquarium. The building itself goes back to the 1930s, and very little has changed since the first cocktail was shaken, because the current owners still work from the old manuals and see no reason to tinker.

Pay attention to the staff, because every waiter wears the same formal uniform, addresses you politely, and keeps a professional discretion that regulars prize, since for decades this was where the city's landowners, lawyers and businessmen came to talk over things they'd rather not discuss in the office.

The food sticks to the classics, with sepia, salt cod, toasted bread with tomato and oil, and a pepito sandwich filled with tender beans, while the drinks list runs from the dry martini the regulars call the king of the house to negronis and vermouth.

Aquarium is worth the detour for one simple reason: in a city reinventing itself at speed, standing still has quietly become the most fashionable thing a bar can do, and younger Valencians now come in alongside grandparents who've been ordering the same drink at the same table for half a century.
