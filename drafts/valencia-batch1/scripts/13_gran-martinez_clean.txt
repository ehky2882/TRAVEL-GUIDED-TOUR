GRAN MARTÍNEZ.

In 1881 this was a pharmacy, and in a sense it still dispenses remedies, only now they arrive in a cocktail glass.

Number three hundred and eighteen, Avenida del Puerto, close to where the long road from the city finally reaches the port, is the spot, and the bar fills two neighbouring ground-floor units, the old pharmacy on one side and a long-closed watchmaker's shop on the other.

Josan Peris and Jaime Pérez, the partners behind it, opened Gran Martínez in November 2021 after two years of building work that the pandemic stretched out considerably. The pharmacy is a protected interior, so its century-old chequered black-and-white floor, its wooden ceiling beams and even the original pigments on the walls have been kept, while the watchmaker's side, too far gone to save, was left as raw concrete, and the contrast between the two is a large part of the charm.

The name has two sources: Martínez was the pharmacist who ran the original shop, and the Gran came about when the two spaces were joined, with a nod to Scott Fitzgerald and his Jazz Age parties.

Look at the cocktail list and you'll see the pharmacy joke carried all the way through, because the drinks are grouped by their effect: stimulating, relaxing, fruity or aphrodisiac. The food is a short list of snacks, from pastrami sandwiches to boards of cheese and cured meat, while the music comes from vinyl DJs, a piano and the occasional jam session.

What it pulls off is the trick of keeping a theme without becoming a theme bar. The old apothecary isn't dressed up as anything, it simply carries on, and the cocktails fit into it the way medicines once did.

It opens in the evenings from Thursday to Sunday, and if you arrive early, walk the fifteen minutes down to the marina first, where Chipperfield's white Veles e Vents building catches the last of the light over the water.
