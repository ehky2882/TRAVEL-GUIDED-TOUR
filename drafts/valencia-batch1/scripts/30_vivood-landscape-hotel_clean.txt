VIVOOD Landscape Hotel

Twenty-five small suites are scattered across a hillside in the Guadalest valley, and from a distance you'd struggle to count them all.

Out here in the mountains of Alicante province, near the village of Benimantell, the hotel is spread across a steep, terraced slope, and the buildings around you are low, modern cabins with big glass fronts, each set on its own terrace looking out over the valley below.

VIVOOD opened in the summer of 2015, designed by the architects Daniel Mayo, Agustín Marí and Pablo Vázquez, and it was described as the first landscape hotel of its kind in Spain. The idea was to take a huge plot of land, some eighty-four thousand square metres, and build on barely a thousand of them, so that the hillside does most of the talking.

The suites were made by modular construction, prefabricated and then set into the hillside, each with its own terrace, and some with a private hot tub, so the hotel feels less like a building and more like a small village of cabins.

There's a restaurant and lounge at the heart of things, and a swimming pool whose edge seems to drop away into the valley. The whole place is aimed at peace and quiet rather than conventional luxury, and the architects talked about designing an experience rather than a hotel.

The lesson of this place is about restraint. Most hotels try to make the most of their land, but here the trick was to build as little as possible, and the result is that the view, the light and the silence are what you pay for, with the architecture politely stepping back.

Before heading home, drive the few minutes to Guadalest, where an old castle and a bell tower perch on a rocky crag above a turquoise reservoir, one of the most memorable views in this part of Spain.
