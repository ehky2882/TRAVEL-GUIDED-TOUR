Central Market of Valencia

Somewhere up on the roof of this market, a metal parrot has been trading gossip with a sparrow for nearly a hundred years.

Find a spot in the square with your back to the Gothic bulk of the Silk Exchange, and the market fills your view: a long frontage of iron, glass and glazed tiles, decorated with ceramic fruit and foliage, with small towers on the roofline and a great dome rising from the middle of the roof, some thirty metres high.

The design won a competition held in 1910, and its architects, Alejandro Soler March and Francisco Guardia Vial, had both trained in the Art Nouveau world of Barcelona; building took fourteen years, and it opened in 1928 looking like a world's fair pavilion that decided to stay on and sell vegetables.

Now look up at the towers and search for the weathervanes. One is a parrot, known locally as the Cotorra del Mercat, and the story goes that she spent her days chattering about the city's affairs with the Pardal de Sant Joan, the sparrow on top of the church of the Santos Juanes next door, the market's voice of commerce answering the church's voice of heaven.

Inside are more than three hundred stalls, and among the beans you'll find garrofó, the broad flat white bean that goes into a proper Valencian paella, and Ricard Camarena, one of the city's best known chefs, runs a bar in the middle of it all.

And here's the thing about this building, which was made to give the everyday act of buying lunch the same dignity as the cathedral and the Silk Exchange around it, a temple to the farmland that feeds the city, with its own resident bird keeping watch.

Head inside to look up into the dome, then hunt for a stall selling garrofó, and if you fancy lunch, the bar in the middle will cook up something from whatever the market has in that morning.
