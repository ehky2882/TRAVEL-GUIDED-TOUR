Hotel Hospes Palau de la Mar

Two nineteenth-century town houses stand side by side here, and at some point they agreed to become a single hotel.

Calle Navarro Reverter runs past the door here at number fourteen, a minute's walk from the old Porta de la Mar and the river gardens, and the twin facades before you are Valencian town houses of the 1800s, built for families who had done very well indeed.

This stretch of the city grew up as Valencia expanded beyond its old walls, and these two palaces, as Valencians tend to call such grand houses, were part of that new prosperity. Today they're joined as the Hospes Palau de la Mar, with sixty-seven rooms and suites spread across the original buildings.

The interior work was done by the Spanish designer Tomás Alía, who played dark woods against bright white walls. If you step into the entrance hall, look for the grand staircase, with its intricately carved wooden banister rising through a loggia, and above it a stained-glass skylight that pours coloured light down the stairwell.

Out of sight there's a pool and a spa called Bodyna, which is a long way from the original purpose of the place, though perhaps not so far from the idea of rest and comfort that the families who built it had in mind.

What the hotel shows, quite elegantly, is a particular Valencian habit of keeping the face and changing the body. Up and down this part of the city, nineteenth-century facades have been carefully preserved while whole new buildings have grown up behind them, and here the join has been handled with real tact.
