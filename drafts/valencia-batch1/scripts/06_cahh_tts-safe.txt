Thentro deh Arteh Ortensia Erreyro

Under this palace runs the line of a Roman racetrack, and inside it a cloud of giant glass bubbles hangs above a staircase.

Look for the long, restored front of the seventeenth century Palow deh Valereeola at number thirty one on Kahyeh del Mar, in the old town just east of the Plaza deh la Rayna, with the name of the art centre beside the entrance.

The palace once belonged to Jwan deh Valereeola, a descendant of Valencia's most important fourteenth century banker, and when the collector Ortensia Erreyro, vice president of the supermarket chain Mercadona, bought it and spent some forty million euros on its restoration, the excavations uncovered remains of the Roman circus where chariots once raced, an Islamic fountain, a medieval oven and traces of the Jewish quarter.

The art centre opened in November twenty twenty three with more than a hundred works by artists from Anselm Keefer and Georg Bazelits to Jwan Meero and Anish Kapoor, but the real treats are the pieces made for the building itself. Tomas Saracheno hung his huge soap bubble spheres above the foyer stair, Olafur Eliasson turned a corridor into a shimmering tunnel of coloured glass, and Sean Scully filled the old chapel with stained glass that throws red, yellow and blue light across the floor.

There's also a room of thirty six screens by David Hockney, showing a single Yorkshire wood across the four seasons, which is worth sitting down for.

What's notable, standing here, is the order of things. A fortune made selling groceries has paid to dig up two thousand years of Valencia, then fill the result with contemporary art at a modest ticket price, and the palace now does both jobs at once, part archaeology site and part gallery.

Wednesday afternoons have offered free entry with an advance booking, so it's worth checking before you come, and once inside, save some time for the new building next door, where interactive works by team lab and Julian Opie play with crowds, movement and city scenes.
