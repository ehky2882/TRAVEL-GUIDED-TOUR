Biblioteca delle Oblate

There's a library in Florence where the reward for going up to the roof is the dome of the cathedral, very close indeed.

Via dell'Oriuolo, a few minutes' walk east of the Duomo. You should be at number twenty-six, outside a long, plain medieval building with the entrance to the Biblioteca delle Oblate. Inside, the building is arranged around a cloister.

This was the house of the Oblate, a community of women who worked at the nearby hospital of Santa Maria Nuova, caring for women who were ill. The building dates back to the fourteenth century. The city library moved here in nineteen fifty-three, and after a long renovation it reopened as the Biblioteca delle Oblate in May two thousand and seven.

Have a look at the reading rooms first. It's a working public library, busy with students and readers, and the old convent spaces carry that well. Then head up to the top floor and out onto the altana, the rooftop terrace. In twenty twenty-one it was named after the Brazilian activist Marielle Franco.

And there it is. Brunelleschi's dome, right in front of you, close enough that you can make out the red tiles and the white ribs climbing to the lantern. There's a café up there too, so you can have a coffee with the view.

What I love about this is that it isn't a viewpoint. It's a library that just happens to have the best seat in the house, and it's open to anyone who walks in.

When you leave, walk down Via dell'Oriuolo towards the Duomo. You'll come out right at the cathedral's east end, and after seeing the dome up close from the roof, you'll find yourself looking at it differently.
