AdAstra

Behind the wall at the end of this street is a garden you're not allowed into. This is the next best thing.

You should be on Via del Campuccio, in the Oltrarno, a quiet residential street a short walk from Santo Spirito. Look for the entrance to AdAstra, set in a long, plain-fronted old mansion. Behind the houses on this side lies the Giardino Torrigiani, and if you look above the rooftops you might catch the top of a slim, castellated tower.

The hotel occupies the first and second floors of an old family mansion. Sixteen rooms, some lining this street, a couple tucked into the green of the garden itself. The architecture was handled by Francesco Maestrelli, and the interiors by the brothers Matteo and Marco Perduca, who mixed original mouldings and high ceilings with Italian furniture from the mid-century to the seventies, and framed art down to old Pucci scarves.

The prize is the terrace. A wraparound terrace of around two hundred and seventy square metres, looking straight over the Torrigiani garden. It's often described as the largest private garden in Europe inside city walls, at around seven hectares, and it's still privately owned, which is why most people never see it.

Now, that tower. It's neo-Gothic, built in eighteen twenty-four by the architect Gaetano Baccani for the Torrigiani family, and it was fitted out as an astronomical observatory, with instruments, a library and an open terrace at the top. The hotel's name comes from it. Ad astra, Latin for "to the stars".

What I like about this place is that it makes a virtue of looking at something you can't touch. Florence is full of private gardens hidden behind long walls. Here, for once, you get to see over one.

Walk to the end of Via del Campuccio and turn along Via de' Serragli. The garden wall runs beside you most of the way, and every so often a gate or a gap gives you a glimpse of the trees.
