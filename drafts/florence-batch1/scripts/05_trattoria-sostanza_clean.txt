Trattoria Sostanza

There's one dish people come here for, and it involves an alarming quantity of butter.

Via del Porcellana is a narrow lane between Santa Maria Novella and the river. You should be outside a small shopfront with the name Sostanza above the door, and through the window, a narrow room lined with white tiles. If there's a queue, you're in the right place.

The trattoria has been open since the eighteen sixties, by most accounts since eighteen sixty-nine, and locals still call it by its nickname, "il Troia", which is Florentine slang and best not translated in polite company. Inside, it looks like an old butcher's shop. White tiled walls, marble, shared tables and waiters in grey jackets that feel as though they've been here since the nineteen fifties.

Have a look towards the back. The kitchen is right there in the room, and the cooking is done over charcoal. Some dishes arrive in small metal pans that have been sitting over the embers.

That's how they do the famous one, petti di pollo al burro, chicken breasts cooked in butter. They come to the table still sizzling, glossy and brown, and you mop up the butter with bread. Then there's the bistecca, grilled over the same coals. And for pudding, the torta meringata, a tall meringue cake layered with cream and wild strawberries, looking slightly too pleased with itself.

Here's what's worth knowing. Places like this survive because they refuse to change the room. The tiles, the tables, the open kitchen. The food tastes the way it does partly because you're eating it in exactly the same space people have eaten it in for well over a century.

[beat]

It isn't cheap for a trattoria, and it isn't trying to be charming. It just carries on.

Book ahead, because walk-ins rarely work. And afterwards, wander to the end of the lane and out onto the riverbank. The Ponte alla Carraia is just to your left, and the evening light on the water does the digesting for you.
