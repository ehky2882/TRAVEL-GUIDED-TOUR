Giulio Giannini e Figlio

Opposite the grandest palace in Florence is a shop that has been binding books by hand since eighteen fifty-six.

Piazza de' Pitti. Stand with your back to Palazzo Pitti and look across the sloping square. On the far side, at number thirty-seven, you should see the old shopfront of Giulio Giannini e Figlio, with marbled papers, notebooks and leather-bound books in the window.

It was founded in eighteen fifty-six by Pietro Giannini, who was above all a bookbinder, and it has been passed from father to son for six generations. In the nineteenth century the shop became a favourite of English visitors, who were living in Florence in large numbers and who loved fine paper and fine bindings. Today the family still does artistic bookbinding and book restoration.

Have a look at the marbled paper. It's made by floating colours on the surface of a liquid bath, combing them into patterns, and then laying a sheet of paper on top to lift the design off in one go. No two sheets are ever quite the same, and the patterns are as old as the trade.

Then look at the bindings, the leather, the gold tooling, the boxes and albums. It's what's sometimes called the Florentine style of bookbinding, and this family is among the last to keep it going in its own shop.

What I find touching is the scale of it. Across the square is a palace built to be seen from miles away. Over here is a craft measured in millimetres, done by a family, at a bench.

If you'd like the full set, cross back over and climb the slope into the Boboli Gardens behind Palazzo Pitti. Keep going uphill to the top, and the view back over the palace roof to the city is one of the best in Florence.
