Lo kah lay

The cocktails are thoroughly modern. The cellar underneath them was dug in the twelve hundreds.

Vee ah del lay sej jo lay is a narrow street between the bar jello and santa kro chay. You should be outside number twelve, a big old palazzo front with a discreet entrance and the name lo kah lay beside the door. In the evening, look for the low light through the windows and people drifting in.

The building is pa lat so kon chee nee. Its vaulted cellars date to the twelve hundreds. Above them are Renaissance reception rooms added in the fifteen hundreds, when the house belonged to a prominent diplomat in the service of the med ee chee. Inside, you drink under stone arches and old chandeliers, on velvet, in candlelight.

Have a look, if you're shown downstairs, at the medieval basement. It's called the lab oh tay ga, a play on laboratory and bot tay ga, the Italian workshop. It's where the bar develops its flavours, with modern techniques sitting beside very old ones, and it also hosts small, multi course food and cocktail pairings.

The approach upstairs is low waste and seasonal, reworking familiar classics with a twist. One house favourite, foo mah ray, is a herbal gin sour with basil, mint and hemp. The menu is kept short on purpose. And in twenty twenty six, lo kah lay was ranked number thirty three on the Fifty Best Bars list.

And here's what's worth knowing. Florence has spent five hundred years using these grand rooms for exactly this sort of thing, hospitality, display, a good evening among friends. The furniture has changed. The purpose hasn't.

It's an old palazzo doing the job it was built for, just with better ice.

When you leave, walk two minutes south to pyat sa santa kro chay. At night the square is quiet, the basilica's marble front is lit, and it's one of the loveliest places in the city to finish an evening.
