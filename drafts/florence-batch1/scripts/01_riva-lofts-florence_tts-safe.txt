The furniture is a mix of antique, mid century and contemporary pieces, and the palette is mostly stone, wood and glass. Nothing shouts. It's the kind of interior that only works if the bones are good, and here the bones are a working building from the eighteen eighties.

And here's the thing. Florence is a city that tends to preserve itself as a monument. This is the other approach, quieter and rather more useful. Keep the old structure, let people live in it, and add only what's needed.

It's a reminder that the best conversions don't hide what a building used to be. They let you feel it every time you look up at the ceiling.
