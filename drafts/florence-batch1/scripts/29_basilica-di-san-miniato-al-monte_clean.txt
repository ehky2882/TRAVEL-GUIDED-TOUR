Basilica di San Miniato al Monte

Carved into the marble here is an old inscription. It says, "This is the gate of heaven." Standing on these steps at sunset, you can see why someone thought so.

The hill above Piazzale Michelangelo. You should be at the top of the broad stairway leading up to the church, facing its front of white and green marble, with a golden mosaic in the upper half and a small copper eagle perched at the very top. Turn round, and the whole of Florence is laid out below you.

The church you see was begun in ten eighteen under Bishop Alibrando, with the support of the Holy Roman Emperor Henry the Second, and completed in the twelfth century. It's dedicated to Miniato, said to be Florence's first martyr, who, according to legend, after being beheaded, picked up his own head and walked back across the Arno to this hillside.

Have a look at the front. White marble and dark green serpentine from Prato, cut into pure geometry, circles, squares and arches. The mosaic of Christ between the Virgin and Saint Miniato dates from twelve sixty. And the eagle on top is the emblem of the Arte di Calimala, the guild of cloth merchants who paid for the upper part of the façade.

Inside, look down at the floor of the nave, an inlaid marble floor of twelve oh seven with the signs of the zodiac. Then up to the raised choir and the apse mosaic of twelve ninety-seven.

During the siege of Florence in fifteen twenty-nine and fifteen thirty, this hill was fortified, and Michelangelo had the bell tower protected because it was being used for artillery. Since nineteen twenty-four Benedictine monks have lived here again.

Nearly a thousand years after it was begun, it still does what the inscription promised. It gives a city a door to look out of.

Before you leave, wander into the cemetery of the Porte Sante beside the church. It's full of nineteenth-century Florentine families, and its paths look straight over the rooftops.
