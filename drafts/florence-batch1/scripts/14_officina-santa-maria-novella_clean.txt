Officina Profumo-Farmaceutica di Santa Maria Novella

It looks like a church, it smells like a garden, and it's a shop. Rather a good combination.

Via della Scala, a few minutes' walk from Santa Maria Novella station. You should be outside the entrance with the name Officina Profumo-Farmaceutica di Santa Maria Novella above it. Behind this wall lies the Dominican convent of Santa Maria Novella, and the shop occupies part of it.

The house traces its story back to twelve twenty-one, when the Dominican friars arrived here and began to grow herbs for their medicines. In sixteen twelve the pharmacy opened its doors to the public. It's sometimes called the oldest pharmacy in the world, though strictly it no longer is one, since it doesn't sell medicines. What it sells are perfumes, soaps, creams and the like, many still made to very old recipes.

Have a look, once you're inside, at the rooms. The historic sales halls run one into another, with old wooden cabinets, glass jars and the hush of a church. There's a small museum and a library telling the history of the place. It's one of those shops where you can easily spend half an hour without buying a thing.

The house's favourite story is about its most famous patron, Catherine de' Medici. For her wedding in fifteen thirty-three, which would eventually make her Queen of France, she's said to have commissioned a special scent, and the house still makes a fragrance, Acqua della Regina, in tribute.

[beat]

Here's what I like about it. In most cities, a building this beautiful would be roped off. Here it's still doing a version of what it has always done, making things and selling them.

When you come out, walk round the corner into Piazza Santa Maria Novella and look at the basilica's marble front. Alberti finished it in the fourteen hundreds, and it's the same convent you've just been shopping in.
