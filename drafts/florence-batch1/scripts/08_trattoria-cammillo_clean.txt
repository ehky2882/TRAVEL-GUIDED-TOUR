Trattoria Cammillo

There's a dish on this menu that has no business being in a Florentine trattoria, and it's been here since the nineteen fifties.

Borgo San Jacopo, on the south bank, a short walk west of the Ponte Vecchio. You should be outside number fifty-seven, a traditional trattoria front with the name Cammillo above the door.

The same family has run it since nineteen forty-five. The story travels through Bruno Masiero, who came from Bologna, and that's why the handmade tortellini here are so good. Bologna is the home of tortellini, and the recipe came with him. His daughter Chiara runs the place now, and her brother Francesco makes the olive oil and the house wine on the family farm, Il Peraccio.

The menu is the Florentine canon done carefully. Bistecca alla fiorentina, a mixed Tuscan fry, and in season, fried artichokes, light and crisp, which are reason enough to come in winter.

And then the curiosity. Curry prawns with rice and homemade mango chutney. It's been served in Florence, by this family, since the nineteen fifties. Nobody quite expects it, and regulars order it without a second thought.

[beat]

Which says something rather nice about tradition. In Florence it's never quite as pure as it claims to be. A Bolognese grandfather and a plate of curry are part of this city's food history too.

Before your meal, or after it, walk back towards the Ponte Vecchio along the Borgo. On the way you'll pass the Torre dei Marsili, a twelfth-century tower whose doorway carries a glazed terracotta Annunciation in the della Robbia manner. The one you see is a copy protecting the original, but it's easy to miss and well worth a look.
