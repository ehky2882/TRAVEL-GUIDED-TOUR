Le Volpi e l'Uva

The name is a fable. A fox can't reach the grapes, so it decides they must be sour. The people who run this bar have spent over thirty years proving the fox wrong.

Piazza dei Rossi is a tiny square tucked just off the south end of the Ponte Vecchio, a few steps from the crowds and quite separate from them. You should be in front of a small wine bar on the corner, with Le Volpi e l'Uva over the door and a few tables outside when the weather allows.

It was founded in nineteen ninety-two by three friends, Emilio Monechi, Riccardo Comparini and Ciro Beligni. Their idea, unusual at the time, was to build relationships with small, independent winemakers, and to make good wine feel accessible rather than grand.

That's still the house rule. The wines are chosen to express their own corner of their own region. Native grape varieties, organic and biodynamic growing, producers you probably won't have heard of. The team buys directly from the winemakers, and visits them, so that each bottle comes with a story about the person who made it.

Look at the counter. It's a small room, and it works like a proper neighbourhood wine bar. You lean on the bar, ask what's open, and let someone pour you something you'd never have chosen.

Here's the interesting bit. Florence sits in the middle of some of the most famous wine country in the world, and it would be easy to pour the same few big names all night. This place does the opposite. It argues, glass by glass, that Italian wine is far bigger than its labels.

[beat]

Which, given how close you are to the busiest bridge in the city, is a rather quiet act of defiance.

When you're done, walk back to the Ponte Vecchio at dusk and stop in the middle, at the gap between the goldsmiths' shops. The view downstream, with the light going, is the one everyone comes for, and it earns it.
