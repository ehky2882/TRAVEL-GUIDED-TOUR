Antico Setificio Fiorentino

Somewhere behind this door, a machine built to a design by Leonardo da Vinci is still being used to make silk.

Via Lorenzo Bartolini, in San Frediano, the old artisans' quarter at the western end of the Oltrarno. You should be at number four, in front of a modest entrance with the name Antico Setificio Fiorentino beside it. The workshop is behind it, and visits are by appointment, so book ahead.

The workshop's history goes back to seventeen eighty-six, and it's one of the very few places in the world still producing silk this way. The weavers work on old looms, with hands and feet moving together, an action the house compares to playing a vast instrument.

The gift detail is the warping machine. Warping is the stage where the long threads are laid out before weaving begins, and the machine here was built in the eighteenth century to a design by Leonardo. It's still in use.

Have a look at what comes off the looms. Renaissance silk damasks, brocades of silk and linen, eighteenth-century patterns, all from an archive of designs that the workshop has kept for generations. Its fabrics have gone into palaces, private houses, yachts and hotels, Villa Cora on the hill among them.

And here's the thing. Florence's wealth was built on cloth. Silk and wool paid for a great deal of the Renaissance art you've come to see. Places like this are the last working link to that economy, not a reconstruction of it.

When you've finished, walk the length of Borgo San Frediano towards the old city gate, Porta San Frediano. It's the best street in the city for workshops, small bars and the kind of everyday Florence that still makes things.
