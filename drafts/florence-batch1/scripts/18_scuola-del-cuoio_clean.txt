Scuola del Cuoio

This leather school began in nineteen fifty, to give the orphans of the war a trade. It's tucked inside a Medici building behind Santa Croce.

Via di San Giuseppe runs along the side of the Basilica of Santa Croce. You should be at the garden entrance to the Scuola del Cuoio, on the side of the church, rather than at the main front on the square. A short walk through the garden brings you to the old friars' building.

In nineteen fifty, Marcello Gori, together with his brother-in-law Silvano Casini, both from Florentine leather families, agreed with the Franciscan friars of Santa Croce to teach orphans of the war the craft of leatherwork. The friars gave them their old dormitory, and the first workbenches were set up in the corridor.

Have a look at that corridor. The building was commissioned by the Medici from the architect Michelozzo in the fifteenth century and given to the Franciscans. The ceiling is vaulted, there are Medici coats of arms over the doorways, and the walls carry frescoes by followers of Domenico Ghirlandaio. That's where people still sit cutting and stitching.

The location makes sense. Since the thirteenth century this was the tanners' district, because tanning needs a great deal of water, and the Arno is just there. The street names still remember it. Via delle Conce, the street of the tanneries, and Corso dei Tintori, the dyers' street.

And then the flood. On the fourth of November nineteen sixty-six, the Arno burst its banks, and Santa Croce was the hardest hit part of the city. The school was engulfed in water and mud, and it took almost a year to get going again.

[beat]

It did, and it's still run by the family. A school built to give lost children a living, inside a building the Medici gave to the friars.

Before you go, walk round to Piazza Santa Croce and look for the small plaques on the buildings marking how high the water rose in nineteen sixty-six. Some of them are well above your head.
