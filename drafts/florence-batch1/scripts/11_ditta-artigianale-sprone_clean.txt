Ditta Artigianale

The coffee here is roasted by one of the most talked-about roasters in Italy. The building it's served in was designed by one of the best architects Florence had in the twentieth century.

Via dello Sprone, in the Oltrarno, a short street running off Via de' Guicciardini near the little square of Piazza della Passera. You should be at number five, outside a wide shopfront with Ditta Artigianale above it, a big airy room inside, and people working on laptops at the long tables.

Ditta Artigianale was founded in Florence in twenty thirteen by Francesco Sanapo, a much-decorated champion barista and coffee taster, together with Patrick Hoffer. It began as a micro-roastery with one coffee bar on Via de' Neri. This was the second Florence site, and it's the one I'd send you to. The idea throughout has been specialty coffee, small lots bought from individual farms and roasted to bring out each one's character, at a time when that was still unusual in Italy.

Now look at the building itself. It was designed by Giovanni Michelucci, the architect behind Santa Maria Novella station, and the café's interior takes its cue from his era, with clean lines and plenty of room to sit.

That matters because this whole area was rebuilt after nineteen forty-four, when the retreating German army destroyed the buildings around the approaches to the Ponte Vecchio. Michelucci was among the architects who shaped how that part of the city was rebuilt, modern, but not out of place.

So you're drinking a very modern cup of coffee inside a quiet piece of post-war Florence that most people walk past without noticing.

When you've finished, walk round the corner into Piazza della Passera. It's one of the smallest squares in the Oltrarno, with a couple of good places to eat and a lovely, slightly crooked village feel.
