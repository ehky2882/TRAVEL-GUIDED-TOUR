Il Santo Bevitore

The name means "the holy drinker". It comes from a novel, and the room has been many things before it was a restaurant.

Via di Santo Spirito, in the Oltrarno, one block back from the river. You should be outside the restaurant's doorway, at numbers sixty-four and sixty-six, with the name Il Santo Bevitore above it. Through the glass, a long dining room, and at one end a big wall of shelving.

The space has a properly layered history. It began as a coaching inn, a place to stable horses, then became a garage, then a shop selling riding equipment, then a restaurant called Taverna Ceccobeppe. In the early two thousands three friends took it over, Marco Baldesi, his sister Martina and the lawyer Stefano Sebastiani, and renovated it largely by hand, with family and friends. A local craftsman built the big bookshelf that still defines the room.

Their idea was a bistro, which in Florence at the time was rather new. Good, lighter food rooted in Tuscan tradition, fairly priced wine, and service that's professional without being stiff. The name points the same way. "Il Santo Bevitore" is the Italian title of Joseph Roth's last novella, "The Legend of the Holy Drinker", and the restaurant's website still opens with Roth.

Look a few doors along the street. Their wine bar, Il Santino, opened nearby because the restaurant didn't have a cellar. And round the corner on Via di Santa Monaca is their bakery, Sforno, in the space of the baker who had supplied the neighbourhood for over a century.

[beat]

So it's less a single restaurant than a small piece of the neighbourhood, kept alive one shopfront at a time.

If you're early for your table, stop at Il Santino for a glass first. Then, after dinner, walk two minutes to Piazza Santo Spirito, where Brunelleschi's plain church front glows under the streetlights.
