Loggia Roof Bar, Palazzo Guadagni

At the top of this palazzo is an open loggia, a kind of room with no outside wall. It's one of the best ideas the Renaissance ever had.

Piazza Santo Spirito, the heart of the Oltrarno. You should be in the square, facing the big palazzo on the corner at number nine. Look right up to the top floor and you'll see it, a long open gallery tucked under the roof, with the sky showing through.

The palazzo was built in the early fifteen hundreds for a Florentine silk merchant, Rinieri Dei, replacing some of his family's older houses. In sixteen eighty-three the Guadagni family bought it, and it has carried their name ever since. It's now a hotel, and the loggia is a roof bar.

Have a look at how light that top floor is compared with the solid walls below it. A roof loggia like this was where a Florentine family went for air, for shade in summer and for a look across the city.

From up there, the view is the whole point. Across the square sits Brunelleschi's church of Santo Spirito, its front left as a plain, unfinished wall, and behind and around it the roofs of the Oltrarno roll away towards the hills.

The bar is open from lunchtime into the evening, for a light lunch, tea or an aperitivo, and it's worth booking.

What I love about it is the idea. Not a balcony and not a room, but something in between. Open to the weather, sheltered from the sun, and built purely for the pleasure of looking out.

Once you've looked out, come down and look in. Santo Spirito is open most days, and Brunelleschi's interior, all grey stone columns and calm proportion, is one of the most serene spaces in the city.
