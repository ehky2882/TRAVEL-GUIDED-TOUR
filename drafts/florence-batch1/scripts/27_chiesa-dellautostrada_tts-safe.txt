Kyeh za dee san jo vah nee bat tee sta, the Church of the Motorway

A church built beside a motorway, to remember the men who died building it.

Kam pee bee zen tsyo, just outside Florence, beside the fee ren tsay nord exit of the ay one motorway. You'll most likely have come by car. You should be standing by the church, looking at a low base of hand cut stone, and above it a huge, swooping roof of copper sheet, peaked and folded like a tent.

The ow toh strah da del so lay, the great motorway linking Milan and Rome, was built in the nineteen fifties and sixties, and many workers died building it. The motorway company commissioned this church to remember them, on a site roughly halfway along the route. Jo vah nee mee kel loo chee took over the project in September nineteen sixty, and it was inaugurated in April nineteen sixty four, by which point he was in his seventies.

Have a look at the roof. Mee kel loo chee said "the general form is that of a tent", a building for people passing through rather than settling down. The copper weathers green on the outside, and inside it looks golden.

Then go in, if it's open. Reinforced concrete supports lean and branch through the space. Writers on the building often reach for three images, the tent, the ark and the tree, and inside you can see all three. The plan is unconventional, with a baptistery gallery, an entrance and the church laid out in three parts, little gardens of olive trees between them, and the altar turned away from the traditional axis. There are ten bronze reliefs of patron saints.

When it opened, critics were divided. Broo no dzeh vee praised its break with tradition. The architect loo ee jee fee jee nee called the interior "controlled anarchy", and he didn't entirely mean it as an insult.

It's the same mee kel loo chee who designed the calm, orderly station in the city centre, thirty years earlier. Here he let everything move.

Spend a moment walking right round the outside before you leave. The roof changes completely from every side, and from one angle, against the traffic, it really does look like a tent pitched for the night.
