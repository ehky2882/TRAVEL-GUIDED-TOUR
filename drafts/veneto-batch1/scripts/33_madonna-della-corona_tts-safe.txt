Santuario ma don na del la ko ro na

Seven hundred and seventy four metres up, on a ledge in a cliff, someone decided to build a church. The mountain is part of the wall.

Come down from spyat see on foot, along the road with its bronze Stations of the Cross, through the tunnel cut into the rock in nineteen twenty two, and stop on the walkway where the sanctuary comes into view. It clings to the vertical face of mon tay bal doh, with the valley of the ah dee jeh dropping away beneath you.

Hermits linked to the abbey of san zeh no in veh ro na were living on this cliff around the year one thousand. By the twelve hundreds there was a small chapel. In the fourteen thirties the site passed to the Knights of Malta, who kept it for nearly four hundred years.

The heart of the place is a small statue, a pyeh tah about seventy centimetres high, carved in local stone and painted, with the Virgin holding the dead Christ. An inscription on it gives the year fourteen thirty two and the name of lo doh vee ko kas tel bar ko, and it's traditionally taken as his gift. There's a lovelier story, that angels carried the statue here from Rhodes in fifteen twenty two as the island fell to the ot o mans. The sanctuary itself calls that a pious tradition, and gently points out that the dates don't work.

The church you'll walk into is surprisingly recent. It was rebuilt between nineteen seventy five and nineteen seventy eight, and at the back, the bare rock of the mountain forms part of the apse. Set into it, around the pyeh tah, is a bronze crown of thorns with golden rays and groups of angels. John Paul the Second came here in nineteen eighty eight.

Look, too, for the ex vo toes, the painted thank you tablets left by people who believed their prayers were answered. There are a hundred and sixty seven, and the oldest is from fifteen forty seven.

If you've the legs and the time, there's another way up: the Path of Hope, from bren tee no in the valley below, climbing about six hundred metres on steps cut into the rock. It takes an hour and a half, and there's no bus back, so it's a walk for the committed.
