dohjes Palace, Venice

Most buildings put the heavy part at the bottom. This one does it the other way round, and somehow gets away with it.

You're on the pyatsetta, the open space between St Mark's Square and the water, facing the long side of the dohjes Palace. The lagoon is to your right. In front of you, two storeys of slender white arches hold up a great flat wall of pink and white stone, laid in a diamond pattern and pierced by only a few tall windows.

For centuries this was the heart of the Venetian Republic: the home of the dohj, its elected ruler, and the seat of its councils, its law courts and its prison. Have a look at the columns of the arcade at ground level. Each has a carved capital, full of figures, virtues and vices, the months, the trades. Most of what you see are copies. In the restoration of the eighteen seventies dozens of the worn originals were taken down and put inside, where you can still see them.

Now look along to the far end, by the basilica. That tall carved gateway is the porta della karta, built between fourteen thirty eight and fourteen forty two by jovannee and bartolomayo bon, the ceremonial way in. jakomo kazanova left by it after escaping from the prison cells under the lead roof, the pyombee, which is a fairly stylish way to leave a jail.

Inside, the Great Council Hall burned in fifteen seventy seven, taking with it paintings by Bellini, karpatcho and Titian. It was rebuilt, and tintoretto painted Paradise across the wall behind the dohjes throne. Round the top of the room run the portraits of the dohjes, except one. mareeno falyairo tried to seize power in thirteen fifty five, and where his face should be, there's a black cloth.

What I love about that heavy wall on its lace of arches is the message. One of the great powers of the Mediterranean chose not to build a fortress. It built something that looks as if it's floating.

Before you go in, walk down to the waterfront and onto the little bridge, the pontay della palya. Look along the canal at the side of the palace and you'll see the Bridge of Sighs, the covered bridge that took prisoners across to the jail.
