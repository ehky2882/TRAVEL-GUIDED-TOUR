Teatro Massimo Bellini, Catania

Catania spent almost eighty years trying to build an opera house worthy of its most famous son. When it was finally finished, it had to sit empty for three more.

From the piazza in front of the theatre, look up at its grand facade. This is the Teatro Massimo Bellini, named after Vincenzo Bellini, the composer of Norma, who was born in Catania.

The first stone for a great new theatre was laid back in eighteen twelve, and the project stalled for lack of money. In eighteen seventy the site was settled, and the building finally went up from eighteen eighty-three, in seven years, to designs by Andrea Scala and the Milanese architect Carlo Sada. It cost around a million lire. And then, with the building ready, there was no money left to hire an opera company, and cholera broke out. The doors stayed shut for three years.

It opened at last on the thirty-first of May, eighteen ninety, lit by gaslight, with Bellini's Norma. Of course it did.

Inside is a horseshoe of four tiers of boxes around a hall of about twelve hundred seats. Look up at the ceiling, painted by Ernesto Bellandi with the apotheosis of Bellini, surrounded by scenes from his great operas: Norma, La sonnambula, I puritani and Il pirata. The great tenor Beniamino Gigli said this was the best theatre in the world for acoustics.

[beat]

Its finest nights have mostly belonged to Bellini. In nineteen thirty-five, for the centenary of his death, there was Norma. And in nineteen fifty-one, for the hundred and fiftieth anniversary of his birth, Maria Callas sang Norma here.

The theatre is having major safety and restoration work during twenty twenty-six, so check before you come whether you can get inside.

If you do get in, look in the foyer for the bronze statue of Bellini by Salvo Giordano. He's the reason for everything here, and it's only fair he gets to greet you at the door.
