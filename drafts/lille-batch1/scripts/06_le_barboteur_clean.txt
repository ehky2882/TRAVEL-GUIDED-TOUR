Le Barboteur

A canard barboteur is what French birdwatchers call a dabbling duck, the kind that tips up in the shallows rather than diving, and it's a fine name for a place that spends most of its time looking at the water.

The big, high-ceilinged restaurant with the wide terrace, at ninety-one Quai de l'Ouest in the Bois-Blancs neighbourhood, is your reference point. With your back to its doors, the water ahead is the Gare d'eau, a mooring basin off the Deûle, and the long boats tied up along its edge are barges and houseboats, some of which have been lived on for decades.

The boats explain the neighbourhood, since Bois-Blancs became an island in the 1950s, when canal works for the port of Lille cut it off from the mainland, and for years the houseboat community here was regarded as a slightly eccentric corner of the city.

The basin itself was reworked between 2021 and 2023. The original plan was for a full marina, but local residents had their doubts, and what opened in September 2023 was a smaller, gentler stopping place for boats that left the character of the basin more or less intact.

Le Barboteur arrived just before all that, at the end of 2019, with a lunchtime menu that changes about every fortnight and leans on fresh local produce, mixing modern French cooking with flavours from Asia, North Africa and the Middle East. In the evening it turns into more of a bar, with boards to share and local beers.

[beat]

Timing is what makes it work, since it opened just as an old industrial waterfront was being given a second life, and it has managed to become part of that change without crowding out the barges that were here first.

If you've time, follow the quay a short way north towards EuraTechnologies, where a vast former textile mill has kept its brick shell and filled its insides with technology companies.
