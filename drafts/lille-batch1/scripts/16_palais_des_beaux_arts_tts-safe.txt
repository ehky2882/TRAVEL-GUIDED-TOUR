Palay day Bozar de Leel

This museum owes its existence to the French Revolution, since in eighteen oh one a government decree shared out confiscated works of art among fifteen cities, and Lille was one of them.

Plass de la raypoobleek opens out around you, and the grand stone palace on its southern side, all columns and carved decoration, is the palay day bozar. If you turn round, the building facing it across the square is the prefecture, the seat of the state in the region.

The palace itself was put up between eighteen eighty five and eighteen ninety two by the architects aydwar bayrar and fairnahn delmah, at a time when cities wanted their museums to look like palaces. The collection that fills it now runs to more than seventy thousand objects, from Raphael and Donatello through to Picasso, making it one of the largest art collections in France outside Paris.

Behind and beneath it is another story, since between nineteen ninety one and nineteen ninety seven the architects zhahn mark eebos and meerto veetar reworked the whole place, clearing out a nineteen seventies extension and adding a glass building at the back, covered in a fine silver dot pattern, so that the old palace is reflected in it like an image made of tiny points.

The best of it, though, is underground, in a room dug out during that renovation to hold the plahn ruhlyef, scale models of fortified towns made from sixteen sixty eight onwards for military planners, at one six hundredth of real size, showing six French towns, seven Belgian ones and one in the Netherlands, built from wood, painted card, silk and sand.

Those models were made for generals planning defences and sieges, and the museum has turned them into one of its most absorbing rooms, which seems a fitting fate for a collection that began with art confiscated in the Revolution and handed to the public.

Head inside and down to that basement first, and lean in close to the models, where whole towns of tiny houses, gardens and star shaped ramparts are laid out in astonishing detail.
