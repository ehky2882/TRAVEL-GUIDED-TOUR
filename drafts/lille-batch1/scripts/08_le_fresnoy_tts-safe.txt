Luh Frenwah, national studio of contemporary arts

Before it was a school for artists, this corner of toorkwan was where the town came to enjoy itself, and from nineteen oh five until nineteen seventy seven it offered silent films, a dance floor, a roller skating rink laid with wooden parquet, a restaurant, a carousel for the children and, on certain nights, wrestling.

Stand far enough back from the entrance to take in the whole roofline, and you'll see what happened next: a vast metal canopy laid right over the top of a cluster of older brick buildings, as though someone had decided to put a lid on an entire block.

That lid is the work of the architect bairnar choomee, who, rather than knock the old entertainment halls down or restore them as a museum piece, kept them and covered everything with one enormous roof, which takes care of the insulation, the ventilation and the weather for old and new alike.

The clever bit is the gap, because between the old pitched roofs and the new one there is a space that would normally be wasted, and here it has been threaded with catwalks and suspended walkways and lit by skylights shaped like clouds, so that the leftover space in the middle has become the building's signature.

Then there's what goes on underneath, since luh frenwah opened in October nineteen ninety seven as a national studio for contemporary art, offering a two year postgraduate course in film, video and digital work to students from dozens of countries, and every autumn their new pieces go on show in an exhibition called Panorama.

Seen that way, the building makes its own argument. It turns down the usual choice between demolition and preservation, keeps the old fun palace, and treats the space in between as the place where new things get made, which suits artists who work in the gaps between film, sound and screen.

Panorama twenty eight runs until early January twenty twenty seven, and the exhibitions and the cinema are open to everyone, so if the doors are open, go inside and look up at the old roofs sitting beneath the new one.
