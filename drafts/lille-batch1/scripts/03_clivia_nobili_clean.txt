Clivia Nobili

Bleu de travail, the French working blue, is the colour that dressed generations of mechanics, painters and factory hands across this country, and one designer on this street has spent her career treating it as seriously as couture.

Forty bis Rue Saint-André, a narrow street in the Vieux-Lille, is the address, and the blue in the window is the giveaway. With the shop in front of you, the wine bar JaJa sits just down the street, at number thirty-six.

Look first at the shapes on display, because Clivia Nobili has been building collections around workwear since 2009, reworking artists' smocks, sailor shirts, overalls and aprons into clothes you might happily wear to dinner. One of her lines is called Prêt-à-Travailler, a neat play on prêt-à-porter that swaps ready to wear for ready to work.

The fabric deserves a second look, since there is a story woven into it. After the pandemic, Nobili discovered that the blue cloth she relied on was being made outside Europe, and rather than shrug it off she set about bringing the weaving of that blue back to the north of France. The project, called Bleu à Tisser, was backed by a culture ministry programme, and the finished garments are sewn in Merville, at a workwear workshop that has been running since 1920.

In early 2023 the project went on show in the chapel of the Musée de l'Hospice Comtesse, which is a fair sign of how the city sees it: not simply as a fashion label but as a piece of local heritage.

[beat]

Lille grew rich on textiles, and for generations this region wove the very cloth that working France wore, so a shop built on the belief that the right garment belongs with the right people is, in its own quiet way, picking up an old thread and carrying it on.

The Hospice Comtesse itself is only a short walk away on Rue de la Monnaie, if you'd like to see the chapel where the blue had its moment.
