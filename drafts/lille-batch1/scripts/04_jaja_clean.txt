JaJa, Planches & Vins

In French slang, jaja is the cheap everyday red you might pour from a carafe without a second thought, which makes it a cheeky name for a bar with more than two hundred wines on its list.

Look for the door at number thirty-six, which is easiest to spot after six in the evening, when the lights go on and the tables start to fill. Across the road, at number thirty-seven, sits its younger sibling, Petit JaJa, so if you stand between the two you can take in both at once.

Through the window, the room mixes the old bistro with something sharper, with parquet floors, contemporary tiles and leather banquettes, while one wall is given over almost entirely to bottles, so that the cellar doubles as the decoration.

The list itself ranges across France and well beyond, and you can drink by the glass, by the bottle or, if the evening calls for it, by the magnum. The food comes on planches, the wooden boards of charcuterie, cheese and seafood that give the bar the other half of its name, alongside a few treats such as a croque monsieur made with truffle.

Across the way, Petit JaJa keeps a slightly different rhythm, opening from Tuesday to Saturday and sending out a chef's small plates for sharing rather than boards.

What I like about the pairing is the conversation it sets up. Two wine bars facing each other across a street this narrow could easily have been rivals, yet here they behave more like two rooms of the same house, with the road running between them like a corridor.

[beat]

That name, in the end, is the clue to the mood, because this is serious wine served without any fuss at all. If you're passing in daylight, the shop window of Clivia Nobili, a little further up at forty bis, shows off workwear in deep French blue.
