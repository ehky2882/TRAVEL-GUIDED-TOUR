Le Tripostal

The name is the job description: tri postal is French for postal sorting, and for decades this long concrete building did exactly that, sending the region's letters and parcels on their way.

Avenue Willy Brandt runs past the front, and your landmark is the broad, plain block wedged between the two railway stations, with Lille Flandres on one side and Lille Europe on the other. It was put up in the 1950s as the sorting centre for the whole metropolitan area, which explains the location, since the post simply moved from one set of trains to the next.

The sheer bulk of it is the point, because inside there are three floors, each one an open platform of around two thousand square metres, which made it ideal for handling sacks of mail and, as it turned out, just as handy for art.

That second life began in 2004, when Lille was European Capital of Culture and the city went looking for big, flexible spaces in which to put on shows. The sorting centre was pressed into service, and it never quite went back, becoming one of the main venues for lille3000, the cultural programme that grew out of that year.

Over the years it has hosted works from the Centre Pompidou's collections, large themed exhibitions, club nights and performances, with whole floors rebuilt for each new show and then stripped back again afterwards.

[beat]

There's something fitting about that afterlife, since a building designed to take things in, sort them and send them out again now does much the same with ideas, and a temporary arrangement made for a single festival year has quietly turned into a permanent habit.

The building has been closed between shows, with an exhibition built around laughter and drawn from France's regional contemporary art collections due to open this autumn, so check what's on before heading in, and if the doors happen to be shut, a stroll along Avenue Willy Brandt from one station to the other traces the very journey the mail used to make.
