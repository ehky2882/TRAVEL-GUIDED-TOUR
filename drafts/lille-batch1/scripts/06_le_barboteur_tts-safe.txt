Luh Barbotur

A kanar barbotur is what French birdwatchers call a dabbling duck, the kind that tips up in the shallows rather than diving, and it's a fine name for a place that spends most of its time looking at the water.

The big, high ceilinged restaurant with the wide terrace, at ninety one kay de lwest in the bwah blahn neighbourhood, is your reference point. With your back to its doors, the water ahead is the gar doh, a mooring basin off the durl, and the long boats tied up along its edge are barges and houseboats, some of which have been lived on for decades.

The boats explain the neighbourhood, since bwah blahn became an island in the nineteen fifties, when canal works for the port of Lille cut it off from the mainland, and for years the houseboat community here was regarded as a slightly eccentric corner of the city.

The basin itself was reworked between twenty twenty one and twenty twenty three. The original plan was for a full marina, but local residents had their doubts, and what opened in September twenty twenty three was a smaller, gentler stopping place for boats that left the character of the basin more or less intact.

Luh barbotur arrived just before all that, at the end of twenty nineteen, with a lunchtime menu that changes about every fortnight and leans on fresh local produce, mixing modern French cooking with flavours from Asia, North Africa and the Middle East. In the evening it turns into more of a bar, with boards to share and local beers.

Timing is what makes it work, since it opened just as an old industrial waterfront was being given a second life, and it has managed to become part of that change without crowding out the barges that were here first.

If you've time, follow the quay a short way north towards yoora technologies, where a vast former textile mill has kept its brick shell and filled its insides with technology companies.
