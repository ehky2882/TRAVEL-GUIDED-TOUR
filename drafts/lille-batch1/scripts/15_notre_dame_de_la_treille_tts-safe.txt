Notruh Dam de la Tray Cathedral

The first stone of this cathedral was laid in eighteen fifty four, and the front you're looking at was finished in nineteen ninety nine, which makes it a building that took very nearly a hundred and fifty years to complete.

The square forecourt around you sits on raised ground, and that's no accident, because this is the old castle mound where the counts of Flanders once kept their stronghold. Face the main entrance and the west front is the startling part, because its tall, pointed central arch is filled with pale, translucent stone that looks nothing like the older walls behind it.

Take the body of the church first, which is neo Gothic, designed by sharl leurwah in a nineteenth century spirit of enthusiasm for the Middle Ages. Money kept running out, so the building went up in stages over the following decades, became a cathedral in nineteen thirteen when the diocese of Lille was created, and stood for a long time without a proper front at all.

That front was designed by pyair lwee karlyay, with the engineer Peter Rice and the artist ladeeslas keezhno, who made the rose window, and the central arch, around thirty metres high, is clad in a hundred and ten panels of Portuguese marble, cut so thin that light passes through them, while the doors below were sculpted by zhorzh zhahnklo.

As for the name, a tray is a trellis, and it refers to a statue of the Virgin, venerated here since the Middle Ages, which once stood inside a gilded wooden lattice.

Rather than fake a medieval front to match, the builders finished it honestly in the language of their own time, so the seam between centuries is left in full view and the long wait becomes part of the story the building tells.

Go inside on a bright day and turn round to face the entrance, because from within, that marble glows like a lantern, and the crypt below holds a collection of modern sacred art.
