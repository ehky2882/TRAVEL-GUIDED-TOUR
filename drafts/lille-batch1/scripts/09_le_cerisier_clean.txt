Le Restaurant du Cerisier

Griotte, Amarelle and Burlat are all varieties of cherry, and at this restaurant they are also the names of the tasting menus, which tells you fairly quickly how seriously the place takes its own title.

Number fourteen Avenue du Peuple-Belge is a contemporary building, and Le Cerisier spreads itself across two of its floors. Facing it, the ground floor holds the relaxed brasserie, while the Michelin-starred dining room sits on the first floor above it.

Downstairs is the easy way in, with a shorter menu that changes every week and prices that are a good deal kinder than the floor above.

Then lift your eyes to the first floor, where the room is softly lit and arranged around an open kitchen in which chef Guillaume Barengo cooks a creative menu led by whatever the market delivers. The star first arrived in 2021, under a previous chef, and the restaurant has held on to it since, which is often the harder trick.

The wine makes up the third part of the picture. The list runs to more than six hundred references, but the more personal detail is that the family behind Le Cerisier comes from Mayenne, in western France, where they keep a vineyard of their own, and some of its bottles find their way onto the tables here.

What the building offers, really, is a ladder you can climb at your own pace, from a weekday lunch downstairs to a tasting menu for a special occasion, or simply a cocktail made with the house gin, all of it under one roof and one kitchen's sense of taste.

[beat]

Before you go, look along the avenue itself, because beneath it runs the old Basse-Deûle, the canal where Lille did its trading for centuries until it was filled in in 1930, and whether to bring the water back is a question the city is still debating, after a 2022 public vote narrowly favoured a park instead.
