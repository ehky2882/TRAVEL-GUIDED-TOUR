Bee yan nur ruh, hotel and restaurant

Bee yan nur ruh is a French word that sits somewhere between blissful and blessed, which is a fairly bold thing to promise on a sign.

The red brick manor in front of you stands at thirty five avenoo de la marn in vask ahl, set back among the trees of the park doo vair so, with a slate roof above and a garden terrace laid out at ground level. The centre of Lille is around fifteen minutes away, which is part of the appeal, since inside the grounds the main road seems to fall away behind you.

The building itself is a northern manor house of the kind you find dotted around the edges of Lille, which has been reworked into a hotel of fifteen rooms. Each room is built around what the owners call a different sensory world, so one leans on dark tones, another on deep crimson, and a third on round windows or slatted wood.

The ground floor is where the house works hardest, because it changes its job through the day: breakfast is served at the bar, lunch moves into the dining room, the afternoon drifts out onto the terrace, and dinner brings everyone back indoors. In summer there is a gang get in the garden as well, the old French name for a garden bar where people gather to drink and dance in the open.

The kitchen belongs to chef lookah grabovskee, who arrived by way of Paris and cooks mostly seafood and vegetables, with Asian notes such as Thai style oil, yakitori seasoning and kimchi, while keeping his suppliers as close to home in the oh de frahnss as he can.

What the house is really after is to be treated less as a hotel and more as a place to live in for a day, where ordinary moments are given a little more care than usual, and that, rather than any single dish or room, is what the name is promising.

Before you leave, ask to see the wine cellar, which is open to browse, with bottles to buy and carry home as a souvenir of the afternoon.
