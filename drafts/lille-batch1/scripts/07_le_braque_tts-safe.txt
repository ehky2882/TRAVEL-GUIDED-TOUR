Luh Brack

A brack is a pointer, the gundog breed, and the one this restaurant is named after is called Marcel, who belongs to the chef and has a habit of sleeping near the kitchen.

Roo de la monay takes its name from the mint that once stood here, and the restaurant is at number forty five, just a short walk north of the cathedral. Look for the discreet shopfront with the words kontwar goormahn painted on it, which is about as close as the place comes to announcing itself.

Begin with that phrase, since a kontwar is a counter, and inside the seats run along the walls in the manner of a bar, against bare brick and light wood, so that dinner here feels closer to sitting at the kitchen pass than to a formal dining room. The kitchen itself is open, under a glass roof that lets the daylight in.

The chef is damyan lafors, who comes from French Flanders, appeared on the television competition Top Chef, and trained at the obairzh doo vair mon, a farm restaurant out near the Belgian border. He opened luh brack in twenty twenty one, and the habits of that training show in the way he works, sourcing from within about fifty kilometres of Lille and, where he can, from his own hunting and fishing.

Then there's the larder, which is where the cooking gets interesting. Very little is thrown away, so vegetables are fermented, trimmings become vinegars and syrups, and the charcuterie is made in house, which means the menu shifts with whatever the season and the store cupboard have to offer.

It's easy to take a restaurant like this for a passing trend, but the approach is closer to an old country habit than anything new: cook what's near, waste nothing, and let the dog sleep by the stove.

When you leave, take the few steps west to the little plass oz onyon, a cobbled square of old weavers' houses whose name hides a surprisingly good linguistic joke.
