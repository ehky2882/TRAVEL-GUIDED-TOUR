WARSAW — 27 — WARSZAWA OCHOTA STATION
VISIT · single stop · British register · ~297 words (~2.0 min)
Google Maps: Warszawa Ochota · 52.225616, 20.990102
Vantage: at Plac Zawiszy, where Aleje Jerozolimskie meets Towarowa, beside the station's ticket pavilion with its folded concrete roof
---
This may be the most beautiful thing in Warsaw that hardly anyone looks at, and plenty of people walk past it every day.

You're at Plac Zawiszy, the busy junction where Aleje Jerozolimskie crosses Towarowa Street. Beside you, sitting on the edge of the railway cutting, is a small ticket pavilion. Look at its roof: a thin concrete shell that sweeps up at the corners and dips in the middle, like a saddle, or a piece of paper lifted by the wind.

That shape has a name: a hyperbolic paraboloid. The gift is how little holds it up. The whole roof rests on just three supports. The engineer was Wacław Zalewski, who also worked on the famous saucer-shaped Spodek arena in Katowice. The architects were Arseniusz Romanowicz and Piotr Szymaniak, and it was built in nineteen sixty-two and sixty-three, for the city's cross-town railway line.

Romanowicz designed a whole family of stations in Warsaw: Powiśle, both Śródmieście stations, Stadion, Wschodnia. Before the war, at twenty-eight, he'd co-designed Poland's pavilion at the New York World's Fair. These little buildings were where post-war modernism let itself be light-hearted.

[beat]

Here's what I want you to take away. In a city full of monuments built to impress, the most inventive architecture is often the most modest. This pavilion is tiny, it sits by a traffic junction, and it does something structurally daring with almost nothing. It's now protected as a monument, and rightly so. The register covers not just the pavilion but the platforms, the canopies and the footbridges too.

Go down to the platforms and look back up at the roof from underneath. Then, if you have time, take a train a couple of stops east along the line to Warszawa Powiśle, and compare this roof with its upside-down cousin.
