## Script

Królikarnia means "the rabbit warren". And yes, it's named after rabbits. A king kept them here.

You're in Mokotów, standing in front of a neat white palace with a portico and a dome, perched on the edge of the escarpment. Below you, the park drops away down the slope, dotted with sculpture.

First, the rabbits. In the early eighteenth century, the Saxon King of Poland, Augustus the Second, known as Augustus the Strong, used this hill as a royal rabbit warren. The name stuck long after the rabbits had gone.

The palace came later, in the seventeen-eighties. It was designed by Domenico Merlini, a royal architect, for the theatre impresario Carlo Thomatis, a chamberlain at the court of King Stanisław August. Here's the gift: Merlini modelled it on Palladio's Villa Rotonda, near Vicenza in Italy, one of the most copied buildings in the world. A little piece of Italy on a Polish hilltop.

In seventeen ninety-four, during his uprising against the Russians, Tadeusz Kościuszko stayed here. The palace burned in eighteen seventy-nine and was rebuilt, and then in nineteen forty-four, during the Warsaw Uprising, it was completely destroyed.

*[beat]*

It was rebuilt in the nineteen-sixties for a single purpose: to house the work of the sculptor Xawery Dunikowski. Since nineteen sixty-five it has been a museum of sculpture, part of the National Museum in Warsaw. And I find that rather moving. A palace that kept being destroyed has been given over to sculpture, the most solid, most permanent of the arts.

Go inside for Dunikowski's work and the temporary shows. Then walk down into the sculpture park, which is open every day. Follow the paths down the slope to the bottom, and turn round. The view back up to the white palace on the hill is the best one in the park.
