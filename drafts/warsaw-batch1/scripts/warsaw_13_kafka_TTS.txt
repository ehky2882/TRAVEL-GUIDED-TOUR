Kafka

Every university town needs a cafe where you can stay all afternoon with one coffee and a book, and nobody minds. This is Warsaw's.

You should be on o bozh na Street, at number three. o bozh na runs down the escarpment from the old university quarter on krakoff skyeh pshed myesh cheh towards the riverside district of po veesh leh. Kafka is right here on the slope. Outside, there are chairs scattered about, usually occupied by people in their twenties doing very little, very contentedly.

Step inside and the gift is immediately obvious: books. The walls are lined floor to ceiling with shelves, and the furniture is cheerfully mismatched, as though it has been collected from a dozen different flats. It's the kind of room that asks you to sit down and read, which is exactly what most people here are doing.

The menu is straightforward: coffee, cakes, sandwiches. And there's a breakfast on the menu named after the People's Republic, the old communist state. The menu itself apparently declines to describe it, and says you'll just have to try it. I'd take that as a challenge.

Here's why I've put a small cafe into a guide that's mostly about architecture. Cities aren't only made of buildings. They're made of rooms like this, where nobody hurries you, where you can be alone in company. The University of Warsaw is just up the hill, and generations of students have done their real thinking in places like this rather than in lecture halls.

When you've finished, head downhill and a little north, a few minutes' walk, to dobra Street and the University Library, with its walls of text and its garden on the roof, which is where the more serious reading gets done.
