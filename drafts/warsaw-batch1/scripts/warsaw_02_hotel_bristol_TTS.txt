The story of the design is a small comedy. A competition was won by two architects from krakoff with an Art Nouveau scheme. Then the builders lost their nerve and switched to something safer, a neo Renaissance front. So the outside is sober. The original interiors, though, were by Otto Wagner Junior, son of the great Viennese architect. The cafe opened in October nineteen hundred and one, the hotel a month later, with two hundred rooms and what the hotel's history calls the first lift in the country.

Now the gift. Look up at the balcony. On the second of January, nineteen nineteen, pah deh reff skee came home to a newly independent Poland and spoke to the crowd from up there. He became Prime Minister, lived in this hotel, and resigned here at the end of that year. A hotel, briefly, doing the job of a state.

And here's the remarkable thing. Most of this street was destroyed in nineteen forty four. The Bristol survived the war and the Uprising largely intact, and reopened in nineteen forty five. Then came nationalisation, a long state run stretch, and in nineteen eighty one it simply closed and stood empty for about a decade. Margaret Thatcher cut the ribbon on its reopening in nineteen ninety three.

Marie Curie stayed here. So did Picasso, Marlene Dietrich and John F. Kennedy. The Cafe Bristol on the corner is the place to sit, with a slice of cake and a window onto the Royal Route. Then walk north a few minutes and you'll reach the Castle Square and the old town beyond it.
