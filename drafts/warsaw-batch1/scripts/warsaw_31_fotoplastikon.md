## Script

Before cinema, this was how you travelled the world without leaving your seat. Sit down, press your face to the eyepieces, and the photographs come to you.

You're on Aleje Jerozolimskie, at number fifty-one, a short walk from the Palace of Culture. Go through the entrance into the old Hoser tenement. Inside, in a dim room, is a large round wooden cabinet with pairs of eyepieces set around it, and seats. That's the fotoplastikon.

It's a stereoscopic viewer. Inside, a wheel of three-dimensional photographs turns slowly past every viewer in turn, each one shown for about fifteen seconds before it clicks on to the next. Here's the gift: it's been at this address since October nineteen forty-six, and even then it was about fifty years old. Before that, it had worked in Lwów and in Kraków.

It was built for twenty-five viewers, and twenty-four seats are used today. A full show is forty-eight images and takes twelve and a half minutes. A family called Krempa ran it after the war, then a man called Józef Chudy. It closed when he died in nineteen eighty, and his grandson reopened it in nineteen ninety-two. Since twenty twelve it has belonged to the Warsaw Rising Museum, and the collection now runs to more than ten thousand photographs.

*[beat]*

Here's why I'd send you here before almost anywhere else. Around a third of the pictures are of Warsaw, and among them is the city as it was before nineteen forty-four. Streets that no longer exist, in three dimensions. It's the closest thing to time travel the city can offer.

Take a seat and look at whatever's playing; the shows change. When you come out, step back onto Aleje Jerozolimskie, look along the avenue, and try to imagine it as you've just seen it.
