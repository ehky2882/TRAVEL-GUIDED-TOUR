bazar roo zhits kyeh go

For forty years, while the rest of the country ran on a planned economy, this market quietly ran on the free one. Everybody knew. Very few complained.

You're in praga, on the east bank of the Vistula, at the gate of bazar roo zhits kyeh go on tar go va Street. In front of you are rows of stalls and newer green pavilions of corrugated metal. Behind you is tar go va, one of praga's main streets, with its trams.

The market is named after yool yan roo zhits kee, a pharmacist who bought this land in eighteen seventy four. It opened officially in eighteen eighty two. Here's the gift detail: around nineteen hundred and one, a kiosk was put up at the tar go va gate in the shape of a giant blue soda siphon, seven metres tall, selling the owner's soda water. It became the symbol of the place until it was removed in the nineteen thirties.

The war years here are complicated. Under the occupation, the market was where you could buy what the Germans rationed. It was also where weapons sold off by German soldiers were bought up for the Polish underground. Most of the traders before the war were Jewish, around three hundred families, and in nineteen forty they were driven out into the ghetto.

After the war, the communist state nationalised the market in nineteen fifty, and yet private trading carried on. In a country of state shops, this was an island of the free market: clothes, deals, and, people said, a section where you might buy back what had been stolen from you. Its hot tripe soup and its pi zi, the round potato dumplings, were famous.

Today it's in an odd, in between state. Part of it belongs to the city, which has put up those green pavilions, and some of the new stalls sell vintage clothes and ceramics. Part has gone back to the founder's heirs. It may not look like much, but this is one of the most storied acres in Warsaw.

From here, head to zomb koff ska Street on the market's north side and walk east for a few minutes. You'll reach the red brick vodka factory of ko neh ser, another of praga's survivors, now reborn.
