## Script

If you want to understand how Warsaw thinks about itself, don't start with a guidebook. Start with this shop.

You're on Mokotowska Street, at number sixty-five, in the city centre's south. Behind the shopfront is a bookshop, a small gallery, and the office of the foundation that runs them, all under one roof. The name is a bit of wordplay: bęc is the sound of something landing with a thump, and zmiana means change. Which tells you something about the spirit of the place.

The foundation was registered in two thousand and two by Bogna Świątkowska, who still leads it. Its job is to support art, architecture, design and urban culture, and a great deal of its work happens in public space rather than in galleries. It runs competitions for artists, architects and designers. It started a free magazine in two thousand and three, and it has run a free university of Warsaw, open to anyone.

Now, the gift, which is on the shelves. The foundation is also a publisher, with its own book series on contemporary art, graphic design, architecture and visual culture. Architecture and the city run all through the list. If you want a book on Polish architecture and design that you won't find at the airport, this is where you look.

*[beat]*

Here's why I think this matters. Warsaw had to be rebuilt by decree, from the top down, after the war. Places like this are the opposite. They're the city talking to itself from the bottom up, asking who public space belongs to, and what should be built next. It's a small shop with an outsized voice.

Have a browse, and ask about the events: there are often openings, talks and readings. Then walk west a few minutes towards Marszałkowska, and you'll find the city of grand avenues that so many of these books are arguing with.
