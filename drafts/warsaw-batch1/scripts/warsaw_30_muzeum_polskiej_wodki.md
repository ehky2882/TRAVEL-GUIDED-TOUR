## Script

Wyborowa. Luksusowa. Żubrówka. If you know any Polish vodka at all, there's a good chance it was born in these buildings.

You're on Plac Konesera, in Praga, on the east bank of the river. Around you are red-brick industrial buildings with narrow arched windows and Gothic-style details. In front of you, the building that now holds the Polish Vodka Museum.

This was the Koneser distillery. Its oldest buildings went up between eighteen ninety-five and eighteen ninety-seven, when this part of Poland was under Russian rule, and the site was split between a private spirits company and a state-owned spirits store. The style is neo-Gothic, all red brick and pointed windows, a factory dressed up as a castle.

The gift is in the names. In the nineteen-twenties, this factory was producing Wyborowa, Luksusowa and Żubrówka, the bison-grass vodka. Brands that are still on shelves around the world.

The factory survived the war, kept going under the communist state, and finally stopped production in two thousand and seven. Then it was redeveloped. Google opened a campus for start-ups here in twenty fifteen. The museum opened in June twenty eighteen, in the old rectification building, where the spirit was purified. The new main square opened that September.

*[beat]*

What interests me here is that Praga, the poorer side of the river, survived the war far better than the city centre. So while the left bank was rebuilt from scratch, this side still has its nineteenth-century factories and tenements. Koneser is what happens when one of them gets a second life: offices, flats, restaurants, a hotel, and a museum about the thing it used to make.

Go in and take the tour. Then walk out onto Ząbkowska Street and wander west for a few minutes to the old Różycki market.
