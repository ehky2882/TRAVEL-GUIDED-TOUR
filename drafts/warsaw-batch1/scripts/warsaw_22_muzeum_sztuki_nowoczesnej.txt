WARSAW — 22 — THE MUSEUM OF MODERN ART
VISIT · single stop · British register · ~322 words (~2.1 min)
Google Maps: Muzeum Sztuki Nowoczesnej · 52.233129, 21.008984
Vantage: on Plac Defilad, between Marszałkowska and the Palace of Culture, facing the long white museum building
---
Few buildings have a harder neighbour. This one was built right in front of Stalin's Palace, and it doesn't flinch.

You're on Plac Defilad, the parade square, with the Palace of Culture rising behind you and a long, low, very white building in front of you. Smooth white concrete, crisp edges, very little ornament. That's the Museum of Modern Art in Warsaw, the city's newest major public building.

It was designed by the New York architect Thomas Phifer, whose practice won an international competition against eleven other firms. It's the first building his studio has completed in Europe. It opened to the public on the twenty-fifth of October, twenty twenty-four. The concrete was poured in place and left white, so the building reads almost as a single block cut from one piece.

Some Varsovians weren't charmed. People compared it to a bunker. Phifer's answer was that a calm white backdrop lets the art do the talking. And from out here, you can see what else it does: it doesn't try to compete with the Palace's towers and turrets. It refuses to play that game altogether.

[beat]

Now go inside, because that's where the gift is. In the middle there's a great double staircase in white concrete, climbing through the building to a glazed ceiling, so daylight pours down through the whole space. Phifer meant it as a place for people to stop, meet and talk, not just a way upstairs. I think it's the best new room in Warsaw.

And there's a second part still to come. The plan for this square includes a theatre for TR Warszawa, also by Phifer, to stand beside the museum. When it's done, this square will have been reclaimed from parade ground to cultural quarter.

When you come out, stand back on the square and look from one building to the other. It's quite a conversation: nineteen fifty-five and twenty twenty-four, face to face across the square.
