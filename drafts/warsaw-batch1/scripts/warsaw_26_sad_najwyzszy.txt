WARSAW — 26 — THE SUPREME COURT
VISIT · single stop · British register · ~324 words (~2.2 min)
Google Maps: Sąd Najwyższy · 52.249808, 21.005280
Vantage: on Plac Krasińskich among the green-topped pillars of the court's colonnade, with the Warsaw Uprising Monument close by
---
Read the columns. On this building, the law is literally written in stone, and you're invited to stand among it.

You're on Plac Krasińskich, in front of Poland's Supreme Court. Ahead of you, a long colonnade of tall green pillars, and on top of the pillars, plants growing. Nearby stands the great bronze monument to the Warsaw Uprising.

The building was designed by Marek Budzyński and Zbigniew Badowski, the same pair behind the University Library, with a team of twenty-one architects. It was built between nineteen ninety-six and nineteen ninety-nine. There are seventy-six pillars in all, and the greenery on top was, the architects said, inspired by the cities of the Incas. Look at the tops of the columns, and you'll see the scales of justice worked into them.

The gift is the writing. Eighty-six legal maxims are carved on the pillars, in Latin and in Polish, chosen by a team of scholars of Roman law led by Professor Witold Wołodkiewicz. Many come from the Roman jurists and the great Roman law books of Justinian. Walk along and read a few. "Nulla poena sine lege": no punishment without law. "Ius est ars boni et aequi": law is the art of the good and the fair.

[beat]

And here's why this building matters so much. It went up in the nineteen-nineties, just after Poland came out of communism. A country that had lived for decades under a system where law bent to politics built its highest court out of the oldest principles of law it could find, and carved them into the street for everyone to read. That is a statement, and it's not a subtle one.

Now look at the pillars nearest the Uprising monument. On those, you'll find the anchor symbol of Fighting Poland, the emblem of the wartime underground. Then go round to the back of the building and find the three figures holding it up: caryatids standing for Faith, Hope and Love.
