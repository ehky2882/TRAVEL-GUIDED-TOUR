ka to veets ka Street, sas ka kem pa

Most of central Warsaw had to be rebuilt after the war. This quiet street on the far bank still has its interwar houses, and that makes it a time capsule of the city's most hopeful years.

You're in sas ka kem pa, a leafy district of villas on the east bank of the Vistula, on ka to veets ka Street. In front of you, at numbers nine to eleven, a crisp, plain walled building with long horizontal windows. Look along the street and you'll see more: flat roofs, plain walls, clean geometry, set in gardens.

This street is a small catalogue of Polish interwar modernism. Thirteen of its buildings are on the heritage register. The one in front of you is by boh dan la hert and yoo zef sha nai tsa, built in nineteen twenty eight and twenty nine. It's a house for three families, and la hert's own father paid for it.

Here's the gift. The house follows Le Corbusier's famous five points of modern architecture: walls freed from the structure, open plans, long ribbon windows. And one Polish architecture writer put it beautifully: from the front it looks like a fortress, and from the back it opens up completely to the garden.

Walk on, and the names multiply. There's a villa by shi mon and helena sir koos, at number twenty six, two of the most important figures of the Polish avant garde. There are houses by stah nee swaff ba ril skee, hen rik oder feld and mar yan la leh vich, and a row of houses from nineteen twenty six by vwo jee myesh gall. Several of the architects were linked to pray sens, the radical modernist group of the period.

What strikes me, every time, is the optimism. These architects were building for a new country, independent for the first time in over a century, and they believed design could change how people lived. Within twenty years the war would take almost everything. But not this street.

Walk the whole length of ka to veets ka, slowly, and look over the fences. Then wander the neighbouring streets of sas ka kem pa: the quarter is full of cafes, and many more villas of the same era.
