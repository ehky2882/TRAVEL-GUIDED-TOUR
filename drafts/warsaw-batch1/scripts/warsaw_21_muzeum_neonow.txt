WARSAW — 21 — THE NEON MUSEUM
VISIT · single stop · British register · ~331 words (~2.2 min)
Google Maps: Muzeum Neonów · 52.231878, 21.006114
Vantage: inside the Palace of Culture, on the fourth floor, in the Maria Skłodowska-Curie Hall; reached by the side lifts from the cloakroom level
---
After the war, Warsaw was a city of grey reconstruction. And then, at night, it lit up in pink, blue and green.

You should be in the Palace of Culture, on the fourth floor, in the hall named after Maria Skłodowska-Curie. To get here, you take the side lifts from the cloakroom level. You'll know you've arrived when you're surrounded by glowing letters, glass tubes bent into words, animals and flowers, all humming softly in a dark room.

The collection was started by two people: Ilona Karwińska and David Hill. From two thousand and five they set out to rescue the neon signs of communist-era Poland before they were all scrapped, and in twenty twelve they opened a museum in a former factory in Kamionek, across the river in Praga. In twenty twenty-five the museum moved here, into the very building that defined the post-war city. In a sense it's a homecoming: the founders held their first neon exhibition in this Palace almost twenty years ago.

Now, the signs. Find the names of cafés, cinemas and restaurants you'll never visit: the Szanghaj restaurant, the Berlin, the Syrenka. Look for the Cepelia sign, from the folk-art shop on Marszałkowska. And find the cow from the Bambino milk bar on Krucza Street, recreated here. A neon cow advertising a milk bar is about as cheerful as communist marketing got.

[beat]

Here's what I find touching about this place. Under communism there was very little to advertise and almost no competition. And yet the city was covered in these signs, designed by serious graphic artists, with real wit and real craft. They weren't selling anything, really. They were giving people a little bit of joy on the way home in the dark.

When you leave, go back down and out onto the square at night. Look across Marszałkowska at the shop fronts and signs. After an hour in this room, I promise you'll never read a Warsaw shop sign in quite the same way again.
