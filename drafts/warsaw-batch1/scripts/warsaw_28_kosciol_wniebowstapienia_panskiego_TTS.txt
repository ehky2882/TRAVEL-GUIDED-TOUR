The Church of the Ascension, oor si noof

This church was built by a parish in a brand new housing estate, through the hardest decade of late communism, and it was almost a decade before the upper church could open its doors.

You're in oor si noof, in the south of the city, in the park in front of the church. In front of you, a low, broad church of unplastered brick and concrete, spreading wide rather than rising high, with a huge cross on its front. Around you, the tall blocks of the estate.

It was designed by marek boo jin skee and zbeeg nyeff ba doff skee, with pyotr vee ha, between nineteen eighty and nineteen eighty five. boo jin skee was also one of the creators of the oor si noof estate itself, so he designed the neighbourhood and then its church. Building began in March nineteen eighty two, in the months of martial law. The lower church opened, unfinished, at Christmas that year. The upper church opened, still raw, in May nineteen eighty nine.

Now look at the shapes, because this is the gift. The curves of the front remember the Baroque churches of small Polish towns. The buttresses along the sides remember Gothic. The bare brick is honest about what it's made of. In the middle of a modern concrete estate, the architects made something that feels as if it had always been here.

And here's a lovely detail for film lovers. kshish tof kyesh loff skee shot his great television series, The Decalogue, among the blocks of this estate, and this church appears in the very first episode. So this building is a small part of cinema history too.

I'd say it's one of the best Polish buildings of the nineteen eighties, and the culture press has called it one of the most interesting churches of that decade. In those years the Church was one of the few places where Poles could build something on their own terms, and this is what they built.

Walk all the way round the outside and look at how the brick walls step and fold. Then come back by metro: the line along this avenue runs straight back into the city centre.
