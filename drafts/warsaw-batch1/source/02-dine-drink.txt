=== 08 | charlotte-plac-zbawiciela | Charlotte, Plac Zbawiciela | Charlotte | 52.220090, 21.018640 | DINE
Vantage: at a table outside Charlotte, on the curved edge of Plac Zbawiciela at Aleja Wyzwolenia eighteen, facing the church across the square
Sources: https://www.inyourpocket.com/poland/warsaw/venues/charlotte-chleb-i-wino ; https://myartguides.com/leisure/restaurants/warsaw/charlotte ; https://warsawinsider.pl/best-of-warsaw-2024-bars/ ; https://www.inyourpocket.com/poland/warsaw/venues/plac-zbawiciela
---
Breakfast in Warsaw has a front row, and this is it.

Plac Zbawiciela, the Square of the Saviour. Find a seat at Charlotte, at number eighteen Aleja Wyzwolenia, on the curve of the square. In front of you, the round square itself, with traffic and trams swinging around it. Across the way, the great twin-towered church that gives the square its name. And around much of it, the tall, even fronts of the early nineteen-fifties, following the curve of the square.

Charlotte is a French-style bakery and bistro, and the bread is baked on site. The gift detail is on the table: the jams and the chocolate spread are made to the house's own recipes and set out in big jars, so you help yourself. Inside, there's one long communal table down the middle, which is the best seat in the house if you're on your own.

The building around you is worth a look too. After the war, this square was meant to be rebuilt as a single grand classical set piece, part of the new socialist city centre. Only some of that plan was carried out. What you get is a square that's half ceremonial and half neighbourhood: big architecture, small cafés.

[beat]

And here's the thing I love about this spot. Warsaw's planners in the fifties designed squares like this for marching. They got breakfast instead. The local writing about the square says that the bar upstairs may have been here first, but it was Charlotte that put the square on the map, and turned it into the place where the city comes to see and be seen over coffee.

There's a quieter detail on the church side. The twin towers belong to the Church of the Holiest Saviour, finished in the nineteen-twenties in a neo-Renaissance style. Finish your coffee, then walk the full circle of the square and look up at the fronts, to see where the grand plan stops and the older city carries on. Five minutes south down Marszałkowska brings you to the old milk bar Prasowy.

=== 09 | kulturalna | Kulturalna | Kulturalna | 52.231263, 21.007087 | DRINK
Vantage: at the side entrance of the Palace of Culture used by the Teatr Dramatyczny, on the Plac Defilad side; then inside, in the bar next to the theatre's lobby
Sources: https://www.ebilet.pl/miejsce/cafe-kulturalna/ ; https://www.kulturalna.pl/en/ ; https://spottedbylocals.com/warsaw/teatr-dramatyczny ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Culture_and_Science

There is something deeply satisfying about having a cheap beer inside a building that Stalin gave you.

You're at the Palace of Culture, on its south-eastern corner, where the Teatr Dramatyczny, the Dramatic Theatre, has its entrance. Look for the doors and the signs for Kulturalna, and go in. The bar sits beside the theatre's lobby, inside the Palace itself.

First, a little background. The Palace was finished in nineteen fifty-five, a gift from the Soviet Union, and from the start it was meant to be full of culture: theatres, a concert hall, museums. The Dramatic Theatre moved into this wing that same year. Decades later, the theatre made some of its space available, and Kulturalna moved in. It's been running for close to twenty years now.

What you'll find is a café by day and a club by night. The kitchen does food. The stage has hosted indie bands from Poland and well beyond, and there are readings and literary evenings too. People come before a play, after a play, and very often instead of a play.

[beat]

Here's the reason I'd send you here, and it's not the drinks. The Palace is the most argued-about building in Poland. For many, it's a symbol of domination, built to be looked up to. Kulturalna is what Warsaw does with a symbol like that. It doesn't knock it down. It moves in, turns the music up, and makes it its own. The city has slowly domesticated its most overbearing monument, one bar at a time.

When you come out, walk round to the main front of the Palace, facing the wide expanse of Plac Defilad, and look up at the tower. If it's daytime, you can ride the lift to the thirtieth-floor terrace. Either way, look across the square at the white box of the new Museum of Modern Art, which was built to stand up to this tower.

=== 10 | warszawa-powisle-bar | Warszawa Powiśle | Warszawa Powiśle | 52.234078, 21.029892 | DRINK
Vantage: at the lower ticket pavilion of Warszawa Powiśle station, on Kruczkowskiego Street beside the Poniatowski Bridge viaduct; the bar is inside the pavilion and on the terrace around it
Sources: https://www.whitemad.pl/en/the-modernist-warszawa-powisle-station-a-pearl-on-the-rails-designed-by-romanowicz-and-szymaniak/ ; https://kathmanduandbeyond.com/?p=251680 ; https://warsawinsider.pl/landmark-ochota-station/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warszawa_Powi%C5%9Ble_railway_station
---
Look at the roof first. People have compared it to a spaceship, and once you've heard that, you won't unsee it.

You should be at the foot of the great viaduct that carries the road up to the Poniatowski Bridge, on Kruczkowskiego Street. In front of you is a small pavilion with a roof shaped like a slice of a sphere turned upside down. That's the old ticket office of Warszawa Powiśle station. Today it's a bar, and on any warm evening the terrace around it will be full.

The station was designed by Arseniusz Romanowicz and Piotr Szymaniak, two of the most inventive architects of post-war Poland. It was built between nineteen fifty-four and nineteen sixty-three. Romanowicz had co-designed Poland's pavilion at the New York World's Fair before the war, and after it he gave this city a whole family of railway stations.

The trick of this station is where it sits. It's halfway up the escarpment between the low riverside district of Powiśle and the city centre above. For a while it was even going to be called Warszawa Skarpa, "Warsaw Escarpment". There are two ticket pavilions: an upper one, still a ticket hall, whose roof is stretched between two arches like a kite, and this lower one, the upside-down dome.

[beat]

Since two thousand and nine, the lower pavilion has been the café-club called, simply, Warszawa Powiśle. And here's what I find rather lovely. Modernism promised people light, air, and public life. In this case, the promise came good half a century late, not in the waiting room but on the terrace with a drink in hand.

Before you settle in, walk up the stairs towards the upper pavilion and look back down at this roof from above. Then follow Kruczkowskiego down to the river and the boulevards, a few minutes away. You'll be on the Vistula in time for the sunset.

=== 11 | pawilony-nowy-swiat | Pawilony Nowy Świat | Pawilony za Nowym Światem | 52.232943, 21.020321 | DRINK
Vantage: at the gateway into the courtyard behind Nowy Świat twenty-two to twenty-eight; then a few steps inside, among the pavilions
Sources: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pawilony_Nowy_%C5%9Awiat ; https://www.inyourpocket.com/warsaw/nowy-swiat_42003v ; https://warsawluke.pl/pl/wycieczki-po-warszawie-z-przewodnikiem/nowy-swiat-historia-najslynniejszej-ulicy-handlowej-warszawy/

Behind one of Warsaw's smartest streets is a maze of tiny sheds full of bars. Nobody planned it that way. It just happened.

You should be on Nowy Świat, the elegant street that carries the Royal Route south towards the city's grand avenues. Find the passage at numbers twenty-two to twenty-eight and walk through it into the courtyard behind. Suddenly you're in a different city: a tight warren of low, single-storey pavilions, each a separate bar with its own door, sign and music.

Nowy Świat itself was levelled in the war and rebuilt in the late nineteen-forties in a calm, neoclassical style. The pavilions came later. In nineteen seventy-two a group of small craftsmen set up a company and were given about one and a half thousand square metres of land back here on a forty-year lease. They built little workshops and shops. At a time when state shops were often empty, a newspaper called this place "an enclave of elegant trade".

Then, in the late nineteen-nineties, things changed fast. Owners and their children swapped workshops for bars, one by one. Today there are well over a dozen, packed into what was once a little arcade of craftsmen's workshops.

[beat]

And here's the interesting bit. The forty-year lease ran out in twenty twelve. The businesses went to court and won an extension, but the city hadn't signed a new agreement years later, and the area's local plan now allows it to be redeveloped. So this beloved mess survives on borrowed time, as a good deal of Warsaw always has.

As an architect, I'd say this is the most honest building type in the city: no architect, no masterplan, just a hundred small decisions. Wander in, take a lap, and pick whichever bar has the music you like. When you've had enough, step back through the gateway onto Nowy Świat and walk north towards the university. Ten minutes brings you to Krakowskie Przedmieście and the old hotels.

=== 12 | plan-b | Plan B | Plan B | 52.220004, 21.018694 | DRINK
Vantage: on Plac Zbawiciela, on the pavement below the bar's windows at Aleja Wyzwolenia eighteen, looking across the round square
Sources: https://warsawinsider.pl/best-of-warsaw-2024-bars/ ; https://www.lonelyplanet.com/poland/warsaw/nightlife/plan-b/a/poi-dri/417731/360342 ; https://tvn24.pl/tvnwarszawa/najnowsze/tecza-zaslania-kosciol-ls219661 ; https://www.se.pl/warszawa/nie-bedzie-powrotu-teczy-na-plac-zbawiciela-zamiast-niej-kwietnik-aa-upA4-Dsgi-7jo2.html ; https://www.rp.pl/wydarzenia/art11554211-pozegnanie-teczy-zostanie-rozebrana ; https://dzieje.pl/node/36375

On a warm night in Warsaw, the bar isn't really the bar. It's the pavement outside it.

You're standing on Plac Zbawiciela, below the windows of Plan B, at number eighteen Aleja Wyzwolenia. The bar is upstairs. Down here, the edge of the square fills with people holding drinks, perched on steps and kerbs. In front of you is the round square with its traffic, and the twin towers of the Church of the Holiest Saviour across the way.

Plan B is, in the words of the local city magazine, a dive bar with cult status: low light, strong drinks, loud music, and a crowd of students and seasoned night owls. It may well have been the first bar on this square, years before the cafés made it fashionable.

Now look at the middle of the square, because this is where the story gets good. In twenty twelve the artist Julita Wójcik's rainbow arrived here: a steel arch covered in artificial flowers, which had already stood in front of the European Parliament in Brussels. When it was rebuilt after one attack, more than twenty-two thousand flowers went back onto it. To some people it was a cheerful work of art. To others it was a provocation, sitting in front of a church. It was set on fire more than once, repaired, and attacked again. In twenty fifteen it was finally taken down for good.

[beat]

So this little circle of tarmac has hosted one of the sharpest public arguments in modern Poland: about art, faith, tolerance, and who gets to decide what a public square is for. And through all of it, people kept sitting on these steps with a drink, talking it over. That, if you ask me, is exactly what a square is for.

The rainbow hasn't come back, despite campaigns to return it. But keep an eye on the planters in the middle of the square in summer. The city has talked of a flowerbed in rainbow colours as a quieter nod to it. Then head up the stairs and see what the noise is about.

=== 13 | kafka | Kafka | Caffe Kafka | 52.239532, 21.022711 | DRINK
Vantage: on Oboźna Street at number three, outside the café's windows and the chairs set out in front
Sources: https://www.inyourpocket.com/poland/warsaw/venues/kawiarnia-kafka ; https://laptopfriendlycafe.com/cafes/warsaw/cafe-kafka
---
Every university town needs a café where you can stay all afternoon with one coffee and a book, and nobody minds. This is Warsaw's.

You should be on Oboźna Street, at number three. Oboźna runs down the escarpment from the old university quarter on Krakowskie Przedmieście towards the riverside district of Powiśle. Kafka is right here on the slope. Outside, there are chairs scattered about, usually occupied by people in their twenties doing very little, very contentedly.

Step inside and the gift is immediately obvious: books. The walls are lined floor to ceiling with shelves, and the furniture is cheerfully mismatched, as though it has been collected from a dozen different flats. It's the kind of room that asks you to sit down and read, which is exactly what most people here are doing.

The menu is straightforward: coffee, cakes, sandwiches. And there's a breakfast on the menu named after the People's Republic, the old communist state. The menu itself apparently declines to describe it, and says you'll just have to try it. I'd take that as a challenge.

[beat]

Here's why I've put a small café into a guide that's mostly about architecture. Cities aren't only made of buildings. They're made of rooms like this, where nobody hurries you, where you can be alone in company. The University of Warsaw is just up the hill, and generations of students have done their real thinking in places like this rather than in lecture halls.

When you've finished, head downhill and a little north, a few minutes' walk, to Dobra Street and the University Library, with its walls of text and its garden on the roof, which is where the more serious reading gets done.

=== 14 | rozbrat-20 | Rozbrat 20 | Rozbrat 20 | 52.224200, 21.035099 | DRINK
Vantage: on the corner of Rozbrat Street at number twenty, in Powiśle's Solec quarter, facing the restaurant's windows

If you want one bottle of wine in Warsaw chosen by someone who really cares, come to this corner.

Rozbrat Street, number twenty. You're on a quiet corner in the lower part of town, near the river, where residential streets give way to the green of the park along the escarpment. The restaurant is a smart corner room with big windows. Nothing shouts. It's the sort of place you find on purpose.

Rozbrat 20 started life with a reputation for its wine and its bread, and grew into something more ambitious. The chef is Bartosz Szymczak, who trained in Britain, in the kitchens of Tom Aikens and Lee Westcott, before coming home to Warsaw. His cooking is Polish at the root, with a curiosity for Asian flavours and a British training in technique. In twenty twenty-four the Michelin Guide gave the restaurant its first star.

The menu is a tasting menu that follows the seasons, and the kitchen likes to divide the year more finely than the usual four. But the real gift is the wine list, which leans heavily on Italy, and especially on organic and biodynamic growers, many of them brought into Poland by the owner himself. It's often named among the best wine lists in the country.

[beat]

Here's what interests me about it, standing on this ordinary corner. For a long time, grand dining in Warsaw meant hotel restaurants with chandeliers. Now the most serious cooking in the city is happening in rooms like this, in residential streets, run by people who simply want to do one thing properly. It's a shift in the map of the city as much as in the food.

Book ahead, as it's dinner only, Tuesday to Saturday. If you're early, walk a couple of minutes towards the escarpment and into the park, then follow the slope north. Or head downhill to the river and the boulevards, and come back hungry.
