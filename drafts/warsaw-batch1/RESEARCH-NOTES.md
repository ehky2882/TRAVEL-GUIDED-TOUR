# Warsaw batch 1: research notes (primary and secondary sources read before writing)

## 01 Raffles Europejski Warsaw
- raffles.com/warsaw: built 1857 by Enrico Marconi; about 500 artworks by 120+ artists; Long Bar, Europejski Grill, Lourse patisserie (elite took tea there in the early 20th c.), Humidor.
- en.wikipedia Hotel_Europejski: opened 1 Jan 1857; 1915 Jawornicki upgrade; WWII Wehrmacht officers only; burned by retreating Germans Dec 1944–Jan 1945; seized 1948 (Bierut decrees); 1949–51 Pniewski redesign as military school, grand staircase + ballrooms removed; Military Political Academy 1951–54; 1956–57 housed Jewish emigrants from USSR; reopened as Orbis hotel 2 July 1962; heirs sued 1993, won after 12 years; closed 30 June 2005; reconstruction from July 2013; Raffles 2018, 106 rooms.
- Search (luxurytraveladvisor / journaldespalaces): art curated by Anda Rottenberg (ex-director of Zachęta); Kantor, Sasnal, Sosnowska, Macuga.

## 02 Hotel Bristol
- en.wikipedia Hotel_Bristol,_Warsaw: 1895 Paderewski, Roszkowski, Zaremba bought Tarnowski Palace site; competition won by Stryjeński & Mączyński (Art Nouveau), switched to neo-Renaissance, Władysław Marconi (son of Enrico) revised; interiors Otto Wagner Jr.; cornerstone 22 Apr 1899; café opened 22 Oct 1901, hotel 19 Nov 1901; 1928–31 Jawornicki Art Deco; WWII German district HQ, survived largely intact, reopened 1945; Orbis 1952; university 1977, closed Nov 1981, empty ~decade; reopened 5 Dec 1992, Thatcher 17 Apr 1993; Luxury Collection 2013; guests Curie, Grieg, Caruso, Dietrich, Picasso, JFK.
- Press (hoteldesigns / journaldespalaces): 2013 interiors Anita Rosato.

## 03 Autor Rooms
- wallpaper.com: Lwowska 17/7, 19th-c. residential building; Mamastudio (Michał Pawlik); four rooms; architect Mateusz Baumiller; Beza Projekt, Purpura tiles; art via Starter Gallery, for sale.
- stylepark.com: started 2015; ~200 m², one floor; Ultralight lamps, Maria Jeglinska glasses/mirrors/door handles, Beza Projekt bedding, Segiet Oniszh oak table; shared breakfast room; everything Polish design; guests can contact designers to buy pieces; honesty bar, library, terrace.
- Faculty of Architecture, Warsaw University of Technology, is one street away (mitmagazine).

## 04 Bar Mleczny Prasowy
- bryla.pl (23 Oct 2012): ran ~50 years before closing; residents pushed the city into a "profiled competition" requiring the tenant keep it a milk bar; rent about 1/3 of neighbours'; new tenant Kamil Hagemajer; interior by Mikołaj Wojciechowski & Konrad Sojka; kept part of black-and-white floor mosaic, the serving hatches, the name + neon; wall menu modelled on the old black board.
- search (multiple): opened 1954 ("a year older than the Palace of Culture"); closed 2011; reopened 2013 after residents' protests.

## 06 Hala Koszyki
- en.wikipedia Koszyki_Hall: Juliusz Dzierżanowski (+ Zygmunt Otto); built 1906–08; opened 2 March 1909; name from wicker baskets that reinforced the Lubomirski Ramparts; Warsaw mermaid cartouches; damaged/burned 1944, steel frame stood; restored 1949; heritage-listed 1965; 2009 Avestus demolished most except two side wings + north arcade wall on Koszykowa; Griffin 2012; JEMS Architekci; reopened Oct 2016.
- JEMS (via brickarchitecture): never fully built to Dzierżanowski's design; only the gateway façades got the Art Nouveau detail.

## 07 Elektrownia Powiśle
- warsawinsider (20 May 2020): unveiled 1904 as Warsaw's main power source; 1955 heating unit installed for the Palace of Culture; deactivated 2001; reopened 2020 after Covid delay; >5,000 preserved artefacts.
- metalocus / archilovers: APA Wojciechowski Architekci; boiler house → retail, engine room → food hall; heritage-listed.
- dzieje.pl (Uprising): 63 employees formed the unit of Capt. Stanisław Skibniewski "Cubryna", held the plant and kept power flowing to the fighting city for over a month; fell 4 September 1944.

## 10 Warszawa Powiśle (bar)

## 15 Pawilon Cepelii
- en.wikipedia Cepelia_Pavilion: Zygmunt Stępiński with Andrzej Milewski, engineer Aleksander Haweman; 1966, beside Hotel Metropol (opened a year earlier); Stanisław Kucharski's turquoise ceramic tiles, each with a geometric pattern; Cepelia = state network of folk/craft cooperatives (1949–1990); declined in 1990s (billboards, Ferment club in basement); McDonald's plan rejected 2019, overturned on appeal, McDonald's withdrew; municipal heritage list 2017, national 2019; restoration by Andrzej M. Chołdzyński from Feb 2024; reopened Dec 2024 as an EMPIK bookshop; neon rooster (Cepelia logo) on Nowogrodzka side; original wavy aluminium façade exposed on first floor.

## 17 Pan Tu Nie Stał
- pantuniestal.com: Koszykowa 34/50, Mon–Sat 11–19, Sun closed; clothes, ceramics, home, books, postcards.
- natemat.pl (12 June 2014): Warsaw shop opening on Koszykowa; founders Justyna Burzyńska and Maciek Lebiedowicz; began in Łódź, clothes designed and sewn there.

## 18 Hala Mirowska
- en.wikipedia Mirów_Halls: Bolesław Miłkowski (+ Ludwik Panczakiewicz façades, Nieniewski, Kozłowski); cornerstone 15 Oct 1899, finished 1902 on the site of the Mier Barracks; Warsaw's first market-hall complex, 515 stalls; 1940 ghetto wall alongside; Aug 1944 burned; 6 Aug captured by Dirlewanger, residents executed; East Hall a bus depot 1948, then boxing (1953 European Amateur Boxing Championships), weightlifting 1959; West Hall back as a market Nov 1962; protected 1986; 2011 West Hall renovation KEPT the bullet holes; East Hall renovated 2017, Feliks Stamm Boxing Museum.

## 19 Bazar Różyckiego
- pl.wikipedia: pharmacist Julian Józef Różycki bought land 1874, opened 1882; c.1901 seven-metre blue kiosk shaped like a soda siphon at the Targowa gate (removed after 1935); WWII: rationed goods, weapons sold by German soldiers bought up for the Underground; ~300 Jewish trading families forced out 1940; burned 1944; nationalised 1950 but private trade carried on — an enclave of the free market in the PRL; 1960s demolition plans fought off; famous for hot flaki and pyzy; heirs took 2/3 back in 2017; city part modernised from 2020 with green corrugated pavilions, vintage shops; heritage-listed 2019.

## 20 Pałac Kultury i Nauki
- en.wikipedia: Lev Rudnev; built 2 May 1952 – 22 July 1955; gift of the USSR; 3,500–5,000 Soviet workers + ~4,000 Polish; 16 died; 237 m to the spire tip; 42 floors; 3,288 rooms; parapets modelled on Renaissance houses of Kraków and Zamość; originally named for Stalin, name removed in destalinisation; 30th-floor terrace at 114 m; clock faces 6.3 m, running from 31 Dec 2000; Congress Hall 3,000 seats; Rolling Stones 1967, first major Western rock band behind the Iron Curtain.

## 21 Muzeum Neonów (Neon Muzeum)
- neonmuzeum.org: founders Ilona Karwińska and David Hill, documenting neon since 2005; museum opened 2012 in Kamionek (Soho Factory); moved to the Palace's 4th floor, Maria Skłodowska-Curie Hall; "City Lights" exhibition from 1 August 2025; signs include Berlin, Szanghaj restaurant, Syrenka, Biedronka milk bar, Cepelia, Sezam, Jaś i Małgosia café; the cow from the Bambino milk bar recreated. Lifts from the -1 cloakroom level.
- dzieje.pl / muzea.waw.pl: the move is a return — first neon show in the Palace almost 20 years earlier.

## 22 Muzeum Sztuki Nowoczesnej
- finestresullarte / AP (washingtontimes) / interiordesign / e-architect: Thomas Phifer and Partners won a 12-firm competition for the museum + TR Warszawa theatre; public opening 25 October 2024 (NOT 2023 — my listing was wrong); inaugural exhibition Feb 2025; white cast-in-place architectural concrete; three storeys, two stacked rectangular forms; double staircase to a glazed ceiling, which Phifer called a social space; 150-seat cinema/auditorium; sits on Plac Defilad facing the Palace; some locals compared it to a bunker; TR Warszawa theatre still to come.

## 23 Zachęta
- en.wikipedia: Stefan Szyller won 1894 competition; built from 1898; front opened Dec 1900; portal sculptures by Zygmunt Otto; "Artibus" on the architrave; Society for the Encouragement of Fine Arts, founded 1860; President Gabriel Narutowicz shot here 16 Dec 1922 by painter Eligiusz Niewiadomski (five days after taking office — NOT in source; do not claim); WWII House of German Culture; 2000 Cattelan "La Nona Ora" (pope struck by meteorite) attacked by MP Witold Tomczak; director Anda Rottenberg dismissed.

## 24 POLIN
- en.wikipedia: Lahdelma & Mahlamäki (Helsinki), won 2005; Kuryłowicz & Associates; building opened 19 April 2013, core exhibition 28 Oct 2014; glass fins + copper mesh; "Polin" silk-screened in Latin and Hebrew letters; Polin = "Poland" or "rest here", legend of the first Jews arriving; undulating main hall = fracture; faces the Monument to the Ghetto Heroes in Muranów, former ghetto; European Museum of the Year 2016.

## 25 Biblioteka Uniwersytecka (BUW) + roof garden
- pl/en.wikipedia: Budzyński & Badowski won the 1993 competition; blessed by John Paul II 11 June 1999; opened to readers 15 Dec 1999; Dobra Street "cultural" façade: eight panels as book pages with Szymanowski music, maths formulas, Sanskrit, Hebrew, Arabic, Greek, Old Russian, Old Polish (Kochanowski, Plato, Ezekiel); bronze open book "Hinc omnia" over entrance; four Lwów–Warsaw School philosophers on columns (Twardowski, Łukasiewicz, Tarski, Leśniewski) by Adam Myjak, at the top of the stairs to the catalogue hall inside; roof garden by Irena Bajerska opened 12 June 2002, over one hectare, upper + lower garden linked by a stream; upper garden open April–October.

## 26 Sąd Najwyższy
- pl.wikipedia (Gmach Sądu Najwyższego): Budzyński & Badowski + team of 21; built 1996 – 11 Nov 1999; postmodern; 76 pillars with greenery on top (architects cited Inca cities); scales-of-justice capitals; 86 Latin + Polish legal maxims chosen by Roman-law scholars under Prof. Witold Wołodkiewicz (e.g. "Nulla poena sine lege"); pillars nearest the Warsaw Uprising Monument carry the Kotwica (Polska Walcząca anchor); three caryatids on the rear for Faith, Hope and Love.

## 27 Warszawa Ochota
- en.wikipedia + tvn24 / conservator: 1962–63 by Arseniusz Romanowicz and Piotr Szymaniak; hyperbolic-paraboloid reinforced-concrete shell on three supports, recreated ceramic tiles; engineer Wacław Zalewski (Spodek in Katowice, Warsaw's Supersam); heritage register (provincial) — 2018/2020 sources differ → say "listed"; at Plac Zawiszy, Al. Jerozolimskie × Towarowa; serves SKM/KM + WKD.
- warsawinsider: Romanowicz also did Powiśle, both Śródmieście stations, Stadion, Wschodnia; co-designed the Polish pavilion at the 1939 New York World's Fair at 28.

## 28 Kościół Wniebowstąpienia Pańskiego (Ursynów)
- en.wikipedia + culture.pl: Marek Budzyński, Zbigniew Badowski (Piotr Wicha listed); design 1980–85; groundbreaking 5 March 1982; Glemp laid stone 28 Nov 1982; lower church opened raw Christmas 1982; upper church opened raw 4 May 1989; consecrated 29 May 2003; 44 × 32 m; brick and concrete; squat, spread-out massing, provincial-Baroque curves, Gothic buttresses, unplastered brick, a great cross on the façade; appears in Kieślowski's Decalogue I.

## 29 Królikarnia
- en.wikipedia + krolikarnia.mnw.art.pl: name from Augustus II's rabbit warren; Domenico Merlini 1782–86 for Carlo (Charles) Thomatis; modelled on Palladio's Villa Rotonda; Kościuszko stayed 1794; fire 1879; destroyed 1944; rebuilt 1964–65; Xawery Dunikowski Museum of Sculpture since 1965, part of the National Museum in Warsaw; sculpture park open daily.

## 30 Muzeum Polskiej Wódki / Koneser

## 32 Rynek Starego Miasta
- en.wikipedia: four sides named after 18th-c. parliamentarians — Dekert (north), Barss (east), Kołłątaj (west), Zakrzewski (south); blown up by the Wehrmacht after the Uprising; rebuilt 1948–53; UNESCO 1980; Konstanty Hegel's mermaid, 1855.
- Guardian (via clippings) / Atlas Obscura / Culture Trip: Bellotto's 18th-c. views used as references in the rebuild; paintings now in the Royal Castle's Canaletto Room; Bellotto took liberties, so the rebuild carries his idealisations.

## 33 Plac Konstytucji (MDM)
- en/pl.wikipedia: MDM built 1950–52, opened 22 July 1952 with the square; architects Józef Sigalin, Stanisław Jankowski, Jan Knothe, Zygmunt Stępiński; three giant candelabra placed to fix the square's symmetry; dozens of worker reliefs; slogan "the people will enter the city centre"; 1953 song "MDM"; MDM Hotel = first hotel opened in Warsaw after the war; Koszykowa 34/50 (Pan tu nie stał's building) has allegories by Józef Gosławski.

## 34 Osiedle Za Żelazną Bramą
- pl.wikipedia: SARP competition 1961 won by Jerzy Czyż, Jan Furman, Andrzej Skopiński, Jerzy Józefowicz, Marek Bieniewski, Stanisław Furman; built 1965–72; 19 sixteen-storey blocks; 25,000+ residents (planned ~18,000); NOT large-panel prefab — cast-in-place monolithic concrete ("Stolica" system); name from the old Iron Gate to the Saxon Garden; area nicknamed "the Wild West" in the 1960s.
- 🔴 My listing said "prefabricated slab blocks" → corrected.

## 35 Katowicka Street, Saska Kępa
- en.wikipedia Katowicka_Street + muratordom + culture.pl: 13 interwar buildings on the heritage register; Lachert & Szanajca triplex at 9/11/11A, 1928–29, financed by Lachert's father, Le Corbusier's five points; Szymon & Helena Syrkus villa at no. 26; Barylski, Oderfeld, Lalewicz; Łaski colony row houses 17–23 by Włodzimierz Gall (1926); Praesens-linked architects; "fortress at the front, open to the garden at the back" (muratordom).

## 36 Bulwary Wiślane
- pl.wikipedia: 1970s concrete boulevards by Zofia Dziewulska, cut off by a busy road; rebuilt in stages: first section opened 2 Aug 2015; Świętokrzyski → Karowa 14 June 2017; Śląsko-Dąbrowski → Karowa 9 Aug 2017; Syrenka section March 2019; old concrete steps with PRL-era anti-communist inscriptions survive; IAHR Industry Innovation Award 2021.
- sarp / wprost: designed by RS Architektura Krajobrazu (competition winner).
- pik.warszawa.pl: sections named for patrons incl. the Vistula Flotilla, Patton, Jan Karski, Zbigniew Religa; Copernicus Science Centre, Multimedia Fountain Park nearby.

## 37 Złota 44
- en.wikipedia: Daniel Libeskind with Artchitecture; 192 m, 52 storeys; started 2007, stalled 2009, resumed Jan 2011, topped out 3 Feb 2012, completed 2016/17; inspired by an eagle's wing; nicknamed Żagiel ("sail"); 25 m pool, wine cellar for 10,000 bottles; ORCO sold it unfinished 2014 for €50m.
- en.wikipedia Libeskind: born Łódź 12 May 1946 to Holocaust survivors; left Poland 1957.
