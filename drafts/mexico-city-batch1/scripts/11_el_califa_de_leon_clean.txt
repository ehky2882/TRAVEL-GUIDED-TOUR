El Califa de León

This little stand in San Rafael sells three kinds of taco from a counter only a few metres wide, and for two years it held a Michelin star, the first taquería in the world to win one.

Number fifty-six on Ribera de San Cosme is barely wider than a garage door, so look for the narrow opening on the busy avenue, with a steel griddle running almost its entire width, a taquero working the meat in front of you, and, more often than not, a queue on the pavement waiting its turn.

The star is the gaonera, named after Rodolfo Gaona, the celebrated Mexican bullfighter of the early twentieth century. It's beef fillet sliced thin, seasoned with nothing more than salt and a squeeze of lime, seared on the griddle and folded into a fresh corn tortilla, and the man who has run that griddle for around two decades, Arturo Rivera Martínez, says the secret is simply the simplicity.

Alongside it you'll find bistec and chuleta, steak and pork chop, with red or green salsa to finish, and that's more or less the whole menu, which has barely changed since the stand opened in 1968.

The Michelin Guide gave El Califa its star in May 2024, the first time it had reviewed Mexico at all, and in May 2026 the star was withdrawn, although the stand remains on the guide's list of recommended places. What's worth holding on to is that the taco didn't change in either direction, because the gaonera tastes the same now as it did the week before the inspectors arrived.

[beat]

Order a gaonera first and eat it standing, which is how it's meant to be done, then, if you fancy a walk afterwards, the Museo Universitario del Chopo, an iron-and-glass hall shipped over from Germany at the turn of the twentieth century, is a short stroll away.
