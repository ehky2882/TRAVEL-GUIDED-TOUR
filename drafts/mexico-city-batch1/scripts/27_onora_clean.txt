Onora

Everything in this shop was made by hand somewhere in Mexico, and yet hardly any of it looks the way you might expect Mexican craft to look, which is exactly what its founders set out to do.

Peer through the front windows at Lope de Vega three three zero, in Polanco, and you should see a calm, uncluttered room in muted beiges, blacks and greys, with woven throws and cushions, pottery on low tables and lacquered gourds catching the light.

Onora was started in 2014 by María Eladia Hagerman, who is Mexican, and Maggie Galton, from New York, and they work with family workshops and communities in more than ten states, including Oaxaca, Chiapas, Puebla and Michoacán.

Look at the colours first, because they're the key to the whole place. Rather than the bright pinks and turquoises of a typical craft market, the founders asked artisans for a pared-back palette, so that a Talavera plate from Puebla or an embroidered tunic from Oaxaca reads as something you'd put in a minimalist flat rather than a souvenir.

Then the making, since you'll find black pottery, rebozo shawls, wool throws, hammocks and stone molcajetes for grinding salsa, each piece made using techniques that go back generations in the place it comes from.

Scale is the other thing they've thought hard about, since Onora keeps its orders deliberately small so that the artisans can carry on farming and taking part in the life of their villages, which means the business is shaped around the makers' lives rather than the other way round.

Ask the staff which pieces come from Michoacán, home to some of the most striking pottery in the shop, then head west along the Polanco streets to Alejandro Dumas, where the perfume house Xinú bottles Mexico's plants in a very different way.
