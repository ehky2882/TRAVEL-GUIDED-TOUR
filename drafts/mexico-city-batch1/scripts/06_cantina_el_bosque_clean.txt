Cantina El Bosque

It's 1937, and on a corner in San Miguel Chapultepec someone opens a little cantina that also sells tortas, the kind of place where a man might stop for a drink and a sandwich on his way home, and it has been pouring ever since.

Find the corner where Calle Trece de Septiembre meets the street, and the cantina should be the low building facing you, with its terrace spilling onto the pavement, wooden furniture inside, and, from about five in the afternoon, the sound of a live trio drifting out of the door.

Begin with the drinks, because the house cocktail is the Cascada, built on vodka, lime and something sparkling, and it's the thing regulars order without looking at the menu. Alongside it come the botanas, the small free dishes that arrive with your drinks and change from day to day, and on Sundays that usually means chicharrón in green sauce.

The kitchen has grown up around the cantina classics, so there's a salt-baked fish meant for sharing, a breaded milanesa, and tacos of braised beef shank, plus a potato-and-cheese dish that started as one customer's request and simply never left the menu.

Here's the thing about a cantina like this one, which closed during the pandemic, took on new partners and reopened in 2021. Places this old usually change hands quietly or vanish, and what's notable is how little it seems to have changed in the process, because the corner, the trio and the free botanas all came through intact.
