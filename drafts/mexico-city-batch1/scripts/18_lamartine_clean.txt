Lamartine Boutique Hotel Polanco

Ten rooms isn't much of a hotel, by Polanco standards, and that is very much the point of the slim building in front of you.

Stand across the road from the slim new building on Lamartine, the quiet Polanco street it's named after, and look up past the entrance to a veil of fine metal mesh drawn across the upper floors, with the shapes of balconies just visible behind it.

That mesh is the first thing to notice, since it's doing three jobs at once, giving the guests privacy, letting them see out over the street, and shading the glass from the high-altitude sun so the rooms stay cool.

The hotel was designed by the Mexico City studio PPAA, the practice of Pablo Pérez Palacios, and it opened in 2026 on an awkward, narrow plot that left very little room for daylight. The answer was a skylight at the very centre of the building, with a metal-clad staircase beneath it that bounces the light down through every floor.

Inside it's all beige textured plaster, pale wood and floors of volcanic stone, with a garden at the back and a system for harvesting rainwater, which is the kind of detail you rarely see in a hotel this small.

The street name hides a nice detail, because Alphonse de Lamartine was a French Romantic poet, and Polanco is laid out as a sort of library, with streets named after writers and thinkers, from Homer and Horace to Petrarch and Dumas.

What PPAA have done here, and done well, is build something new that doesn't shout, which in a neighbourhood full of glossy towers is a rather confident thing to do.

Wander a few blocks from here and read the street signs as you go, and you'll find Alejandro Dumas, Petrarca and Edgar Allan Poe all within easy walking distance, with shops and bars tucked into the old houses along each one.
