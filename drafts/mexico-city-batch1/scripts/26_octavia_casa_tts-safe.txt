Oktahvia Kahsa

This hotel is also a clothes shop, or perhaps it's a clothes shop with seven bedrooms upstairs, and the people who made it seem quite happy for you to decide which.

Number one two six on Amatlahn, a tree lined street in Eepodromo Kondaysa, has a narrow facade of glass screened by a row of slim vertical timber louvres, with a deep metal awning jutting out over the entrance below.

Oktahvia is a Mexican fashion label, and its founder, Robairta Masayda, took an old, run down Art Deco building on this street and turned it into a small hotel that works as a showcase for the brand's way of seeing.

The architect was Pahblo Pairess Palahsios, whose studio, pee pee ay ay, had to work with a plot so narrow that only one wall faced the street, and the answer was those timber louvres, which filter the light and give the rooms privacy, while on the ground floor glass panels and screens slide along tracks so the front can open to the street.

Inside, everything is organised around a central courtyard, which is the hotel lobby and the shop at the same time, set with wicker furniture, terracotta vases and plants, in a palette of ochre, grey and warm wood, and at the top there's a terrace on the roof.

Cleverly, the building does the brand's job without a logo in sight, so if you like the way the rooms feel, you're already halfway to liking the clothes, which is a much subtler kind of shop window.

Two minutes' walk away, Aveneeda Amsterdam curves in a long oval because it follows the line of a racetrack that once stood here, which is how the neighbourhood got the name Eepodromo, and loop round it under the trees.
