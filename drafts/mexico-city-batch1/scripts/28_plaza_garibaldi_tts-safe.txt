Plahsa Garibaldi

At almost any hour, several bands are playing in this square at once, a few metres apart, each trumpet section doing its best to drown out the next, and somehow it all comes out as one great noise of welcome.

Off the Ehay Sentrahl, on the northern edge of the old centre, step into the open square and turn slowly, with the mareeahcheess in their silver studded charro suits gathered in groups around you, bars and dance halls lining the edges, and a row of standing figures of famous singers along one side.

The square got its name in nineteen twenty one, after Joozeppay Garibaldi, grandson of the Italian hero, who fought in the Mexican Revolution, and the music arrived soon afterwards, when the Salonn Tenampa opened around nineteen twenty three and brought in a mareeahchee band from Kokoola, in Haleesko, the region where the style was born.

The Tenampa is still here, on the square, with murals inside celebrating the plaza's musical life, and the golden age of Mexican cinema did the rest, with stars like Paydro Eenfantay and Hosay Alfraydo Heemayness filmed singing here, which is why their figures now stand among the others in the square.

The system is simple, because the musicians are independent, so you agree a price with a band for a song, or for a stretch of time, and they'll play it right there for you, at your table or in the middle of the plaza.

Garibaldi works, in other words, as a hiring hall and a public jukebox at once, where many of the most skilled musicians in the country wait to be chosen, and where a love song can be bought for the price of a decent lunch.

If you're hungry, the Mairkahdo day San Kameeleeto, just off the square, keeps its food stalls going around the clock with broths and dishes from Haleesko, and there's a museum of tequila and mezkahl on the plaza for a little background with your drink.
