Beeblioteka Vaskonsaylos

Look up, then keep looking up, because the shelves in this library don't sit on the floor at all, they hang in the air above you like the stacks of some enormous filing system that has been pulled apart and left floating.

Walk in past the security desk and carry on until the building opens out overhead into one long, tall hall, crossed by metal walkways and lined on both sides with suspended bookcases, and somewhere near the middle, slung in the air, you should see the white skeleton of a whale.

The building came first, opening in May two thousand and six, designed by the architect Albairto Kalatch, and it holds around six hundred thousand books, with room planned for a couple of million more, while the glass walls let in so much daylight that you'll notice the lights barely seem to matter on a bright afternoon.

Now the whale, which is a real one, a young grey whale that washed up on the coast of Baha California. The artist Gabriel Orosko had its bones assembled over two months in the train station lobby next door, then drew across them in graphite, tracing arcs and circles with a compass so that the lines map how the animal once moved, and the piece is called Maytrix Mohveel.

The library is named for Hosay Vaskonsaylos, the philosopher and education minister who argued, a century ago, that reading should belong to everyone, and the building takes him at his word. Nobody asks you why you've come, you don't need a card to wander, and the whole place feels designed for drifting rather than for finding.

Before you leave, find the doors out to the back, where a botanical garden of around twenty six thousand square metres wraps round the building, and on a Saturday the Teeangeess Kooltoorahl del Chopo, the city's long running alternative street market, sets up a short walk away.
