Kanteena el Boskay

It's nineteen thirty seven, and on a corner in San Meegel Chapooltepek someone opens a little cantina that also sells tortas, the kind of place where a man might stop for a drink and a sandwich on his way home, and it has been pouring ever since.

Find the corner where Kahyay Traysay day Septyembray meets the street, and the cantina should be the low building facing you, with its terrace spilling onto the pavement, wooden furniture inside, and, from about five in the afternoon, the sound of a live trio drifting out of the door.

Begin with the drinks, because the house cocktail is the Kaskahda, built on vodka, lime and something sparkling, and it's the thing regulars order without looking at the menu. Alongside it come the botahnas, the small free dishes that arrive with your drinks and change from day to day, and on Sundays that usually means cheecharronn in green sauce.

The kitchen has grown up around the cantina classics, so there's a salt baked fish meant for sharing, a breaded meelanaysa, and tacos of braised beef shank, plus a potato and cheese dish that started as one customer's request and simply never left the menu.

Here's the thing about a cantina like this one, which closed during the pandemic, took on new partners and reopened in twenty twenty one. Places this old usually change hands quietly or vanish, and what's notable is how little it seems to have changed in the process, because the corner, the trio and the free botahnas all came through intact.

When you've finished, the gallery Kooreemanzooto is only a few streets away in the same neighbourhood, so you can walk off lunch among some of the most talked about contemporary art in Mexico, which feels like exactly the sort of contrast this part of the city does well.
