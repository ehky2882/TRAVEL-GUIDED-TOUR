Sandra Wail

This shop is laid out like a walk through a landscape, from forest to marble quarry to garden, and the clothes are almost the last thing you notice.

Parkay Amaireeka's trees should be behind you, and across Aveneeda Orahsio, at number nine zero seven, you'll be facing a former private house with a little balcony overflowing with plants above the entrance.

Sandra Wail is a Peruvian designer who has built her label in Mexico City, known for elegant, delicate pieces in natural fabrics, and the shop will alter any design to fit, which is a quietly old fashioned service in a neighbourhood of big international brands.

The house itself was reworked by the Mexico City architects pee pee ay ay, who built everything around a spiral staircase winding up through the middle, and each set of rooms has its own colour, its own materials and its own character.

The brown rooms come first, with jute on the floor, textured walls and a whole tree trunk hanging horizontally from the ceiling, with handbags dangling from it like fruit.

Next come the white rooms, finished in pale marble, where a single grey boulder sits reflected over and over in mirrors, and finally the green rooms, where potted plants double as hat stands and old tree stumps serve as display pedestals.

All of this is selling a feeling rather than a garment, the sense of clothes that belong to the natural world, and it does it so confidently that walking through feels more like visiting an installation than going shopping.

Once you've climbed to the top of the staircase, step out onto the little balcony if it's open, look down over Parkay Amaireeka, then head across the road and find a bench under the trees.
