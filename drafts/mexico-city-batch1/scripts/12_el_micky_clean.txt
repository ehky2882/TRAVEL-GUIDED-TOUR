El Micky

The name, according to the people behind it, comes from Miguel Hidalgo, the borough you're standing in, and the cartoon mouse in the logo arrived by happy accident, courtesy of the designer.

After dark, the building at Avenida Jalisco one six five, in Tacubaya, gives itself away with white walls washed in purple neon, music thumping through two storeys, and a queue that starts forming on the pavement well before ten.

El Micky began life around 2022 in a smaller space nearby and filled up so quickly that within a couple of years it had moved here, to a bigger two-floor building, with a sister room called La Minni opening alongside as a place to eat before the dancing starts.

The food is the surprise, because the owner's mother is from Chiapas, in the far south, and the kitchen leans on that, so there's a plato botanero of refried beans, garnachas and Chiapas-style quesadillas that's built for sharing across a crowded table.

Then there's the drink that everyone photographs, the gomichela, a litre of beer in a plastic cup, rimmed with chamoy, the sweet-sour chilli sauce, and dotted with gummy sweets, and alongside it come the bright blue cocktails the regulars call azulitos.

Strip away the neon and you've got a cantina remixed for a new crowd, with plastic cups and Chiapas home cooking on one side and a playlist that lurches from pop to regional Mexican to reggaeton on the other, and the artists, actors and designers who fill it seem to love it for exactly that high-low mix.

[beat]

It opens from Wednesday to Saturday, from seven in the evening, so arrive early, start at La Minni with a plate of those quesadillas, and work your way next door to the dance floor once the room has properly filled up.
