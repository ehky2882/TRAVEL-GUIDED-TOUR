el Tooreesh

Tooreesh is the Maya word for dragonfly, which is a delicate name for a place that does one heavy, glorious thing, slow cooked pork, and does almost nothing else.

Halfway along Emeelio Kastelar, at number two one two, look for a tiny shopfront a couple of blocks from Parkay Lincoln, with a counter facing the pavement, a glass case of glistening meat, and people standing outside eating off paper, since there's nowhere at all to sit down.

That meat is kocheeneeta peebeel, the great dish of the Yookatahn peninsula, pork marinated in acheeohtay paste and bitter orange juice and then slow cooked until it falls apart, and it comes topped with pink pickled onions and, if you're brave, a fiery abanairo salsa.

You'll get it three ways, in a taco, in a torta, which is a crusty bread roll, or in a panoocho, a thick tortilla stuffed with refried beans and fried until crisp, and it's the panoocho that people tend to come back for.

The family who run it came here from Yookatahn, and they've been serving the same short menu in this spot for around thirty years, through every change Polanko has gone through in that time.

The nice irony is the setting, because you're in one of the most expensive neighbourhoods in the country, a few steps from luxury boutiques, and one of its most loved lunches is eaten standing on the kerb or perched on the edge of a planter, with lawyers, builders and shop assistants all elbow to elbow.

Afterwards, walk the two blocks to Parkay Lincoln, find the clock tower and the long reflecting pools, and settle on a bench to work out whether you've room for a second panoocho after all.
