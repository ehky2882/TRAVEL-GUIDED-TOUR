Hotel CondesaDF

On a clear evening, from the roof of this triangular building, you can see the trees of Parque España below you and, far beyond them, the castle on Chapultepec hill.

With Parque España at your back, on the corner of Avenida Veracruz and Guadalajara, look up at a pale building shaped like a wedge, with tall windows, wrought-iron balconies and a little greenery trailing over the top floor.

It was built in 1928 as an apartment block of twelve flats for well-off couples, in the French style that was fashionable in the Condesa at the time, when the neighbourhood was being laid out on the grounds of an old hacienda.

Grupo Habita bought it in 2001 and opened it as a hotel in 2005, with the architect Javier Sánchez restoring the building and the Paris designer India Mahdavi doing the interiors, down to the furniture in the rooms and the cutlery in the restaurant.

There are forty rooms now, arranged around a courtyard where the restaurant, El Patio, serves Mexican food with a French accent, and the Sunday brunch has become a neighbourhood fixture, drawing locals as well as guests.

Then there's the roof, the reason many people come, a triangular terrace with a small sushi bar that sits rather incongruously among the plants and the treetops, given the bustle a few floors below.

The real story is that this hotel arrived just as the Condesa was becoming the neighbourhood of choice for a new generation of Mexico City, and it became one of the places where that change could be seen, and drunk, and argued about, all in one room.

Cross straight into Parque España as you leave and walk through it, then carry on south into the bigger Parque México, where the Art Deco apartment blocks around the edge are some of the best in the city.
