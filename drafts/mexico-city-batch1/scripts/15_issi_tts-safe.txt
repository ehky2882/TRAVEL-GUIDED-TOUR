Eesee

Eesee was a childhood nickname, and the woman who chose it for her restaurant, Isabella Romairo, is happy to tell you she isn't a chef at all, but that food is simply the way she shows people she cares.

Maiyorga is a short, easily missed residential street in Lomass day Chapooltepek, and the restaurant sits at number one eighteen, a small, modern dining room behind glass, warmly lit and set out more like a family kitchen than a restaurant.

Begin with the menu, which is built around comfort food, things like fish and chips or a spicy bee el tee, given a careful, grown up treatment and shifting with the seasons, so it's always worth asking what's new.

Then there are the dishes that come from her own life, and the montadeeto day pahpa is the one to know, three neat cubes of layered potato with beans, chorizo and sour cream, all of them things she ate for breakfast as a child.

Next to it sits a carpaccio of raw chaiyotay, the pale green squash you'll see in every Mexican market, sliced paper thin with cherry tomatoes, hazelnuts and alfalfa sprouts, which is a family recipe passed down rather than invented for the menu.

Lomass is a neighbourhood of high walls, long driveways and very few people out walking, and what Eesee tries to do is give it a place that works the other way, somewhere you might walk to on a weekday evening and find your neighbours already at the next table.

If it's your first visit, order the montadeeto day pahpa to start, keep the chaiyotay for the middle of the meal, and book ahead, because the room is small, it's closed on Mondays, and on Sundays it only serves lunch.
