Casa Bosques

A bookshop that also makes its own chocolate sounds like a whim, and yet Casa Bosques has turned that pairing into one of the most quietly influential shops in the city.

Córdoba twenty-five, Roma Norte, a protected house built in the early nineteen hundreds: its facade and front door face you across the pavement, and inside there are white walls, pale shelves and plants in nearly every corner, with books laid face-out on long tables rather than squeezed spine-first onto shelves.

Start with what's on those tables, which leans towards art, design and architecture, alongside independent magazines and small-press publications from around the world, grouped by ideas such as migration, feminism or community rather than by the usual categories. The founders, Jorge de la Garza and Rafael Prieto, have said they want people to discover rather than search, and the layout is built to make that happen.

The building itself is a catalogued heritage house, with a chunky concrete staircase and windows that owe something to Frank Lloyd Wright, and the white interior was shaped by the design studio Savvy, so the architecture does a lot of the browsing for you.

Then there are the chocolates, made in collaboration with artists and designers, each with its own wrapper and often a suggested drink to go alongside, which makes them rather more like small editions than sweets.

The thread running through all of it is a belief that objects should be made with the same care as the books, which is why the same people also run the Index Art Book Fair every September, bringing independent publishers to the city for a few busy days.

When you leave, ask for whichever chocolate bar is newest, then walk the three blocks to Plaza Río de Janeiro, the leafy Roma square with a bronze copy of Michelangelo's David at its centre, and find a bench to unwrap it.
