LAGO

Look at the roof first, because it's the whole reason this building is famous, a great curved sheet of concrete that dips in the middle and sweeps up at the edges towards the lake.

On the shore of the Lago Mayor, in the Second Section of Chapultepec, with the water on one side of you and the low glass building under its saddle-shaped roof on the other, you should be able to see diners on the terrace and the lake stretching out behind them.

That roof is what engineers call a hyperbolic paraboloid, and it was designed in 1964 by Alfonso Ramírez Ponce, who was just twenty-four at the time, working with Leónides Guadarrama, and he had studied under Félix Candela, the master of thin concrete shells.

For decades this was the Restaurante del Lago, one of the city's grand places for a special occasion, but a renovation in the late nineties carved up the open interior, and after the pandemic it closed for good.

In 2022 it reopened as Lago Algo, after the architects of the studio Naso stripped back the later additions, and it now has two halves, LAGO, a restaurant and café cooking with local and sustainable ingredients, and ALGO, a free contemporary art gallery programmed by Galería OMR.

What's changed, and it matters, is who the building is for. It used to be a place for a smart dinner with a view, and now you can wander in off a running path, look at new art for nothing and sit by the lake with a coffee, which makes it feel like it finally belongs to the park.

Before you go inside, walk a little way along the shore so you can see the roof side-on, where the curve is clearest, and if you've got your trainers on, the Pista El Sope running track loops through the trees nearby.
