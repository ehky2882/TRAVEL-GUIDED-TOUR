Seenko Jazz Club

The door you're about to walk through was built to keep people out, because this jazz club sits inside the old vault of a bank.

Outside Motoleeneea twenty, on the corner of Seenko day Maio in the historic centre, you should be looking at a handsome old stone building with a modest entrance and a stairway leading down below street level.

Head down those stairs and you'll meet the heavy steel doors of the original vault, swung open now, and beyond them a low room of black walls, exposed brick and dark crimson upholstery, with small round tables crowded towards a little stage.

The vault does wonders for the sound, since the thick walls shut out the traffic above and keep the acoustics close and warm, so the bands can play with very little amplification, and the instruments sound much as they would in your living room.

The programme runs from traditional jazz and bebop through to funk and contemporary fusion, with Mexican musicians and touring players from abroad, and the club has long been one of the places that proves the city has a serious jazz scene of its own.

There's a cover charge, which goes towards paying the musicians, and with so few tables in a space this size, it's well worth booking ahead, then arriving early to claim a seat near the front.

Seenko's best trick is the swap, because a room that was built to guard money has ended up guarding something much harder to keep, which is a good night of live music.

When the set finishes and you climb back up to the street, walk a few blocks east to the Sokalo, which is at its most atmospheric late at night, with the cathedral lit up and the great square almost empty.
