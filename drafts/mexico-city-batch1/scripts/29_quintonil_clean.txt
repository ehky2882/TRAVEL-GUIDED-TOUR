Quintonil

Quintonil is a wild green, a leafy kind of amaranth that grows almost as a weed in Mexican cornfields, and it's also the name of one of the most celebrated restaurants in the world.

Very little announces the restaurant at Newton fifty-five, in Polanco, just a modest frontage that gives almost nothing away, and inside a small, warm dining room, its floors of dark volcanic stone and walls clad in wood.

The restaurant was opened in March 2012 by the chef Jorge Vallejo and Alejandra Flores, who runs the dining room, and the two of them had met a few years earlier working at Pujol, which is about as good a training ground as Mexico offers.

It began as a fairly casual place serving set lunches, and grew into fine dining over the years, but it has kept the scale small, with only around forty-two seats, so it still feels more like being looked after than being performed at.

The cooking is almost entirely Mexican in its ingredients, around ninety-eight per cent by Vallejo's reckoning, with vegetables and herbs from Xochimilco and Milpa Alta, at the city's southern edge, pork from Michoacán and Yucatán, and fish from Baja California, so a tasting menu here is also a map of the country.

It was awarded two Michelin stars in 2024, and in 2025 it was ranked third on the World's Fifty Best Restaurants list, which is rather a lot of attention for a place named after a weed, although the name, I suspect, is exactly the point, because it puts the humblest ingredient in the field at the very head of the menu.

When you step back out, look at the street signs around you, because this corner of Polanco is named after scientists, and within a block or two you'll find Kepler, Galileo and Arquímedes keeping Newton company.
