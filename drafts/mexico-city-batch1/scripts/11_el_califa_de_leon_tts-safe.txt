el Kaleefa day Layon

This little stand in San Rafa ell sells three kinds of taco from a counter only a few metres wide, and for two years it held a Michelin star, the first takaireea in the world to win one.

Number fifty six on Reebaira day San Kozmay is barely wider than a garage door, so look for the narrow opening on the busy avenue, with a steel griddle running almost its entire width, a taquero working the meat in front of you, and, more often than not, a queue on the pavement waiting its turn.

The star is the gahonaira, named after Rodolfo Gahona, the celebrated Mexican bullfighter of the early twentieth century. It's beef fillet sliced thin, seasoned with nothing more than salt and a squeeze of lime, seared on the griddle and folded into a fresh corn tortilla, and the man who has run that griddle for around two decades, Artooro Reevaira Marteeness, says the secret is simply the simplicity.

Alongside it you'll find beestek and choolayta, steak and pork chop, with red or green salsa to finish, and that's more or less the whole menu, which has barely changed since the stand opened in nineteen sixty eight.

The Michelin Guide gave el Kaleefa its star in May twenty twenty four, the first time it had reviewed Mexico at all, and in May twenty twenty six the star was withdrawn, although the stand remains on the guide's list of recommended places. What's worth holding on to is that the taco didn't change in either direction, because the gahonaira tastes the same now as it did the week before the inspectors arrived.

Order a gahonaira first and eat it standing, which is how it's meant to be done, then, if you fancy a walk afterwards, the Mooseo Ooneebairseetahrio del Chopo, an iron and glass hall shipped over from Germany at the turn of the twentieth century, is a short stroll away.
