The Kisswa Factory of the Holy Kahbah

Every year the Kahbah is dressed in new clothes. This is the workshop that makes them.

The King Abdul Azeez Complex for the Holy Kahbah Kisswa is in Oom al Jood, about ten kilometres from the Harahm, beside the Exhibition of the Two Holy Mosques Architecture. Guided visits walk you through the production halls, so you should find yourself among looms, embroidery frames and long tables of black silk.

Start with the silk itself. The kisswa uses about six hundred and seventy kilograms of white silk thread, dyed black. Jakard machines weave it into forty seven panels, each fourteen metres long and just under a metre wide, with the declaration of faith woven into the pattern.

Then find the embroiderers, because this is the part that is done by hand. More than two hundred artisans work here, and the Koranic verses are stitched in gold and silver thread: around a hundred and twenty kilograms of twenty one carat gold thread, and a hundred of silver. The band two thirds of the way up the Kahbah, the hizaam, is forty seven metres long. The finished kisswa covers six hundred and fifty eight square metres and costs about twenty two million riyals to make.

What's worth knowing is how recently this all came here. For centuries the kisswa was made in Egypt and carried to Makkah in a ceremonial procession. Production began moving to Makkah in nineteen twenty seven, became fully local in nineteen sixty two, and moved into this complex in nineteen seventy seven. In two thousand and twenty four, for the first time in recorded history, women took part in the ceremony of replacing it.

The complex opens to visitors on weekday mornings, so plan to arrive early. Afterwards, look closely at the corners of the Kahbah the next time you're in the mosque. The roundels of gold embroidery you'll see there were stitched in this building.
