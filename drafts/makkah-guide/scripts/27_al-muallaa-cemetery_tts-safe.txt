al Moo alla Cemetery

There are no grand tombs here, and that is the whole point.

Jannat al Moo alla lies on the northern approach to the Grand Mosque, in the al Hajoon district, beside the Masjid al Jinn. Through the gates you should see a broad, walled hillside of plain ground, the graves marked by little more than low heaps of stones.

Start with who is buried here. This was a burial ground before Islam, used by the Koraish and the old families of Makkah. Khadeeja, the first wife of the Prophet Muhammad and the first person to accept his message, is buried here, as are his uncle Aboo Taalib and his grandfather Abdul Muttalib, who raised him.

Then imagine what once stood here. In the seventeenth century, the Ottoman traveller Evliya Chelebi counted seventy five domed tombs in this cemetery. In nineteen twenty five and nineteen twenty six, after the Hijaaz came under the rule of Ibn Saood, those structures were removed, as part of a wider programme intended to discourage the veneration of graves. The same happened at the Bakee cemetery in Madinah.

And here's what's worth sitting with. To some, the bare ground is a loss, and it is still mourned. To others it is the point: the grave of the Prophet's beloved wife marked no differently from anyone else's. Either way, this is one of the few places in central Makkah where nothing has been built up at all.

If you cannot go in, stand at the gates and look up the hillside. And then walk back south towards the Harahm, on the road that the people buried here once walked themselves.
