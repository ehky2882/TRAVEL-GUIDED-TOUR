Mecca Gate

You will probably pass under this monument at a hundred kilometres an hour. It is still the front door to the city.

The Mecca Gate spans the Jeddah to Makkah expressway on the western approach to the city. As you drive east, look ahead and up: a great concrete open book, the Koran, resting on a carved book stand, straddling the carriageway.

Start with the idea. The book stand is a rihaal, the X shaped stand that holds a Koran while it is being read. Designer Deeya Azeez Deeya scaled that domestic object up to span a motorway, and he worked with architects, engineers and calligraphers to finish it. The architect was Sameer el Abd, and the gate was built in nineteen seventy nine, in reinforced concrete, with mosaics and stained glass beneath its arches and lights for after dark.

Then what it means. The gate marks the boundary of the harahm, the sacred precinct of Makkah. Beyond this point, non Muslims are not permitted to enter.

And here's the thing. Most city gates are walls with a hole in them, built to keep people out. This one is built from the object at the centre of the faith, and its message is not defence, but welcome, to those it welcomes.

If someone else is driving, look up through the windscreen as you pass beneath the open pages. It is the briefest of tours, and for most people it is the first moment they know they have arrived.
