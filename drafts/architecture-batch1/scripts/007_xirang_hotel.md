# Xirang Hotel, Yixing

**Place:** Dayouqiu, Zhoutie, Yixing, Wuxi, Jiangsu, China (by Taihu Lake)  
**Coordinate (WGS-84):** 31.43710, 119.99948 (area only: Zhoutie town. Get a map pin before use)  
**Length:** 327 words (about 131 seconds)

## Script (clean)

Nine courtyards, one long covered walkway, and not a single grand gesture. That restraint is the whole point.

Start on the north side, by the Shatanggang Canal, and look along the water's edge. You should see a tall tower of glass bricks rising beside the canal, twenty-four metres high, and behind it a cluster of low brick buildings whose eaves step down in tiers. The tower is where that staircase of roofs comes to an end.

The roofs are stepping down from Zhushan mountain, which is to the south. The hotel answers its surroundings on all four sides: Taihu Lake to the east, the canal to the north, the mountain to the south and a community market to the west. Vector Architects, led by Dong Gong, laid the ground along the original slope, so it drops gently east towards a pond, and you feel the hillside under your feet as you walk through.

Now the courtyards. The architects call the plan a juxtaposed maze, and the courtyard is its basic unit. There are nine at ground level, strung along a covered verandah from the western edge to the east, each with its own job and mood. The western courtyard holds a series of small gardens in full daylight. The largest, the Camphor Courtyard, opens towards the lake, and the corridors to the guest rooms beside it are screened in double-height copper mesh.

The heart of it is the Raindrop Atrium, a circular bronze funnel sixteen metres high, which gathers rainwater through its eaves. It sits in the middle, and courtyards open off it on all four sides.

[beat]

Plenty of country hotels try to impress you. This one would rather you noticed the weather.

If you're staying, find the Raindrop Atrium on a wet afternoon and simply watch it work. If you're not, walk along the canal at dusk, when the glass in that tower picks up the changing colours of the sky.
