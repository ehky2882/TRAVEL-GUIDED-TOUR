# Oslo Opera House

**Place:** Kirsten Flagstads plass 1, Bjørvika, Oslo, Norway  
**Coordinate (WGS-84):** 59.90752, 10.75268 (exact)  
**Length:** 347 words (about 139 seconds)

## Script (clean)

Most opera houses keep you off the roof. This one practically insists you walk on it.

Stand at the foot of the long white slope that rises from the water's edge. You should see a great sheet of white stone tilting gently up from the fjord towards a tall white tower, with a band of tall glass below it, and people strolling, sitting and sunbathing all over the top.

Start with the slope. It's a public square, set at an angle. The architects, Snøhetta, designed the whole roof so anyone can walk up it at any time, no ticket, no queue, and the view from the top looks out over the city and the fjord. It's clad mostly in white Carrara marble, the Italian stone Michelangelo carved, along with white granite.

Now look at where the stone meets the water. The building seems to slide down into the fjord, like an iceberg or a glacier meeting the sea. And out in the water, look for a strange, tilted structure of steel and glass panels. That's She Lies, a sculpture by Monica Bonvicini, which turns and shifts on its platform with the wind and the tide.

Then look up at the tower. It's the stage tower, clad in white aluminium. Below it, the tall windows of the foyer face the water, and inside, warm oak covers the walls. The main hall seats one thousand three hundred and sixty-four.

It opened on the twelfth of April, two thousand and eight, as the home of the Norwegian National Opera and Ballet, and, unusually for a grand building, it came in ahead of schedule and under budget.

[beat]

What I love is how democratic it is. Plenty of people who have never seen an opera have stood on its roof. That isn't a side effect. It's the whole design.

Walk right up to the top before you go, and look east along the harbour. You'll see Munch's museum nearby, and all the new waterfront that this one white slope helped to start.
