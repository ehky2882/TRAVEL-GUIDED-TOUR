AYDC Public Art Center, Guiyang

The name comes from the language of the Yi people of Guizhou, and it means, roughly, our land of dreams. Under the karst mountains, someone has built three stone pavilions to dream in.

Stand in the public square and look around. You should see three separate buildings in pale travertine stone, square and solid from outside, their surfaces finely perforated, set into a landscape at the foot of the steep, green karst hills that Guizhou is famous for.

Start with the stone. All three pavilions share the same skin: square blocks of travertine, pierced with small holes so wind and light can filter through. From the outside they look calm and almost identical. Inside, each one is completely different.

Now find the first, the Xima Library, beside a reflecting pool. Its stone mass has been carved out to hold a metal dome, and inside, curved stainless-steel plates soften the daylight into a glow, so you read in a kind of silver light.

Then walk a little way uphill to the Ginkgo Chapel. Four great stone volumes, shaped after ginkgo leaves, hold up a vaulted space that's open to the sky. It isn't a church in the religious sense. It's a place to sit and be quiet.

And back by the square is the Dali Stage, facing the open space. Its shell is shaped like the inside-out of an arch, with mirror-polished steel and fibre-optic lights set into the floor, for performances and gatherings.

It's by Atelier XI, and it started as a plan for a single building before growing into this little family of three, built in phases from twenty twenty-two.

I like that it's three small buildings rather than one big one. Reading, quiet and performance each get their own room, and you walk through the landscape to get from one to the next. It's a village for the arts, rather than an institution.

Before you go, climb up to the Ginkgo Chapel and look out through the open roof at the karst peaks. It's the best seat in the place.
