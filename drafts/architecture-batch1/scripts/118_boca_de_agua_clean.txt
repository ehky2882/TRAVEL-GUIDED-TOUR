Boca de Agua, Bacalar

The lagoon here is called the lake of seven colours, because its water shifts from turquoise to deep blue. The hotel was built up in the trees, so as not to get in the way.

Follow the paths through the jungle until you see the rooms. You should see a scatter of dark wooden cabins raised high on stilts among the trees, with steeply sloping roofs, reached by pale concrete stairs with fine metal railings, and glimpses of the bright water of the lagoon beyond.

Start with the stilts. The rooms are raised like tree houses, so the ground below stays almost untouched, with the plants and animals carrying on underneath. The architect, Frida Escobedo, from Mexico City, set twenty-two of these pavilions through the site.

Now look at the wood. It's chicozapote, a dark, dense local hardwood, from a certified sustainable source, used in lattices, slatted screens and railings that filter the light and the view. Much of the furniture was made on site or by local craftsmen, using offcuts from a nearby timber works.

Then find the heart of the place. Among four big old chaká trees there's a spa, and there are two restaurants, one with a platform for stargazing, as well as a deck on the lagoon. The founder, Rodrigo Juarez, has spoken about struggling with anxiety since his twenties, and he built Boca de Agua around the idea of nature as a cure, as well as conservation and work for local people. It opened on the first of November, twenty twenty-three.

I think the stilts say it all. The hotel lifts itself up off the jungle floor, as if apologising for being there at all, and that's exactly the right attitude in a place this delicate.

Before you go, walk down to the lagoon deck and look at the water. Watch it change colour as clouds pass over, from turquoise to sapphire, and you'll see why it has its name.
