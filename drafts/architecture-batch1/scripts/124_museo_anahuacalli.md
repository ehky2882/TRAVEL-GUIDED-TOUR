# Museo Anahuacalli, Mexico City

**Place:** Museo 150, San Pablo Tepetlapa, Coyoacán, Mexico City, Mexico  
**Coordinate (WGS-84):** 19.32258, -99.14408 (exact)  
**Length:** 353 words (about 141 seconds)

## Script (clean)

Diego Rivera spent years building himself a temple. Not to a god, but to the art of the people who lived in Mexico before the Spanish arrived.

Stand in front of the building. You should see a massive, dark, almost black stone pyramid, its walls rough and heavy, rising out of a rocky landscape of lava and wild plants, more like an ancient temple than a museum.

Start with the stone. It's volcanic rock from an eruption of the Xitle volcano, which flowed across this area some two thousand years ago, and it was quarried right here on the site. Rivera designed the building in the style of ancient Mesoamerica, borrowing mainly from Maya and Toltec architecture. The name, in the Nahuatl language, means house of Anahuac, the land surrounded by water, the old name for the Valley of Mexico.

Now go inside and look up. The ceilings are covered in mosaics designed by Rivera himself, in coloured stone. And in the dim rooms, look at the collection. Rivera gathered tens of thousands of pieces of pre-Hispanic art, around thirty-nine thousand of them, of which about two thousand are on display. On the upper floor is his studio, with sixteen of his sketches for murals.

Rivera started building in nineteen forty-two, and he didn't live to finish it. After his death, his daughter Ruth, with the architects Juan O'Gorman and Heriberto Pagelson, completed it, with help from his patron Dolores Olmedo. It opened in September nineteen sixty-four. Around it, about twenty-eight thousand square metres of wild land are protected as an ecological reserve. In twenty twenty-one, the architect Mauricio Rocha added new buildings, quietly, nearby.

[beat]

Rivera's words are on the foundation stone: I return to the people what I was able to rescue from the artistic heritage of their ancestors. That's what this is: one man's enormous gift back to his country.

Before you go, climb up to the roof terrace. From the top, look out over the lava field and, on a clear day, to the volcanoes on the horizon.
