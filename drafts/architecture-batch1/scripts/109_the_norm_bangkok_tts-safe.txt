The Norm, Bangkok

Forty-two floors above Bangkok, there's a bar furnished like a museum of Danish design, and you're allowed to sit in all of it.

Take the lift to the forty-second floor and step out. You should be in a long, warm room of wood, stone and leather, with a bar along one side, the city spread out below the windows, and dotted around, more than a hundred chairs and tables you might recognise from design books.

Start with the furniture. It all comes from fritz hansen, the Danish maker founded in eighteen seventy-two. Look for arneh yahkobsen's Egg chair and Swan chair, both from the nineteen fifties. In the Living Room, a curved lounge, there are eleven Egg chairs in a row. Along the bar are Jacobsen's series seven chairs. And look for the low, steel-framed pieces by pole kyair holm.

Now find your way around. There are four zones. The Main Hall, with the long wooden bar. The Terrace, outdoors, looking over loom pinee Park, which comes alive around sunset when the DJ starts. The Living Room with its Egg chairs. And the Whispering Room, a hidden, quieter bar, if you can find it.

Then order a drink. The signature cocktails are arranged in three eras of Bangkok: Nostalgic, Modern and Progressive, so you can drink your way through the city's history. The founder, terk sittahn sahng wahn kool, wanted it to feel like a cosy lounge, under the motto: here, you belong.

What I like is the contrast. Bangkok is all heat, noise and neon, and up here it's Scandinavian calm, wood and soft leather, with the whole city glittering through the glass.

Before you leave, go out onto the Terrace at dusk and look down over loom pinee Park, a dark green square in the middle of the lights. It opens at five, and on Fridays and Saturdays it's wise to book.
