# Timber Studio, Tokyo

**Place:** Shibuya, Tokyo, Japan (under a kilometre from Shibuya Crossing; no street address published)  
**Coordinate (WGS-84):** none: the architects publish no address. Locate before use  
**Length:** 337 words (about 135 seconds)

## Script (clean)

Less than a kilometre from one of the busiest pedestrian crossings in the world, somebody has drawn a Zen circle on the front of an office.

Look for a five-storey block on the street with a skin of horizontal bands in warm, wood-grain tones, wrapped right round it like the layers of a tree. On the front, the bands curve inwards to make one big, soft-edged opening, a sort of cave in the façade, with the entrance tucked inside.

Start with the bands. They're louvres, wrapping a plain rectangular building, and their job is to soften it. The architect, Koichi Takada, took the idea from the growth rings of a tree, the lines you see when you cut through a trunk, and you can read the whole façade that way, ring after ring.

Then look at the opening. It's based on the ensō, the circle a Zen calligrapher draws in a single brush stroke. It's meant as a place to pause before you go in, a small hollow of calm off a very loud street.

If you can see inside, look for the staircase. A white spiral rises through a sunlit atrium, linking all five floors, with little pocket spaces off it for people to stop and talk. The inside walls carry the same flowing wood-grain lines as the outside.

It was built for an international technology company as a studio for young creative people, and finished in twenty twenty-one. Takada said he wanted to bring the calming warmth of natural materials and organic lines into the design.

[beat]

Shibuya is a place of hard edges, screens and glass. What I like is that this building doesn't shout back at it. It just curves, quietly, and makes a space for you to step out of the flow.

Before you go, walk back down to Shibuya Crossing and wait for the lights to change. After the calm of that circle, it's quite something to stand in the rush again.
