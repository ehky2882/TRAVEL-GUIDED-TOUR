# The Twist, Kistefos

**Place:** Kistefos Sculpture Park, Jevnaker, Norway  
**Coordinate (WGS-84):** 60.22167, 10.36386 (exact; same point as the existing catalogue pin)  
**Length:** 357 words (about 143 seconds)

## Script (clean)

Here's a museum that's also a bridge, and a bridge that can't quite decide which way up it is.

Find a spot on the riverbank where you can see the long, silver building spanning the water. That's the Randselva below it. Follow the building from one bank to the other and you'll notice it doesn't stay level, or even upright. Near the middle, the whole thing turns through ninety degrees, so the bank it leaves and the bank it reaches meet it at different angles.

Look at the skin first. It's made of straight aluminium panels, each forty centimetres wide, stacked like books on a shelf and fanned out a fraction at a time. Every single one is straight, yet together they make a surface that curves two ways at once. Quite a trick, done with nothing but patience.

Inside, if you go in, the floor, walls and ceiling are all lined in white-painted fir slats eight centimetres wide, and the twist gives you three different galleries in one. A tall, dark room at the south end, a wide, bright one at the north with a full-height window over the river and the old mill, and in the middle, a narrow, warped space with a sliver of roof light. The architects say walking through it feels like passing through a camera shutter.

This is BIG, Bjarke Ingels's practice, and it was their first building in Norway, opened in twenty nineteen. It added about a thousand square metres of gallery space, doubling Kistefos's indoor exhibition space, and it gave the sculpture park a second way across the river.

[beat]

Which is the clever bit. The building isn't sitting in the park, it's stitching it together. You can't finish the walk without going through the art.

When you come out on the far side, walk back along the bank towards the old pulp mill buildings, which are the reason Kistefos is here at all. And keep an eye out along the way: the park's sculptures include work by Anish Kapoor, Olafur Eliasson and Yayoi Kusama.
