Pearling Path Car Parks, Muharraq

Here's an awkward truth about protecting an old town. People still need somewhere to park. Bahrain asked one of Europe's most serious architects to solve that, and he made car parks worth looking at.

Stand on the Pearling Path near the entrance to Shaikh Isa bin Ali House, the old palace. Until recently, there was a car park at the junction opposite: a stack of thin, bare concrete floors, each one subtly bent and split, with a staircase corkscrewing up through them.

Start with the idea. The Pearling Path is a walk of more than two miles through Muharraq, tracing the houses, warehouses and shoreline of the old pearl trade. UNESCO listed it in twenty twelve. Restoring it meant cutting traffic out of the narrow lanes, so the plan included four multistorey car parks around the edge, by the Swiss architect Christian Kerez.

Kerez called parking garages a neglected typology, and said he thought it would be fascinating to do one. Instead of flat floors and ramps, he used thin concrete slabs that split and curve, so one level peels away into the next and you drive round in a continuous movement. They were built with ordinary local formwork, nothing exotic. The first held over a hundred cars.

[beat]

And then, in March twenty twenty-six, two of the four began to be demolished, to make a grander entrance to this palace, and the one at this junction appears to be among them. The car parks had been opened only in twenty twenty-two and twenty twenty-three. It's a reminder that in a historic town, new buildings are always on probation, and some don't make it through.

Before you go, walk on along the Pearling Path. Look out for the other buildings designed for it, including Valerio Olgiati's visitor centre, and find the old pearl merchants' houses that all of this was built to protect.
