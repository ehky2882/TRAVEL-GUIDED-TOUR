Ember Cafe and Wine, Bangkok

In a quiet side street off rahma nine Road, there's a café that looks as though it was moulded rather than built: soft curves, earthy colours, and walls rippled like sand after the tide.

Stand outside and look at the building. You should see a low, rounded building in warm, earthy, brick-orange tones, its walls textured with wavy ridges, and an orange Ember sign standing out against them.

Start with those walls. The ripples in the surface catch the light, so the building looks different at every hour. Regular visitors say the best time to come is the morning or late afternoon, when the light is soft and the textures really show.

Now step inside. It's calm and warm, with wooden furniture in the same orange and brick tones, and openings in the ceiling that let daylight fall into the room. There's seating inside and out.

Then look at the menu, because it's a little unusual. During the day there's coffee and non-coffee drinks, homemade cakes, food and brunch. But the café's own description puts the emphasis on small plates and wine, especially volcanic wines, grown on the slopes of volcanoes, which tend to be mineral, salty and lively. It's a café that turns gently into a wine bar.

It's open from eleven to six, and it's closed on Tuesdays. It's also cashless, so bring a card.

I like that it's made for staying. Most cafés want you in and out. This one, with its soft walls and a glass of wine on the menu, seems built for an unhurried afternoon.

Before you go, order a glass of one of the volcanic wines and sit where the light comes down through the ceiling. Then step back outside and look at the walls again. The light will have moved, and so will the ripples.
