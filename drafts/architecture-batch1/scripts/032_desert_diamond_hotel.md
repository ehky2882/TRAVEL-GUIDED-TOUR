# Desert Diamond Hotel, Zhongwei

**Place:** Shapotou Scenic Area, Zhongwei, Ningxia, China (southeast edge of the Tengger Desert)  
**Coordinate (WGS-84):** 37.46563, 104.99653 (area only: Shapotou. Move onto the hotel before use)  
**Length:** 339 words (about 136 seconds)

## Script (clean)

In the middle of the desert, somebody has dropped a diamond. It has twenty-seven facets, sharp as a cut stone, sitting on the soft curves of the dunes.

Stand on the approach and look at the hotel as a whole. You should see a long, white, crystalline building, all sharp angles and flat faces, set against the sand. Its walls aren't glass. They're a slightly rough, greyish-white render, chosen to cope with the fierce desert sun, with a faint mineral sparkle in it.

Now look for the little triangles scattered across the surface. They're star lights, in two sizes, clustered more densely towards the corners. Each sits in a small box with mirrored walls inside, so the light bounces round and round before it comes out. At night they flicker slowly, as though the building is breathing.

This is the second phase of a hotel complex in the Shapotou scenic area, on the south-eastern edge of the Tengger Desert, designed by SHUISHI, led by Xie Yuan, and opened in twenty twenty-four. The architects said they wanted it to be respectful of the desert, without bowing to it.

Go inside if you can. The entrance hall contains a diamond in reverse, a faceted hollow lined with mirrors, so it glitters. There are thirty rooms, arranged in an arc, each turned five degrees from the next so they all face the dunes, every one with its own terrace pool. And at the northern end is the Star Chapel, a small room of just over a hundred square metres under a ceiling of stars twenty-seven metres high, with a triangle of glass more than ten metres tall facing the sunset.

[beat]

A diamond is the hardest, most permanent thing we know, set in the softest, most shifting landscape there is. That's the joke, and it's a good one.

Before you go, stay out after dark. Watch the star lights come on across the building, then look up. Out here, the real ones win.
