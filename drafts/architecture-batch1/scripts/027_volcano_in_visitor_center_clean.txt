Volcano-In Visitor Center, Xilingol

This building is inside a volcano. It's an extinct one, mind, and it stopped erupting about a hundred and fifty thousand years ago.

Stand at the entrance and look around you. You should see the rim of the old volcano curving round in a C shape, and nestled into its western side a long, low building: a sweeping curved roof, three round pavilions at different heights, and glass walls that reflect the grass and sky.

That western side is where the cone had already been dug into, excavated long before the architects arrived. PLAT ASIA chose to build exactly there, on the existing scar, rather than cut into an untouched slope. They describe the building as a way to heal the site. The three round forms echo the shape of the cone itself.

Now walk the corridor. It winds for two hundred and seventy-four metres between the pavilions, and almost every step of it looks out at the landscape. Inside you'll find a café and bookshop, exhibition halls and a restaurant.

The shapes aren't just for looks, either. In winter it can fall to minus forty-three degrees here, with high winds and blizzards. The curving forms cut the wind pressure, and the long eaves keep snow off the courtyards and doors. Outside, the landscaping uses volcanic stone and weathering steel.

This volcano is one of a hundred and eight in the area, on the southern edge of the Xilingol grasslands, with sandy land to the south and Dali Lake to the east.

[beat]

There's something rather fitting about putting a visitor centre in the one place that was already damaged. It means the volcano you came to see is still the main event.

Before you leave, walk up onto the rim of the cone. From the top, you can see the whole C shape of the crater and the building tucked into its side, and beyond it, the steppe in every direction.
