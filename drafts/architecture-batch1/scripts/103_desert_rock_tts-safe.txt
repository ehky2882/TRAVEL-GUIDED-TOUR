Desert Rock, Red Sea

Two thousand years ago, the nabba tee ans carved their tombs and temples straight into the rock, at petra and at hegra. This hotel borrows the idea, and carves its rooms into the mountain.

Follow the road into the valley and stop where the mountains close in. You should see sheer walls of rock all around, and set into them, almost hidden, dark openings like cave mouths, with villas tucked low at the foot of the cliffs and others clinging higher up the rock face.

Start with the openings. The architects, oppenhime Architecture, from Miami, made the large windows and raised rooms echo the mouths of caves. From a distance, the resort barely shows. It's meant to look as if the mountain has simply been inhabited.

Now look at how the rooms are arranged. Down in the valley floor are the Wadi Villas, with private pools, for families. Higher up are the Cliff Hanging Villas, perched for the view. Then there are the Mountain Cave Suites, built right into the rock, and the Mountain Crevice Villas, hanging on the edge of the massif. And there's a Royal Villa, tucked away in its own corner of the valley. There are about sixty in all.

The building tried to tread lightly. Rock dug out during construction was reused, the rooms are cooled partly by the mountain itself, and native plants have been put back into the gaps in the stone. It opened at the end of twenty twenty-four, part of the vast Red Sea tourism project on Saudi Arabia's west coast.

What I find interesting is the idea of luxury as disappearance. A hotel that wants to be noticed builds a tower. This one hides in the rock and lets the mountains be the building.

Before you leave, look up at the cliffs at sunset, when the stone glows red. And if you have the chance, travel inland to al oola, to see hegra, the real nabba tee an tombs that inspired this place.
