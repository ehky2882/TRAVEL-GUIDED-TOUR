# OVO Patagonia, El Chaltén

**Place:** Estancia Bonanza, Río de las Vueltas valley, Provincial Route 41, about 12 km north of El Chaltén, Santa Cruz, Argentina  
**Coordinate (WGS-84):** none: cliff above Estancia Bonanza; no published coordinate. Locate before use (a ground-level viewpoint, not the capsules)  
**Note:** Reached only with a guide via a forest hike and a via ferrata; season November to April. Overnight insurance covers ages 14–65  
**Length:** 345 words (about 138 seconds)

## Script (clean)

Somewhere up on that rock face, people are going to sleep tonight in a glass egg, bolted to a cliff, with nothing below them but Patagonian air.

Look up at the cliffs above the valley of the Río de las Vueltas. Somewhere up there, high on the rock, you may be able to pick out a few small, pale capsules, stuck to the face like eggs laid by some enormous bird. That's what OVO means in Spanish: egg.

Start with the height. The capsules hang roughly two hundred and seventy metres above the valley floor. Each one sleeps two, and each has three levels: a bed at the top, a tiny sitting room in the middle with a dry toilet, and at the bottom, a net, where you can lie out over the drop. Every level has a view all the way round.

Now think about how they got there. Each capsule weighs a couple of tonnes, and they were hauled up the mountain on an aerial lift. The idea came from a highliner, Ezequiel Ruete, and an architect, Luis Aparicio, and it took about three years to engineer.

And the way up for guests is no lift. You walk through native lenga forest for half a day, then clip on a harness and helmet and climb a via ferrata, the iron path of cables and rungs, with a mountain guide alongside you. The power up there comes from the sun. And if the wind gusts over a hundred kilometres an hour, which happens here, the night is called off.

[beat]

What I find rather wonderful is the honesty of it. Most hotels try to keep the weather out. This one hands you the weather, the stars and a dawn view of Fitz Roy, and asks only that you climb for it.

If you'd rather keep your feet on the ground, head back towards El Chaltén and take the trail up to Laguna de los Tres. You'll get the same mountain, at sunrise, from a rock you didn't have to sleep on.
