# The Whale, Andenes

**Place:** Andenes, northern tip of Andøya, Vesterålen, Norway  
**Coordinate (WGS-84):** 69.32291, 16.11445 (exact)  
**Note:** Opens to the public on 4 June 2027 (thewhale.com). Script is written to work from outside before and after opening  
**Length:** 342 words (about 137 seconds)

## Script (clean)

Somewhere just off this coast, there's very probably a sperm whale. On land, there's a building pretending to be one.

Stand on the shore side and look at the roof. You should see a long, low, curving hump of grey stone that rises out of the ground and dips back down again, like the back of a whale, or its tail sliding under the water. Below it runs a curved wall of glass facing the sea.

Start with the stones. They aren't cladding from a catalogue. They're natural, unworked rocks collected round about, and they're meant to weather and settle until the roof looks almost like part of the shore.

Underneath the stones, the roof is a single curved shell of concrete, held up at just three points. That's the clever bit. With only three supports, the big hall inside needs no columns at all.

Then look at where the roof meets the ground on either side. It slopes right down to it, so you can walk straight up onto the top. The roof is a viewing platform, looking out over the islands and the sea, and on the right winter night, the Northern Lights.

It's by the Danish architects Dorte Mandrup, who won the competition in twenty nineteen, with the landscape around it by Marianne Levinsen. The Whale is due to open on the fourth of June, twenty twenty-seven, with exhibitions about whales and the ocean, told through science, art, stories and sound.

[beat]

What I like is the humility of it. Andenes is famous for whales because the deep water comes so close to shore here. The building doesn't try to compete with that. It just lies down at the edge, keeps its head low, and points you at the sea.

If you've got a few hours, take a whale safari from the harbour. Sperm whales are here all year round, and in winter there are orcas and humpbacks too. Then come back to the roof and look out for spouts.
