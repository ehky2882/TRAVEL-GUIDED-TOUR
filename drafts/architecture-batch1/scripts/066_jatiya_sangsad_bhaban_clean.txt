Jatiya Sangsad Bhaban, Dhaka

A parliament designed for one country and finished for another. When the American architect Louis Kahn began drawing it, Dhaka was in Pakistan. By the time it opened, it was the capital of Bangladesh.

Stand on the far side of the lake and look across. You should see a vast cluster of grey concrete towers rising straight out of the water, with huge circles, triangles and rectangles cut deep into their faces, and thin white lines running across the concrete in horizontal bands.

Start with the cut-out shapes. They're not windows in the usual sense. Kahn set the actual glass well back, behind these deep, open shapes, so they act like giant sunshades and air scoops. The fierce sun of Bengal never hits the glass directly, and the breeze can move through.

Now look at those white lines. They're bands of white marble set into the concrete. The concrete was carried up by hand, so the walls could only rise about five feet a day, leaving a visible join each time. Kahn hated the join, so he recessed the wall every five feet and filled the gap with a strip of marble. What would have been an untidy seam became a pattern.

Then look at the water. The lake wraps round the building on three sides. It's beautiful, but it's also practical. The water helps keep the air cool.

Inside the ring of towers is the assembly chamber, which seats up to three hundred and fifty-four members. Kahn was fully commissioned in nineteen sixty-three, and construction started in sixty-four. A war of independence intervened, and Kahn died in nineteen seventy-four, with the building about three-quarters done. It was completed in January nineteen eighty-two and opened that February.

[beat]

What astonishes me is that a new nation, born in war and very poor, chose to finish this, of all things: a vast, slow, uncompromising building in concrete and marble, for its democracy. It's a statement about what a parliament is for.

If you've time, walk round the grounds, more than two hundred acres of them. From every side, the building gives you a different composition of circles and triangles against the sky.
