Harudot by Nana Coffee Roasters, Chonburi

Most cafés put a tree in a pot. This one pulled its roof apart to make room for one.

From the entrance, look at the building as a whole. You should see three simple black gables, the shape of a child's drawing of a house, sitting in a grove of trees. Now look at where the roofs split. They don't just stop; they're peeled open and curved apart, and through the biggest gap rises a baobab, the bottle tree, its swollen trunk standing in a courtyard in the middle of the café.

That's the idea of the whole place. Harudot was made for two clients at once: Nana Coffee Roasters, and the landlord, who collects plants with unusual shapes. IDIN Architects were asked to make somewhere people would travel to, and to carry the landlord's passion into the building. So the tree comes first, and the architecture looks as if it has made way for it over the years.

The name helps, too. Haru is Japanese for spring, new beginnings and growth. Dot is a starting point. Look for the circles once you've noticed them: in the custom lettering on the signs, and in the terrazzo floor, where circular divisions carry little words and quotes, and a pattern of petals radiates out as if they'd fallen from the trees.

Inside, the black outside gives way to warm pine. Where the roof is open in the courtyard, the ceilings indoors carry glowing oval panels in the same shapes, so the light feels like it comes from the same sky. Seating runs round the walls like a ribbon.

It opened in June twenty twenty-three, after more than a year of building. Plenty of places say they're designed around nature. Here, you can see exactly who got to go first.

Before you leave, sit outside for a moment. The outdoor seats are made of resin mixed with coffee grounds, rice and leaves.
