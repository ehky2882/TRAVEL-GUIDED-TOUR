# Gran Acuario Mazatlán, Mar de Cortés

## Script (clean)

Jacques Cousteau is supposed to have called the Sea of Cortez the world's aquarium. Mazatlán took him at his word and built one, the largest in Mexico, devoted to that one sea.

Stand outside in Parque Central and look at the building. You should see a great, raw concrete structure, chunky and porous, with plants growing on top, and openings punched right through it, so you can see sky and greenery inside and out. It looks less like a box and more like a rocky outcrop.

Start with the concrete. The architect, Tatiana Bilbao, of Mexico City, wanted a building that felt monumental but open, more like a ruin or a reef than a sealed box. Its walls are rough, the roof is planted, and the gaps let in the sea air and the light. It was a finalist for the Mies Crown Hall Americas Prize, one of the most important architecture awards in the Americas.

Now go in. The aquarium is organised in three zones, Costa, Tierra and Mar, the coast, the land and the sea, with more than two hundred and fifty species and thirty habitats, all from the Sea of Cortez. Look for the Ventana Oceánica, the ocean window, which the aquarium says is the largest acrylic window in Mexico, looking into a vast tank of the open sea.

It also works as a research centre, with marine scientists and a sea turtle project, so it's a place for science as much as for school trips. It opened in twenty twenty-three, close to Mazatlán's seafront, beside the old aquarium it replaced.

[beat]

What I find powerful is the idea of building an aquarium for one particular sea, rather than a little bit of every ocean. It asks you to care about the water right outside.

Before you go, walk a few minutes down to the malecón, the long seafront promenade. That's the Sea of Cortez itself, just over the wall, and after the aquarium you'll know a little of what's swimming in it.
