Zaishui Art Museum, Rizhao

Zaishui means in the water. And that's exactly where this museum is: a building about a kilometre long that seems to rise out of a lake, with the lake flowing through it.

Stand on the shore and look out. You should see a long, low, thin concrete roof, gently rising and falling, stretched out across the water like a bridge, held up on a forest of slim columns standing in the lake, with water running underneath and inside.

Start with the length. It's roughly a kilometre long, a single, slender line laid across the water. The architect, Junya Ishigami, from Japan, is famous for making buildings look as light and thin as possible, as if they were barely there.

Now look under the roof. The columns rise straight from the bottom of the lake, and the roof sits on top, a thin, wavy concrete surface. Ishigami wanted the building to look as though it was emerging from under the water. And here's the lovely bit: the lake runs inside the museum. Even in winter, when the surface outside freezes over, the water inside stays liquid and keeps flowing.

It was built as a museum, and also as a visitor centre for the new Bailuwan area next to it, at the entrance to the development. It opened at the end of twenty twenty-three, with around twenty thousand square metres inside.

What I find magical is the idea of a museum that is half building and half lake. You walk through the galleries with the water beside you, and the line between architecture and landscape disappears.

Before you go, walk along the shore to the far end, if you can. Look back down the whole length of the building, and you'll see it shrink into a thin grey line on the water.
