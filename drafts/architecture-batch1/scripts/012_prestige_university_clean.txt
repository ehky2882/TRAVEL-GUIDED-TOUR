Prestige University, Indore

Four hundred and sixty-three steps, and you're encouraged to sit on all of them. This is a university building that doubles as a hillside.

Approach from the north, the way the building was designed to be met. Instead of a great façade, you should see a broad slope of stepped platforms rising diagonally away from you, planted and paved, climbing to a height of about twenty-eight metres. There's no front door in the usual sense. The building simply gets higher as you walk up it.

Have a look at those platforms first. There are four hundred and sixty-three of them, making about nine thousand square metres of rooftop that students and staff can walk on, sit on, and use as an outdoor amphitheatre. The architects, Sanjay Puri's studio, took the idea from the traditional Indian stepwell, the baoli, which was both a way of reaching water and a place where people gathered.

Now think about the heat. In Indore it runs between thirty and forty degrees for around eight months a year, so the building is built against the sun. The east, west and south sides are wrapped in a ventilated brick screen. Inside, a covered street runs diagonally through the whole building, acting as a channel for air, and the floors step in section so warm air can rise and escape. Landscaped courtyards bring in soft, indirect daylight.

Underneath the steps is a full campus: a food court, an auditorium and offices on the ground floor, a library above linked by a bridge across that inner street, then classrooms, tiered lecture rooms, and faculty spaces at the top.

The trick here is generosity. Most university buildings keep the best view for the vice-chancellor. This one gives its roof to everybody.

So climb it. Go right to the top platform, turn round, and look back down the slope you've just walked up. It's the best seat on campus, and it's free.
