Flaine, Haute-Savoie

It's been called the ugliest ski resort in the world. A ski guide once nicknamed it Phlegm. And yet the French state has officially declared it part of the nation's architectural heritage. Both can be true.

Stand in the Forum, the square at the heart of the lower resort. You should see blocks of grey concrete all around you, square-cut and stacked, with flats above and shops at street level, round glass globes for streetlights, and beyond them a great white bowl of mountains.

Start with the concrete. This whole place was designed by Marcel Breuer, a former student and teacher at the Bauhaus. He used precast concrete partly because it sat well with the grey rock of the mountains around. Look closely at the façades and you'll see they're cut into angled, faceted panels, so they catch the light differently through the day.

The idea came from a geophysicist, Éric Boissonnas, and his wife Sylvie, who found this site in nineteen fifty-nine and wanted a resort that took architecture as seriously as skiing. It opened in January nineteen sixty-nine, and Breuer kept working on it for about sixteen years. Cars stay outside the edge of town. You get about on foot, on skis, or on two inclined lifts.

Now find the art. Near the cable car is a twelve-metre head of a woman, an enlargement of a little Picasso sculpture from nineteen fifty-seven. Dubuffet's Le Boqueteau, a cluster of seven strange trees, stands in the Forum. And Vasarely's Trois Hexagones decorates the shopping centre.

[beat]

In two thousand and eight, the Ministry of Culture gave Flaine its label for twentieth-century architectural heritage. I think the real lesson is how taste turns. What people called ugly in the seventies looks bold and honest now.

Before you go, call in at the art centre. It runs free guided walks of the architecture and the art. And notice the signs as you walk round, all in the same mid-century lettering. It's a resort that was designed right down to its typeface.
