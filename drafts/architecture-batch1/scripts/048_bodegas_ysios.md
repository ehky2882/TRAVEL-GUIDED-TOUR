# Bodegas Ysios, Laguardia

## Script (clean)

It's a wine cellar designed to look like a row of wine barrels, under a roof that looks like the mountains, by an architect who was later taken to court for the leaks.

Stand in front of the winery with the vineyards around you and the Sierra de Cantabria rising behind. You should see a long, low building with a wavy, silver roof, and a front of warm wood that curves in and out along its whole length, with a tall glass window over the entrance.

Start with the walls. They're two load-bearing concrete walls, twenty-six metres apart, but each one follows a wavy line, and they're clad in timber. The rhythm of those curves is meant to recall a row of barrels lying on their sides, which is exactly what's inside.

Now look up at the roof. Laminated timber beams span from wall to wall, and over them is a skin of aluminium, in a rolling surface that rises and falls along the building. As the sun moves, the light slides across it, so the roof seems to move against the stillness of the vines and the mountains behind. Then look down at the pools in front, lined with broken white tiles, a Calatrava signature you'll also find in Valencia and Barcelona.

The architect is Santiago Calatrava. Building began in nineteen ninety-eight and the winery opened in two thousand and one. The name Ysios is a tribute to Isis and Osiris, the Egyptian gods: in the legend, he taught people to farm, and she watched over the grapes turning into wine.

[beat]

In twenty thirteen, the winery sued Calatrava over leaks and damp. The case was settled amicably in the end. It's a useful reminder that a building can be a famous image and still have to keep the rain off the wine.

If you've time, book a tour and a tasting. And afterwards, drive up to the hill town of Laguardia, a few minutes away. From its walls, you can look down over the vineyards and pick out that silver roof.
