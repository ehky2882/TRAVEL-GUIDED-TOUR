# Chapel of Sound, Chengde

**Place:** Valley below the Great Wall, Aranya Jinshanling, Luanping, Chengde, Hebei, China (about two hours' drive from central Beijing)  
**Coordinate (WGS-84):** 40.68229, 117.27666 (area: Jinshanling junction point; the hall is in the valley below the Great Wall. Move before use)  
**Length:** 347 words (about 139 seconds)

## Script (clean)

It looks like a boulder that has rolled down into the valley and come to rest. Then you notice the doorway, and the stage, and the Great Wall on the ridge above.

Stand on the path in the valley floor and look at the building. You should see a big, rough, rock-like mass of grey concrete, angular and craggy, with dark openings cut into it, and high on the mountains around, the ruined towers of the Great Wall.

Start with the surface. It's concrete, but it's mixed with crushed local rock, full of minerals, so it has the colour and texture of the mountains around it. The architects, OPEN, led by Li Hu and Huang Wenjing, wanted it to look like a mysterious boulder that had gently fallen into place.

Now look for the openings. Inside is a concert hall that's only half indoors. It's a semi-open amphitheatre, with an opening in the roof and a skylight over the stage, so the sound of the valley, the birds, the wind, even the rain, becomes part of the music. On the outside there's also an open-air stage and platforms where you can climb up and sit on the rock and look out.

Then find the view. From inside and from the platforms, the openings are aimed at the Great Wall, the Ming Dynasty ruins running along the ridges. It's about as dramatic a backdrop as a stage could have.

It was finished in twenty twenty-one, and when there's no concert, it's simply a place to sit and be quiet. The architects said they hoped people would calm down and listen to the sound of nature.

[beat]

I find that rather wonderful: a concert hall whose best performance might be silence. Sit inside for a few minutes on your own, and you'll hear what they mean.

If you've got the legs and the time, climb up to the Great Wall itself. Then look back down into the valley, and try to spot the boulder from above.
