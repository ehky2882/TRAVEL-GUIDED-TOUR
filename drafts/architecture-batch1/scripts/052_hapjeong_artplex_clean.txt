Hapjeong Artplex, Seoul

At first glance it looks like a plain white block. Then you notice that the top of it is leaning, just slightly, by three degrees.

Stand across Poeun-ro and look at the building. You should see a pale, softly glowing white mass, five storeys tall, with odd geometric cuts taken out of its base, a band of glass running across the second floor, and an outdoor staircase sinking down below the pavement.

Start with the shape. Most of it was decided by planning rules. Seoul's setback limits shaved bits off the block, and the architects, NOTNOT, led by Hojung An, kept the leftover angles rather than smoothing them away. Then, from the third floor up, they tilted the whole thing three degrees, just enough to make the street around it feel slightly too straight.

Now look at the surface. Those curved white panels aren't concrete or plaster. They're a translucent artificial stone, heat-formed into shape, and they soften the daylight to a matt glow. Watch them for a minute and you'll see faint gradients shifting as the light changes.

And the glass band on the second floor works as a shop window. Behind it, and below it, is a concept store. A stair climbs inside the diagonal cut to connect the two floors. The third to fifth floors are galleries, there's a café underground, and two floors down, an independent cinema. The sunken staircase on the street, with its stepped seats, is how you get there, and sometimes it hosts events of its own.

It was finished in August twenty twenty-four, where three neighbourhoods meet, Hongdae, Sangsu and Mangwon, a few steps from the YG Entertainment headquarters.

[beat]

The architects call it a quiet monolith that filters its surroundings. In a district this loud, I think quiet is the boldest thing you can build.

If you've time, follow the stair all the way to the top. There's an open roof terrace looking out over the rooftops of Hapjeong, and from up there you can see why a building here would want to keep its voice down.
