Hotel Hubertus Sky Pool, Dolomites

A swimming pool, twenty-five metres long and full of warm water, sticking straight out of a hotel into thin air, twelve metres above the ground. It's every bit as unlikely as it sounds.

Stand below the hotel and look up. You should see a long building clad in angled tree trunks, and coming out of it towards you, a straight, slim box, edged in dark stone, held up on a forest of bare, crooked tree trunks. Under its far end, there's nothing at all but air.

Start with the reach. The pool runs out about seventeen metres from the front of the hotel. It's five metres wide and a metre thirty deep, heated to about thirty-three degrees, warm enough for a swim when there's snow on the ground.

Now look at the end of it, and at the bottom. The front and part of the floor are glass. So the swimmers get the mountains through the wall, and the drop through the floor.

Then look at the legs. They're tree trunks with the bark stripped off, and the same idea runs right across the hotel's front, where angled trunks act as sun shades and screens. They make the pool look as if it's being held up by the forest it sits in.

It's by noa*, the Network of Architecture, a South Tyrolean practice, as part of a renovation for the Gasser family, who have turned a small mountain inn into a five-star hotel. It sits at about thirteen hundred and fifty metres, on the slopes of the Kronplatz.

[beat]

It's a show-off, of course. But it's a show-off with a point, because the pool doesn't look at the hotel. It looks at the mountains. It's the hotel admitting what you really came for.

If you've got the afternoon, take a lift up the Kronplatz. From the top, you can look back down the valley and find the pool, a thin line of blue sticking out from the trees.
