# Slovak Radio Building, Bratislava

**Place:** Mýtna 1, Old Town, Bratislava, Slovakia  
**Coordinate (WGS-84):** 48.15409, 17.11384 (exact, OpenStreetMap)  
**Length:** 304 words (about 122 seconds)

## Script (clean)

It's a pyramid, upside down, balanced on its point. People in Bratislava call it the Radio Diamond. A British newspaper once called it one of the ugliest buildings in the world. Make up your own mind.

Stand on the pavement opposite and look up. You should see a great dark-brown pyramid, eighty metres tall, standing on its tip, its sloping sides lined with rows of windows, and the whole thing propped on a lower base so it doesn't topple.

That precarious look was hard won. The design came second in a competition in nineteen sixty-three. The winner was Miloš Chorvát, but the people paying for it chose this one instead, by Štefan Svetko, Štefan Ďurkovič and Barnabáš Kissling. Work began in nineteen sixty-seven and didn't finish until nineteen eighty-three, partly because it was one of the first big buildings in Slovakia to use a steel frame rather than prefabricated concrete, and the builders were learning as they went.

Inside the base is a concert hall with five hundred and twenty-two seats and a large concert organ. Svetko called the building a temple of word and music, and he meant it. Regular broadcasting started in March nineteen eighty-five, and it's still the home of national radio today.

[beat]

So, ugly or not? In twenty seventeen, Slovakia declared it a cultural monument, and plenty of architects were annoyed by the newspaper's list. It's had a film career too, as a backdrop in Red Sparrow. Buildings from this era are easy to sneer at. This one has outlasted the sneering.

Walk right round it before you go. From the side, the pyramid's angle looks even steeper. And if you can get inside for a concert, you'll get to hear what the temple was for.
