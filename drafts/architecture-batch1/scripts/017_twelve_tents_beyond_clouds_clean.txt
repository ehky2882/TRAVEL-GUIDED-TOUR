Twelve Tents Beyond Clouds, Xiayanbei

Up here, the clouds often sit below you. The village built a cluster of glowing peaks to keep them company.

You should be at the entrance to Xiayanbei, near the car park, looking at a group of pointed white forms gathered round a courtyard. There are twelve of them, angular like small mountains or rock crystals, and above them, a broad flat roof with platforms stepping away towards the cliff edge.

Look at the tents first. Each one is a steel frame, mostly made in a factory and bolted together here, then stretched with a translucent white membrane. At night, lights under the skin make all twelve glow, and the architect, Zhu Peidong of the studio line+, says that's the point: a beacon, not just for visitors but for villagers coming home.

Now look at what used to stand here. This was a tea processing factory, a big white box about fifty-five metres long, completely out of scale with the village houses, and it blocked the straight route from the village to the cliff. The architects reworked the site from there: the tents gather on the ground, and the platforms cascade down from the old factory's upper floor to the existing viewpoint over the cliffs.

It's not just for tourists. The space hosts village gatherings and markets as well as a visitor centre, a tea room, a sunset café, local food and a handful of guest rooms facing the cliff.

[beat]

What I like is the honesty of the trade. The village needed a place to meet and the scenic area needed a front door, and instead of building two things, they built one.

So follow the walkway out along the platforms to the cliff viewpoint. On a good morning, you'll be looking down onto the sea of clouds, with the Nineteen Peaks breaking through.
