Jingyang Camphor Court, Jingdezhen

In the porcelain capital of China, an old factory has been turned into a hotel, and the guests of honour are the trees.

Step into the central courtyard. You should see big, old camphor trees, their dense, glossy canopies spreading overhead, surrounded by a cloister of wooden columns, and beyond it, low buildings in warm red and orange brick.

Start with the trees. This was an industrial site, with a long, narrow factory building, machine rooms, a workers' dormitory and a rundown pavilion. When the architects, Vector Architects, arrived, the mature camphor trees were the most precious thing here, and they shaped everything. The wooden cloister wraps around them to make the courtyard, and from it you reach the guest rooms, the restaurant and the meeting rooms.

Now look at the walls. The bricks are a mix of kiln bricks and old clay bricks, in reds and oranges. That's not just a pretty choice. Jingdezhen has made porcelain for more than a thousand years, and its kilns needed bricks, so the walls carry a bit of that history.

Then look at the old buildings. The former dormitory now holds guest rooms. The long factory building is the restaurant, with a new metal roof and skylights letting light down onto the tables. And the architects wanted the courtyard to be as large and as public as possible. More than half of the place is meant to be open to everyone, not just hotel guests.

It took from twenty nineteen to the end of twenty twenty-three to design and build, in a district where many old ceramics factories are being brought back to life.

I like that the most important design decision here was what not to cut down. The camphor trees were already here, and now they're the reason for the hotel.

Before you go, take a walk through the surrounding old factory district. Jingdezhen's industrial ceramic works are being turned into studios, shops and galleries, and you can still buy porcelain made a few streets away.
