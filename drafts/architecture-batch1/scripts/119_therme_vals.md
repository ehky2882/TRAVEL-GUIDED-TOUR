## Script (clean)

Peter Zumthor said he started this design with the presence of the mountain. So he built a bathhouse that feels as if it's been quarried out of the mountain, rather than built on it.

Stand on the slope below the hotel and look at the baths. You should see a long, low block, half sunk into the hillside, made of layer upon layer of thin, grey-green stone, with deep, dark openings looking out over the valley.

Start with the stone. It's Valser quartzite, quarried just above the village, about sixty thousand slabs of it, laid in thin horizontal layers with fine concrete joints, like the strata of the mountain itself.

Now go inside and down. The baths are a sequence of stone rooms, dim and quiet, lit by thin slots of daylight. The main indoor pool is thirty-six degrees. There's a fire pool at forty-two and a cold pool at fourteen, so you can go from one shock to the other. There's a flower bath, shallow and scented with petals. Look for the sound cave, a stone chamber where your voice and the dripping water echo back. And outside, there's a pool cut into the hillside, with the whole valley in front of you.

It opened in nineteen ninety-six. Thirteen years later, Zumthor won the Pritzker Prize, and this is the building most people think of when they hear his name. Since twenty twenty-two the baths have been run by a foundation tied to the village, and they're open to day visitors as well as hotel guests.

[beat]

What I love is how little it shouts. No logo, no grand entrance, just stone, water, heat and silence. It reminds you that a building can be felt more than seen.

Before you go, drink from the spring. There's a fountain of the thermal water, cold and mineral, coming straight out of the mountain that inspired all of this.
