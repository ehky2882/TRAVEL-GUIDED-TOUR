Tianfu Habitat Pavilion, Chengdu

From the air, it's a set of overlapping circles laid across a hillside. From the ground, it barely seems to be there at all.

Stand on the path as you approach the pavilion. You should see a low building that follows the gentle slope, under broad, round roofs, with planted courtyards tucked between them and people walking along the rooftops.

Start with the circles. There are three overlapping rings, and each one holds a different landscape: a courtyard, a lotus pond, and a hill of bamboo. The circle was chosen deliberately. It stands for Chengdu's place in the bowl of the Sichuan Basin, and for the round table, where Sichuan families eat together and drink tea.

Now look at the roofs. They're not just covers. There's a promenade along the top, so you can walk over the building and look out across the exhibition grounds. Keeping the building low means it sits into the land rather than standing on top of it.

If you can go inside, look up in the main exhibition hall. It's fan-shaped, held up on a steel frame, and the ceiling is made of folded aluminium panels, patterned after Sichuan bamboo weaving, a local craft of very fine, flat woven strips. And find the tea room, which is framed in real bamboo.

The pavilion was built as one of the main venues for the International Horticultural Exhibition in twenty twenty-four, designed by China Southwest Architectural Design and Research Institute. After the show closed, the bamboo hill was meant to live on as a retreat and a place for visitors to enjoy the landscape.

What I like is how ordinary its inspiration is. Not a symbol of power, just a round table, a pond, and some bamboo: the things that make everyday life pleasant in Chengdu.

Before you go, walk up onto the roof promenade. From up there, you can see how the three rings knit together, and how the building simply melts into the hill.
