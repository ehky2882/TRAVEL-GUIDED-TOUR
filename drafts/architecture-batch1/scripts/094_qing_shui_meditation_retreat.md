# Qing Shui Meditation Retreat Center, Anxi

**Place:** Core scenic area of Qingshuiyan Ancestral Hall, Penglai Mountain, Anxi, Quanzhou, Fujian, China (the former bus station)  
**Coordinate (WGS-84):** 25.12361, 118.10759 (area: scenic-area visitor centre point; the retreat is the old bus station nearby. Move before use)  
**Length:** 328 words (about 131 seconds)

## Script (clean)

Most pilgrims arrive at a temple through a gate. Here, they used to arrive at a bus station. Now the bus station has become the gate.

Stand in the open square under the old banyan tree. You should see a great, spreading tree draped in moss, native rocks and mossy paths around it, forest on every side, and beside the square, a low, two-storey building that was once the scenic area's bus station, now quietly remade.

Start with the tree. The whole project was organised around it, an ancient banyan in a clearing in the woods. The architects wanted the building to defer to it.

Now look at the building. It was a two-storey bus station, left empty and falling apart after the buses moved elsewhere. The architects, RESP Studio from Xiamen, led by Chen Yan'an and Zhou Weidong, didn't knock it down. They renovated it where it stood, and turned it into tea houses, vegetarian restaurants and places to rest, to serve the people who come up the mountain.

And what they come for is Qingshuiyan itself, the Clear Water Rock temple, an ancestral hall founded in the Northern Song Dynasty, about a thousand years ago, at the foot of Penglai Mountain. It's a centre of folk belief for communities across Fujian, Taiwan and South-East Asia, and many of them trace their families to this area.

Anxi is also one of China's great tea counties, the home of Tieguanyin oolong, so a tea house is exactly the right thing for a pilgrim's rest stop.

[beat]

I love the modesty of it. Instead of a grand new visitor centre, an old bus station gets a second life, and the tree gets to be the main event.

If you have time, sit down in one of the tea houses and order a pot of the local oolong. Then walk on up the path to the temple itself, on the mountainside above.
