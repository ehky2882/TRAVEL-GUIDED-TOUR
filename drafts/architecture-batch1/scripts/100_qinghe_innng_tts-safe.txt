Ching huh Inning International Talent Community, Beijing

Imagine being handed a triangle of land squeezed between an elevated motorway and a busy road, and being asked to make it a place where young people would actually want to live.

Stand on shyao ying West Road and look at the building. You should see three linked towers that zig-zag across the site, with big triangular gaps and courtyards cut between them, the elevated expressway running alongside, and a green slope rising up at one corner.

Start with the shape. The site is a triangle, with the Beijing to Tibet Expressway on one side, raised up on its bridge, and crowds coming from the nearby metro stations. The architects, joo shyao dee Architects with the Beijing Institute of Architectural Design, joined three towers in one continuous zig-zag, so they open up three big triangular courtyards between them, letting light and air through instead of making one solid wall against the traffic.

Now look at the slope. On the north-east side, the green rises gently by six metres to become the main entrance platform for the flats, so you walk up a little hill to get home. Even the leftover space under the expressway bridge has been stepped and terraced, rather than left as dead ground.

Then look at the edges at ground level. There's an Urban Gallery, a Shared Staircase and an Innovation Lobby, spaces for art, events and meeting the neighbours. Higher up, from the tenth floor, terraces step back, with views west to the shee shahn hills. Inside, the flats have movable partitions and furniture, so a home can turn into an office.

It was finished in twenty twenty-four, as homes for young tech workers and entrepreneurs, from studios to two-floor suites.

It's a brave thing to try to build a community next to an expressway. The architects called their landscape idea breaking ground, embracing new life. It's a useful reminder that awkward sites don't have to produce awkward buildings.

Before you go, follow the walking path through the courtyards, from the green slope to the sports park at the south-west corner. It's the best way to see how the three towers fit together.
