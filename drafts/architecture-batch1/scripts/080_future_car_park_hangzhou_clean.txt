Future Car Park, Hangzhou

Five hundred cars, stacked by machine inside a cluster of towers, with a garden on the roof. It's a car park that would rather you thought of it as a park.

Stand on the plaza at ground level and look up. You should see a group of tall towers, some round and some square, wrapped in a see-through skin of metal louvres, and at the top, the towers spread out and join into a broad, sheltering roof, like the canopy of a group of trees.

Start with the louvres. Look closely and you can see through them, to the machinery inside. Three round towers in the middle are where the cars go. You leave your car at the bottom, and an automated lift and stacking system carries it up and away. Three more round towers hold lifts for people.

Now look up at the canopy. The architects, Daniel Statham Studio, from London and Shanghai, describe the towers as blossoming into the roof. Up there is a public park, with gardens, entertainment spaces and views over the city, shaded from the sun and sheltered from the rain.

And there's a hidden job going on. The porous skin isn't just for show. It stiffens the structure and helps ventilate the car levels below, so the towers work like chimneys, drawing the fumes up and out. The building leans on natural air and daylight rather than machinery wherever it can.

The client asked for what they called a parking oasis, a building that would ease congestion and clean up the air in the business district. It was designed and built between twenty sixteen and twenty twenty-two.

What I like is the honesty of letting you see the cars. Most car parks hide what they are. This one puts the machine on display, and then gives the roof back to people.

Before you go, take the lift up to the roof garden. It's one of the odder pleasures of a modern city: a park on top of five hundred cars.
