Mr Pop, Club Rose Bay, Sydney

Before Sydney had a proper international airport, it had this bay. Flying boats took off from the water just out there. So the bar inside this club pretends to be a nineteen fifties departure lounge.

Find Club Rose Bay on Vickery Avenue, with the water of the bay close by. Go in and look for Mr Pop: a low-lit room in warm peach tones, with carpet on the walls and corduroy seats.

Start with the walls. They're carpeted, which is a deliberate bit of nineteen fifties luxury, and with the plush corduroy banquettes, the whole room is soft and hushed, the way an airline lounge was when flying was glamorous. Look up at the lighting too. Patterned glass lightboxes give everything a gentle peach glow.

Now listen. It's a record bar, a cocktail bar built around vinyl, and look for the speakers in cherry wood. The idea is the jet age, when getting on a plane was an occasion and you dressed for it.

The whole club has just been redone, in its first full refurbishment since nineteen seventy-nine, by the hospitality group merry vale with the Sydney design studio akin atelyay. Its founder, kelvin ho, says they drew on the nostalgia of the classic Australian r s l club, a place of warmth and community. You'll find the rest of the club much as a club should be: a pizza and pasta restaurant called nottys, pool tables, a courtyard, fish and chips.

And it is still a club with a memory. Every evening at six, the Ode of Remembrance is read, followed by a minute's silence. I rather like that the glamorous bar and the old ritual share a roof.

If you've got time before a drink, walk down to the waterfront at Rose Bay and look out at the seaplanes. A few still take off from the bay, which is the best possible way to understand what Mr Pop is remembering.
