Termeh Building, Hamedan

A brick building whose front lifts up off the pavement, ripples like cloth, and lets you walk straight up onto its roof.

Stand on the pavement in front of the building. You should see a low, two-storey façade of local brick that seems to flow: it rises over the ground-floor windows, then curves down towards the street and becomes a sloping surface you can walk on.

Start with the brickwork. It's all local brick, laid in the traditional patterns of this region, so although the shape is new, the texture matches the old city around it. The architects, Farshad Mehdizadeh and Ahmad Bathaei, named the building after termeh, the fine, hand-woven Iranian cloth, and that undulating façade is meant to recall fabric draped and folded.

Now look at the windows. On the ground floor they're big, so you can see straight into the shop and be tempted in. On the office floor above they're narrow vertical slits, which keep out the harsh afternoon sun from the west.

Then walk up the slope. The brick surface leads you onto the roof, which was designed as a garden for the office, for gatherings and parties. Inside, a neat trick: the ceiling of the shop bends upwards to become a flight of stairs, connecting the office directly to the walkway at the front. And notice the low wall around the roof, two and a half metres high, set to match the skyline of the building next door.

It's only about six hundred square metres, finished in twenty fifteen.

What I like is the generosity. A small commercial building could easily have turned a blank wall to the street. This one bends down to meet you and invites you on top.

Before you go, take a look at the older brick buildings nearby. Hamedan is one of the oldest cities in Iran, and once you've seen this, you'll start noticing the patterns in its traditional brickwork everywhere.
