Prairie Ark, Oo lahn chahb

Out on the grassland, something seems to have landed. Half buried, half rising, a great slanting roof pokes up out of the steppe, like a spaceship that came down at an angle.

Stand on the lakeshore and look at the gallery. You should see a long, low form with a sloping roof that sinks into the ground at one end and lifts towards the sky at the other, and across the water, a tall, thin observation tower.

Start with the roof. It's the whole idea. The architects, buzz, led by dzuh yoo jwahng, made it sink straight into the earth, so you can walk up onto it directly from the grass. Climb it, and the building becomes a viewing platform over the lake and the grassland.

Now go inside, if it's open. The gallery is one flexible space for exhibitions, talks and gatherings, under a gridded ceiling with skylights that let in a soft, even light. The entrances are tucked into the slope of the ground.

Then look across the lake at the tower. It's called the Nomads' Beacon Tower. A winding path leads past a small amphitheatre and then spirals up to a lookout over the grasslands. When the lake floods in season, the water surrounds it and it becomes almost an island.

The architects were deliberate about what they avoided. No yurts, no nomad clichés, no genghis kahn. Instead, the strange, futuristic shape was meant to jolt visitors out of their city lives and into the scale of the landscape. Not everyone was won over. One online critic called it properly dystopian. Another said it looked like a boat stranded on a beach.

I can see why it divides people. But on a landscape this empty, any building is a stranger. At least this one admits it, and then lies down low so the sky stays the biggest thing in view.

If you've got time, walk round the lake to the beacon tower and climb it. From the top, look back at the ark, and you'll see how it tilts its whole roof up to meet you.
