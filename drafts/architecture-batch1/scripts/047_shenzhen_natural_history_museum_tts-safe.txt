Shen jen Natural History Museum

Nothing in nature is square. That's how the architect sums this place up, and once you've seen it, you'll find it hard to disagree.

Stand by yahn dzuh Lake and look at the museum. You should see a long, low mass of grey granite that rises gently out of the ground in sloping planes and planted terraces, more like a hillside than a building, with deep openings cut into its face like caves in a cliff.

Start with the stone. The outside is wrapped in strips of granite, and the openings are shaped to look carved, as if water had worn them out of the rock. The design is called Delta, after the river deltas of this part of southern China, and the idea is that it should look like a piece of landscape that had always been here.

Now look at the roof. It's a park. You can walk up the sloping terraces straight from the ground and across the top, without going into the museum at all, among planted gardens and viewing terraces looking out over the lake and the hills.

Inside, the galleries are arranged in five great cone-shaped halls, linked by passages and atriums that feel like a cave system. Across eight themed halls, it tells four point six billion years of the Earth's history, from the origins of the universe to the creatures and plants of this region.

It's by the Danish practice three x n, with B plus H Architects and joo bo Design. They won the competition in twenty twenty, and it opened in July twenty twenty-six, at over a hundred thousand square metres, the largest natural history museum in southern China.

There's a neat idea in a museum of nature that pretends to be nature. The building teaches the first lesson before you've bought a ticket: everything here was shaped by water and time.

Before you go, walk right up onto the roof and look back across the lake. From up there, it's hard to tell where the building stops and the hills begin.
