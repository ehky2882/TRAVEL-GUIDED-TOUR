Nine giant ice cubes, stacked at odd angles beside a lake, in a part of China that is building one of the country's biggest indoor ski slopes. The building is an advert for winter.

Stand by the water on the plaza and look up. You should see a tall stack of frosted-glass boxes, each turned at a slightly different angle, some jutting out over your head, some leaving small gaps you can walk between, and the whole lot reflected in the lake.

Start with the glass. It's a double skin of glass panels, each about two and a half metres square, hung on stainless-steel cables, and printed with a pattern of snowflakes, so it looks frosted, like ice crystals. The panels vary in how see-through they are, so the light inside changes from cube to cube.

Now walk round it. Because every cube sits at its own angle, the building looks different from every direction. The architects, the Paris practices Zone of Utopia and mat yuh forray Architecte, say the façade is meant to echo water in all its states: solid, liquid, and vapour.

Inside there are restaurants and bars, exhibition space, children's areas and a reception centre, with terraces where the cubes step back, and a sky lounge at the top. It's fifty-three metres tall, and it was the first building in a whole new district planned around winter sports, inspired by the Beijing Winter Olympics.

mat yuh forray called it a living room for the neighbourhood. He also said it's like a painting: each time you see it, you discover something new.

I like that it's honest about its job. It's there to sell an idea of a place. But it does it with a bit of wit, and it gives people a terrace and a view while it's at it.

If you can, come back after dark. The lighting inside the cubes and in the gap between the two skins of glass makes the whole stack glow, like ice lit from within.
