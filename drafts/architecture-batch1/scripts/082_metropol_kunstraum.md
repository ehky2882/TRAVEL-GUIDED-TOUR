# Metropol Kunstraum, Munich

**Place:** Georgenstraße 42, 80799 Munich (Schwabing/Maxvorstadt), Germany (former Metropol petrol station)  
**Coordinate (WGS-84):** 48.15530, 11.57690 (from the owner)  
**Note:** Normally open Wed 13:30–17:30 and by appointment. As of 8 October 2026 the site says it is temporarily closed after water damage; current show Fred Sandback, Negativraum, 24 Sep–16 Dec 2026  
**Length:** 335 words (about 134 seconds)

## Script (clean)

In nineteen fifty-five, you'd have pulled in here for petrol. Today you come for some of the quietest, most minimal art in Munich, under the same swooping roof.

Stand on Georgenstraße in front of number forty-two. You should see an apartment block, and at its base, a low former filling station, with a big, flaring, trumpet-shaped canopy sweeping out over the forecourt.

Start with that canopy. It's the building's signature, a confident piece of nineteen fifties optimism, when the motor car was the future and petrol stations were built to look like it. The station and workshop were built in nineteen fifty-five as the base of the flats above. An architecture exhibition once picked it out as one of Munich's hidden gems.

Now look inside. Since two thousand and seven, the old garage has been an art space, set up by the collector Markus Michalke to show work from his collection. It isn't a commercial gallery. The focus is on drawings and sculpture, and above all on American minimalism and the artists who came after it, names like Donald Judd, Sol LeWitt and Fred Sandback. The very first show was Gordon Matta-Clark, the artist famous for cutting buildings open.

Each exhibition runs for three or four months and concentrates on one artist through several works, with a little booklet and an essay to go with it. The current one is on Fred Sandback, who made sculptures out of nothing more than stretched coloured thread, drawing lines through empty space.

[beat]

I love the fit. Minimal art is about space, light and looking hard at simple things. A glass box built for selling petrol turns out to be a perfect place to do that.

Before you go, check the opening times. It's normally only open on Wednesday afternoons, and at the moment it's closed after water damage. Even shut, the canopy is worth the walk, and the English Garden is only a few minutes east.
