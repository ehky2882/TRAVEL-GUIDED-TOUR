# Zayed National Museum, Abu Dhabi

**Place:** Saadiyat Cultural District, Saadiyat Island, Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates  
**Coordinate (WGS-84):** 24.53349, 54.40670 (exact; note the existing catalogue pin 24.53456, 54.4188 is about 1.2 km east)  
**Note:** Opened 3 December 2025. Tickets at zayednationalmuseum.ae  
**Length:** 341 words (about 136 seconds)

## Script (clean)

Five great steel feathers rise over this museum, and the tallest reaches a hundred and twenty-three metres. They're shaped like the wing of a falcon in flight.

Stand in the garden at the approach and look up. You should see a low, grassy mound of a building, and rising from it, five tall, curved, sail-like steel wings, tilted against the sky, their glazed surfaces catching the sun.

Start with the wings. The architect, Norman Foster, of Foster and Partners, took the image from falconry, one of the great passions of Sheikh Zayed, the founder of the United Arab Emirates. But the wings have a job too. Foster calls them thermal chimneys. As the sun heats them, they draw warm air up and out, pulling cooler air through the museum below, an old desert trick in a new form.

Now look at the path you've walked. The visit begins outside, along Al Masar, a six-hundred-metre garden walk, before you ever go in. Inside, six permanent galleries tell the story of Sheikh Zayed's life, the natural landscapes of the Emirates, and three hundred thousand years of people living here, with more than fifteen hundred objects.

Look out, too, for the treasures other sources highlight: one of the oldest natural pearls ever found, pages from the Blue Qur'an, and a reconstruction of an ancient Magan boat.

It opened on the third of December, twenty twenty-five, the day after National Day, at the heart of the Saadiyat Cultural District, alongside Jean Nouvel's Louvre Abu Dhabi.

[beat]

It's a building for a nation's founder, and it could easily have been a monument. What I find more interesting is that it chose a bird and a breeze: a symbol of the desert, and a way of surviving its heat.

Before you leave, stand directly under one of the wings and look straight up. Then walk over to the Louvre Abu Dhabi, a few minutes away, to see a very different way of keeping the sun off.
