# Backyard Community Club, Accra

**Place:** Osu, Accra, Ghana  
**Coordinate (WGS-84):** 5.56059, -0.18244 (area: Osu neighbourhood; move onto the club before use)  
**Note:** A community tennis club; check access before visiting  
**Length:** 334 words (about 134 seconds)

## Script (clean)

In a crowded part of Accra, where green space is getting scarce, someone built a tennis court, and surrounded it with walls of earth.

Stand on the street and look at the club. You should see a ring of tall, angular, earth-coloured walls, about four metres high, each one a wedge set at an angle, with gaps between them, so you can catch glimpses of the court inside.

Start with the earth. The walls are rammed earth, soil packed down hard in layers, so each wall shows stripes like a cut through the ground. It's a very old, very low-carbon way of building. The new part is how these were made: cast in sections off site and then put up here in about twelve days, which the architects, DeRoche Projects, say is a first for Ghana.

Now look at the shape of the walls. There are thirty-three of them, all wedges. They're angled so passers-by can see in, so spectators get shade, and so the wind across the court is broken up, which every tennis player will appreciate.

Then walk along the entrance path. It's lined with a garden, about two hundred and thirty square metres of it, with more than twenty kinds of edible and medicinal plants. There are changing rooms and built-in seats, too.

The studio's founder, Glenn DeRoche, describes it as a platform for community, mentorship and movement, and the design as deliberately open-ended. It opened in twenty twenty-five, and a second phase, a community garden, has already begun.

[beat]

What I love is that it's a tennis club that refuses to be exclusive. The walls are made of the local ground, they have gaps in them on purpose, and the first thing you meet is a vegetable garden.

Before you go, take a walk round Osu. It's one of Accra's liveliest districts, and you'll see how precious a patch of open space like this really is.
