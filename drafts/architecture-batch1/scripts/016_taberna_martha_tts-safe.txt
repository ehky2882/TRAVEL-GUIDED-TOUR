Taberna Martha, Madrid

The woman this tavern is named after is ninety-three, and she lives in Buenos Aires. She's never been here. Her recipes have.

Find number fifteen on Calle de mee gell sair vet, and look through the window before you go in. You should see a long wooden bar, old tiles with leafy patterns on the walls, and a floor of red granite worn to a soft shine. That's all from the tavern that was here before. The studio that redid the place, co doo, kept it, restored it, and added long mirrors and a bench along the bar.

marta is the grandmother of pablo van deh roosten, who came to Madrid from Buenos Aires, worked as a journalist, and wrote down years ago that he wanted a place of his own. The kitchen is run by paul cahpra, who trained in some serious Barcelona kitchens.

The food is Madrid tavern meets Argentine Sunday lunch. There's tortilla made with salt cod in peel peel sauce, ensaladilla topped with red tuna tartare and a fried egg, and the grandmother's dishes: a pork milla nessa with sage-butter pasta or mash, nyokees on the twenty-ninth of the month, as is the Argentine custom, and her flan with dool seh deh letcheh, served straight from the dish.

Then walk through. The back room, al fondo, is built round a DJ booth, turntables and big speakers, and it's darker and quieter, the street noise gone. On Wednesdays they play five albums all the way through. Beyond it is a small private room with an original pink marble fireplace and a green marble floor.

What I like is that nothing here is pretending. The tavern bones stayed Madrid, the cooking stayed Buenos Aires, and the two get along perfectly well.

If you've got a seat, ask what's on the turntable. And if it's the twenty-ninth, order the nyokees.
