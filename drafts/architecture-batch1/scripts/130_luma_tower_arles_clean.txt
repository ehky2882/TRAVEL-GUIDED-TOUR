LUMA Tower, Arles

Vincent van Gogh painted The Starry Night while he was living in this part of Provence. A hundred and thirty years later, Frank Gehry built a tower that tries to catch the same restless light.

Stand in the park and look up at the tower. You should see a fifty-six-metre tower that seems to twist as it rises, its upper part covered in thousands of small, angled, shining metal boxes, like crumpled foil or a cliff face, sitting on a glass base.

Start with the skin. There are about eleven thousand stainless steel panels, each set at a slightly different angle, so they catch and break up the sunlight and the sky. As the day goes on and the clouds move, the tower flickers and changes colour. Gehry said his ideas came from Van Gogh's painting and from the Alpilles, the rocky little mountains just outside Arles.

Now look down at the park around you. This was a railway yard, the old railway workshops of Arles, and the site is still called the Parc des Ateliers, the workshops park. It's been turned into a new landscape by the Belgian landscape architect Bas Smets, while some of the old industrial sheds have become galleries.

The whole project is the creation of Maja Hoffmann, through her LUMA Foundation, as a centre for art, ideas and research. It took about seven years to build and opened in June twenty twenty-one.

Arles is a town of Roman arenas and medieval cloisters, and plenty of people found this tower a shock. But Arles has always taken in strangers. Van Gogh himself was one, and the town didn't much like him at first either.

Before you go, if the tower's upper terrace is open, take the lift up. From there you can look out over the Roman amphitheatre and the old town, the landscape Van Gogh painted, spread out below.
