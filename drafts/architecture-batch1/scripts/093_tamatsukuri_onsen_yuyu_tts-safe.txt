This is an onsen town named after jewels. tamatsookooree means making jewels, and for centuries this valley produced the curved magatahma beads you'll find in ancient Japanese tombs. So its public bath, of course, is shaped like one.

Stand by the tamayoo River, in the middle of the hot-spring town. You should see a striking, modern building among the traditional inns, with a bold, curving form that echoes the comma-like shape of a magatahma bead.

Start with the architect. It's by shin takamatsoo, from shimahneh, this prefecture, famous for his dramatic, machine-like buildings in Kyoto and elsewhere. Here, he took the town's jewel as his theme. The whole building is a play on the magatahma, the curled bead with a hole at one end, a shape that's been sacred in Japan for more than two thousand years.

Now go up, if you're bathing. The big baths are on the top floor, the fifth, and the tubs themselves are shaped like magatahma. There are outdoor baths, a waterfall shower to massage your shoulders, saunas, and a lounge to recover in afterwards.

And look up as you come in. The glass roof over the entrance hall uses see-through solar cells, so it lets the daylight in and makes electricity at the same time.
