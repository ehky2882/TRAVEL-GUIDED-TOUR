Galleree Sankont Kat and the Otel Martel, Paris

This whole street is named after one architect, and every house in it is his. It's a little modernist village tucked into Paris, and one house has been brought back exactly as it was.

Stand in rue mallay stevens and look along it. You should see a short, private-feeling street of crisp white houses, built in cubes and blocks, with flat roofs, balconies and terraces. Find the one on the corner with a tall, round stair tower showing on the street front.

That's the otel martel, built in nineteen twenty-seven by Robert mallay stevens for twin brothers, yan and zho el martel, both sculptors, who shared a studio inside. The brothers had worked with him at the great Paris exhibition of nineteen twenty-five, where they made a garden of trees out of concrete.

Start with the details. Look for the black lacquered steel doors, the dark gridded windows, the white plaster, and the zig-zag base along the bottom. The house steps back as it rises, giving balconies and terraces. And inside, I'm told, the stair spirals up with mirrored sides, so it seems to go on forever.

Now the story. The Martel family kept the house until the nineteen nineties. Then the dealer eric tooshaloam bought it, restored it to its original form and colours, and moved in. In the old sculpture studio, he runs galleree sankont kat, showing sculpture, painting and mid-century furniture, by figures like zhon proovay, sharlot pereeon, Le Corbusier and pyair zhannerett.

What I like is that the house has gone back to doing what it was built for: a home and a workshop for art, in the same rooms. That's a better kind of restoration than a museum.

Before you go, walk the length of rue mallay stevens and look at the other houses. Most have been altered over the years. See if you can tell, from this one, what they all once looked like.
