Tunnel of Light, Kiyotsu Gorge

For more than twenty years, this was simply a tunnel to look at a gorge. Then an architect turned it into a journey, and at the end of it, the gorge turns upside down.

Stand at the tunnel entrance. You should be at the mouth of a long, dark passage cut into the mountain, with the Kiyotsu Gorge, one of the three great gorges of Japan, hidden somewhere off to one side, beyond the rock.

Start with the tunnel itself. It's seven hundred and fifty metres long, built in nineteen ninety-six so visitors could see the gorge safely, from viewpoints cut through the rock. By twenty eighteen it had become rather tired. For the Echigo-Tsumari Art Triennale, a festival founded to bring life back to this ageing rural area, the Beijing practice MAD, led by Ma Yansong, was asked to renew it.

Now walk in. Along the way, there are installations at each lookout: a Periscope, a room called Expression of Colour, another called Invisible Bubble, one called The Drop. Each one changes the light and colour of the tunnel, and turns a walk through a tunnel into a sequence of moods.

Then keep going to the very end, the Light Cave. Stainless steel lines the ceiling, reflecting the rock, and on the floor, a shallow sheet of water mirrors the gorge outside, so the cliffs and the river seem to fold back into the tunnel. This is the picture everyone has seen, and it's even better in person.

The effect was immediate. In twenty eighteen, the tunnel had more than a hundred and eighty thousand visitors, three times the year before.

[beat]

I like the idea that you don't need to build anything new to make a place extraordinary. The tunnel was already here. The gorge was already here. MAD just changed how you arrive.

Before you leave, take your time at the Light Cave. Stand still at the edge of the water and wait for the people in front to move. For a moment, you might get the whole gorge reflected to yourself.
