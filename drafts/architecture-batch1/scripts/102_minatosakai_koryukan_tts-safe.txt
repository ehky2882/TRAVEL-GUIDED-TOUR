Stand by sakai minato Station and look at the building beside it. You should see a large, dramatic, machine-like structure of stone and metal by the harbour, and a mural of cartoon characters on its granite wall.

Start with the architect. It's by shin takamatsoo, from neighbouring Shimane, one of the most theatrical Japanese architects of the nineteen eighties and nineties, famous for buildings that look like engines and machines. It was built by tottoree Prefecture, finished in nineteen ninety-seven, and it's the biggest building in sakai minato.

Now look at the details, and the scars. In twenty twelve, the prefecture spent a large sum fixing leaks. The triangular glass windows high on the south side were covered over in steel plates, and some of the decoration on the north side was stripped away. So what you see today is a slightly tamed version of Takamatsu's design.

Then look at the mural. sakai minato is the home town of shigeroo mizookee, creator of the manga GeGeGe no kitahro, about a boy who lives among yoh kye, the spirits and monsters of Japanese folklore. In twenty twelve, kitahro and his friends were painted onto the granite wall.

Inside, it's a working building: a tourist office, a conveyor-belt sushi restaurant, a souvenir shop, and the ferry terminal for the oh kee Islands. Since twenty twenty-two, there's also a walk-through haunted house of yoh kye.

There's something rather wonderful about a building this serious, all steel and granite and drama, ending up as the home of cartoon ghosts. Buildings outlive the intentions of their architects, and sometimes they find a better job.

Before you go, walk down Mizuki Shigeru Road, starting near the station. It's lined with more than a hundred bronze statues of yoh kye, and you'll understand why kitahro ended up on the wall.
