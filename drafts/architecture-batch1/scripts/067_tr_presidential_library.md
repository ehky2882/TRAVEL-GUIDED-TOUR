# Theodore Roosevelt Presidential Library, Medora

**Place:** On a butte above Medora, North Dakota, USA (edge of Theodore Roosevelt National Park)  
**Coordinate (WGS-84):** 46.91734, -103.54941 (exact)  
**Note:** Opened 4 July 2026. Hours vary by season (summer 9am–6pm daily); check trlibrary.com  
**Length:** 327 words (about 131 seconds)

## Script (clean)

Theodore Roosevelt came out here as a grieving young man in the eighteen eighties, to ranch cattle and recover. He later said he'd never have been president without his time in North Dakota. So here, of all places, is his library.

Stand on the path as you approach. You should see a long, low building that barely rises above the prairie, its roof covered in grass, sweeping gently up and over, with bands of glass and timber beneath, and all around, the wild, banded hills of the Badlands.

Start with the roof. It's a green roof of native grassland, and you can walk on it, day or night. The architects, Snøhetta, made it crest over the building and slope down to meet the ground, so the library looks less like a monument and more like part of the hill it sits on. At night, it's meant for stargazing.

Now look at the walls. There's mass timber, wooden screens against the sun and wind, and stone from the local area. Underneath the grass roof there are two separate blocks, with a breezeway between them: one for the theatre and classrooms, the bigger one for the galleries and a café.

It opened on the fourth of July, twenty twenty-six, the two hundred and fiftieth birthday of the United States. And it's aiming for four zeros: zero energy, zero carbon, zero water and zero waste. Unusually, it's privately funded and run independently of the National Archives.

[beat]

What I like is the modesty of the gesture. Roosevelt was not a modest man, but he was a great conservationist, and he set aside vast areas of American wilderness. A library that lies low and lets the Badlands do the talking feels like the right tribute.

Before you go, walk up onto the roof and look out over the national park. Then take the boardwalk trail out through the Badlands. That landscape is the real exhibit.
