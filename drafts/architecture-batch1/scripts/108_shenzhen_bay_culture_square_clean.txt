Shenzhen Bay Culture Square

A design museum that looks like a cluster of smooth white boulders washed up on a green hillside by the sea.

Stand in Shenzhen Talent Park and look towards the bay. You should see a rolling green landscape that rises gently into planted roofs, and sitting on top of it, a group of big, pale, rounded stone volumes, stepping down in height towards the water.

Start with those stones. They're the museum's galleries, each one wrapped in white granite cut into long, slender strips that follow the curves, so from a distance they look like river pebbles, and up close like carved rock. The architects, MAD, led by Ma Yansong, Dang Qun and Yosuke Hayano, broke the building into separate pieces, so it doesn't loom over the bay as one great block.

Now look at the ground they sit on. The undulating green roofs aren't separate from the park, they carry straight on from it, with slopes and lookouts over the water, planted only with native species. You can walk up over the museum without going in. And on the east side, the road has been sunk underground, so the whole ground level belongs to people on foot.

Inside, there's much more than you'd guess from the green hill: exhibition halls, education spaces, a design library and a theatre. It's huge, about a hundred and eighty-eight thousand square metres of building, though most of it is hidden under the landscape.

It took eight years, from twenty eighteen, and opened in twenty twenty-six, one of Shenzhen's major new cultural buildings, looking out across the bay towards Hong Kong.

I like the trick of it. A building this big could have been a wall between the city and the sea. Instead, MAD buried most of it, and left only a few stones showing, with the park running straight over the top.

Before you go, climb up onto the green roof and walk out to a lookout. On a clear day, you can see across the water to the hills of Hong Kong.
