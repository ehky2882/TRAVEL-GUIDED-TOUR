Jong shoo guh, Shenzhen

It looks like a spiral staircase. Try to climb it and you'll find it's a bookshelf, lying on its side.

Come in through the corner of the building, behind the big glass front, and you're straight into what the designers call the concept area. Almost all of it is taken up by one thing: a vast spiral that curls through the room from the entrance to the exit, its treads lined with books. Under your feet, the floor is glossy black tile, so the whole spiral is doubled in reflection.

Now find the spot in front of the spiral where it lines up. From there, a clock dial appears, cut out of the structure, and the handrails become the hands. The designer, lee shyang of ex plus living, says the idea is history being pushed through time at speed, and that the spiral stands for Shenzhen itself, a young city built by people who came here from everywhere else.

Li has also said where the feeling for this kind of space began: in two thousand and eight, on a study trip to Germany, standing in front of ah neesh ka poor's sculpture Memory. You can see the debt in the way the shape swallows the room.

Walk on through. There's a forum where the shelves climb a towering ladder, a children's area with a Ferris wheel and a castle painted in childlike brushstrokes, and tables that seem to float. The colours shift from a warm, bright orange to hazy blues and purples.

jong shoo guh is a chain, and its shops are famous for being photographed. It would be easy to be cynical about that. But a bookshop that makes people want to come in is doing its job, whatever brings them through the door.

Before you go, take a book off the spiral and actually read a page or two at one of the floating tables. It's the one thing the designers can't do for you.
