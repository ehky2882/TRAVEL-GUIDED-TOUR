House in Nishizahkee, Okinawa

A house with no windows sounds like a punishment. This one was built to give its owners exactly what they asked for: light, air and nobody looking in.

This is a private home, so please stay on the street. Look for a plain, solid block of raw grey concrete, in a neighbourhood of houses, shops and roads, with no windows facing you at all, just a sheltered porch at the front.

Start with the walls. They're exposed concrete, rough and left as they came out of the formwork, inside and out. nishizahkee is built on reclaimed land in the south of okinawa, with tightly packed houses to the north and a busy road and shopping area to the south. The owners wanted a home that would shut out the noise and the neighbours' sight lines completely.

So where does the light come from? From above and from within. The architects, Studio kochee, cut courtyards and skylights into the block, lined up from north to south. The courtyard at the front buffers the street and leads from the entrance to the living room. The one at the back is tall and deep, like a cave open to the sky, and shields the house from the commercial area behind. Sunlight and breezes come down into them, and from inside you look out at the sky, not at the road.

Inside, dark wooden joinery softens the concrete, there's a tatahmee room on the ground floor, and a skylit passage lined with wooden cupboards leads to the bedroom. It was finished in twenty twenty-three.

On an island of typhoons, there's a real logic to a house like this. It turns its back on the weather and the world, and keeps its sky private.

Before you go, look around at the neighbours' windows, all facing the street and each other. Then look back at this one. Its windows face only the sky.
