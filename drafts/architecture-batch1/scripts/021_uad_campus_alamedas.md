# Torre UAD Alamedas, Durango

**Place:** Campus Alamedas, Universidad Autónoma de Durango (UAD Lobos), Las Alamedas, Durango, Mexico  
**Coordinate (WGS-84):** not located; get a map pin for the tower before use  
**Length:** 326 words (about 130 seconds)

## Script (clean)

Most university buildings show you a wall. This one shows you the whole inside from the street, like a lantern with the lid off.

Stand on the promenade in Las Alamedas, among the trees, and face the tower. You should see a broad flight of steps rising from the pavement to a raised entrance, and above it a tall glazed front. Through the glass, you can see right into the lobby and up through a central atrium that runs the full height of the building.

Start with those steps. They're more than an approach. The architects, Jonathan Corral Arquitectos, made them a public plaza, somewhere to sit between classes, and lifting the entrance also helps protect the building from flooding. It's one of those cases where the practical answer and the generous one turned out to be the same.

Now look at the glass. Its pattern is taken from the stained-glass windows of Gothic cathedrals, blown up to the scale of a street. Behind it, a suspended sculptural staircase hangs in the middle of the atrium, which is how you move between floors, and from almost anywhere inside you can see out to the trees.

On the south side, look for the classrooms with odd, angled recesses. Those are the diagonal braces that hold the tower up, and instead of hiding them in the walls, the architects turned them into niches you can sit in.

Down below there's a digital research space in the basement, and at the top, three auditoriums and a roof terrace looking out over the city.

[beat]

Jonathan Corral was born in Durango and studied architecture at this very university, so this is an alumnus building for his old school. It shows. It gives the street something back.

If the building's open, go up to the roof terrace. And on your way out, look for the Torre Negra, the dark laboratory tower he also designed for this campus.
