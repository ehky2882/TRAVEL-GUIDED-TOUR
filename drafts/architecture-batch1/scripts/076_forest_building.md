# Forest Building, Richmond

**Place:** 9008 Quioccasin Road, Henrico County, Richmond, Virginia, USA (now West End Presbyterian Church)  
**Coordinate (WGS-84):** 37.60659, -77.57154 (exact)  
**Note:** Now a church; be respectful during services  
**Length:** 344 words (about 138 seconds)

## Script (clean)

A brick shop front with a forest growing straight through the middle of it. It was built that way on purpose, in nineteen eighty, as a shop.

Stand on the car park in front of the building. You should see a plain, long, brick façade, the kind of box you'd find on any American retail strip, except that it's been cut clean apart, and through the gap there's a strip of woodland, with trees and grasses growing right where the wall should be.

Start with the gap. It's about thirty-five feet wide. The trees in it were already here, and the building was designed around them. Look at the edges of the cut, lined with rounded concrete, sprayed on, which makes the brick look as if it's been torn rather than built. Shoppers used to walk over little bridges through the trees to get in.

It was designed by James Wines and his group SITE, which stands for Sculpture in the Environment, for a catalogue retailer called Best Products, and finished in nineteen eighty. Best commissioned SITE to do nine showrooms, and each one played a joke on the big-box store. One looked as if its bricks were peeling off. Another seemed to be crumbling at the corner.

[beat]

Here's the sad part. When Best Products went out of business in the nineteen nineties, almost all of those showrooms were demolished or changed beyond recognition. This one is described as the last surviving Best store with its original front. And it survived because a church bought it. West End Presbyterian moved in, in two thousand.

There's a lovely irony in that. A building that made a joke about consumerism and nature is now a place of worship, still with the forest running through it.

Before you go, walk up close to the cut and look at how the trees have grown since nineteen eighty. The building was making a point about nature taking back the suburbs. Forty-odd years on, nature is making it rather well.
