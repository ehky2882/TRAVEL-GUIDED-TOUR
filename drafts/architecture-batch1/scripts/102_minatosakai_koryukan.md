# Minatosakai Koryukan, Sakaiminato

**Place:** 215 Taisho-machi, Sakaiminato, Tottori, Japan (next to JR Sakaiminato Station)  
**Coordinate (WGS-84):** 35.54528, 133.22278 (from Japanese Wikipedia's 35°32′43″N 133°13′22″E; confirm on the building before use)  
**Length:** 334 words (about 134 seconds)

## Script (clean)

It's a ferry terminal that looks like a giant machine about to sail off, and today it's full of ghosts. Friendly ones, mostly.

Stand by Sakaiminato Station and look at the building beside it. You should see a large, dramatic, machine-like structure of stone and metal by the harbour, and a mural of cartoon characters on its granite wall.

Start with the architect. It's by Shin Takamatsu, from neighbouring Shimane, one of the most theatrical Japanese architects of the nineteen eighties and nineties, famous for buildings that look like engines and machines. It was built by Tottori Prefecture, finished in nineteen ninety-seven, and it's the biggest building in Sakaiminato.

Now look at the details, and the scars. In twenty twelve, the prefecture spent a large sum fixing leaks. The triangular glass windows high on the south side were covered over in steel plates, and some of the decoration on the north side was stripped away. So what you see today is a slightly tamed version of Takamatsu's design.

Then look at the mural. Sakaiminato is the home town of Shigeru Mizuki, creator of the manga GeGeGe no Kitaro, about a boy who lives among yokai, the spirits and monsters of Japanese folklore. In twenty twelve, Kitaro and his friends were painted onto the granite wall.

Inside, it's a working building: a tourist office, a conveyor-belt sushi restaurant, a souvenir shop, and the ferry terminal for the Oki Islands. Since twenty twenty-two, there's also a walk-through haunted house of yokai.

[beat]

There's something rather wonderful about a building this serious, all steel and granite and drama, ending up as the home of cartoon ghosts. Buildings outlive the intentions of their architects, and sometimes they find a better job.

Before you go, walk down Mizuki Shigeru Road, starting near the station. It's lined with more than a hundred bronze statues of yokai, and you'll understand why Kitaro ended up on the wall.
