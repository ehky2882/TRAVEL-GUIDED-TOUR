# Uchida Shoten Head Office, Fujisawa

**Place:** 1-4-23 Honcho, Fujisawa, Kanagawa, Japan (Fujisawa-juku, on the old Tokaido road)  
**Coordinate (WGS-84):** 35.34751, 139.48290 (exact: GSI geocode of 1-4-23 Honcho)  
**Note:** A company office with an artist residency at the front; view from the street  
**Length:** 358 words (about 143 seconds)

## Script (clean)

In old Japan, you paid tax on how wide your shopfront was. So people built houses that were very narrow at the front and very deep at the back. This office is a new building in that very old shape.

Stand on the street outside number one, four, twenty-three, Honcho. You should see a simple, narrow front, like a box set end-on to the road, with a row of awning windows across the façade that tilt open outward towards you.

Start with those windows. When they're open, they lift like flaps. The architect, Jo Nagasaka of Schemata Architects, describes them as opening as if breathing, and he says that's the point: they show a company that's continuously changing.

Now think about who's inside. Uchida Shoten is a hardware maker that's been in Fujisawa for about a hundred and sixty years. Its old building across the road was falling apart and couldn't meet modern earthquake rules, so it needed a new one. Because the budget was tight, the architects used a series of simple frames to make one long box.

At the front is a surprise. It isn't a reception desk, but an artist-in-residence space, which doubles as the company's entrance and common room. The offices are at the back. In between is what they call the nedoko, a berth, with somewhere to rest and a loft up a spiral staircase.

That narrow, deep plan is the machiya, the classic Japanese townhouse, and this street is where it makes sense. In the Edo period, Fujisawa-juku was the sixth post town on the Tokaido, the great road from Edo to Kyoto. The building was finished in May twenty twenty-five.

[beat]

I like that a hundred-and-sixty-year-old firm put artists at its front door. It's a hardware company saying that making things and making art aren't so different.

Before you go, walk along the old Tokaido route through the town. Look out for other long, narrow plots between the newer buildings, the shape of the old post town still hiding in the street.
