American Bar, Vienna

It's one of the smallest famous rooms in the world: about four and a half metres by six. But with a few mirrors, Adolf Loos made it feel endless.

Find the narrow passage off Kärntner Strasse and look above the door. You should see a glass mosaic of the American flag with the bar's name, sitting on four small marble pillars. Then step inside.

Start with the size. The room is about twenty-seven square metres, smaller than many living rooms. Loos designed it in nineteen-oh-seven and eight, and it's been a bar ever since.

Now look up, and then look at the walls. The ceiling is coffered in honey-yellow marble, square panels set in a grid. And the upper walls are mirrors. So the marble ceiling, the pillars and the beams are reflected again and again, carrying on far beyond the walls, and the tiny room seems to open out into a vast hall. It's one of the oldest tricks in architecture, done perfectly.

Then look down and around. The floor is a checkerboard of green and white marble. The booth seats and lower walls are panelled in mahogany, with brass details. And above the entrance, a wall of onyx glows softly with the light behind it.

It's still a working cocktail bar, open every day from noon until four in the morning. Dress the part: smart-casual, and leave the backpack and the flip-flops at the hotel.

[beat]

Loos was famous for hating ornament. And yet here, in a room smaller than a garage, he uses the richest materials he can find. The lesson, I think, is that he hated decoration, but loved materials. The marble and the mahogany are the decoration.

Before you leave, order a classic cocktail and sit in one of the booths. Then look up into the mirrors, and count how many times the ceiling repeats before it disappears.
