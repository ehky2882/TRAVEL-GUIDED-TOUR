Hípico Piedra Grande, Huixquilucan

A hundred and ten horses live here, in a building that's arguably nicer than most people's homes. And the clubhouse above it was built to keep out of their way.

Stand by the riding tracks and look uphill. Below you, the long two-storey stables: grey concrete, raw timber window panels, and a sawtooth roof on one wing. Behind them, stepped into the slope, a long, low pavilion with stone walls and a broad terrace pushed out over the hill. That's the clubhouse.

Start with the stables, because they set the rhythm. They're built on a grid of four metres by three, in three separate blocks joined by steel bridges around an open courtyard. Even the floor has been thought about: a recycled surface that gives the horses grip and keeps the noise down.

Then look up at the clubhouse. It's a single long building on a steel frame, but it's divided in two by a central patio, with heavy walls of local stone marking the line between the public rooms and the private ones. The stone does a second job too. It frames the views out to the valley, so as you walk through, the landscape appears in pieces.

The terrace is the generous bit. It cantilevers out over the slope, partly hovering, with views across the valley, the mountains and the whole of the club below. And the roof was engineered strong enough to hold events on later.

[beat]

The architects, Studio rc, led by Juan Pablo Ramírez Capdevielle and Pablo Germenos, said they wanted to change the site as little as possible, using stone, wood, steel and concrete in a plain modern language. On eight hectares of forest, that restraint is what makes it feel at home.

If you're here as a guest, take a drink out onto the very end of the terrace and look back at the stone walls. They're the same rock the hills are made of.
