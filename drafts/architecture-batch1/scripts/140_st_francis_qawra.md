# St Francis of Assisi Church, Qawra

**Place:** St Francis of Assisi Parish Church, Qawra (Buġibba area), St Paul's Bay, Malta  
**Coordinate (WGS-84):** 35.94822, 14.41690 (exact)  
**Note:** A working parish church; visit outside service times. Some sources place it in Buġibba, which adjoins Qawra  
**Length:** 323 words (about 129 seconds)

## Script (clean)

Most churches in Malta are built in the shape of a Latin cross, with a dome. This one isn't. It looks as if a cluster of geometric shapes has risen out of the ground and collided.

Stand in front of the church. You should see a large, modern building made of bold geometric forms, cylinders, blocks and planes, pushing up towards the sky, with a cloister alongside.

Start with the shapes. The architect, Richard England, is Malta's best-known modern architect, and he worked on this church and its cloister between nineteen ninety and nineteen ninety-six. In that period his buildings were compositions of solid and void, volumes and flat planes in Maltese stone and concrete, with arched and square openings. Look for the big cylinders around the building. Some local people say they represent the fingers of God. That's their reading, not his, but it's a lovely one.

Now go inside, if it's open. Visitors describe an unusual, airy interior with an unexpected shape, and a balcony. England has said that beauty in architecture, what the Roman writer Vitruvius called venustas, is really about atmosphere, something you feel with all your senses. This is a place to test that.

England started young. His first church, at Manikata, inspired by Malta's prehistoric temples, was commissioned by his father when he was in his twenties, after he'd worked in Milan with Gio Ponti. This church in Qawra came later, and it only became a parish in its own right in two thousand and four.

[beat]

What I like is that it doesn't pretend to be old. Malta has hundreds of baroque churches. This one asks what a church on a modern seafront might look like instead.

Before you go, walk round the outside and through the cloister. And if you have a car, drive out to Manikata to see his first church, on its rocky hillside.
