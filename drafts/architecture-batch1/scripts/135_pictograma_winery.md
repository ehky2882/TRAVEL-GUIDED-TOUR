# Vinícola Pictograma, Valle de Guadalupe

**Place:** Camino Vecinal 179, El Porvenir, Valle de Guadalupe, Ensenada, Baja California, Mexico (within the Banyan Tree Veya resort)  
**Coordinate (WGS-84):** 32.07506, -116.62867 (area: El Porvenir point; move onto the winery before use)  
**Note:** A working winery within a resort; check tasting and visit arrangements before use  
**Length:** 317 words (about 127 seconds)

## Script (clean)

The winemaker wanted to stand in the middle of his winery and see everything happening at once. So the architects built it in a circle.

Stand in the central courtyard. You should be in an open, round space under the sky, ringed by heavy walls of brick and coloured concrete that fan out from the centre, with rows of low barrel vaults behind them, and the vineyards of the valley all around.

Start with the circle. The winemaker, Lucas Dacosta, asked for it, and the architect Michel Rojkind explained why: from the courtyard, you can watch people going in and out of every room and understand the whole process of making the wine. The rooms are arranged around you in two rings, for production, storage and hospitality.

Now look up at the vaults. Rainwater runs off their curves into channels in the walls, and then down into the courtyard, where it soaks back into the ground. In a dry valley like this one, that's not decoration, it's survival.

Then look at the materials and the earth bank around the outside. The building is wrapped by a planted slope, which helps keep it cool, along with the thick walls, natural ventilation, and careful orientation to the sun. It's by Rojkind Arquitectos with AMASA Estudio, both from Mexico City, finished in twenty twenty-three, and it won an Architectural Record award in twenty twenty-five. The winery focuses on grenache, studying and celebrating the region's farming roots.

[beat]

What I like is that the shape comes straight from how the wine is made. It's not a monument to wine. It's a tool for making it, which happens to be beautiful.

If you can, book a tasting and drink a glass of the grenache in the courtyard. Then walk out onto the planted bank and look back at the vaults rising out of the vines.
