Zhardan deh Markay sak, Dordon

A hundred and fifty thousand box hedges, every one clipped by hand into a soft, round shape, rolling along a cliff top like a flock of green sheep.

Stand on the terrace by the château and look out over the garden. You should see waves of rounded, clipped box, tight green domes and mounds, spread across the slope in endless curves, with paths winding between them, and beyond, the valley of the dordon far below.

Start with the box. There are about a hundred and fifty thousand plants, some of them very old, and they're all pruned by hand. The shapes aren't geometric, like a formal French garden. They're soft and organic, like clouds or a herd of animals.

That style comes from one man. In the eighteen sixties, the estate passed to zhoolyan deh sairval, a magistrate from nearby sarla. He'd served in Italy, and he came back in love with Italian gardens. For about thirty years he planted box, cypress and stone pines, and laid out kilometres of walks. The estate itself was older, built in the late seventeenth century for bairtron vairnay de markay sak, an adviser to Louis the Fourteenth.

By the late twentieth century the gardens had fallen into neglect. In nineteen ninety-six, klaybair rossee yon, whose family already ran the nearby Château de kastel noh, bought them and brought them back.

What I find touching is the sheer patience of it. A garden like this is never finished. It has to be clipped, by hand, every year, for ever, or it melts back into a hillside.

Before you go, take the long walk out along the ridge to the Belvedere, on its rocky spur. The paths run for more than six kilometres in all, and from the end, high above the river, you can see the valley and its châteaux spread out below, kastel noh and bay nak among them.
