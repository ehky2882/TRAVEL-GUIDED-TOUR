# Biblioteca Vasconcelos, Mexico City

**Place:** Eje 1 Norte s/n, Buenavista, Cuauhtémoc, Mexico City, Mexico (beside Buenavista station)  
**Coordinate (WGS-84):** 19.44749, -99.15081 (exact)  
**Note:** All services free. Briefly closed in April 2025 during a staff dispute, reopened 20 April 2025. Check current hours before use  
**Length:** 391 words (about 156 seconds)

## Script (clean)

Imagine a library where the bookshelves don't stand on the floor. They hang in the air, stacked floor upon floor, and in the middle of it all floats the skeleton of a whale.

Step inside and look up. You should be standing at the bottom of a vast, open hall, with rows of black steel bookshelves hanging above you, level after level, linked by walkways and stairs and filled with light from the glass, more like the inside of a spaceship than a reading room.

Start with the shelves. There are more than forty thousand metres of them, open and suspended from the steel structure, holding the general collection, around six hundred thousand books. The architects, Alberto Kalach and Juan Palomar, arranged the building as three long blocks side by side, six floors each, in steel, concrete, glass, stone and wood. People sometimes say it inspired the endless bookshelves of the film Interstellar. Nobody has ever confirmed it, but you can see why they say it.

Now find the whale. Hanging in the middle of the hall is Matrix Móvil, by the Mexican artist Gabriel Orozco. It's the real skeleton of a grey whale, about eleven and a half metres long, and Orozco drew over every bone in graphite, covering them in fine, looping lines. In two thousand and nine it was shown at the Museum of Modern Art in New York.

Then look out of the windows. Around the building is a garden of twenty-six thousand square metres, with nearly sixty thousand plants of a hundred and sixty-eight species.

It opened in May two thousand and six, named after José Vasconcelos, the writer and education minister who spread public libraries across Mexico. The start was rocky. Less than a year later it closed over construction defects, and it didn't reopen until late two thousand and eight. And everything here is free.

[beat]

I love the generosity of it. A building this dramatic is usually a bank or a corporate headquarters. Here it's simply a public library, open to anyone who walks in from the station.

Before you go, climb up through the floating stacks to one of the high walkways. Look down at the whale from above, then out over the garden, and pick a book from shelves that seem to hang in mid-air.
