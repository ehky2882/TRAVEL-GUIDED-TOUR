# Xiao Feng Art Museum, Hangzhou

**Place:** Foot of Daci Mountain, Xihu District, Hangzhou, Zhejiang, China (reached on foot from a forest road)  
**Coordinate (WGS-84):** none: Daci Mountain site in the West Lake Scenic Area, not yet geocoded. Locate before use  
**Length:** 331 words (about 132 seconds)

## Script (clean)

The concrete here has been mixed with black ink. For a museum devoted to two painters, that seems exactly right.

Follow the forest road at the foot of Daci Mountain until the trees open. You should see a low, dark, charcoal-grey building, mostly blank-walled, wrapping around the hillside, with a bridge-like section that you walk underneath to get in.

Start with the colour. The concrete was tinted with black ink pigment and mixed on site, so it has a deep graphite tone, like the ink of a Chinese painting. Look closely and you'll see the grain of the timber moulds it was cast in, pressed into the surface.

Now walk under the bridge into the courtyard. Here, everything opens up. There's a still pool and small trees in the middle, and around it, the galleries push out as sharp-edged, angular rooms with big windows, so from the courtyard they seem to float. The architect, Zhang Ke of ZAO standardarchitecture, took the layout from the old Chinese scholar's garden, with its ring of rooms around an irregular courtyard.

Inside, the route goes round in a loop, through narrow passages that open into larger rooms, with ramps between the different levels, following the slope of the hill.

The museum is a tribute to Xiao Feng and Song Ren, a husband and wife who were both painters, and who died in twenty twenty-three. It holds around five hundred of their works and was completed in twenty twenty-five, among the hills of the West Lake scenic area.

[beat]

I like the idea of a building that's dark on the outside and full of light within, a bit like an ink stone, plain until you add the water and start to paint.

If you've time, walk on from here into the hills around West Lake. This landscape has been painted for centuries, and after the museum, you'll look at it with an artist's eye.
