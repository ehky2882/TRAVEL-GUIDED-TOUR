# Salt Salon, Borough

## Script (clean)

Most hair salons play music in the background. This one is built around the sound system, and the haircut is almost the excuse.

Find the old brick storehouse on Stoney Street, a few steps from Borough Market. From outside, there's not much to see: the street front is discreet, a Victorian warehouse with little to tell you what's inside. That's deliberate.

Step in, and start with the ground floor, the Listening Room. There are cutting stations and a reception bar, and standing in the middle, two big loudspeakers made of galvanised steel, built with the London hi-fi makers Friendly Pressure. At night, the bar becomes a venue.

Now go upstairs to the Cutting Floor. A long, curved mirror of stainless steel runs along the room, and as you move, the reflection shifts from hazy to sharp. More speakers hang from the ceiling.

Then go to the top, the Colour Floor. Here a huge mirrored workstation of stainless steel, weighing about half a tonne, hangs from the building's original timber rafters. The designers expect the hair dyes to stain it over time, so it slowly becomes a record of everyone who's sat there. Look too at the details: chairs of recycled foam to soak up the bass, silicone panels to soften the echo, and lights cast in bio-resin.

It's by the London studio Unknown Works, for Salt's founder, John Paul Scott, who used to produce electronic music, and it opened in twenty twenty-five as the brand's second home, after the original in Dalston.

[beat]

I like the idea of a hairdresser as a listening room. You sit still for an hour anyway. Why not with really good sound?

Before you go, walk back round into Borough Market. It's one of the oldest food markets in London, and after all that steel and sound, the noise of the stalls feels like a different kind of music.
