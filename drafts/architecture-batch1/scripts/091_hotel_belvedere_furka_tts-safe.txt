Hotel Belvedair, Foorka Pass

Sean Connery drove his Aston Martin round this bend in Goldfinger. The hotel is still here. The view it was built for is mostly gone.

Pull in at the hairpin bend and look at the building. You should see a tall, pale hotel, perched right on the outside of the curve of the mountain road, its shuttered windows looking up the valley, with bare grey rock and scree behind it, and somewhere up there, the rone Glacier.

Start with the position. In eighteen eighty-two, a man called yozef zyler built a lodge right in the hairpin the new pass road had made, so that travellers could stop and look at the glacier, which then came almost to the door. By eighteen ninety it had become a hotel. belvedair means beautiful view, and for decades it lived up to the name.

Now look for the ice. The rone Glacier has retreated more than a kilometre from the hotel. What was a wall of ice beside the building is now a long view up a rocky valley to where the glacier has shrunk back.

The hotel had a moment of fame in nineteen sixty-four, when the Bond film Goldfinger was shot on these bends. It closed in nineteen eighty, when the canton bought it and considered building a dam here. A family bought it in nineteen eighty-eight and reopened it in nineteen ninety. And it closed again in twenty fifteen, and it's been shut ever since.

It's hard to think of a building that tells the story of a changing climate more plainly. It was put here for one reason, to look at the ice, and the ice has walked away.

If you've got time, carry on up the road to the glacier itself. You can walk in and see the ice up close, and look out for the white blankets laid over parts of it in summer, to slow the melting down.
