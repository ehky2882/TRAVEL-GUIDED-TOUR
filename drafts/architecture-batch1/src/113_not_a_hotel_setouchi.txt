It's called NOT A HOTEL, and it's not, quite. It's a set of private villas on a little island, and they're the first buildings a famous Danish practice has completed in Japan.

From the shore, look towards the south-west cape of the island. You should see low buildings following the curve of the hillside, with long roofs that bend and slope with the land, and earth-coloured walls, all looking out over the calm, island-scattered Inland Sea.

Start with the walls. They're rammed earth, made from soil dug out of this very site and pressed into layers, so the buildings are literally made of the hill they stand on. You can see the stripes of the layers, like a cutaway of the ground.

Now look at the roofs. They're covered in solar panels, and they twist and turn to follow the slope, so the buildings look less like boxes placed on the hill and more like ribbons laid across it.

Then think about the names. The villas are called ninety, one-eighty, two-seventy and three-sixty, after the number of degrees of sea view each one gets. There's also a beach terrace restaurant.

It's by BIG, Bjarke Ingels Group, and these are its first completed buildings in Japan, opened in twenty twenty-six. Ingels calls them extensions of the dramatic topography, and, rather grandly, architectural oxymorons.

[beat]

I think the earth walls are the real achievement. Luxury villas could easily have been glass and concrete shipped in from elsewhere. Instead, they're made from the island itself, and in time they'll weather like it.

The villas are private, so enjoy them from the shore. Sagishima is a quiet island, and a slow walk along its shore is the best way to see the Inland Sea at its most peaceful.
