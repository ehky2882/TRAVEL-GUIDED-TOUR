In winter, Harbin is one of the coldest big cities on earth, and its streets fill with ice sculpture. So when it built an opera house, it built one that looks like it's been carved by the wind.

Stand on the plaza in front of the main entrance. You should see a huge, smooth, white form sweeping up out of the wetlands, with no straight lines anywhere, and a long path climbing across its surface. Above the entrance, a crystalline wall of glass catches the light.

Look at the skin first. It's white aluminium, in smooth panels that wrap the whole building in one continuous surface, meant to echo the marshes and the snow. The architects, MAD, led by Ma Yansong, said they shaped it as if by wind and water.

Now the glass above the lobby. It's made of small glass pyramids, some smooth and some faceted, held on a light diagonal grid, so it glitters like ice. The lobby behind it has full-height glass walls that open straight onto the plaza.

Then the path. Those carved routes on the outside aren't decoration. You can climb them, right over the top of the building, to an open-air stage on the roof that doubles as a viewing platform. It's open to everyone, not just ticket holders, and it looks out over the wetlands and the city skyline.

[beat]

Inside, it's the opposite. The grand theatre, for more than sixteen hundred people, is lined in warm Manchurian ash, shaped as though a block of wood had been worn away. After all that white, it's like stepping inside a cello.

There's a smaller theatre too, for about four hundred. But before you go in, take the outside path to the very top and look back at the river. Then come down and see how different the same building feels from inside.
