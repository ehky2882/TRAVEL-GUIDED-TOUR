In eighteenth-century France, a folie was a garden building with no real purpose except pleasure. Christian Louboutin, the shoe designer, has built himself a modern one.

This tower is private, on Louboutin's own land among the pines, so you're unlikely to see more than a glimpse. Picture a broad, round concrete tower, punched with geometric openings, standing in a grove of umbrella pines on a little peninsula that pushes out into a lagoon, with the Atlantic just beyond.

Start with the idea. Its architect, Tarek Shamma, an Egyptian architect who has designed several of Louboutin's shops, said it has no other function than to please the eye and the mind. That's exactly what a folie should be.

Now imagine going inside. A web of stairs crisscrosses the interior, climbing round and over each other. The inspiration came from two places in India: the deep, stepped wells of Rajasthan, and the Jantar Mantar, the giant stone astronomical instruments of Jaipur and Delhi. As you climb, the view opens up, until at the top you look out across the land and the ocean.

In the middle there's a fireplace, lit in the day by sunlight falling through an opening in the roof. And at night, coloured lights set into the walls wash it all in pink, purple and blue. Louboutin opened it in the summer of twenty twenty-one with a birthday party for a hundred and fifty people. He sees it as a place for both parties and meditation.

[beat]

There's something rather wonderful about a building whose whole job is joy. Most architecture has to justify itself. This one simply gets to be beautiful, which is more or less what Louboutin's shoes are for too.

You can't visit the tower, but you can stay nearby. Louboutin's small hotel, Vermelho, is in the village of Melides. Have a drink there, then walk down to the lagoon at sunset, the same view the tower was built to see.
