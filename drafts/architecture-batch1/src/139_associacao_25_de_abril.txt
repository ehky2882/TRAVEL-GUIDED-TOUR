This one building has told Portugal's story in newspapers: first the birth of the Republic, then the propaganda of the dictatorship, and now the memory of the revolution that ended it.

Stand on Rua da Misericórdia and look at number ninety-five. You should see an early twentieth-century building with an asymmetric front, inspired by art nouveau, in the street that runs up from Chiado towards the Bairro Alto.

Start with the first newspaper. This was the headquarters of O Mundo, the first paper to break the news of the proclamation of the Portuguese Republic in nineteen ten. The street itself was renamed in its honour, Rua do Mundo, the street of The World.

Then came the dictatorship, the Estado Novo. The building housed the Diário da Manhã, the official paper of the regime's party. And, wonderfully, right across the street were the offices of República, a newspaper of the opposition. For years the two sides stared at each other across this pavement. Later it was home to another regime paper, Época, and after the revolution of the twenty-fifth of April, nineteen seventy-four, it stood empty.

Now it belongs to the Associação 25 de Abril, founded by the officers of the Armed Forces Movement who carried out that revolution, to keep its values alive: democratise, decolonise, develop. In two thousand, the architect Álvaro Siza worked on the building. Today there's an exhibition space on the ground floor, a restaurant on the first, offices and a library above.

[beat]

I love the poetic justice of it. The building that printed the dictatorship's news now belongs to the soldiers who brought it down.

Before you go, look across the street at the building opposite, where República used to be. Then walk up into the Bairro Alto. In April, you'll see red carnations everywhere, the flower of the revolution.
