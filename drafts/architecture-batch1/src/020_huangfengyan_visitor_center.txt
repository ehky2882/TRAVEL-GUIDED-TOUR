It looks like a stack of beehives on stilts. That's not a coincidence: this building's starting point was the humble wooden beehive box.

Stand on the approach and look at the building as a whole. You should see a row of timber boxes stacked two high, held up on a forest of slim metal poles, with the ground left open underneath. Higher up, on top, there's a deck with nothing above it but sky.

We're thirteen hundred and twenty metres up, on the slopes of the Jinfo Mountain area, at the entrance to a camping park of more than fifty hectares. Beekeeping is part of life in these mountains, and the architects, XJ Design, took the local beehive box as their symbol: a small, man-made thing that lives in balance with the forest around it.

Now look at the poles. They're scaffolding, the ordinary kind you'd see on any building site, combined with a steel frame. It was quick to put up on a steep site, and it can be taken down again without leaving a trace. The whole building is raised off the ground so the forest floor stays open, for plants and animals as much as for air, and the shade underneath helps keep things cool.

Inside the boxes, there's a drinks space themed on honey, with open sides so it's hard to tell where inside ends and outside begins. Smaller boxes at the ends hold the toilets, a shop and a studio for the operators. The busiest rooms sit in the middle, where the temperature stays steadiest.

[beat]

It was designed in January twenty twenty-five and finished that August. That speed, and the fact that it can all come apart again, says something good about how to build in a place like this. Touch lightly, and be ready to leave.

Climb up to the top deck before you go. It looks right round the mountains, and on a clear night it's a place for stargazing.
