The architects describe this building as an apparatus of the steaming hot spring. Which is a rather grand way of saying: it's a tower for having a bath in the trees.

Cross the steel arch bridge over the stream and walk through the poplar wood until you see it. You should see a slim, tall building of grey concrete standing on columns above the valley floor, with a steep rock cliff behind it to the west, and reeds and grasses all around.

Start with how it stands. The whole bathhouse is raised on ten columns, so it touches the slope as lightly as possible, and the water, the plants and the ground can carry on underneath. Instead of spreading out, the rooms are stacked one on top of another, to keep its footprint small.

Now go inside and up. On the ground floor, there's a box of teak with the reception, lockers and showers. On the first floor, there's a lounge wrapped in glass, sitting right among the tops of the poplar trees. And at the very top are the baths, pools at different temperatures, with long, low windows set just above the surface of the water, so as you soak, you look out at the cliff and the treetops.

Look up while you're there. Daylight comes down through eight light wells, about eight metres tall, and the concrete inside them has been roughened with a hammer, so it softens the light into a glow. The architects, Vector Architects, led by Gong Dong, spent nearly five years on it, and it opened in twenty twenty-four.

[beat]

I love that it goes up, not out. In a beautiful valley, the kindest thing a building can do is take up as little ground as possible. And the bather gets the best view in the valley as a reward.

Before you go, walk back down to the stream and look up at the building from below. You'll see the strip windows of the baths, high in the trees, and maybe a little steam drifting out above the valley.
