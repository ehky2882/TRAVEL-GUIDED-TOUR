Here's a library that treats the sea as the main event. The books are almost the supporting act.

Stand on the sand facing the building. You should see a low, chunky block of grey concrete sitting right on the beach, with long horizontal openings, and on the side facing the water, glass walls that can pivot open onto the sand.

Start with the reading room. Inside, the seating steps up towards the back like a little theatre, so that every seat looks over the heads in front, straight out to sea. The architects, Vector Architects, called the ocean a drama of nature, always changing. In good weather, the glass walls swing open and the reading room simply runs out onto the beach.

Now look up, if you go in. The roof is a half-arch, carried on steel trusses and dotted with small round holes, thirty centimetres across. From spring to autumn, the afternoon sun comes through them and drifts across the floor and the walls as moving spots of light. Beside the trusses, walls of hand-made glass blocks soften and filter the light.

Then find the meditation room. It's the opposite: dim and closed, with just two narrow slots, one facing east for the sunrise and one west for the sunset. Above it is a terrace where you can hear the sea without seeing it.

It's a small building, only about four hundred and fifty square metres, finished in twenty fifteen.

[beat]

What I think it does well is remind you that a library isn't really about books. It's about a quiet room and a long look at something.

Before you go, sit for a minute in the reading room, if you can, and watch the light spots travel. Then walk along the beach. The Seashore Chapel, by the same architects, is not far, raised up above the sand.
