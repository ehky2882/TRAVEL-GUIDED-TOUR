Fifty-six terraces, stacked up a trunk, and every one of them a garden. Heatherwick Studio calls this a tree. It's really a park, turned on its end.

Stand in the central plaza, where the district's outdoor streets all meet, and look up at the tall, branching structure in the middle. You should see broad, rounded platforms fanning out from it at different heights, planted and spilling over at the edges, with people on most of them.

Start with the petals, as the studio calls those platforms. There are fifty-six of them, and you're allowed to climb the lot. The planting changes as you go, and it follows a journey: the biomes of the old Silk Route, from alpine tundra to dry steppe. Xi'an was where that road began, so you're walking the Silk Road vertically, which is a rather good joke if you think about it.

The whole thing is just over fifty-seven metres tall, counted from the basement, and from the upper petals you can see across the district's roofs to the city beyond.

Now look at the buildings around you, and especially at the columns and curving beams. They're clad in tiles, more than a hundred thousand of them, made with local makers after over two thousand glaze experiments. The colour is no accident. This is the city of the Terracotta Army, and the roofs of the district borrow their shapes from Xi'an's old temples.

[beat]

What I like is that none of this is fenced off. The public spaces opened in December twenty twenty-four, free to enter. A shopping district could easily have been a box with a car park. This one gives the best view in the place away.

So climb. Pick a petal about two thirds of the way up, find the plants that look like they belong on a mountain, and look out over the district. It sits between two landmarks, the ruins of the old Temple of Heaven and the Shaanxi TV tower, and from up there you can try to find both.
