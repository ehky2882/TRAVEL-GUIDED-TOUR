From the sea road, it looks like a red fortress tumbling down the hill. It's actually a holiday village, and its family tree goes back to a famous wall in Spain.

Find a spot where you can see the whole resort on the slope above the water. You should see a cluster of square, red-earth blocks stacked at different heights, linked by outside staircases and bridges, with one long, grand staircase running straight down the middle towards the sea, ending in a big framed opening.

The architects are Ricardo Bofill Taller de Arquitectura, the Barcelona studio now run by his son Pablo. In the nineteen seventies, Ricardo Bofill built La Muralla Roja, the red wall, a pink-red housing complex on the cliffs at Calp in Spain, all stairs, courtyards and bridges, which has since become one of the most photographed buildings on the Mediterranean. Red Sol is its descendant.

Look at how it's laid out. In plan it's a grid, a sequence of identical squares, and the villas sit on those squares at different heights, stepping up and down so that each one can see the sea. Some squares were left as untouched landscape for everyone to share. The ground-floor homes are entered from private courtyards, and the upper ones open out, each with its own pool on the roof.

The model they had in mind was the kasbah, the fortified hill town of North Africa, tightly packed and full of shared corners. The colour comes from the earth, chosen to belong to the hillside.

[beat]

What's clever is the trade-off. A kasbah is built for defence. This one uses the same tangle of stairs and bridges to do the opposite, to make you bump into your neighbours.

Before you go, find the bottom of the grand staircase and look up through the portal at the whole thing climbing the hill. That view is the one the architects built the resort around.
