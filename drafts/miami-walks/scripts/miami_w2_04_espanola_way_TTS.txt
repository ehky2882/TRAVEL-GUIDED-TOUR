# MIAMI W2 · 04 — Española Way, from the Drexel end
W2 · Ocean Drive · segment 04 · S3 · TTS-safe · 24 September 2026

---

The west end of es pan yoh luh Way, where the lane meets Drexel Avenue, gives you the whole street in a single look back east: two short blocks of arcades and striped awnings running to Washington Avenue with nothing above three stories in the frame.

The walk pauses here, between one lost hotel and the next, on a street that shows what the rules produce once they work. The first buildings on this lane went up in nineteen twenty two as a small development called Whitman's Spanish Colony, and for most of the century after, the street was handled like any other, repaved, rewired and patched as needed.

When the city set out to rebuild the lane in twenty sixteen, the job had to satisfy the same preservation board that had once let the Revere go. The new paving, lighting and landscaping went before that board for approval, and on the sixth of June, twenty seventeen, es pan yoh luh Way reopened as a pedestrian only street, with the facades left as they were and the cars gone.

By then preservation on the Beach no longer stopped at the buildings, and it reached down to the pavers under your feet, so a lane that began as a developer's imitation of Spain was being rebuilt with the care a city gives to its own history.

Turn north on Drexel Avenue and follow it two blocks to Lincoln Road; if work is under way on the side street when you arrive, the posted walkways will carry you through.
