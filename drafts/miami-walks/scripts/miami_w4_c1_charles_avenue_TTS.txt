# MIAMI W4 · C1 — Charles Avenue, walking east

The cemetery you just passed has been in the community's own hands for more than a century. Its original burying ground was a lot the town opened in nineteen oh four farther up this street and soon judged too small, and moving the graves led the families to form their own association; in nineteen thirteen five of them, the Burrows, Higgs, Reddicks, Rosses and Stirrups, bought this ground as trustees for a hundred and forty dollars. Their descendants keep it still; the name honors Charlotte Jane Stirrup, wife of one of the five.

Before it was Charles Avenue this was Evangelist Street, named for its churches, and the marker ahead records that most of the families who built here came from the island of ee loo ther uh, where their parents had been enslaved. The small wooden houses surviving between newer ones are the original form here, narrow, raised off the ground, porch to the street; one of the oldest, a frame house from eighteen ninety that belonged to Mariah Brown, still stands in the thirty two hundred block.

And the road itself was among the last in the Grove to be paved or given sewers, decades after the families laid its first surface in stone; the people along it walked it every working day toward the bay.

Ahead, where the avenue meets Main Highway, the old playhouse stands on the corner behind construction fencing; turn right there and follow Main Highway a quarter mile south to a church made of the same stone as the first road.
