# MIAMI W2 · 05 — Lincoln Road, the east end
W2 · Ocean Drive · segment 05 · S3 · walk close (beat) · clean · 24 September 2026

---

Lincoln Road's pedestrian mall ends at Washington Avenue, one block east of where Drexel brought you in, so walk to that eastern mouth and face east, away from the shops. Beyond the crossing, Lincoln Road runs on as an ordinary street for a short block to Collins Avenue and the oceanfront hotels.

The walk began at the most recent of the Beach's preservation fights and has moved backward ever since, from a garden wall on Ocean Drive to a corner on Collins, and here it reaches the first one. At the end of that block, at sixteen eleven Collins Avenue, stood the New Yorker, an eight-story hotel of 1940 that some admirers considered the masterpiece of Henry Hohauser. It stood inside the newly listed district, its owners held a demolition permit, and on the twenty-third of April, 1981, without public warning, a crane and a wrecking ball began taking it apart.

The city's rules at the time could do nothing to stop it, since the listing two years earlier was an honor rather than a lock, and local protections had barely begun. The loss became the rallying point for local designation, and the argument that started that week ran on through the Senator, the Revere and every amended ordinance that followed.

*[beat]*

The preservation league that fought for these streets still carries the New Yorker in its logo, so the district's own defenders chose a building they lost, rather than one they saved, as their emblem. Every eyebrow you passed on the way here, every porch and parapet on Ocean Drive, stands inside protections that began with a hotel you cannot see from this corner.

Behind you the mall runs west under the canopies and follies Morris Lapidus gave it in 1960, which the city now plans to restore as history in their own right. Turn around and walk into Lincoln Road; the walk ends here, and the mall is a fair reward for a city that learned, one loss at a time, to keep what it had.
