# MIAMI W1 · 01 — The Royal Palm's Other House
W1 · The Pitch · segment 1 · S1 · TTS-safe · 24 September 2026

---

Stand at the gates of Fort Dallas Park on Southeast Fourth Street, a small walled green on the north bank of the river, with the Metromover's Riverwalk station beside you. Inside the fence is a two and a half story frame house with a shingled gable roof, painted yellow. If the gates are shut when you pass, the house reads well enough through the fence.

It is called Palm Cottage, and it was built around eighteen ninety seven as one of at least thirty identical rental houses in a row a few blocks north, on the street then called Fourteenth and now Southeast Second. They were built for the men putting up the Royal Palm Hotel, the railroad's winter resort, which stood near the river mouth just east of here. In nineteen eighty the house was moved to this park to save it from demolition, and by the state's own historical marker it is the last building in the city known to be tied directly to the man who built the railroad and the hotel.

The hotel is long gone; the cottage isn't. The Royal Palm was the showpiece, the thing the railroad could advertise up north, built to persuade people with money that a raw settlement at the end of the line was worth a winter. The cottage was the part nobody advertised: a rented house for someone who had to be here in August, when the guests had gone home.

The resort was the argument, and it was made to people who would leave by April. The cottage was the first piece of proof, because an argument for a city only comes true when somebody stays through the summer, and the last survivor of that first push was built for the people who did.

From here the walk heads inland, north to Flagler Street and then west toward a stepped tower, a little under half a mile; if the sun is already high, the Metromover from this station to Government Center covers the leg for you. Your next stop is the Dade County Courthouse.
