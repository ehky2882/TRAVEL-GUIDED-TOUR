# MIAMI W3 · 02 — Connective: the Walk of Fame stars
W3 · Calle Ocho · segment 2 · S5 · connective · clean · 24 Sept 2026

---

You're walking the old dividing line itself now, and the sidewalk has been turned into a register of names: pink marble stars, set into the concrete along Calle Ocho between Twelfth and Seventeenth Avenues, each carrying the name of a performer.

The stars were begun in 1987, the idea borrowed from Hollywood, and at first they honored Cuban performers, many of whom had built their careers in exile, with Celia Cruz, Gloria Estefan and Willy Chirino among the names you will pass; the city has also given this stretch of the street Celia Cruz's name as an honorary title. Over the years the list widened to singers, actors and television stars from across Latin America, performers whose tie to this street is their audience rather than their birthplace.

The names turn the old geography inside out. For decades this stretch of sidewalk was the edge of two neighborhoods, the place where one side's business ended and the other's houses began; now it is the strip of Little Havana that belongs to both sides equally, shared ground where the neighborhood keeps its roll of honor underfoot, on neither side and in the middle of each.

Keep walking west toward Fifteenth Avenue, where a steel tower rises over the south side of the street and the theater beneath it is the next stop.
