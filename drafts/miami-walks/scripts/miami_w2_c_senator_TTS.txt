# MIAMI W2 · C — The Senator corner, Twelfth Street and Collins Avenue
W2 · Ocean Drive · segment C · S3 · walk payoff (beat) · TTS-safe · 24 September 2026

---

The corner of Twelfth Street and Collins Avenue looks ordinary, and whatever stands on it the day you pass, for decades after nineteen eighty eight it held little more than parked cars. Before that it held the Senator, a three story hotel of nineteen thirty nine by the architect L. Murray Dixon, with a stepped roofline, porthole windows, and eyebrows that ran unbroken around the corners of every floor.

By the late nineteen eighties the Beach had a preservation ordinance, but the version in force let the city delay a demolition permit for six months and no longer, so an owner willing to wait could still demolish. In nineteen eighty seven the preservation league launched a campaign to save the Senator, with meetings, protests and demonstrators chained to the front door, and they held off the demolition for more than a year. The owner waited, and in October nineteen eighty eight the building came down.

The outcry did what the protest could not, and the City Commission passed a strengthened ordinance designed to block demolitions like this one. The hotels you passed on Ocean Drive have stood ever since partly because this one did not.

Seen from this corner, the district's protections look less like a monument than like scar tissue, each rule laid down the morning after something fell, and every building still standing owing a little of its survival to one that is gone.
