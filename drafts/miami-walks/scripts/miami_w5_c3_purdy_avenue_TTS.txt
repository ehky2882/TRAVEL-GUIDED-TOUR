# MIAMI W5 · C3 — Purdy Avenue

W5 · The Water Line · segment C3 · walk connective · TTS-safe · 24 September 2026

---

Walking north on Purdy Avenue, you have the marina on your left and, on your right, a row of low, flat roofed buildings put up for work rather than for show. They served the boats across the street and the trades behind them, and the neighborhood took its name from the harbor it once kept running. Many of them now hold places to eat and exercise and shop, with apartment buildings rising behind them toward Bay Road.

The fronts of the older buildings give their first job away. Several still carry raised platforms at the height of a truck bed, so that crates could slide straight in, and a few doorways sit up a short flight of steps for no reason a diner would guess. Other entrances seem to do the opposite, sinking a little below the sidewalk, and the difference between the two kinds is where this walk is headed.

Nobody built this stretch to be admired. A working waterfront on the bay side of a resort island was the part of the Beach the advertisements left out, the place where boats were repaired and supplied so that the other shore could stay picturesque, and its buildings were set down plainly on whatever level the ground offered.

As you walk, glance at each doorway and note whether it steps up or down. Keep going to the corner of Twentieth Street, where Purdy meets the heart of Sunset Harbour, and stop beside the fenced and screened machinery at the corner.
