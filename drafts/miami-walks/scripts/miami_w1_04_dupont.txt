# MIAMI W1 · 04 — Both Ends of the Swing
W1 · The Pitch · segment 4 · S6 · clean · 24 September 2026

---

On the Flagler Street sidewalk in front of the duPont Building, put the doors behind you and look across the street at the ten-story office block on the opposite corner. Tucked into that block is the Olympia Theater, the next stop; for a minute, take the two buildings together.

Between them they hold both ends of the city's first great swing. The theater opened in February of 1926, at the very top of the land boom, when money moved through these blocks faster than anyone could count it and the thing to sell was pleasure. This building was begun in 1937, after the boom had broken, a hurricane had struck, and the national Depression had followed close behind. What it offered was the opposite of pleasure: a bank's steadiness, rendered in granite and limestone, for a city that had learned what a crash felt like.

Look from one to the other. Across the street, arches and ornament and a building that wanted to be a party; behind you, straight lines and setbacks and a building that wanted to be trusted. Thirteen years separate their openings, and so does an entire theory of what Miami was for.

Neither one was wrong; the city has kept alternating between those two moods ever since, and a good part of downtown can be sorted into one pile or the other once you start looking.

Cross Flagler Street at the light and stand by the theater's entrance, under the office floors. The Olympia is waiting.
