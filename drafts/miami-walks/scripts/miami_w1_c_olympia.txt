# MIAMI W1 · Connective — The Olympia Theater
W1 · The Pitch · connective · S1 · clean · 24 September 2026

---

Standing at the Olympia's entrance on Flagler Street, you're under ten floors of offices, with the theater itself running back along Second Avenue behind them.

From the sidewalk it looks like an office building with a door in it, and that was the point: the floors above paid rent, and the auditorium hid behind them. When it opened on February eighteenth, 1926, audiences walked through that door into a Moorish courtyard at night. The architect, John Eberson, specialized in what the trade called atmospheric theaters. Instead of a ceiling there was a painted sky, deep blue, with small lights for stars and clouds drifting slowly across it, and around the walls rose the towers and balconies of an imaginary Mediterranean town. The air inside was cooled by machinery.

Think about what that sold. Outside was the real sky, the one the whole boom advertised to the country. Inside was an improved version, with the weather taken out.

By the 1960s and 1970s, downtown was emptying toward the suburbs, and movie palaces across the country were coming down one after another. This one nearly followed. A businessman and philanthropist named Maurice Gusman saved it from demolition and, in 1975, handed it to the city, and the painted sky has been restored more than once since.

Two short blocks north, where Northeast Second Street meets Northeast First Avenue, a church finished a year before this theater opened wears clothes from the same wardrobe. Next comes Gesu Church.
