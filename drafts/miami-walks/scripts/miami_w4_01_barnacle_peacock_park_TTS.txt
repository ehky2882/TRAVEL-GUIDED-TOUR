# MIAMI W4 · 01 — The Barnacle and Peacock Park, arriving from the south

W4 · The Grove · segment 01 · 10 walk version · T1 · S4 · TTS-safe · 24 September 2026

---

You have come up Main Highway the way the working day once came up it, north from the West Grove toward the water, and the trees closing overhead mark the old bayfront properties. The gate of the Barnacle is on your right, the bay side, and Peacock Park opens a little farther along at McFarlane Road.

The land behind the gate cost four hundred dollars and a boat. In eighteen eighty six Ralph mun roh, the yacht designer from Staten Island, bought forty acres of shoreline for four hundred dollars in cash plus a sailboat he valued at the same again, built a boathouse the next year and lived over it until the house was finished in eighteen ninety one. Five of the forty acres remain, held by the state since nineteen seventy three, with the house, its hammock and a strip of shore.

The park is the older site. In eighteen eighty two mun roh had encouraged Charles and Isabella Peacock to open a hotel here, and the Bay View House, later the Peacock Inn, stood here for decades as the settlement's front door. In thanks the Peacocks gave mun roh land nearby, and he passed a lot on for the community's first chapel, the congregation you left at Devon Road. If peacocks are crossing the grass, the name is a coincidence: the park is named for the family.

Look back down the road. The inn's kitchen and grounds and the houses along this shore were staffed from the street behind you, and the walk you have just retraced was, for years, simply how the Grove got to work.

The state park calls this the oldest house in the county still standing where it was built. The people who came up this road to work in it were already living along Charles Avenue when its frame went up, so the house has never been older than the neighborhood that served it.

From the park, follow South Bayshore Drive north. It's about two and a half miles to the next gate, walked or ridden, and the story ends where the estate begins.
