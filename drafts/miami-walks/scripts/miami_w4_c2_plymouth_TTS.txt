The church at the corner of Devon Road looks older than it is. Plymouth Congregational went up between nineteen sixteen and nineteen seventeen in the manner of a Spanish mission, with twin bell towers and a deep arched doorway, and the whole of its rough stonework was laid by one man, a Spanish born mason named Felix reh bohm, who by local account worked with a hatchet, a trowel, a plumb line and a T square. The rock is the local oh uh lit ik limestone, the same stone the Charles Avenue families had cut for their road a generation earlier, here dressed block by block over eighteen months.

The congregation is older than the building; it began in eighteen ninety seven as Union Chapel, which the church counts as the first in the Grove, on a McFarlane Road lot given by Ralph mun roh. The Grove had two churchgoing communities from the start, and this was the one with money enough for a New York architect and a stone sanctuary; the churches that gave Evangelist Street its name were raised by the other.

The great wooden door arrived around nineteen twenty nine, said to have come from a ruined monastery, though accounts disagree about which country; the small opening cut low into it was made for a cat.

Turn back north on Main Highway. In a few minutes the sidewalk enters the shade of the hammock and the gate of the Barnacle appears on the bay side, where a yacht designer once paid for forty acres with cash and a boat.
