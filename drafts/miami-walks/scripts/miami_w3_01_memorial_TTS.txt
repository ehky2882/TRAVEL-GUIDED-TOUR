# MIAMI W3 · 01 — Cuban Memorial Boulevard, down the median
W3 · Calle Ocho · segment 1 · S5 · docket 6 · TTS-safe · 24 Sept 2026

---

The flame is at your shoulder, on its column at the kah yay oh choh end, but turn so the boulevard runs away from you to the south and look down its length instead: a grassy median between two lanes of traffic, shaded by trees, with monuments set along it at intervals, leading into blocks of houses.

Everything on this side of Eighth Street was Shenandoah. The land was farmland and pinewoods until around nineteen nineteen, when developers from Virginia began laying out subdivisions and gave them the name of the valley back home, and through the boom of the nineteen twenties the lots filled with the stucco and barrel tile houses you can still pick out along the side streets. The avenue in front of you belonged to that plan, a quiet street in a suburb named for Virginia, running from the Trail toward front porches.

What stands on it now was placed there by later arrivals. The column honors the exile brigade that landed in Cuba in April nineteen sixty one, and over the decades the community set more along the median, a map of the island, a memorial to hoh say mar tee, a statue of the Virgin, busts and statues of its own heroes, and a say buh tree near the center, each one facing the traffic of kah yay oh choh rather than tucked into a plaza somewhere out of sight.

A boundary is usually somewhere people cross on the way to one side or the other. Setting the flame at this edge, on the line where Shenandoah stopped and Riverside began, turned the crossing itself into a place to stop, and gave a neighborhood named for a Virginia valley a second layer of memory, laid over the first without erasing it.

The street keeps both. Walk back to kah yay oh choh, head west along the sidewalk, and look down as you go: the stars begin right away, set into the sidewalk under your feet.
