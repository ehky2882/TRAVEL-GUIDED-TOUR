# MIAMI W2 · 03 — Casa Casuarina, from across the street
W2 · Ocean Drive · segment 03 · S3 · docket 9 · locked treatment 3 (zero clauses on 1997) · clean · 24 September 2026

---

From the park side of Ocean Drive, directly across from eleven sixteen, the whole front of Casa Casuarina reads at once: the coral-rock walls, the arched windows of a Mediterranean house, with a stretch of garden wall running south from it to the corner of Eleventh Street. The house went up in 1930, and when the district was listed in 1979, it was one of the buildings the district was meant to protect.

The corner beside it was not. Until 1993 it held the Revere, a small hotel built around 1950, and under the rules of the day a building that young was classed as non-contributing, on the district's map but outside its protection. When the designer Gianni Versace bought the house in the early 1990s, he bought the Revere as well, meaning to take it down for a garden and a pool. Preservationists demonstrated on the sidewalk, the city's Historic Preservation Board approved the demolition regardless, and in September 1993 the hotel came down.

The loss moved the city to amend its preservation ordinance so that non-contributing buildings in South Beach were protected too, and the garden behind that wall is part of the reason the next small postwar hotel on the Beach proved much harder to remove.

The rules the Revere slipped through had themselves been written after an earlier defeat, one block inland. Walk north to Twelfth Street, turn left, and follow it to the corner of Collins Avenue, where the Senator stood.
