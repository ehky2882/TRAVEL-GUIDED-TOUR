Stand on the corner of kah yay oh choh and Thirteenth Avenue facing west, the way the traffic runs, and each of your hands points at a different neighborhood. On your right is Riverside, one of the city's early districts, which by the nineteen thirties had filled with apartment houses, synagogues and Jewish owned shops; on your left is Shenandoah, pinewoods and farmland until the boom of the nineteen twenties covered it in stucco houses with tile roofs. For most of the years before nineteen sixty, the road under your feet marked where one ended and the other began.

The road had a second identity as well. Eighth Street is the last stretch of the tam eye am ee Trail, the highway that crosses the Everglades to the Gulf coast and then runs north to Tampa, and much of what you will pass on this walk went up facing traffic on its way into or out of town.

The walk is short and flat: a memorial boulevard, two blocks of sidewalk, a theater and a park, all within three blocks of this corner, then a last stop roughly a mile and a half farther west, at the gate of a cemetery. County buses run along Eighth Street in both directions and cover that final stretch in a few minutes; on foot it takes about half an hour, and in the afternoon heat the shaded side of the street is worth crossing for. The first stop is right beside you, at the head of the boulevard where the flame burns.
