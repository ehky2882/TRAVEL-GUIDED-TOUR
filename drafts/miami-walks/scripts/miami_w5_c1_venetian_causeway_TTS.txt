# MIAMI W5 · C1 — The Venetian Causeway foot

W5 · The Water Line · segment C1 · S2 factual · walk connective · TTS-safe · 24 September 2026

---

The road climbing away to the west is the Venetian Causeway, and this corner, where Purdy Avenue meets Dade Boulevard, is where it comes ashore on the Beach. It opened in nineteen twenty six, replacing an earlier wooden bridge on much the same line, and it crosses the bay to downtown Miami in a string of short bridges and small islands.

The islands are the point. Most of them did not exist before the early nineteen twenties, when dredges drew sand and shell up from the bay floor and shaped the spoil into oblongs sized for house lots, each given a name with a nod to Venice. The developers imagined a chain of resort islands; the collapse of the boom that same year slowed their building for years. Walk out onto the causeway and you cross a landscape assembled to a drawing, with water only where someone chose to leave it.

The first rise, where the road lifts onto its bridge, deserves a second glance. The causeway is old enough now that the county plans to replace eleven of its twelve bridges, one at a time, with copies of the originals down to the pattern cast into the railings, and the copies are drawn to sit a little higher than the bridges they replace. The westernmost one, at the Miami end, has already been rebuilt that way.

Add that small difference to your tally. Then turn north along Purdy Avenue, with the bay on your left, to Maurice Gibb Memorial Park.
