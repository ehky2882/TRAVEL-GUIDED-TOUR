# MIAMI W3 · 00 — Calle Ocho: the Seam
W3 · Calle Ocho · segment 0 · S5 · intro · clean · 24 Sept 2026

---

Stand on the corner of Calle Ocho and Thirteenth Avenue facing west, the way the traffic runs, and each of your hands points at a different neighborhood. On your right is Riverside, one of the city's early districts, which by the 1930s had filled with apartment houses, synagogues and Jewish-owned shops; on your left is Shenandoah, pinewoods and farmland until the boom of the 1920s covered it in stucco houses with tile roofs. For most of the years before 1960, the road under your feet marked where one ended and the other began.

The road had a second identity as well. Eighth Street is the last stretch of the Tamiami Trail, the highway that crosses the Everglades to the Gulf coast and then runs north to Tampa, and much of what you will pass on this walk went up facing traffic on its way into or out of town.

The walk is short and flat: a memorial boulevard, two blocks of sidewalk, a theater and a park, all within three blocks of this corner, then a last stop roughly a mile and a half farther west, at the gate of a cemetery. County buses run along Eighth Street in both directions and cover that final stretch in a few minutes; on foot it takes about half an hour, and in the afternoon heat the shaded side of the street is worth crossing for. The first stop is right beside you, at the head of the boulevard where the flame burns.
