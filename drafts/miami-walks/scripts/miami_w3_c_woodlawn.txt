# MIAMI W3 · 05 — Connective: the gate of Woodlawn Park
W3 · Calle Ocho · segment 5 · S5 · payoff · docket 6 · clean · 24 Sept 2026

---

The gate of Woodlawn Park Cemetery stands before you on the south side of Calle Ocho at Thirty-Second Avenue, and the trees over the wall are the first thing to notice: the founders planted tropical species from abroad when they opened the grounds in 1913.

In those years this was pine woods well beyond the city's edge; the Trail had not yet been started, and a burial ground this far out might have been expected to stay at the margin, but the city grew west around it instead. Over the century its sections filled with the families of many of the groups that came to Miami, Greek and Chinese, Jewish and Syrian-Lebanese, with a large Cuban section among them, while the mausoleum holds pioneers including William and Mary Brickell. In the 1990s two funeral houses founded in Havana, one as early as 1857, which had reopened in Miami in the 1960s, joined the cemetery, and their names now stand on the sign at the gate.

*[beat]*

A burial ground placed at the edge of town now lies in the middle of the neighborhood, the same way the road you followed here stopped being a line between two places and became the center of one, and a community of arrivals now rests beside the city's founders, in ground that was once as far out as Miami went.

The Trail keeps going west from this gate, on across the Everglades, and the walk ends here, with the road still running on.
