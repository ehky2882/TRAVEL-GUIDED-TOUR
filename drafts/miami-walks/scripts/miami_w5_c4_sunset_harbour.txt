# MIAMI W5 · C4 — Sunset Harbour, the raised streets

W5 · The Water Line · segment C4 · S8 payoff · docket 13 passage · beat 2 · clean · 24 September 2026

---

At Twentieth Street and Purdy Avenue, look along the storefronts, then down at your feet. The street and the sidewalk here were rebuilt higher, about two and a half feet in places, finished around 2016, and the buildings stayed where they were. In front of many doors the old ground survives as a sunken patio, a short ramp or steps down from the new sidewalk to a floor at the neighborhood's old level. Two ground levels, one above the other, and every entrance fitted between them.

The screened compound beside you is a pump station, which pushes rain from the raised road out into the bay; these streets were among the lowest on the Beach, and the city tried the idea here first.

Every step on this walk has pointed here. The mall's curb, the causeway's rise, the park's raised edge and the loading docks were each a few inches of a story the doorways finish: the water is the product and the threat, the view that sold the made land and the tide that reaches it first.

What happens next is argued in two places. The city's own adaptation plan, adopted by its commission on the twenty-fifth of June, 2025, looks seventy-five years ahead, identifies more than sixty-seven thousand assets as vulnerable to flooding, and stages its response, with decision points where one strategy gives way to the next. Outside reviewers, from an independent research institute to an expert land-use panel, have urged the city to bring residents further into those choices, and owners beside raised streets have pressed a harder question, in public meetings and in court: when public money lifts the street, the buildings beside it are left lower, and closing that gap falls largely on their owners.

*[beat]*

A city built partly from the bay floor is now deciding, block by block, how high to build it again.

If a door stands open, step down into one of those entrances and back up to feel the height; the walk ends here, where the two grounds meet.
