# MIAMI W3 · 04 — Domino Park, at the fence
W3 · Calle Ocho · segment 4 · S5 · locked treatment 4 · clean · 24 Sept 2026

---

At the fence on the Fifteenth Avenue side, the tables are close enough to count the tiles without looking at anyone's face, which is the right distance to keep.

The set on these tables tells you where the game comes from. It is double nine, fifty-five tiles with up to nine pips on each half, rather than the double-six set of twenty-eight that most American households know; four people play in two partnerships, partners seated across from each other, play moves counterclockwise, and each player draws ten tiles, leaving fifteen face down that will never be played. The double nine, the piece with the most pips, has a nickname, la gorda, the fat one. Across most of Cuba, and especially in Havana, this is the standard game, while in the island's east the smaller set has traditionally held on, so the pieces on these tables carry a regional habit from the far side of the Florida Straits.

The park office keeps the regulars' sets between games, and the arrangement says something about how settled this corner is: the tiles live here, not in anyone's pocket. The fifteen left out of every hand belong to the design of the game, a reserve that no one will see, and good play depends on reasoning out what they must be.

The corner has its own place in the older map. This is the south side of Eighth Street, the edge of what was Shenandoah, and a community that arrived with its own way of playing chose the line between two older neighborhoods for a gathering place in full public view, where anyone passing on the Trail could hear it.

From here the walk leaves the cluster. The last stop is roughly a mile and a half west along Calle Ocho, at Thirty-Second Avenue, where the gate of Woodlawn Park Cemetery stands on the south side of the street; take the bus, a car or the shaded sidewalk, and listen to the final segment at the gate.
