DUBAI W2 — THE OLD QUARTER — 02 AL FAHIDI FORT (walk version)

The square fortress ahead of you, with the round tower on its corner, has stood on this spot since 1787. Nothing else built in this city comes close.

Walk its perimeter slowly — it doesn't take long, and the outside is the visit for now. The fort has been closed for a long restoration, and until it reopens, what you're circling is the exhibit: walls of coral stone and gypsum, cut from the seabed and the shore, because in a land without timber or quarries the sea was the only builder's yard available. There are wooden dhows and cannon displayed around the base, and the contrast is instructive — the whole military establishment of old Dubai, visible in ninety seconds of strolling.

Here's the walk-sized way to understand it. Elsewhere on this creek you can see the trade itself — the souks, the boats, the gold. This is what made the trade possible. A market is only as good as its protection, and this small square of stone was the guarantee behind every deal struck on the creek — garrison, arsenal, ruler's refuge, and later the city's jail, all in one building the size of a school gymnasium. The second kind of old on this walk is the genuine kind, and it comes with a lesson attached: genuine old, in Dubai, is small. Wealth here didn't build cathedrals. It built protection, and it kept the rest liquid.

Now go around behind the fort and cross into the lanes — narrow, sand-coloured, with square towers rising above flat roofs. That's the Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood, the quarter the locals long knew as Bastakiya, and it's our next stop.
