DUBAI W2 — THE OLD QUARTER — 00 INTRO (walk)

In a city this young, "old" is a complicated word — and this walk is going to show you every meaning it has.

You should be in Al Shindagha, at the mouth of Dubai Creek, where the water opens out toward the sea. From here we'll follow the waterfront south along the creek — pedestrianised the whole way, with the water on your left — to Al Fahidi Fort, then turn inland into the lanes of the old quarter, and finish at Al Seef, back on the water. Allow a couple of hours if you linger, and you should linger.

Here is the frame to carry with you. You're about to walk through three kinds of old. The restored: Shindagha's merchant houses, rebuilt as museums on their original footprints. The genuine: the fort and the lanes behind it, the oldest built fabric the city has. And the invented: Al Seef, a heritage district constructed new within the last decade. Most cities have all three and hope you won't notice the difference. This one lays them out in a row, a half-hour apart, and lets you compare.

One honest note: the waterfront stretch is open to the sky, so the light and the temperature are kindest toward the ends of the day — and the creek at sunset is the best free show in this half of the city.

Start where the city started. The ruler's house and the watchtower are just ahead, at the water's edge.
