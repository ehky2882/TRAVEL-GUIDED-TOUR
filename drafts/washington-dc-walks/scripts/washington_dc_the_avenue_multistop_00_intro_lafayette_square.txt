# The Avenue — Lafayette Square

---

This walk is about a street that was built to connect two buildings before either one was finished, and the point of it was the connecting. The city was placed here by a deal in 1790 and the government moved in during 1800, and in between, someone had to cut a line through the trees from the president's house to the hill where Congress would sit. That line is Pennsylvania Avenue, and you are going to walk most of it.

You should be on the north side of H Street, at the corner of Sixteenth, with the square in front of you and the church with the yellow walls at your back. Across the square, past the trees and the statue of a man on a rearing horse, is the north front of the White House, with the gray bulk of the Treasury to its left.

Three closures to know about before you start. The avenue in front of the house has been shut to cars since 1995, which is why this walk picks it up a block east. There is construction on the east side of the grounds, and you may see cranes beyond the Treasury. And the square itself has been closed behind fencing since early 2026, so you may be seeing all this through a fence, or, if it has reopened, from inside it. The two blocks of Sixteenth Street behind you carried a painted street name from 2020 until it was removed in 2025.

[beat]

The walk is flat, about one point six miles, almost all of it on the north sidewalk of the avenue. There are places to sit at the plaza and along Seventh Street, and the finish is on top of a Metro station. Walk east along H Street one block to Fifteenth, turn right, and stop at the corner where Fifteenth meets Pennsylvania Avenue. The avenue begins there, as far as you are concerned.
