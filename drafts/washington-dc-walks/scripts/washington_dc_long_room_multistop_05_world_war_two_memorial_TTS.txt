You should be at the west side of the World War II Memorial, where the Reflecting Pool ends and the ground opens into an oval of granite pillars around a fountain, with the Monument rising on the slope beyond.

Walk the rim. Fifty six pillars, one for each state and territory of the nineteen forties, in a ring around a pool that was here first: the oval basin at the center was built in the nineteen twenties as the end of the Reflecting Pool, and the memorial was built around it in two thousand four. Two arches, one at each end, stand for the two oceans the war was fought across. The wall at the far side carries a field of gold stars.

This is the memorial the room did not want. The site was open ground for eighty years, and the plan to build here was fought in public through the late nineteen nineties, because this is the middle of the vista, the one place along the pool where the room had nothing in it. The memorial won, and it was built low, so that from either end of the room you can see over it. From where you stand the Lincoln is still in view behind you and the Monument ahead, and the ring sits between them like a table set in a hall.

What the room did with it is the third use. People meet here. Veterans of that war were brought here in groups for years to see it, and the groups came down the pool and gathered at the pillar of their own state, and the memorial became the place along this walk where strangers most often speak to one another. It was built to be looked at, and it is used to find people.

Leave by the east side and take the path up the slope. The Washington Monument is the last stop, and the room is about to show you its shape.
