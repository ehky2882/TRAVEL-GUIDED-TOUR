# Around the Water — 01: the cherry trees

---

You should be on the east shore path, a few minutes south of the dock, under the first cherry trees that lean out over the water on your right and over the path on your left. The Jefferson Memorial is ahead of you and the dock is behind.

Look at the height of the trees before anything else. These are Yoshino cherries, and a Yoshino tops out somewhere around thirty or forty feet, the height of a three-story house, and that is the ceiling of this entire walk. Everything built around this water was built to sit under the trees or beside them, never over them, and once you see that here you will see it at every stop.

Look at the age of them next. The thick dark trunks, some of them leaning so far they are propped, are old trees; the first planting here was in 1912, and a few from that spring are said to be standing on the far shore near the stone lantern. The thin ones beside you, no higher than your shoulder, went in the ground in 2026. Several hundred trees came out for the rebuilding of the wall and several hundred went back in. That is how this shore works: the trees are replaced as they go, so the planting is always old and always young at once. In a city where everything is built to last, this is the famous thing that is kept by being renewed.

In spring the loop goes white and pink for about a week and the city comes to see it. The rest of the year it is this: shade, a wide path, water on one side.

[beat]

The trees are the measure of the Basin. Keep them in mind as the Jefferson Memorial gets closer, because the building is about to look very large, and it is not.

Keep going with the water on your right. The path curves toward the memorial and ends at a wide plaza at the water's edge; stop there, under the columns.
