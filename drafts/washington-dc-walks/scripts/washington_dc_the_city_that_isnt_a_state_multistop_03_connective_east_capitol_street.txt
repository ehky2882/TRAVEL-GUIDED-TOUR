# East Capitol Street

---

This is East Capitol Street, and you are walking west along it. Look ahead. The street runs straight for several blocks and ends at the dome, which sits at the far end like something placed there on purpose to be walked toward.

On both sides, the whole way, are houses. Bay fronts, porches, stoops, steps up from the sidewalk, most of them built in the late nineteenth century for clerks and tradesmen and their families, and lived in by families ever since. Trees meet over the roadway in summer. There are bicycles chained to railings, toys on porches, a cat in a window if you are lucky.

What makes this street unusual is not the houses, which could be in a dozen eastern cities, but where it goes. Most streets that lead up to a seat of government get grander as they approach it, clearing out the ordinary for the official. This one does not bother. It stays a residential street almost to the end, and the people who live along it step out in the morning into a view the rest of the country knows from postcards and the evening news. For them it is the end of the street, seen in the rain and on the way to the dentist.

Keep going until the houses give out. On your right, between Second Street and First, is a white building with columns and a broad plaza in front of it. Walk around to the plaza side, on First Street, and stop on the sidewalk facing its steps.
