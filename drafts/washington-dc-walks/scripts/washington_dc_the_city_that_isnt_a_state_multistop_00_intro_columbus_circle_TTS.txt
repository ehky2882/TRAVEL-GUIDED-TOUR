Introduction, Columbus Circle

Washington is a city of people who live here. That would go without saying almost anywhere else, and this walk is about the one way in which it does not. The people who live in this city are governed, in the end, by a legislature that none of them elected and none of them can vote out, and that legislature meets in a building at the far end of this walk. Every stop between here and there is something the city has because people live on this hill, or something the country set down beside them. The walk takes no side. It shows you the city, then the building, and leaves the two together.

You should be on the plaza in front of Union Station, at Columbus Circle, with the station's three big arches behind you and the fountain in front. The wide avenue leaving the circle at an angle on your left is Massachusetts Avenue, and that is the way you will go.

The route is a little over two miles. It climbs gently for the first ten minutes or so, onto Capitol Hill, runs fairly flat along the top, and comes down to the Mall at the end. There are benches in a small park on the way, at the market, and by the Library's fountain, and grass at the finish. The tracks and sheds behind the station are being replanned, so there may be work visible on that side. The terrace below the Capitol's dome has been behind barriers since twenty twenty one, so the last stop is written from the Mall side, at Third Street. And every four years, in the winter before an inauguration, the west front of the Capitol grows a scaffold of platforms and seats for the ceremony; if this is one of those winters, that is what you will see at the end.

Stay where you are, but face the station. It is the first stop, and the next segment plays right here.
