# The Long Room — Washington Monument, the landing

---

You should be on the Monument grounds, on the west side of the obelisk, with the Reflecting Pool and the Lincoln below and behind you to the west.

Turn around and look back the way you came. The whole room is there in one view: the pool, the elms, the ring of pillars, the white building at the far end with its steps. That is the stage seen from the back of the hall, and it is the view the speakers had.

Then turn to your right, north. Across the lawn, past the trees, is the White House. Turn further, east, and the Capitol dome is at the end of the long grass of the Mall. Those two sightlines and the one behind you meet here. The plan of this city was two lines drawn across empty ground in 1791, one from the Capitol west, one from the President's House south, and the crossing was to have a statue at it. You are standing at the crossing, near enough, and the statue became this.

The man it is for picked the ground. George Washington was given the right in 1790 to choose where the capital would sit, and he chose this bend of the Potomac himself, rode the ground with the surveyors, and hired the man who drew the plan. He never lived here. The government arrived in 1800, and he had died the winter before, at his house downriver, so he is the one president who never lived in the house he had placed. The obelisk went up in two campaigns, from 1848 and from 1879, and for the five years before the Eiffel Tower it was, by most accounts, the tallest thing anyone had built.

[beat]

So here is the shape of the room. The plan put a crossing here and an open vista west of it. The city filled the vista with water, trees and a temple, and then the people filled it with themselves, and the crossing became the back wall of a hall a mile long. Washington chose this ground for a capital. What the ground became is a place to stand with a quarter of a million other people and hear someone.
