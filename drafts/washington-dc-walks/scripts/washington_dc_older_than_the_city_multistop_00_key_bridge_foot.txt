# Older Than the City — Start: the Key Bridge foot

---

You are at the Georgetown end of Key Bridge, where M Street meets the bridge and the traffic sorts itself out for Virginia. Put the bridge on your right and the river behind it. Above you, on concrete legs, is an elevated highway; up the hill behind you, the gray spires of a university; ahead, M Street runs east into the brick. The bridge is named for the man who wrote the national anthem, and his house stood about here.

This walk has one idea. Washington was planned, by men with instruments. The river was not, and it was moving before the first line was laid down; the town you are standing in grew up beside it forty years before there was a capital. Everything east of Rock Creek answers to the plan. This piece of the city answers to the river, and you can still watch it answering.

About one point two miles: flat along M Street, then down a flight of steps to the canal path, up one block to the Old Stone House, and down again to the water. The steps are the only grade; if they are a problem, stay on M and rejoin at Thomas Jefferson Street, two blocks past Wisconsin Avenue. Benches at the canal and at the river. Restrooms at the Old Stone House when it is open.

One object before you walk. Down at the water, just upstream of the bridge on this bank, stands a block of stone the size of a small building. That is the end of an older bridge, from 1843, and it did not carry a road. It carried the canal — a wooden trough of water straight across the Potomac to Alexandria. [beat]

Hold that picture, a canal crossing a river. The whole walk is inside it.

Walk east on M Street to Wisconsin Avenue, four blocks, and stop at the corner with the gold dome.
