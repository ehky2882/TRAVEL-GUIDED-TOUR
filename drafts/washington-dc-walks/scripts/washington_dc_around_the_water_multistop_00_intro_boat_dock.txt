# Around the Water — Intro: the boat dock

---

You are on the east side of the Tidal Basin, at the dock where the pedal boats go out, with the parking lot behind you and the water in front of you. Across the water, a little to your left, is the Jefferson Memorial, white and round; straight across, almost hidden in trees, the long low wall of the Roosevelt Memorial; over your right shoulder, the Washington Monument.

Here is the walk. It goes around the water once and comes back to this dock, about two miles, flat the whole way, with benches along the path and restrooms at the two big memorials. Keep the water on your right and you cannot go wrong. If the far shore is closed, walk out to the Jefferson Memorial and back along this side.

Before any of this was here, the river had its people. The Nacotchtank, whom the English called the Anacostans, lived along the eastern river that still carries a version of their name, and the Piscataway lived on this one; their descendants, the Piscataway Conoy, are a people of Maryland today. The city came later, and this water later still, dug out of the river flats in the 1880s.

The wall at the water's edge and the path you are standing on were rebuilt in 2025 and 2026, which is why the path is wide and the smallest trees along it are no higher than your shoulder.

[beat]

One idea for the next two miles. Nothing on this loop is tall. The trees, the memorials, the city behind the trees: all of it stops at about the same height, and the water is what lets you see that. Everywhere else in Washington you are too close to the buildings to notice; out here you are not.

Start walking south, toward the Jefferson Memorial, with the water on your right. Stop where the trees first reach over the path.
