# The Avenue — Freedom Plaza, the Parade

---

You should be up on the plaza itself, a few steps above the sidewalk, standing roughly in the middle and facing east, down the avenue. The Capitol is at the far end, dead center. The building with the clock tower is a few blocks along on the left. The building on your left with the flags out front is the one the District's government works out of. If the plaza is fenced for works when you arrive, stay on the north sidewalk and look east from there; everything that follows still holds.

From here the avenue is a straight line, about a mile to the Capitol steps, wider than it needs to be for the traffic it carries. That width is the point. This is a street built for processions, and it has had them since before it was paved. The inaugural parade comes along it from the Capitol to the house. Two days in May of 1865 it carried the Union armies home, marching past reviewing stands for hours. In March of 1913 several thousand women marched down it demanding the vote, the day before a president was sworn in. Funerals, victory parades, marches for and against most things the country has argued over, all on this stretch, between these buildings.

[beat]

The plaza was finished in 1980, built to be the one place on the route where the crowd could stand above the street. The paving carries a map of the city's plan, but look at the width of the sidewalks on both sides, and the way the buildings are set back. The avenue was laid out wide in 1791 for a reason nobody could have tested at the time, and two centuries of parades have tested it.

Head back down to the north sidewalk and keep walking east. The clock tower you can see ahead is the next stop, three blocks along at Twelfth Street. Cross to the south side of the avenue when you get there, so you can see the whole of it.
