You are at the corner of M Street and Wisconsin Avenue, under the gold dome of a bank building, with the bridge four blocks behind you. Face south, down Wisconsin, toward the river you cannot quite see.

Two things about this corner are older than the names on it. Wisconsin Avenue was High Street, the road the tobacco wagons took down from the Maryland farms to the wharves, and it still runs the way a farm road runs, following the slope rather than any plan. M Street was Bridge Street, named for the bridge over Rock Creek at its far end. Both names went in eighteen ninety five, when the town's streets were relettered and renumbered to match the capital's grid. The grid came to Georgetown; Georgetown did not go to the grid. The brick underfoot is the town's habit, not the capital's.

Walk a block down Wisconsin and stop on the bridge over the ditch.

That ditch is the canal. From up here it is a stone walled trench about the width of a street, with a gravel path along its river side. If there is water in it, it is river water let in from the puh toe mik several miles upstream and brought down here at a walking pace. If the channel is dry, that is repair work on the walls and the gates, not neglect. Either way, look at the walls, dressed stone laid by hand, nearly two centuries old and still square.

Georgetown dug this to stay a port, and this stretch opened in eighteen thirty, when the town was already old. It is the first mile of a hundred and eighty four.

Everything you have walked past so far was renamed to fit a plan. The water in this trench never needed a name; it only needed somewhere lower to go.

Go down the steps on the far side of the bridge to the path, turn left, and walk east with the canal on your left. The locks are the next stop, a few hundred yards along.
