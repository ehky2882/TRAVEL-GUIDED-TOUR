Keep walking. The path runs west along the south shore and brings you to the Inlet Bridge, a short road bridge where the water narrows to a channel and slips under the road toward the puh toe mik. Walk out onto it on the water side.

Under your feet are the gates. This is where the river comes in; it goes out under another bridge on the east side, and that is as much as this walk will say about it. The Basin is a working thing, and this is one of its two valves.

Stop in the middle, at the railing, and turn to face the water, with the road at your back.

This is the one view on the loop that looks back at the city instead of across at a memorial. The far shore is a line of trees; above the trees one thing stands up, the stone needle of the Monument, and nothing else does. There are office blocks a few streets behind that treeline, and a whole downtown behind those, and none of it shows. The city has kept itself below the tops of its trees, and the only way to see that is to stand somewhere low and wide enough to take in its whole edge at once, which is here. A city that builds sideways; you will see it again at the next two stops.

Come off the bridge and keep the water on your right. The Roosevelt Memorial begins almost at once, a low wall of red stone on your left. Go in at the first opening you reach.
