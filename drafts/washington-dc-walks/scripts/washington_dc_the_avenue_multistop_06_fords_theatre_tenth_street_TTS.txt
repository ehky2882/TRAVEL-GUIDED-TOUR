You should be on Tenth Street between E and F, on the sidewalk, with a plain brick building on your left that has arched windows and a flag over the door, and across the street on your right a narrow brick row house with steps up to a green door. The one on your left is Ford's Theatre. The one on your right is the house where a president was taken on the night of the fourteenth of April, eighteen sixty five, after he was fatally wounded in the theater, and where he died the next morning. The street between them is narrow enough that you can see both doors without turning your head.

Notice where you are. A block and a half from the avenue, on a side street, with no columns and no steps and no view of anything. The avenue a block south was where the country did its big public things, and this street was where the city went about its evening. Tenth Street in eighteen sixty five had boarding houses, a few shops, a theater that had been a church until the war filled the city with people who wanted entertainment. It was not a famous street. It became one on a Friday night, for a reason nobody chose, and it has been a kind of quiet place ever since.

The theater is a working playhouse again, with a season every year, and the house across the street is kept as it was. Both are open to visitors most days, with a ticket, and both close from time to time for works. You do not need to go in for this walk. Standing on the sidewalk, between them, is the thing.

The side streets of this city are where its ordinary history happened, and this is the one everyone knows. Walk north to F Street, turn right, and walk east three blocks back to Seventh. The last stop is a block north of that, at H Street, under the arch.
