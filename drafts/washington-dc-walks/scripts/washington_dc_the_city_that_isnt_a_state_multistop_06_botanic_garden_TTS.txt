The Botanic Garden

You should be at the Botanic Garden's conservatory, a long building of glass and pale stone with a tall glass center rising above the rest. The entrance is on the side facing the Mall.

If the doors are open, go in, even for five minutes. The first thing that happens is the air changes. It is warm and wet in there, the windows fog at the edges, and the main space under the tall glass is filled to the roof with palms and tree ferns and vines, with a walkway that climbs up into the leaves. There are desert plants in one wing and orchids in another.

Everything else you have looked at on this walk has been stone, or brick, or bronze, and much of it has been about who decides what. This is the one place on the Hill where nothing is being decided. The plants are kept by the same office that keeps the Capitol building in repair, which is a nice thing to know: the people who look after the dome also water the orchids.

It is worth the pause, because the last stop asks something of you. It is a few minutes' walk from here, and it is not a building to go into but a thing to think about while you look at one.

When you come back out, walk west along the long side of the garden, with the glass on your left and the Mall on your right, to Third Street. Cross onto the Mall and stand at its edge, facing east, back toward the Capitol, with a long rectangular pool in front of you.
