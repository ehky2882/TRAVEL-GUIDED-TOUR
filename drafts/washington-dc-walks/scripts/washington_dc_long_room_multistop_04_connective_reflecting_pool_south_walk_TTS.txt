You are walking east along the south side of the Reflecting Pool, the Lincoln behind you and the Monument ahead.

The pool was finished in nineteen twenty three, and it is a little over two thousand feet long, which is why it reads as a floor rather than a pond. The elms on both sides went in during the nineteen thirties, and the ones you are walking under are the survivors and their replacements. Look across the water and you will see the walk you did not take, and the Korean figures you just left will have disappeared into their trees.

This stretch is the part of the room the crowds stood in. In nineteen thirty nine and again in nineteen sixty three the people who could not get near the steps stood here, along the water, and listened to the voice coming down the pool. On the second of those days the crowd reached from the steps to the Monument grounds, and some of them stood in the pool to keep cool. The walk you are on is the aisle.

If the water is low or the basins are dry today, that is the works of twenty twenty six, not the design.

Ahead, where the pool ends, the ground opens into a circle of stone pillars around a fountain. Keep to the path and it will bring you to the World War II Memorial.
