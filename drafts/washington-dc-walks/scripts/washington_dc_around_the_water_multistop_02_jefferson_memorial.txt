# Around the Water — 02: the Jefferson Memorial from the water

---

You are on the plaza at the foot of the Jefferson Memorial, right at the water, with the Basin at your back. Turn around so the steps rise in front of you and the columns stand above them. From here you cannot see the dome; it was there a minute ago, from the path, and now the porch has swallowed it.

That is the first thing to know about this building. It was made to be seen from across the water, not from underneath, and the closer you get the less of it there is. On a city block it would be a handsome bank. Set at the edge of a quarter mile of still water, with its reflection under it, it is a monument. The water does the work that height does in other cities.

Go up the steps and in, if the doors are open. The figure inside is bronze, nineteen feet tall, standing, and he faces north, out the open front of the building, across the water and the Mall toward the White House. For the first four years after the memorial opened, in 1943, the figure was plaster painted to look like bronze, because the metal was wanted elsewhere; the bronze came in 1947.

Come back out and look at the building from the side, along the plaza. It is not large. The columns are tall but few, the dome is low and wide, and the whole thing is a round white shape at the height of the trees, which from here is all it needs to be.

[beat]

Every memorial on this loop was built after the trees, and every one was built to be seen over the water. This one is the plainest about it: a dome at shoulder height to the shore, which you have to leave in order to see.

Go back down to the water and follow the path west with the water on your right. The next thing you come to is a short stone bridge where the Basin narrows to a channel. That is the Inlet Bridge, and the next segment plays as you cross it.
