You should be at the corner of Seventh and H, facing east along H Street, with the archway just ahead of you spanning the whole width of the road. Seven roofs stacked up on it, green tile and red and gold paint, with columns down to the sidewalk on either side. Behind you to the west is the way you came. Under your feet, more or less, is a Metro station.

Walk up to the arch and stand under it. Look up at the painted beams. Then look through it, down H Street, at the restaurants and the signs, and at the glass buildings rising behind them.

A gate like this is built to stand in a wall, with the city on one side and something else on the other. This one has no wall. It was put up in nineteen eighty six to mark a place rather than to close it, and nothing changes as you pass through it, except that you have been told where you are. That is a particular thing to do with a street. The avenue you just walked was built to connect two points that needed each other, and it has spent two centuries doing that job. This arch does the opposite, connecting nothing, standing over a street and saying, here.

You started this walk at a square you may not have been able to enter, and you have passed barriers, a closed block, and a dozen buildings you can only see from the sidewalk. This is the one structure on the route that was built to be walked under by anyone who comes along, in either direction, at any hour. Stand under it a moment before you go down into the station. It was built for passing through, and so was the street you have just walked.
