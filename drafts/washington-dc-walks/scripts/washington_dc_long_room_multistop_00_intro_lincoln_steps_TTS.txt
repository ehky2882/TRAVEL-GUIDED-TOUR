Stand at the bottom of the steps of the Lincoln Memorial, with the building behind you and the long pool in front of you, and look east.

What you are looking at is one room. It is about a third of a mile of water with a paved walk and a row of elms on either side, and it ends in the obelisk at the far end, which is about a mile away. The whole room is flat. There is no climb anywhere on this walk except the shallow slope up to the Monument at the end, and there are benches the full length of both sides, so take it at whatever pace you like. The walk from here to the Monument, with the detours to each memorial, is about one point three miles.

This walk is about what the room is for. The things along its walls look like monuments, and they are, but every one of them turned into something else once people started using it: a stage, a surface, a place to stand with other people. That is the one idea this walk follows, and by the end you will be able to see it from the far end looking back.

Two notes on the state of the ground. Parts of the Mall are fenced from time to time for works and for events, and if a path along the pool is closed, the other side is the same walk.

Turn around and climb to the top of the steps. The first stop is the Lincoln Memorial, and we are going to start by looking out from it rather than into it.
