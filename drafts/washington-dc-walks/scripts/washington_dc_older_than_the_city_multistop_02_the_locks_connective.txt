# Connective — The Locks

---

You are on the canal path, walking east, the channel on your left and the backs of M Street's buildings above it. Keep going until the trench narrows into a stone box with heavy wooden doors at each end. That is a lock; there are two along this stretch.

A lock is the whole canal in miniature. A boat comes in at one level, the doors shut behind it, and water is let through the upper gates until the boat has risen about eight feet and can float out the other side. The doors meet at an angle, pointing upstream, so the weight of the water above presses them shut; the harder the river pushes, the tighter they hold. No engine, no pump. The only machine is the shape.

Seventy-four of these stand between here and Cumberland, Maryland, and together they lift a boat more than six hundred feet. This end is the bottom of the staircase. The canal exists because the river stops being navigable a few miles up, at a line of rocks, and a port that wants the inland trade has to go around. You are walking beside the going-around.

By the second lock is a low building with a boat beside it, eighty feet long and pulled, when it runs, by mules. In the water, you have seen the lock used; up on blocks, you have seen it waiting. [beat]

The gates are the point. Water, held, and let go on purpose.

Leave the path at Thomas Jefferson Street, beside the boat, and climb the one block up to M. The Old Stone House is straight across the street.
