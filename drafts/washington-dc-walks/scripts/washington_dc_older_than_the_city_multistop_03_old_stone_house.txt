# The Old Stone House

---

You are on the south sidewalk of M Street at Thomas Jefferson Street, and across from you, set back behind a low fence, is a small gray stone house two stories high with a steep roof and a chimney at each end. It is the only building on this block that does not touch its neighbors. Cross when you can, and go through the gate.

Do not go in yet. Go around. The path leads past the house into a garden the width of three storefronts and deeper than you expect — lawn, roses, old trees, a few benches, the back walls of M Street's shops closing it in on either side. Sit if a bench is free.

The house went up in the 1760s, when this was Bridge Street in a tobacco town in the colony of Maryland, and neither the capital nor the country existed. For the next two centuries it did what buildings on a main street do: it sold things, and families lived over the shop. Nobody saved it and nobody needed to; it was useful, so it stayed.

The gap you are sitting in is the part that nearly went. By the middle of the last century this garden was a paved lot where a dealer parked used cars, and the house was his office. The government bought the whole parcel in the early 1950s and took the asphalt up, and the garden is what it planted instead — a quiet that is younger than almost everything around it. [beat]

The house survived by being too useful to knock down, and the garden is a used-car lot that came good. Georgetown is like that.

When you are ready, go back out to M Street, cross it, and take Thomas Jefferson Street downhill — over the canal, under the highway — all the way to the river. The last stop is at the water.
