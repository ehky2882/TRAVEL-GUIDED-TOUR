# The Avenue — Seventh Street

---

You are walking north on Seventh Street, away from the avenue, with the round plaza and its fountains behind you on the right. Keep going for two short blocks, to E Street, then turn left.

Seventh Street is worth noticing while you walk it. The avenue was the country's street from the start, and this one was the city's. It ran from the river landing at the bottom of the hill, where the boats came in, straight up through the middle of town, and for most of the nineteenth century it was where Washington shopped. The city's main market stood at this end of the street from 1801 until the 1930s. The dry-goods stores, the hardware, the hotels for people in town to trade, all ran north along this street, and some of their buildings are still here. Look at the upper stories as you go: brick, cast iron, carved window heads, three and four stories of ordinary nineteenth-century commerce with newer glass set among them.

The federal avenue and the local shopping street cross at a right angle, one block behind you, and they have never had much to do with each other. One carries the parades, and this one carried the groceries. The federal city and the local one are a block apart here, and have been since the beginning, with nothing marking the join but a traffic light.

At E Street, turn left and walk west, three blocks, past a very large concrete building on your left. Turn right onto Tenth Street. The theater is half a block up on your left.
