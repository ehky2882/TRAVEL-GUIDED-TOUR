# The West Front — The Open Question

---

You should be at the eastern end of the Mall, at Third Street, facing east. In front of you is a long rectangular pool. Beyond it, on a wide stone platform, a bronze general sits on a horse with cannon and cavalry charging at either end of him. And beyond the general, up the slope, is the west front of the Capitol, terraces stacked on terraces, with the dome on top. If the terraces are closed, everything that follows works from here.

On January sixth, 2021, a crowd forced its way into that building while Congress was counting the electoral votes.

That building is the legislature of the whole country, and it is also, in the last word, the legislature of this city. About seven hundred thousand people live in Washington. Since the 1970s they have elected their own mayor and council, the local city's own government, and since 1964 they have voted for president. But they have no senators, and their one member in the House can serve on committees and speak but cannot vote when the whole House decides. Congress has the final say over the city's laws and its budget. It can overturn what the city's council passes, and from time to time it does.

[beat]

What to do about that is a question people disagree about in good faith. Some, including many who live here, argue that people who pay federal taxes and serve in the country's wars should have senators and a full vote in the House like anyone else, and that the way to give them that is to make the city a state. Others argue that the Constitution set the capital apart on purpose, so that the national government would sit on ground that no single state controls, and that any remedy should keep it that way.

The question is open, and it belongs, whichever way it is settled, to the people who live here.
