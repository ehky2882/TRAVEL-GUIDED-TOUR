You are on the plaza of the Martin Luther King Junior Memorial, at the water's edge, with the Basin at your back. In front of you is the stone, a single pale block with a figure carved out of its face, arms folded, looking past you at the water. Behind it, across the plaza, is the split mountain you walked through, and curving away on both sides is a long low wall of the same stone.

This is the last memorial built on the Basin, finished in twenty eleven, and it arrived at a shore where the rules were already set by everything you have walked past. Look at what it did with them. The figure is about thirty feet tall, roughly the height of the trees beside it. The wall is long, longer than a football field, and no higher than a house. The mountain behind you is the tallest thing here, and it is still under the treeline. A memorial to a man who stood up was built almost entirely sideways.

The stone is granite, pale and faintly pink, and it came from China, as did the sculptor who carved it; that was argued over at the time, and the stone stayed.

Now look up, past the figure, at nothing. There is sky above the stone. That has been true at every stop on this loop, and it is why this walk goes around water: the Basin is the one place in Washington wide enough to show you the skyline, and there isn't one.

The path continues with the water on your right, under the road bridge at the north end, and comes out at the dock where you began, about ten minutes from here.

You started this walk at the level of the water and you will finish at it, and in between nothing asked you to look up. Most capitals are built to be looked up at; this one was built to be walked around, and you have just done it.
