# The Avenue — The National Archives, 1800

---

You should be at the foot of the Archives steps on the south side of Pennsylvania Avenue, between Seventh and Ninth, facing the building. Columns, a pediment, a doorway at the top of the steps, and two seated stone figures flanking the stairs, each with a book. The building fills its block, and its other front, the one most visitors use, faces the Mall behind it.

This is the one spot on the avenue where the two big open spaces of the city are a single building apart. Behind this block is Constitution Avenue and then the Mall. In front of you is Pennsylvania. The two run toward each other from here and meet at the foot of the Capitol, which you can see down the avenue to your left. Everything the city does in public, it does on the Mall or on this avenue, and this building sits in the wedge between them with its back to one and its face to the other.

[beat]

The paper that put the city here is inside, and you have been walking on the result. The deal was done in 1790, the plan was laid out in 1791, and then for nine years almost nothing happened on this street except clearing, because there was no city yet, only two building sites a mile and a half apart with trees in between. The government arrived in November of 1800 to find one wing of the Capitol finished, a house at the other end with wet walls, and a road between them that was mostly cleared ground and stumps. That was the capital. Not the buildings, which were half-built, and not the people, who were a few hundred clerks living in boarding houses. The capital was the fact that you could get from the house to the hill, and that somebody had decided you should have to.

Everything else about this avenue came later, the width, the parades, the plaza, the tower, the matched stone on this side. In 1800 it was a connection and nothing more, and the connection was enough.

Walk to the corner at Seventh Street, cross the avenue, and head north on Seventh. The next segment plays as you walk.
