# Restaurant Kuurna

You could walk straight past this door. Plenty of people do, which is rather part of its charm.

Meritullinkatu, in Kruununhaka, a few minutes' walk east of Senate Square. Look for number six: a modest doorway at street level in an old stone building. That's Kuurna. The little wine bar next door, Klaava, belongs to the same people.

Inside it's two small rooms, low light, candles, and tables set close enough that you'll hear a bit of the next conversation. Kuurna has been part of Helsinki's bistro wave since two thousand and five — by its own account, it helped start it. It's run by Laura Styyra and Tom Hansen, who met at restaurant school with the plan of raising the standard of the city's restaurants. They didn't open a new one. They took over Kuurna, which had been their own favourite.

Have a look at how the food works, because it's the opposite of a long tasting menu. A short set menu, two or three courses, changing every three weeks with the season, plus whatever's chalked up on the board that day. A couple of dishes never leave: the classic steak tartare, and the pepper steak, which is the one reviewers keep singling out. The meat is Finnish, the fish and seafood Nordic, and the cheese comes from a supplier called Lentävä Lehmä, which translates, rather wonderfully, as the Flying Cow. And Klaava, the bar next door, is the Finnish word for the tails side of a coin.

Here's what I like about this place. Its ambition never went into more courses or grander rooms. It went into the vegetables. The owners now run their own greengrocer, selling organic and biodynamic produce, and twice a week it delivers to other Helsinki restaurants as well. A bistro that ended up supplying the competition.

[beat]

If you've time before or after, walk north across the water to Hakaniemi Market Hall, about fifteen minutes from here. Kuurna's vegetable shop is on the ground floor, with a little side counter for an espresso and a fresh date. It keeps daytime hours, so go in the morning. And if you've turned up here without a booking, Klaava next door doesn't take any. You simply walk in.
