DUBAI W1 — THE CREEK CROSSING — 01 TEXTILE SOUK (walk version)

You're under the wooden roof now — a high arcade with shopfronts pressed together on both sides, and bolts of cloth stacked to the ceiling in every one of them.

This is the bottom rung of the ladder, and don't let that sound like an insult. Cloth is the trade that moves the most and costs the least — silk and cotton and synthetics shipped across the ocean from India, Pakistan, and points east, sold by the metre to anyone who walks in. For generations this arcade was where the city dressed itself: fabric bought on this side of the creek, carried home, and cut by a tailor who could copy any garment you handed him. Many of them still can, and several of them are within fifty metres of where you're standing.

Walk slowly and let the shopkeepers make their case. Pashminas will be draped over your shoulder before you've agreed to anything — that's not pressure, that's the choreography, and it has been rehearsed here for a hundred years. Notice, too, that almost nothing in these shops was made in this city. That's the quiet lesson of the first rung: Dubai's oldest talent was never manufacturing. It was moving things — buying where things are cheap, selling where they're wanted, and living on the difference.

Keep that in your pocket. You're about to watch the same idea performed with a boat.

Follow the arcade toward the water, and where it opens out you'll find the abra station — floating docks, wooden boats, and a crowd that knows exactly what it's doing. That crossing is our next stop.
