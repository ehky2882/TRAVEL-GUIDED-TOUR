DUBAI W1 — THE CREEK CROSSING — 03 SPICE SOUK (walk version)

From the dock, the Spice Souk begins almost immediately — a covered lane just up from the water, announced by open sacks crowding every doorway.

Before you go in, register the geography, because it is the whole story of this stop. You are standing between the boats and the shelves. For most of the souk's history, the cargo in these lanes travelled less than a hundred metres from ship to shop — sacks hauled off wooden hulls at the wharf you just passed and stacked straight into these doorways. This wasn't a market that happened to be near a port. It was the port's front room.

Walk the lane and read the labels, because the labels are a map. Cinnamon from Sri Lanka, cardamom from the hills of India, dried limes from across the water in Oman, frankincense from the south of Arabia. Every sack names a coastline, and taken together the shelves of this souk sketch the entire trading world the creek once served — you can stand in one narrow lane and survey five hundred years of routes without moving your feet.

One rung up from cloth, notice what changed: the goods got lighter, and the margins got better. A sack of saffron is worth carrying a lot further than a sack of cotton. The old traders understood weight-to-value the way pilots understand fuel, and this souk is where that arithmetic lived.

The top of the ladder is a few minutes further on. Follow the lane away from the water and let the shops make the turn for you — when fabric and spice give way to glass windows full of yellow metal, you've arrived. The Gold Souk is our last stop.
