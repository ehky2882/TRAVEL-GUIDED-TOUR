DUBAI W1 — THE CREEK CROSSING — 04 GOLD SOUK (walk finale)

You'll know you've arrived without being told: the light changes. Under the covered arcade, window after window burns yellow — necklaces the size of armour, bangles stacked by the hundred, whole shopfronts glowing like open furnaces.

This is the top of the ladder, and the arrival deserves a moment. You started this walk an hour ago in front of cotton sold by the metre. You've climbed through spice, worth its weight in careful handling, and arrived at the one commodity that ends the game — the cargo so dense with value that a trader could carry a fortune in a coat pocket. Cloth fills a warehouse. Gold fills a fist.

Look closer at any window and you'll find the souk's real secret, which is that very little haggling here is about gold at all. Gold sells by weight, at the day's world price, posted for everyone to see — the metal costs what the metal costs, in this lane and in London alike. What you negotiate is the workmanship: the making of the thing, not the substance of it. It is one of the most honest markets you will ever walk through, dressed up as one of the most theatrical.

[beat]

One last thing before the walk lets you go. You crossed the creek for a coin, and you're finishing in front of the most expensive thing the old city sells. Every trader who ever ran this circuit was attempting the same climb you just made for fun — cargo by cargo, cloth to spice to gold, each rung funding the next. Enough of them succeeded that the city eventually built itself a skyline on the proceeds, ten kilometres down the coast.

But the circuit itself never closed. This evening, when the lights come on in these windows and the abras keep shuttling behind you, it will still be running — with you or without you. Stay as long as you like. The ladder doesn't mind being admired.
