# HANOI — 26 — BÁT TRÀNG HOUSE

*Single stop · Register: British (Atlas)*  
*Vantage: the street outside 188 Bát Tràng, in the ceramics village, looking at the brick façade*  
*Sources: Dezeen (2021, via search summary); VTN Architects (practice site); New Atlas; VietnamNet; Architecture Hunter Hanoi guide*  
*Note: a private family home with a ceramics shop. The tour is written to be heard from the street.*  

---

Every brick on this front was made by hand. About four thousand of them.

You're in Bát Tràng, the pottery village on the Red River just south-east of Hanoi, outside number one-eight-eight. In front of you is a tall, narrow house — five storeys — wrapped from top to bottom in a screen of brick, full of irregular gaps. If you can see daylight and greenery through a wall of brick, you're in the right place.

The house is by VTN Architects, the practice of Võ Trọng Nghĩa, and it was finished around twenty twenty. It belongs to a family of seven who also run a ceramics business, so there's a shop on the ground floor, and they live above it.

Have a look at the bricks. They're ceramic, and each one was made individually — some four thousand and ninety-eight of them — then laid with gaps between, so the whole front becomes a perforated skin. It's the village's own trade turned into architecture. Bát Tràng has been firing pottery for centuries, and much of it in brick kilns, so a house built of hand-made ceramic brick is about the most local thing you could put up here.

Now look through the gaps. Behind the brick screen, the house has a second, inner façade, and between the two are gardens, raised up floor by floor. So the brick shades the plants, the plants shade the rooms, and the breeze comes through the holes. According to the architects, it stays cool enough inside that the family doesn't need air conditioning.

That's rather the point. VTN is known for buildings that let nature in — trees on roofs, plants in the walls — and this one does it in a crowded village street with very little room to spare. In twenty twenty-one, the design magazine Wallpaper named it the best new private house of the year.

*[beat]*

It's a family's home, so admire it from here. But think about it next time you see a solid concrete house sealed up against the heat with an air conditioner on the front. This one breathes, and it's built from what the village has always made.

From here, walk a few minutes to the Bát Tràng Museum, the terracotta building that curves like stacked pots. Then lose an hour in the pottery market, where you can try a wheel yourself.
