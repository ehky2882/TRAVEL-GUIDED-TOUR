# HANOI — 15 — MANZI ART SPACE AND CAFÉ

*Single stop · Register: British (Atlas)*  
*Vantage: the gate of 14 Phan Huy Ích, Ba Đình, looking into the front courtyard*  
*Sources: DestinAsian; Rusty Compass; Vietcetera (Art For You fair); Architecture Hunter Hanoi guide*  

---

Part gallery, part café, part shop, part lecture hall — and all of it in somebody's old house.

You're on Phan Huy Ích, a quiet side street on the edge of the Old Quarter, outside number fourteen. Behind the gate is a leafy front courtyard and, beyond it, a French villa from the nineteen twenties. That's Manzi.

It was set up by three people who described themselves as cultural activists: Trâm Vũ; Giang Đặng, one of the founders of the Vietnamese-language literary journal talawas; and the artist Bill Nguyễn, who has taught at Vietnam's Fine Arts University. What they wanted was simple and, in Hanoi, unusual — an independent place for contemporary art that wasn't trying to sell to tourists.

Go through the gate and have a look. Downstairs is the café, and the rooms around it hang work by contemporary, mostly non-commercial Hanoi artists. There's a small room upstairs that's lovely on a sunny afternoon. And in the evenings the house turns into a venue — talks, film screenings, performances. Manzi also co-organises Art For You, an annual fair of affordable art, now more than a decade old.

Now look at the building itself. A colonial villa, built for one prosperous household, with its shutters and its garden. A hundred years on, it's become a kind of public living room. The courtyard is where people sit and argue.

And here's the thing. A great deal of Hanoi's contemporary art scene has been built in houses like this one — rented, restored, and opened up. It's cheaper than building, of course. But it also means the city's newest ideas keep turning up inside its oldest walls, and that's rather a good thing to see.

*[beat]*

Before you leave, walk round the corner to Manzi's second address, its exhibition space. That one is in a very different kind of building: the ground floor of a modernist terraced house. Two houses, two eras of the city, one programme. Go and compare.
