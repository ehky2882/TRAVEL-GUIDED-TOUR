# HANOI — 06 — MÉMOIRE 15

*Single stop · Register: British (Atlas)*  
*Vantage: the pavement outside 15 Hàng Đường, looking up at the roofline*  
*Sources: Architecture Hunter Hanoi guide; Mémoire 15 Instagram/Threads listing (@15memoire); Vietnam News Agency (Hàng Đường Street)*  

---

You're standing on Sugar Street. Keep that in mind; it matters later.

This is Hàng Đường, in the heart of the Old Quarter, and you want number fifteen. It's a terraced house like its neighbours — narrow front, shop at street level. Now look right up to the roofline. Up there, if you can catch it from this angle, is a tangle of terracotta-red steel. That's Mémoire 15.

The best way to describe it is a café that's moved in on top of someone else's house. Architecture Hunter calls it parasitic, and it means that kindly. It's made almost entirely of red steel — a stack of staircases set slightly out of line with one another, so that the steps double as seats and the landings double as tables. There's no clear point where the building stops and the furniture begins. You climb the architecture, and then you sit on it.

And from the top, you get something the Old Quarter almost never gives you at street level: the whole view, all the way round. A sea of tiled roofs, water tanks, aerials, and other people's rooftop gardens.

Here's why the street name matters. Hàng Đường was named for the sugar trade, and it still sells sugar and sweets, busiest of all before Tết and the Mid-Autumn Festival. Its other speciality is ô mai — dried apricots, plums and kumquats, salted, sugared, sometimes spiced with ginger or liquorice. Mémoire 15 describes itself as a place for tea, coffee and ô mai. So the newest, strangest building on the street is, underneath, a sweet shop. Exactly as the street has always been.

*[beat]*

The Old Quarter has run out of ground. Every lot was claimed centuries ago, and the houses fill them edge to edge. The only free land left is the sky. Mémoire 15 is one of the boldest attempts to claim it, and it's rather wonderful that it does so with stairs.

Before you climb, buy a small bag of ô mai from one of the shops along here, and try a few on the roof. Then come down and carry on north. Hàng Đường runs straight up to Đồng Xuân Market, the great covered market the French built in eighteen eighty-nine.
