# HANOI — 05 — WORKSHOP14

*Single stop · Register: British (Atlas)*  
*Vantage: the wooden gate at 6 Ngõ 5 Từ Hoa, Tây Hồ, beside Kim Liên Pagoda*  
*Sources: The World's 50 Best ("7 reasons this Vietnam bar became an award-winner"); Asia's 50 Best Bars 2025 listing; Architecture Hunter Hanoi guide*  

---

Eight hundred years ago, this lane belonged to a princess who kept silkworms.

You're in a quiet lane off Từ Hoa Street, near the shore of West Lake, next to Kim Liên Pagoda. In front of you is a traditional wooden gate with a small garden behind it. That's Workshop14 — a cocktail bar of about fourteen seats, in what used to be an architect's studio.

The ground has a long memory. In the twelfth century, under the Lý dynasty, this was the palace of Princess Từ Hoa, where silkworms were raised to weave the robes of the royal court. The land later became Nghi Tàm, a weaving village — one of thirteen craft villages that supplied the old capital. The people behind this bar call themselves the fourteenth. You can draw your own conclusions about the name.

The team is three people with three different jobs. Hiếu Long, an architect, designed the conversion. Cường Nguyễn runs the place. And the drinks are Rich McDonough's. They went from signing the lease to opening night in three months. Inside, they kept the studio's footprint, put in a steel beam to hold the ceiling, and opened the roof with skylights. Wood, low light, very little else. It's a room that's been edited rather than decorated.

The menu is edited too: a dozen drinks at most, rotating with the seasons, each worked out in a small lab a few minutes away. There's one that crosses an espresso martini with Hanoi's egg coffee, and it's called "Is One Un Oeuf?" — which tells you something about the sense of humour.

And here's the interesting bit. In twenty twenty-five, only months after opening, it was placed on Asia's Fifty Best Bars list and given the Campari One To Watch award. Yet almost everyone drinking here is local — around ninety-eight per cent. It wasn't built for visitors. It just turned out that visitors wanted it too.

*[beat]*

Before you go in, or once you come out, step next door into Kim Liên Pagoda. It sits on the same old palace ground. Then walk down to the lake edge; on a clear evening the water goes silver, rather like silk.
