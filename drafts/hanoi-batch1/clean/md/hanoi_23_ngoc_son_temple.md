# HANOI — 23 — NGỌC SƠN TEMPLE

*Single stop · Register: British (Atlas)*  
*Vantage: the lakeshore on Đinh Tiên Hoàng, by the Pen Tower, facing the red Thê Húc Bridge*  
*Sources: Wikipedia (Ngọc Sơn Temple) and its sources; Vietnam Airlines travel guide ("Exploring The Huc bridge"); Architecture Hunter Hanoi guide*  

---

The bridge in front of you is painted red for a reason. Its name means something like "the perch of the morning sunlight".

You're on the north-eastern shore of Hoàn Kiếm Lake, on Đinh Tiên Hoàng Street. Ahead of you, a curved red wooden bridge runs out across the water to a small wooded island; that's Thê Húc Bridge, and the island is Ngọc Sơn — Jade Mountain. Beside you on the shore, on a little rocky mound, stands a slim stone tower. Find all three and you're in the right place.

Start with the tower. It's the Pen Tower, and near its top are three carved characters that read, roughly, "writing on the blue sky". Then walk towards the bridge and you'll pass through a gate shaped like an ink stone. A pen, then an inkstand. This is a temple to scholarship, and the approach is laid out like a writing desk.

The temple on the island was built in the early nineteenth century. It honours Văn Xương, a god of literature, and later took in Trần Hưng Đạo, the general who beat back the Mongol invasions in the thirteenth century. Scholars studying for the imperial exams came here to pray. Some, I imagine, still do.

Now the bridge. Before eighteen sixty-five, the way across was a simple bamboo footbridge. Then a scholar called Nguyễn Văn Siêu, given the job of restoring the temple under the emperor Tự Đức, built the arched bridge and painted it red. Come early in the morning and you'll see why it got its name.

And then there's the turtle. Inside the temple is a preserved giant turtle from this lake. The lake's name, Hoàn Kiếm, means "Lake of the Returned Sword": legend says a golden turtle rose from the water to take back a magic sword that had helped the king Lê Lợi drive out an invading army. So the turtle isn't just a curiosity. It's the reason the lake is called what it is.

*[beat]*

That's what this little island does so well. A pen, an ink stone, a scholar's bridge, a general and a sacred turtle, all within a few dozen paces. The whole of Vietnamese learning and legend fits on one rock in a city-centre lake.

Cross over, and on your way back, look south along the water. Out in the middle stands the small Turtle Tower on its own islet. On weekend evenings the streets around the lake close to traffic, and the whole shore turns into a promenade.
