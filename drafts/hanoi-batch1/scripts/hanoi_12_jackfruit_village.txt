HANOI — 12 — JACKFRUIT VILLAGE
Single stop · Register: British (Atlas)
Vantage: beside the meditation house at the centre of the site, Trại Lãng village, Cổ Đông, Sơn Tây
Sources: ArchDaily (Jackfruit Village / 1+1>2 Architects); gooood; Designboom; Architecture Hunter Hanoi guide
---
Before the architects drew a single line here, they counted the trees. Thirty-eight jackfruit, thirteen pomelo.

You're in Trại Lãng, a farming village about an hour west of central Hanoi, near the old town of Sơn Tây. This is Jackfruit Village, a small retreat of brick and thatch spread over about a hectare and three-quarters, with a lake along its southern edge. Find the meditation house at the centre of the site; everything else radiates out from it.

The design is by the Hanoi practice 1+1>2, led by the architect Hoàng Thúc Hào, and it was finished in twenty nineteen. The rule they set themselves was simple: keep the trees. Every one of those jackfruit and pomelo trees stayed where it was, and the houses were placed between and beneath them, so the old trees shade the shared yards.

Have a look at the houses themselves. The walls are local clay brick. The roofs are wide and thatched, with deep overhangs that keep the sun off the walls and the rain away from the doors. And look down at the base: each house sits up on a raised platform. That's partly to keep termites and damp out, and partly so the rainwater still drains across the land the way it always did.

Now look at the meditation house, with its quiet, shaded space for the whole community, set among the homes. That's the point of the plan. Guests and villagers are meant to share it — the people who stay here live alongside working farmers, eat what's grown here, and cycle or row out to the neighbouring villages.

And here's the thing. A lot of new rural building in Vietnam replaces the village with something from the city. This does the reverse. It takes the oldest materials around — brick, clay, thatch, shade — and proves they're still good enough for a new building, as long as somebody is willing to design carefully with them.

[beat]

Take the long way back. Ask about the cycling paths out through the paddy fields, or a boat across the lake. And if you're curious what the same architects do with a very different brief, the Bát Tràng Museum, east of the city, is theirs too — terracotta, curves, and not a jackfruit tree in sight.
