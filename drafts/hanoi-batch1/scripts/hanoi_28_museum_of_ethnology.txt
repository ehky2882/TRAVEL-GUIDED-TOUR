HANOI — 28 — VIETNAM MUSEUM OF ETHNOLOGY
Single stop · Register: British (Atlas)
Vantage: in front of the main exhibition building, the Bronze Drum, Nguyễn Văn Huyên Street, Cầu Giấy
Sources: Wikipedia (Vietnam Museum of Ethnology) and its sources; Architecture Hunter Hanoi guide
---
The building in front of you is shaped like a drum that's two and a half thousand years old.

You're at the Vietnam Museum of Ethnology, on Nguyễn Văn Huyên Street, in the west of the city. Stand in front of the main exhibition building. It's round, with a flat top and gently tapering sides, and if you squint it looks like an enormous bronze drum set down on the grass. That's exactly what it's meant to be.

The architect was Hà Đức Lịnh, himself from the Tày people, one of Vietnam's ethnic minorities. The model was a Đông Sơn drum — the great bronze drums made in northern Vietnam more than two thousand years ago, and still one of the most powerful symbols of the country's ancient culture. Inside, the galleries were designed by the French architect Véronique Dollfus. The museum was built between nineteen eighty-seven and nineteen ninety-five, and opened in November nineteen ninety-seven.

Indoors, the collections cover Vietnam's fifty-four officially recognised ethnic groups — clothes, tools, ritual objects, everyday things. It's very good. But for the architecture, you'll want to go outside.

Behind the drum is a garden of about two hectares, full of full-size traditional houses brought down from all over the country, or rebuilt here by people from the communities themselves. You can walk into them. There's a long house of the Ê Đê people from the Central Highlands, raised on stilts and stretching away from you like a train carriage. And there's a Bahnar communal house, with a thatched roof that rises steep and high, shaped like a blade. Each one solves the same problems — rain, heat, family, ritual — in its own way.

And here's the thing. Most museums show you a culture in a glass case. This one lets you climb the stairs, duck under the eaves, and stand in the half-light inside, which is the only real way to understand a house.

[beat]

Leave yourself at least an hour for the garden, and climb up into the long house. And look back at the drum from outside: it's a modern building that borrowed its shape from the very oldest thing in the collection.
