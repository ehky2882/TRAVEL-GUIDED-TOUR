Silk Path Boutique Hanoi

In Hanoi, the front of a building is rarely the whole story. This one's barely the first page.

You're on hang kai, the street that runs along the southern end of hwan kee em Lake. With the water behind you, look for number twenty one, a narrow neoclassical front squeezed between its neighbours. One travel writer described it as almost invisible, wedged between a perfume shop and a café. That's the Silk Path Boutique.

Have a look at the facade. It speaks the language of the old colonial townhouse, classical mouldings, tall windows, a proper sense of occasion, all folded into a frontage only a shopfront wide. It reads like a small townhouse. You'd guess a handful of rooms at most.

There are thirty three. That's the trick. The front is only a gateway: behind it, the hotel opens out into a much larger property hidden behind a whole row of old terraced houses. From the street, you see the width of one shopfront. Inside, you're in something closer to a block.

And there's an old logic to that. Hanoi's traditional houses were built narrow at the front and long at the back, the famous tube houses, because street frontage was the precious, contested thing. Space was won by going deep, not wide. This hotel plays the same game at a larger scale. It takes the narrowest possible slice of one of the city's best streets, and then quietly expands where nobody can see.

The street itself has a name worth knowing. hang kai means trays, it was once the place for lacquered and mother of pearl inlaid trays and boxes. A street of beautiful containers, which is rather fitting for a hotel that's mostly hidden inside its own box.

So, next time you walk past a slim old front in this city, give it a second look. There may be far more behind it than it's letting on.

Before you go, turn round and cross to the lake. From the southern shore you can see Turtle Tower, the little three tiered tower standing on its islet in the middle of the water. And if you're staying here, ask about the rooftop: it looks straight back over the lake.
