zwee tree

The sign says it plainly: kaw too num, nineteen thirty six. Since nineteen thirty six.

You're on yen foo Street, near the edge of West Lake, outside zwee tree. It's a tall, narrow tube house, a nyah ong, with a slightly dusty, weathered front. If you can smell coffee roasting before you see the door, you're in the right spot. The roasting is done right there at the entrance, and they'll sell you the beans in a paper bag.

The café was opened by a man called fan zwee sen. It's named after his son, zwee tree, and it's run today by zwee tree's daughter, fam tan hoong. Three generations, one name over the door. It hasn't always been this door, mind you, the business has moved several times over the years. But the family, the roast and the habits have come along each time.

Go in and look at the walls. There are framed photographs of zwee tree receiving a civic award, pictures from the war years, and, my favourite, the founder's sword and his badminton racket, given equal honour.

Then look up. The house goes up three floors. The ground floor belongs to the regulars, mostly middle aged, on old dark wooden chairs. A narrow staircase takes you up to the second floor, where the ceilings are low enough to make you duck and the tables are so close you'll be shuffling aside for strangers. Keep going to the third floor, where there's a balcony over the street and, if you lean a little, a glimpse of West Lake.

Order the soo uh choo uh kah feh, yoghurt coffee. The yoghurt is made here and frozen before it goes in the glass, so it arrives thick and half set, with dark coffee poured over the top. It's one of those drinks that sounds wrong and tastes completely right.

Plenty of Hanoi cafés have a story. This one has a family album. The coffee is a house blend of Arabica and Robusta, roasted by the door, and the recipe has travelled with them from address to address for almost ninety years.

Afterwards, walk north to the lake and along tan nee en Road to chun kwock Pagoda, the slender red tower on a little island. It's Hanoi's oldest pagoda, older even than this café's recipe, by the best part of fourteen centuries.
