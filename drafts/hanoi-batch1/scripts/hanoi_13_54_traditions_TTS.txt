You're on hang boon, a few streets from chook bark Lake, outside number forty four B. It's a terraced house, like the others along here, but the windows are full of carved wood, woven cloth and old pots rather than the usual things for sale. This is fifty four traditions, part private collection, part antique shop.

The number in the name is the number of ethnic groups officially recognised in Vietnam: the kinh majority, and fifty three others, many of them living in the mountains of the north and the Central Highlands. This is, as far as anyone knows, the only gallery in the country given over entirely to their art and objects.

It was started by two people. Mark Rapoport is a former paediatrician from New York who first came to Vietnam as a medical volunteer during the war. nwen tee nyoong is a Hanoi native who has spent her life learning to spot what matters. Between them they built up a collection of something like fifteen thousand objects. Some of their early finds are now in the Metropolitan Museum in New York and the moo zay doo kay bron lee in Paris.

Go in and have a look; you'll usually be shown round in person. The interior is kept plain on purpose so the objects can do the talking, textiles, ceramics, carvings, jewellery. Rapoport's particular passion is the equipment of the shamans of the northern mountains. Ask about the power rattle: a sword shaped object strung with antique coins, shaken during ceremonies to keep the spirits listening.

And here's what's worth knowing, standing here. Most of what people buy in Hanoi as ethnic is made for them. What's in here was made for use, for a wedding, a funeral, a harvest, a healing, by people who never imagined it in a shop window. That's what makes it difficult, and rather moving, to look at.

When you leave, walk the few minutes to chook bark Lake and follow its edge to tan nee en Road. On the shore there's a small memorial marking where an American pilot, John McCain, came down in the lake in nineteen sixty seven. The neighbourhood has more stories than its quiet streets let on.
