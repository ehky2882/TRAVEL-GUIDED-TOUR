Imagine seven potter's wheels, each with a pot half thrown on it, all spinning at once. Then build it.

You're at the bat chahng Museum, in the pottery village south east of Hanoi. Stand on the open ground floor and look up. Above you, the building swells outwards in curved terracotta coloured tiers, like a cluster of vessels caught mid turn on the wheel. If you feel a little as though you're standing inside an upturned pot, you're in the right place.

The architects are the Hanoi practice one plus one greater than two, led by hwang took how, and the building opened around twenty twenty three. It's often called the bat chahng Pottery Museum, though officially it's more of a community house for the village's ceramics: exhibitions, workshops, festivals, and the business of selling pots.

Have a look at the shape. It's made of seven segments, each one an inverted dome, seven turntables, the architects say, turning together. The tiers step outward as they rise, exactly the way clay rises and widens under a potter's hands. And the colour is deliberate: it's the colour of fired earth, and of the brick kilns that have stood in this village for centuries.

Now the materials. The main structure is concrete reinforced with fibre, but it's dressed in the village's own products, earthen bricks, ceramic mosaic, pottery tiles. Skylights cut through the domes to let light and air down into the middle.

Inside, there's a lot more than a museum: four storeys holding exhibitions, studios, shops, a restaurant, homestay rooms, and an auditorium for performances. And on the very top, a garden full of plants, with space for workshops and children to play.

And here's the interesting bit. Most museums put the craft in glass cases. This one takes the way the craft is made, the turn of the wheel, the stack of the kiln, and turns that into the building. You're not just looking at pots. You're standing in one.

When you've been through, go up to the roof garden and look out over the village rooftops, many of them still potteries. Then walk to the old market and find a stall where they'll let you sit at a wheel. Try to throw one pot. It's harder than this building makes it look.
