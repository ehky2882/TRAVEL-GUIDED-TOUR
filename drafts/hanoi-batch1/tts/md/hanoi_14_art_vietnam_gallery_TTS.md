# Art Vietnam Gallery

Getting here is a bit of an anticlimax. That's deliberate.

You're in a lane off yen lack Street, in the south east of the city, ordinary, residential, frankly humdrum. Shops, motorbikes, washing, nothing to tell you there's an art gallery anywhere nearby. Find number two, in lane sixty six. Behind it is Art Vietnam Gallery, and it's the American dealer Suzanne lekt's home.

Lecht has been showing Vietnamese contemporary art in Hanoi for a long time, she's closely associated with the generation of painters known as the Gang of Five, who broke away from official, propaganda style art in the nineteen nineties. The gallery used to be public. It began in an old tube house, moved once, and then in twenty seventeen she closed the public space and went back to where she says she started: a salon in her own house. So it's open by appointment only. Ring ahead, and you'll be let in.

Now, the house. It's a restored traditional house of the White Thai, one of Vietnam's upland ethnic groups, the kind of timber house usually found raised on stilts in the mountains of the north west, not in a Hanoi lane. Inside, it's all dark wood, quiet, and low light. Painting and sculpture are shown in a setting that feels much more like visiting someone than visiting a gallery.

And here's the interesting bit. Most galleries do everything they can to make arriving feel grand. This one does the opposite. You come through the most ordinary part of the city, and then a door opens on dark timber and paintings. The contrast is the point. The walk here is the frame.

It's also a fair picture of Vietnamese contemporary art itself, which for decades happened in houses, studios and back rooms rather than in museums. A lot of the best work in this city still does.

If you're coming back by appointment, give yourself time to sit with one picture rather than ten. And on the way out, look back down the lane. Knowing what's behind that door, the humdrum looks rather different.
