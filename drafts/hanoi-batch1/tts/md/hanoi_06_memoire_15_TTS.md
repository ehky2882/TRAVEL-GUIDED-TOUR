You're standing on Sugar Street. Hold on to that; it matters later.

This is hang doo ung, in the thick of the Old Quarter, and you want number fifteen. From the pavement it looks like any of its neighbours: a narrow terraced house, a shop at street level, washing and wires above. Now tip your head back and look right up to the roofline. If the angle's kind to you, you'll catch a flash of terracotta red steel up there. That's mem wahr fifteen.

It's a café that has quietly moved in on top of somebody else's house. Everything up there is made of the same red steel, a stack of staircases, each one set slightly out of line with the last. And the staircases don't just get you to the top. The steps are the seats. The landings are the tables. There's no clear point where the building stops and the furniture starts. You climb it, and then you sit on it.

At the top, you get the one thing the Old Quarter almost never gives you at street level: the whole view, all the way round. A sea of tiled roofs, water tanks, satellite dishes and other people's rooftop gardens, with the city carrying on underneath you as if you weren't there.

Here's why the street name matters. hang doo ung was named for the sugar trade, and it still sells sugar and sweets, busiest of all in the weeks before tet and the Mid Autumn Festival. Its other speciality is oh my, dried apricots, plums and kumquats, salted or sugared, sometimes spiced with ginger or liquorice. And mem wahr fifteen sells itself on tea, coffee and oh my. So the strangest new building on the street is, underneath it all, a sweet shop, exactly as the street has always been.

The Old Quarter ran out of ground a very long time ago. Every plot was claimed centuries back, and the houses fill them wall to wall. The only space left is upwards. This little café is one of the boldest grabs for it, and I rather love that it makes the grab with stairs.

Before you climb, buy a small bag of oh my from one of the shops along here and take it up with you. Then, when you come back down, carry on north. hang doo ung runs straight up to dong swan Market, the great covered market the French built in eighteen eighty nine.
