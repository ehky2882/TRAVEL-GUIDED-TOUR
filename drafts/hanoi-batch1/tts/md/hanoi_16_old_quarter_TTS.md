# The Old Quarter

*TTS-safe version — paste the body below into ElevenLabs.*

---

Nearly every street name around you begins with the same word: hang. It means goods, or merchandise.

You're on hang boo um, in the heart of Hanoi's Old Quarter, outside an old temple gate, the bark mah Temple, the Temple of the White Horse. If you can see the temple, a press of shopfronts on both sides, and motorbikes filling every gap, you're in the right place.

This district has been the city's market since about the eleventh century, when craftsmen and traders from the villages around the capital began settling here, between the royal citadel and the Red River. People from the same village, with the same trade, tended to settle on the same street. So the streets took the names of what they sold. hang boo um, where you're standing, was sails. hang back is silver. hang guy, silk and hemp. hang doo ung, sugar. You'll hear it called the Thirty Six Streets, though there have always been more than that; thirty six is a lucky, symbolic number rather than a count.

Now look at the houses. Narrow fronts, sometimes only a few metres wide, and then they run back a very long way, a shop at the front, then courtyards, then living rooms, then a kitchen at the back. They're called nyah ong, tube houses. Street frontage was what everyone wanted, so the plots were sliced thin and built deep.

Then turn to the temple. The story goes that when King lee tie toh founded the capital, in the year ten ten, his city walls kept falling down, until a white horse appeared and showed him where to build. The temple honours the spirit of that horse. It's thought to be the oldest temple in the Old Quarter.

And here's the thing. Nobody planned this district. There's no grand axis, no square, no master plan. It grew, lot by lot and trade by trade, over a thousand years, and it still works. That's quite something.

Pick a street with a name you like, and follow it. Walk a few minutes north east and you'll reach oh kwan choo ung, the only one of the old city gates still standing. It's small, battered and still in use, which is the Old Quarter all over.
