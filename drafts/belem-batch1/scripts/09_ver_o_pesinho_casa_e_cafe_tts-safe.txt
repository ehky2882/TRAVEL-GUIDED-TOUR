Vair oo Payzeenyoo, Kahza ee Kafeh

Vair oo Payzeenyoo translates roughly as little Vair oo Payzoo, and the joke is that the city's huge, noisy riverside market has been shrunk down to fit inside a house in Nazareh.

Through the front door on Travessa Benzhameen Konstant, you'll find a tall, double height room painted in strong colours, with hollow brick screens and long benches borrowed from the look of the market stalls, a cafe counter ahead of you and a shop running down the side.

It began in twenty eighteen as a pop up in a shopping centre, trying to recreate the feeling of the real market, and in twenty twenty three it settled here as a cafe and shop, with Manwel Netto, a designer turned cook, running the kitchen alongside his family. Have a look at the walls as you wait, where you'll find ribbons from the Seeryoo jee Nazareh, the enormous October procession that takes over this part of the city, along with tee shirts printed with local slang.

The menu is about ninety per cent Amazonian, so breakfast might be toast with Marazhaw buffalo cheese, and the dish people photograph most is a lasagne made with smoked peerahrookoo, the giant river fish, served with fried banana; for something sweet, there's an ahsahee crem broolay topped with bakooree, a tart, creamy fruit you'll rarely meet outside the region.

The shop showcases more than thirty Amazonian makers, from Marazhaw pottery and toys carved from the soft pith of the meereetchee palm to kashahssa infused with zhamboo, and upstairs, visitors are asked to take their shoes off, as if they've been invited home.

The clever part is that this is a translation rather than a replacement. The real Vair oo Payzoo can be overwhelming on a first morning, and this place gives you the vocabulary first, so that when you get there, you know what you're looking at.

So take what you've learned here down to the waterfront and go and find the real thing, ideally early, while the morning's ahsahee is still coming off the river.
