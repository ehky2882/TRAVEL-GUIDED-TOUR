Restaurante Rua Belém

Look for the neon, because the entrance to this restaurant glows like a side street in Tokyo, which is not quite what you expect on Avenida Generalíssimo Deodoro.

From the pavement outside number 468, in Umarizal, you should see the lit-up doorway on the ground floor, with a dining room behind it dressed in pop art and city-street graphics, most of them pointing firmly towards Japan.

Rua simply means street, and the name is the brief: city food, the kind you'd eat on a stool somewhere busy, with an Amazonian accent. The chef, Samila Seki, has described her menu as a reunion, built partly out of her own experiences, and it shows in the dish people come back for, the Lamén Rua. It's a ramen with pork belly chashu, a cured egg, spring onion, sesame and caramelised carrot, plus a tangle of jambu, the local herb that makes your lips tingle.

The broth is where it gets interesting, because it's made with chicken, pork and seaweed, along with chicória, which sounds like chicory but in Pará means a different plant entirely, a sharp, leafy herb related to coriander that finds its way into half the region's cooking. On the drinks side, the Bud Smash is gin infused with cucumber, shaken with lemon and basil, and finished with a pinch of sea salt.

The bit to hold onto is that this isn't as unlikely a pairing as it sounds. Japanese families have farmed in Pará since the late nineteen-twenties, and their influence runs through the state's food, so a ramen seasoned with jambu reads less like a gimmick and more like two old neighbours finally sitting down at the same table.

[beat]

The kitchen opens from Wednesday to Sunday evenings, and if you've room afterwards, the canal park along the Doca is a few blocks away, and makes a pleasant place to walk off the noodles under the lights.
