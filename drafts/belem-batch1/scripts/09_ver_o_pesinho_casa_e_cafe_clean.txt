Ver-o-Pesinho Casa & Café

Ver-o-Pesinho translates roughly as little Ver-o-Peso, and the joke is that the city's huge, noisy riverside market has been shrunk down to fit inside a house in Nazaré.

Through the front door on Travessa Benjamin Constant, you'll find a tall, double-height room painted in strong colours, with hollow-brick screens and long benches borrowed from the look of the market stalls, a café counter ahead of you and a shop running down the side.

It began in 2018 as a pop-up in a shopping centre, trying to recreate the feeling of the real market, and in 2023 it settled here as a café and shop, with Manoel Netto, a designer turned cook, running the kitchen alongside his family. Have a look at the walls as you wait, where you'll find ribbons from the Círio de Nazaré, the enormous October procession that takes over this part of the city, along with T-shirts printed with local slang.

The menu is about ninety per cent Amazonian, so breakfast might be toast with Marajó buffalo cheese, and the dish people photograph most is a lasagne made with smoked pirarucu, the giant river fish, served with fried banana; for something sweet, there's an açaí crème brûlée topped with bacuri, a tart, creamy fruit you'll rarely meet outside the region.

The shop showcases more than thirty Amazonian makers, from Marajó pottery and toys carved from the soft pith of the miriti palm to cachaça infused with jambu, and upstairs, visitors are asked to take their shoes off, as if they've been invited home.

The clever part is that this is a translation rather than a replacement. The real Ver-o-Peso can be overwhelming on a first morning, and this place gives you the vocabulary first, so that when you get there, you know what you're looking at.

So take what you've learned here down to the waterfront and go and find the real thing, ideally early, while the morning's açaí is still coming off the river.
