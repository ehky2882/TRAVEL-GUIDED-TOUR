Beneath this smart avenue runs a creek that people once called the Eegarapeh das Almas, the Creek of Souls.

The canal running down the middle of Avenida Veeskondjee jee Sooza Frankoo is your guide here, so find one of the footbridges or viewing decks over it. Apartment towers and traffic line both sides, and between them a green strip of planting, paths and a cycle lane follows the water for about a kilometre and a quarter, with kiosks, a playground and outdoor exercise machines along the way.

That name goes back to the Kabanahzhem, the uprising that shook Pahrah in the eighteen thirties, when many people here believed the souls of fallen rebels still wandered this wetland; a neighbouring stream earned the name Creek of Arms from stories of weapons hidden along its banks. Afterwards the land turned to dairy farms supplying milk to the old city, then to docks for river trade, and in the nineteen seventies the water was pushed into a channel and the avenue built over the old wetland, named after an imperial era politician, Bernardoo jee Sooza Frankoo. By the nineties this was becoming one of the most expensive addresses in Belaym.

The park you're in opened in October twenty twenty five, ahead of the climate conference, designed by the landscape studio Natoorehza Oorbahna. The canal was cleaned, and new drainage and sewage pipes went in, because in a city where rain arrives most afternoons, a waterway is infrastructure before it's scenery.

The thought worth holding onto is that most cities spent the twentieth century burying their streams and pretending they'd never been there. This one has gone partway back, giving the water room and air again, and letting it keep a name that remembers the people who once fought here.

Follow the canal downstream towards Gwazharah Bay, and at the far end look for where the old creek finally slips out into the river that it was always heading for.
