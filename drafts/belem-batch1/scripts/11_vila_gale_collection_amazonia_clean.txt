Vila Galé Collection Amazônia

Two hundred and twenty-seven rooms make up this hotel, and every one of them is dedicated to a woman.

Count three long warehouses along the Porto Futuro waterfront, numbers seven, eight and eight A, a short walk from Estação das Docas, and you have the whole hotel, with its old iron frames kept in place and Guajará Bay on the far side. These were port buildings put up by English engineers in the first years of the last century, and they're heritage-listed, so the bones you can see from the street are original.

The conversion was done in about a year, at a cost of around a hundred and eighty million reais, so that the hotel could open in late 2025 in time for the COP30 climate summit. Behind the iron, there are two outdoor pools looking straight onto the bay, a heated indoor pool, a spa, and a children's club.

Now for those rooms, each of which honours a woman from the arts, music, literature or humanitarian work, with Elis Regina, Fernanda Torres and Mother Teresa among them, and Fafá de Belém, the city's own singer, holding her place; a code on the wall brings up her biography, a poem and a song, so you go to sleep with company.

The part I keep coming back to is who's doing the restoring. Vila Galé is a Portuguese group, and Belém was founded by the Portuguese in 1616, about a kilometre from here, so this is a long relationship, and not always an easy one, now playing out with hotel keys rather than tax ledgers. The group's founder has said that cities which don't rescue their historic centres lose their character, and the iron frames suggest he meant it.
