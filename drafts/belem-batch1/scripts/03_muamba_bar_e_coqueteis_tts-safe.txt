Mooamba Bar ee Kokeh tayss

There's a curious thing about the name of this bar: in Brazilian slang, mooamba means contraband, the sort of goods that come in under the counter, and that tells you something about the attitude before you've ordered.

Climb to the upper level of Veela Container, the cluster of converted shipping containers on Avenida Govehrnador Magalyoinsh Barahta, and look for a small bar with wooden chairs, an open air seating area and walls covered in white tiles. That's Mooamba, which squeezes around thirty five people into one container and the space in front of it.

Lean in close to those tiles first, because cocktail recipes are written straight onto them, so the wall works as decoration, menu and cheat sheet all at once. The bar was opened in twenty nineteen by bartender Eevens Penna, who was born in Belaym, together with Yooree Fernandez and Broonoo Eyeres, and the idea from the start was cocktails without pretension.

The drink people tend to mention is the oorookoom peesko sour, where the Andean classic gets a dose of oorookoom, the red seed that has coloured food across the Amazon for generations, and is finished with a guava foam. The other is anything built on zhamboo gin, because zhamboo is the herb that makes your mouth tingle and go faintly numb; one visitor described a zhamboo drink here as tasting like a cold, boozy takakah, the hot shrimp soup sold on Belaym's street corners.

If you ask me, the clever part is how little fuss is made of all this. A classic cocktail is really just a structure, and once the lime becomes local citrus and the gin is infused with zhamboo, the drink stops being an import and starts sounding like it was born here.

Order a Kabokleenya, the house's tangerine and lime take on a kypeereenya, and if you fancy it, take it down in a plastic cup and wander the other containers before the Bazeeleeka jee Nazareh, just a short walk away, draws you on.
