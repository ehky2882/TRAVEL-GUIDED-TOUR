# Rumah Penghulu Abu Seman

This house has been taken apart and moved at least twice. That's the point of it.

You're in the garden of the Badan Warisan heritage centre, off Jalan Stonor, a short walk south of the Petronas Towers. In front of you is a timber house raised high on stilts, with steep roofs, carved panels and long rows of windows. It's the only building of its kind for miles around, and it looks it.

It's a village headman's house from Kedah, in the north of the peninsula. It was built in stages from the 1910s into the early thirties. The first part was built for someone else; in 1924 his heirs sold it to Abu Seman, a penghulu or village headman, who moved it to his own village and added wings as his family grew.

That's the gift detail. Malay timber houses were designed to be taken apart. The joints are pegged, not nailed, so a house could be dismantled, carried and put up again, and extended room by room. It's a building that expects to change.

Now look at how it handles the climate. It's raised on stilts, to keep it above floods and let air pass underneath. The roof is steep, to shed heavy rain, and it overhangs deeply, to keep out the sun. There are windows and ventilation panels everywhere, so any breeze goes straight through. This is air conditioning, without electricity.

By the mid-nineties the house had fallen into decay. Badan Warisan, Malaysia's heritage trust, rescued it, and between 1996 and 1997 dismantled it and rebuilt it here as faithfully as it could. The restoration won an honourable mention in the UNESCO Asia-Pacific heritage awards in 2000.

[beat]

Stand here and look past the roof to the towers beyond. Everything modern Malaysian architects have tried to do — shade, air, lightness — this house was already doing, in timber, a hundred years ago.

Guided tours of the interior run on weekdays and Saturdays, usually late morning and mid-afternoon. Go in if you can, and look for the separate rooms for receiving guests and for the family — the house is laid out like a set of manners.
