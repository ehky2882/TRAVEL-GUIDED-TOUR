Day wah kahn

The most decorated restaurant in Malaysia began life as a university project.

Look up. You should be at the foot of Naza Tower on Per see ah rahn Kay El See See, a few minutes' walk from the Petronas Towers. Day wah kahn is on the forty eighth floor, so from the pavement there are forty seven storeys of glass between you and dinner.

It opened in twenty fifteen inside Kay Dee You University College in Shah Ah lahm, as a teaching kitchen with ambitions. The chef, Darren Tay oh, moved it to this tower a few years later, and the stars followed. It now holds two Michelin stars, and it was the first restaurant in Malaysia to be given a Michelin Green Star, the award for sustainability.

The name is the clue to the cooking. It runs two Malay words together: day wah, meaning god, and mah kahn, to eat. And what Tay oh's kitchen eats is almost entirely Malaysian. Not dishes, so much as ingredients, things gathered from farms, jungles, hills and the sea. Kelulut honey, made by tiny stingless bees. A wild citrus from Joh hor called lee mow hahn too, the ghost lime. Boo ah keh loo ak, the black seed that is poisonous until it has been properly prepared.

The gift detail is a wild yam called keh mee lee. The team spent about seven years learning to cook it, and in the end borrowed a technique from Latin America, treating it with alkali the way corn is treated for tortillas, to turn it into a kind of dough.

That's the argument this restaurant makes. Malaysian food is usually celebrated for its famous dishes. Day wah kahn suggests the larder is the real treasure, and that most of it has never been on a menu at all.

If you've eaten here, or are simply passing, walk a few minutes west to Kay El See See Park and look back at this tower from the lake. Then try to work out which window is the forty eighth floor, it's harder than it looks.
