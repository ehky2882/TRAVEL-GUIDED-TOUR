# Pasar Seni (Central Market) (TTS-safe)

This was once the city's wet market, where you came for fish, meat and vegetables. Now it sells batik and paintings.

Jah lahn Hahng Kahs too ree, facing the long front of Central Market. You should see a two storey building in pale blue and white, with a stepped skyline of parapets, vertical fins and a name panel at the top. Along its side runs a covered walkway, Kasturi Walk, with food stalls and street musicians.

There's been a market on this site since eighteen eighty eight. The building you're looking at came later, completed in nineteen thirty seven and designed by the architect Tee Why Lee. It's widely considered the finest Art Deco building in Kuala Lumpur, and it's worth a moment to see why. Look at the way the facade is broken into strips, with stepped tops rising towards the centre. It's a market dressed up as a modern machine, which is exactly what the thirties wanted.

The colours are a story too. It was originally painted baby blue and pink. The pink was later swapped for white, which is a shame, if you ask me.

By the seventies the market was in decline, and there were plans to knock it down. The Malaysian Heritage Society stepped in, and the building was saved. Between nineteen eighty five and nineteen eighty six it was converted into a home for crafts and the arts, officially Pah sar Boo dye ah, the culture market, but known to everyone as Pah sar Seh nee, the art market.

What's worth knowing is how close this came. When the huge Dye ah boo mee complex was built next door in the early eighties, the market was in the way, and it survived. A building that sold fish for half a century is now one of the city's protected landmarks.

Before you leave, walk round to the back of the building. Look up at the rear elevation, then cross the river on the footbridge by the old railway station; from the far bank you can see the market, the white arches of Dye ah boo mee and the station's domes all in one glance.
