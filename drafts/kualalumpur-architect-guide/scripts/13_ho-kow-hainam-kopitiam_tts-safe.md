For more than sixty years this coffee shop lived in a back lane. Then it was evicted, and moved about a minute's walk away.

Jah lahn Bah lye Poh liss, in Chinatown, on the main street rather than the lane behind it. Hoh Cow is in the shophouse in front of you; on most mornings, the queue is the giveaway. The lane behind is Loh rong Pahng goong, better known as Kwhy Chye Hong, and that is where this story starts.

In nineteen fifty six a Hye nah neez man called Hoh Kee Soon opened a small coffee shop there, selling tea, soft boiled eggs and toast. Hye nah neez families ran a great many of Malaysia's old coffee shops, the koh pee tee ahms, and the formula hardly varied: strong coffee, eggs barely cooked, and toast spread with kah yah, the sweet coconut jam. His son, Hoh Cow, gave the shop its name.

In twenty eighteen the family received an eviction notice. Rather than close, they moved round the corner, to the larger shophouse you're looking at now. Hoh Cow's daughter, Hoh Sook Chahn, runs it today with her own children, which makes this the third and fourth generations.

Go in and order the classics. The coffee is koh pee, roasted dark and served with condensed milk unless you say otherwise. The eggs come runny, in a little bowl, and the trick is a dash of soy sauce and white pepper. The toast is thick, with kah yah and a slab of butter.

Here's what I find touching about it. The move should have been the end. Instead the new shop became a destination in its own right, and the old lane it left behind has since been restored and opened to the public. Both sides of the story survived.

When you've finished, step round the back to Kwhy Chye Hong itself. Look for the murals of nineteen sixties street life on the walls, and imagine a coffee shop tucked into the lane, where this one began.
