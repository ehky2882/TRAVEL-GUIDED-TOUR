For about twenty years, this was one of the busiest printing works in Kuala Lumpur.

You're at the very end of Jah lahn Ree ong in Bahng sar, at a cluster of low industrial buildings with cafes spilling out of them. Ay Pee Double you stands for Art Printing Works, and the full name of the place today is A Place Where, a little pun on the initials. Look for the warehouse sheds, the brick and steel and the old factory rooflines.

Art Printing Works was founded in nineteen fifty two, first in Leh boo Ahm pahng in the old city, and moved here to Bahng sar in nineteen sixty five. From the sixties to the eighties it was one of the most prolific printers in the capital, and Jah lahn Ree ong itself was one of the great printing and newspaper streets of the Klahng Valley.

Then technology moved on and left the old presses behind. In twenty thirteen, the family that owned the business decided to open up the underused parts of the factory, rather than sell the land. Today the old printing sheds hold cafes, a coffee roaster, event halls, offices and small gardens. The rough warehouse spaces are hired out for workshops, markets and performances.

The gift detail is the family's other building. In Meh lah kah, the same family owns the Royal Press, an old letterpress print shop that has been turned into a museum, so they have kept both ends of their trade, the hand set type and the factory that replaced it.

The interesting bit is what this place proves. Kuala Lumpur has lost a great many industrial buildings, because a factory rarely looks precious until it's gone. Ay Pee Double you shows that you don't have to choose between keeping an old building and making a living from it. The sheds still look like what they were; they just hold different things.

Before you go, walk back along Jah lahn Ree ong. Newspaper offices still stand further along it, and the road is a short lesson in how much of this city's news used to be printed right here.
