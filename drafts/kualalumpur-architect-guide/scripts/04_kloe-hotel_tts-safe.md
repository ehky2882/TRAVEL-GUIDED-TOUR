The hotel in front of you opened in March twenty twenty, which, you may recall, was not a vintage month for hotels.

You're on Jah lahn Boo kit Bin tahng at number two hundred and twenty seven, the quieter eastern end of the street, away from its busiest stretch. Klo ee is the pair of new towers in front of you. Look at what they're made of: bare concrete for the frame, with clay brick, timber and metal laid against it. Nothing is painted to look like something else.

The name runs Kuala Lumpur's initials into Klo ee, from the Greek word for a young green shoot, and that little sprout appears in the hotel's logo. It was dreamed up by Eng Ping Hoh, a film director and producer, and designed from the ground up by the local practice Domaine Architects. It's small for a city hotel, eighty five rooms, and that's the point.

Here's what I find most interesting. Most hotels hire one designer and let them furnish every corner. Klo ee went the other way and filled itself with the work of the city's own makers: furniture, ceramics and artworks by local designers and craftspeople, alongside mid century Scandinavian pieces. Five of the suites, called the Lofts, were conceived with local tastemakers, each around a creative pursuit. One, a room for cooks and eaters, was put together by the chef and broadcaster Sarah Benjamin.

So the building works as a kind of catalogue. Stay a night and you've been introduced to a generation of Malaysian designers without visiting a single gallery.

From here, walk west along Jah lahn Boo kit Bin tahng and the street gets steadily louder, brighter and more crowded until you reach the shopping malls and the hawker stalls beyond. It's worth doing once, if only to see how quickly the city changes its mind about what it wants to be.
