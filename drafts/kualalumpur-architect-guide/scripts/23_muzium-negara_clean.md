# Muzium Negara (National Museum)

- **Google Maps name:** Muzium Negara
- **Category:** VISIT
- **Coordinates (WGS-84):** 3.137662, 101.687382
- **Version:** clean (authoring script; `[beat]` marks the engineered pause)

---

The museum before this one was hit by American bombers in 1945.

You're on Jalan Damansara, facing the front of the National Museum. Look for the long building under a tall, sweeping roof, with large mosaic murals spread across the front.

It opened on 31 August 1963, the sixth anniversary of independence, and was designed by the architect Ho Kok Hoe. His sources were Malay royal palaces and traditional village houses, scaled up in concrete for a national institution. That steep roof, with its upswept ends, is the giveaway.

The mosaics are the gift. They tell the story of the country, its history and its culture, in coloured tiles. Have a look at them up close. They were made to be read from the street, like a picture book for passers-by.

The site has an older story. Here stood the Selangor Museum, designed by A. B. Hubback, the architect of the railway station and Masjid Jamek. On 10 March 1945, near the end of the war, American B-29 bombers destroyed its right wing. The rest stood until it was cleared to make way for the building you're looking at.

[beat]

What's worth knowing, standing here, is what Ho Kok Hoe was trying to do. The museum before was a colonial building in a borrowed style. This one was meant to show that a new country could build a modern institution from its own traditions. Six years after independence, that was a statement.

Before you go in, walk round to the side of the grounds. There's a steam locomotive that worked the Malayan railways from 1921 to 1969, and Istana Satu, a timber palace from Terengganu, rebuilt here in 1974. Compare its roof with the museum's — you'll see exactly where the architect found his idea.
