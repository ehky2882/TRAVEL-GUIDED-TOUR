# Old Kuala Lumpur Railway Station

- **Google Maps name:** Old KL Train Station
- **Category:** VISIT
- **Coordinates (WGS-84):** 3.139761, 101.693355
- **Version:** clean (authoring script; `[beat]` marks the engineered pause)

---

This is a railway station dressed as a Mughal palace.

Jalan Sultan Hishamuddin, on the pavement opposite the Majestic Hotel. Turn to face the station: a long, white building with arcades along the front, and on top, a skyline of domes, pointed arches and slim towers. Those little domed pavilions on stilts at the corners are called chhatris, a form borrowed from India.

The architect was Arthur Benison Hubback, a British architect in the colonial Public Works Department, and the station was opened on 1 August 1910. It replaced a much smaller station of 1886 on the same site.

Have a look at the roofline. There's a lot going on: domes, pointed arches, slim towers. The style is sometimes called Indo-Saracenic — a British invention that mixed Islamic and Indian forms and applied them to colonial buildings across Asia. It wasn't Malay at all. It was the empire's idea of what an eastern building should look like.

And here's the gift. There were originally six chhatris. When the station was refurbished in 1986, two more were added. So even the decoration kept growing.

The station was more than a station. For decades, the Station Hotel occupied its upper floors and northern wing. Since 2001 the long-distance trains have run from KL Sentral instead, but local Komuter trains still stop here, so it remains a working station.

[beat]

What's worth knowing is how much of colonial Kuala Lumpur Hubback designed. The Masjid Jamek, the old Selangor Museum, a good deal of this city's postcard image — they're his. He gave the city its face, and that face came from India.

Before you leave, turn round and look up the slope to the Majestic Hotel, opposite. The two buildings have faced each other since 1932. Then, if you've the time, walk north to Dayabumi and see how the 1980s answered Hubback's arches.
