The museum before this one was hit by American bombers in nineteen forty five.

You're on Jah lahn Dah mahn sah rah, facing the front of the National Museum. Look for the long building under a tall, sweeping roof, with large mosaic murals spread across the front.

It opened on the thirty first of August, nineteen sixty three, the sixth anniversary of independence, and was designed by the architect Hoh Kok Hoh. His sources were Malay royal palaces and traditional village houses, scaled up in concrete for a national institution. That steep roof, with its upswept ends, is the giveaway.

The mosaics are the gift. They tell the story of the country, its history and its culture, in coloured tiles. Have a look at them up close. They were made to be read from the street, like a picture book for passers by.

The site has an older story. Here stood the Seh lahng or Museum, designed by Ay Bee Hubback, the architect of the railway station and Mahsjid Jah mek. On the tenth of March, nineteen forty five, near the end of the war, American Bee twenty nine bombers destroyed its right wing. The rest stood until it was cleared to make way for the building you're looking at.

What's worth knowing, standing here, is what Hoh Kok Hoh was trying to do. The museum before was a colonial building in a borrowed style. This one was meant to show that a new country could build a modern institution from its own traditions. Six years after independence, that was a statement.

Before you go in, walk round to the side of the grounds. There's a steam locomotive that worked the Malayan railways from nineteen twenty one to nineteen sixty nine, and Iss tah nah Sah too, a timber palace from Teh reng gah noo, rebuilt here in nineteen seventy four. Compare its roof with the museum's, you'll see exactly where the architect found his idea.
