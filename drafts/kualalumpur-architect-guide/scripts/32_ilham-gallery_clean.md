# ILHAM Gallery

There's a public art gallery at the foot of one of Kuala Lumpur's most striking towers, and it costs nothing to go in.

You're on Jalan Binjai, on the plaza in front of Ilham Tower, a short walk east of the Petronas Towers. Look up: the tower is fifty-eight storeys, designed by Foster and Partners, with its structure — a cross-hatched steel frame — on the outside, where you can see it.

The gallery opened in 2015, and it's been run since 2019 by Yayasan Ilham, a non-profit foundation. Its subject is modern and contemporary art from Malaysia and the wider region, and its exhibitions have become some of the most thoughtful in the city. Admission is free.

Before you go in, look around the plaza. Two artworks stand in front of the tower. One is Divina Proportione by the Chinese artist Ai Weiwei, a geometric form that rewards walking all the way round. The other is Breast Stupa Topiary by the Thai artist Pinaree Sanpitak, a shape that is part Buddhist shrine, part human body, made out of greenery.

[beat]

Here's the interesting bit. Most corporate towers keep their lobbies sealed off and guarded. Ilham does something different, opening its lower floors to the public for art, talks, films and children's workshops. It makes a private tower feel, in part, like public property.

Inside, look out for work by Malaysian artists who are much less known abroad than they should be. The gallery's exhibitions have covered pioneers such as Hoessein Enas and contemporary artists such as Yee I-Lann.

When you leave, walk to the corner and look back up at the tower. Then walk a few minutes west to KLCC Park and compare it with the Petronas Towers; it's a good lesson in two very different ideas of a skyscraper — one wrapped in ornament, the other showing its bones.
