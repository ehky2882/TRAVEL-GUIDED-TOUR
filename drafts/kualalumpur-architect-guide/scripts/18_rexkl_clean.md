Inside this building, the bookshelves step down towards where the cinema screen used to be.

Jalan Sultan, on the edge of Chinatown. REXKL is the old cinema building in front of you; its name has kept the Rex of the cinema it used to be. Stand back on the far pavement and look at the frontage. You're looking at a survivor of several fires.

The Rex Theatre first opened in 1947, designed by the architect James Robert Vethavanam in the Streamline Moderne style — that sleek, rounded, speedboat look of the late thirties and forties. In 1972 it burned down, during a burglary that went wrong. It was rebuilt in 1976 as a single-screen cinema with eleven hundred seats.

Then the multiplexes came, and the Rex closed in the early 2000s. It became a backpackers' hostel, and in 2007 another fire gutted it.

In 2019 it was reborn as REXKL, a cultural centre for Malaysian creative businesses, the work of architects Shin Chang and Shin Tseng. They chose to work with the old cinema's structure rather than erase it, so the building still reads as what it was.

The gift detail is upstairs. The bookshop, run by BookXcess, occupies the old cinema hall, and its shelves are arranged across the stepped floor where the seats used to be. So you browse downhill, towards where the screen would have been, as if the books were the audience.

[beat]

And here's the thing. A city like this usually does one of two things with a burnt-out cinema: knocks it down, or restores it so perfectly you'd never guess what happened. REXKL chose a third way — keep the damage, fill the place with people, and let the building tell its own story.

When you leave, walk a couple of minutes south-west and you'll reach Jalan Petaling, the old heart of Chinatown, with its covered market. Look for the green roof over the street.
