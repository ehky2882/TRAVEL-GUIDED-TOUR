According to the people who run it, this little house was once home to prison warders.

Jah lahn Galloway, on the slope between Chinatown and Boo kit Bin tahng. Vee See Ar is the two storey house in front of you, painted black. You'll know it by the people at the outside tables, almost all of them with a coffee.

The building dates from nineteen twenty seven. It stands near the site of the old Poo doo Jail, the great walled prison that has since been demolished, and the cafe's own story is that this street was where many of the warders lived. That's their account rather than a historian's, so take it in the spirit offered. What isn't in doubt is that the house had been through a good deal before twenty thirteen, when Andrew Lee and his team moved in and turned it into a cafe.

Go inside and look at what they kept. The space is high, light and plain, with the old proportions left alone. Much of its original character, the bones of a pre war house, has simply been allowed to stay, while the coffee bar and kitchen were slotted in around it.

The coffee itself is the point. Vee See Ar belongs to the generation of cafes that gave Kuala Lumpur a specialty coffee scene, at a time when the default order in this city was still a sweet, strong koh pee from a coffee shop.

What's quietly radical here is the choice of building. A decade or so ago, an old house like this was more likely to be knocked down than restored. Vee See Ar made the argument that a pre war house, kept largely as it was, is exactly what people want to sit in, and these days the city is full of cafes making the same argument.

If you're in the mood for something stronger later, Pah hit, a gin bar in a nineteen twenties shophouse, is barely a minute away on Jah lahn Sin Chew Kee. Take a stroll round the back streets first, though; this quiet pocket between two busy districts is easy to miss.
