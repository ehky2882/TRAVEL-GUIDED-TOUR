KL Forest Eco Park

This patch of rainforest has been protected since 1906, and it's right in the middle of the city.

You're at the KL Forest Eco Park, on Bukit Nanas, the hill below the KL Tower. There are two ways in, one from Jalan Raja Chulan and one from the KL Tower grounds. Either way, within a minute of leaving the road, you'll be under tall trees, with the traffic noise fading behind you.

Bukit Nanas means Pineapple Hill. In 1906 the colonial government gazetted seventeen and a half hectares of forest here as a reserve, which makes it one of the oldest permanent forest reserves in the country. Development has nibbled away at it since, including the KL Tower, and about nine hectares remain. But what remains is the real thing: lowland tropical rainforest, the kind that covered this whole valley before the tin miners came.

The gift is the canopy walk. A series of suspended walkways takes you up among the treetops, high above the forest floor. Up there, look through the leaves and you'll see office towers and the KL Tower itself, peering in from all sides.

Then look at the trees. Some of them are enormous, with buttress roots spreading out like walls at their base. That's what a tropical tree does when the soil is thin: it braces itself sideways.

[beat]

What's worth knowing, standing here, is how unusual this is. Most cities keep their parks, but few keep a piece of the original forest they were built in. This is what Kuala Lumpur was before it was Kuala Lumpur — and it's still here, in the middle of the skyline.

Before you leave, take one of the trails down to the bottom of the hill, rather than going back the way you came. Look for the moment the forest stops, and the city starts again; it's almost a straight line.
