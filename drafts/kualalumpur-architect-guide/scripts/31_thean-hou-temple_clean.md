# Thean Hou Temple

- **Google Maps name:** Thean Hou Temple
- **Category:** VISIT
- **Coordinates (WGS-84):** 3.121843, 101.687645

A sea goddess, worshipped on a hilltop, in a city that is nowhere near the sea.

You're on Robson Heights, south of the city centre, at the gateway to Thean Hou Temple. In front of you is a broad flight of steps leading up to a temple of six tiers, with sweeping red and gold roofs, and lanterns strung overhead.

The temple was built by the Hainanese community of Kuala Lumpur, and it's run by the Selangor and Federal Territory Hainan Association. Construction began in 1981, finished in 1987, and the temple formally opened in 1989.

It's dedicated to Mazu, or Tian Hou, the Queen of Heaven, protector of seafarers. That makes sense when you know who built it. Hainan is an island, and its people were sailors and traders long before they crossed the South China Sea to settle in Malaya. They brought the goddess who had kept them safe at sea.

Walk up to the prayer hall on the fourth level. There are three altars: the goddess of mercy, Guanyin, on the right, Mazu in the centre, and the goddess of the waterfront, Shui Wei Sheng Niang, on the left.

Then look outwards. The terraces give you a fine view of the city skyline from the south, and on the hillside opposite are large statues of the twelve animals of the Chinese zodiac. Find yours.

[beat]

What's worth knowing is that this temple is a working community building, not a museum. People come to pray, to have their fortunes told, and even to register their marriages here. It's one of the few places in Kuala Lumpur where religion, community and a very good view are all on offer at once.

Before you leave, walk to the edge of the upper terrace and find the Petronas Towers on the skyline to the north. And come back at dusk if you can; the temple, red against the darkening sky, is at its best.
