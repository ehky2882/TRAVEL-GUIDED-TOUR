Only a few years before this cafe opened, much of this street was abandoned shophouses with rotting shutters.

You're on Jah lahn Toon Aitch Ess Lee, number thirty, in the oldest part of the city, a short walk from Meh dahn Pah sar, the old market square. Lokal is the cafe at street level. Look up at the facades along this row: these are colonial era shophouses, two storeys or three, with tall shuttered windows on the upper floors.

Lokal opened in twenty twelve, founded by the husband and wife team of Eng Ping Hoh, a filmmaker, and Cheryl Sah mahd, an actress. It shares its building with a hostel called Back Home, which restored four of these old shophouses into rooms, dormitories and a courtyard. The cafe is the hostel's kitchen, but it was always meant as a neighbourhood place too, somewhere for the office workers round here to sit down.

Go in and look at the walls and ceiling. The original brickwork has been stripped back and whitewashed rather than plastered over, and the old timber beams overhead have been given the same treatment, with a new timber floor below. It's restoration done lightly: clean up what's there, add as little as possible.

What I find interesting is the pairing. A cafe gives a heritage building a reason to have its doors open all day, and a hostel brings in people who would never otherwise sleep in the oldest part of town. Together they did more for this street than a preservation order ever could. The same founder went on to create Klo ee, the design hotel on Boo kit Bin tahng.

When you leave, walk the short distance to Meh dahn Pah sar, the square where Kuala Lumpur's first market stood. Look for the little Art Deco clock tower in the middle, then carry on down Jah lahn Toon Aitch Ess Lee to the Sin See See Yah temple, the oldest Taoist temple in the city.
