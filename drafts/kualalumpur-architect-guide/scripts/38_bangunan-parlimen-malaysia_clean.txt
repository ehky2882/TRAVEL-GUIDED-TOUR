Bangunan Parlimen Malaysia (Parliament House)

The tower of Malaysia's parliament is wrapped in a concrete grille, and that grille is doing two jobs at once.

You're on the approach to Parliament House, on a hill in the Lake Gardens, near the National Monument. You may not be able to go in, but you don't need to. Find the tall white tower, and in front of it, the long, low block where the two chambers meet.

It was designed by William Ivor Shipley, a British architect in the Public Works Department, and opened on 2 November 1963, just weeks after Malaysia was formed. The tower is seventeen storeys and seventy-seven metres high, and it holds the offices of members of parliament. The low block beneath holds the two houses: the Dewan Rakyat and the Dewan Negara.

Have a look at the tower's skin. It's covered in precast panels, sunbreakers, which were lifted into place in 1963. The facade is in two layers, like a grille, and the effect is to keep direct sunlight off the windows while letting air move through. It's also meant to give the structure strength.

That double job — climate and structure — is the gift detail. In the tropics, a sealed glass box overheats. A building that shades itself is simply sensible. Here the shading becomes the whole character of the building.

[beat]

What's worth knowing is what this building represents. It went up in the years between independence and the formation of Malaysia, when the country's new institutions needed new buildings. Its architects chose modernism, not a borrowed eastern style, and made it work for the climate. It's a building of confident, practical optimism.

Before you go, walk down the hill to the National Monument, a few minutes away, and then look back. The tower stands over the trees, and from there you can see how its pattern of sunbreakers reads from a distance — like a screen pulled across the sky.
