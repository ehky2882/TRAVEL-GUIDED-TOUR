# KLCC Park

The man who designed this park died before it was planted.

You're in KLCC Park, at the foot of the Petronas Towers. Find a spot near the lake, where you can see both the water and the towers above you.

The park was designed by Roberto Burle Marx, the great Brazilian landscape architect. It was his last work; he died in 1994, and the park was created in the mid-nineties. It covers about fifty acres, and its planting is deliberately local: some nineteen hundred indigenous trees, sixty-six kinds of palm, and twenty-three mature trees moved here from the old Selangor Turf Club, the racecourse that once stood on this site.

Have a look at the paths and the water. Burle Marx loved flowing curves, and you'll find few straight lines here. The lake is called Lake Symphony, and after dark the fountains put on a show set to music. There's also a jogging track and a children's playground, so the park is in constant use.

Now, look up at the towers. They were designed by the Argentine-American architect César Pelli, and their floor plan is based on the Rub el Hizb, the eight-pointed star made of two overlapping squares, a symbol from Islamic art. Pelli added rounded sections between the points to give more space, which is why the towers look both faceted and soft. At the forty-first and forty-second floors, a double-decker bridge links the two, about a hundred and seventy metres above the ground.

[beat]

What's worth knowing is how the two parts depend on each other. The towers are all about height and symbolism. The park is about shade, water and somewhere to sit. Without the park, the towers would be a spectacle. With it, they're part of the city.

Before you leave, walk round to the far side of the lake, away from the shopping centre. Find the spot where the towers are reflected in the water, and wait for the light to change. It's the best view of them in Kuala Lumpur.
