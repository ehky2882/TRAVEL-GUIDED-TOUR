Books Kubrick Hahkoh zahkee

A bookshop named after a film director, because it opened in the year in the title of one of his best known films: two thousand and one.

You should be on the street just west of jay are Hahkoh zahkee Station, a minute from the exit. Books Kubrick is the bookshop at street level, with a café and gallery on the floor above.

The founder, Meenohroo Oh ee, opened his first shop in April two thousand and one, at the age of thirty nine, on Kayahkee dohree, the tree lined street near Tenjin. He named it after Stanley Kubrick, who directed two thousand and one: A Space Odyssey, because he admired films that managed to be both art and popular. That first shop was tiny, about forty three square metres, and it closed in July twenty twenty six after twenty five years. This one, opened in two thousand and eight, carries on.

Have a look at the shelves. Oh ee orders his books directly from the publishers, by phone, rather than taking whatever the wholesalers send. New titles start near the door and move to themed shelves later, grouped so that one book leads you to the next. There are magazines and picture books, philosophy and manga. It's meant to suit someone who finds a big bookshop overwhelming, and still satisfy someone who reads everything.

Upstairs, the café and gallery hosts talks and exhibitions, and since twenty sixteen there's been a bakery as well.

And here's the thing. Small bookshops across Japan are closing, and this one's own first shop went this year. Oh ee's answer has always been to make the shop a place where things happen. In two thousand and six he helped start Bookoo ohkah, a book festival for the whole city, which is still going.

Before you leave, climb the stairs to see what's on in the gallery. Then walk a few minutes to Hahkoh zahkee Shrine, the great old shrine that gives this district its name.
