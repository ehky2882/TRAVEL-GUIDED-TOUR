There was a primary school on this spot from eighteen seventy three until twenty fourteen, and one of its buildings is still standing in the corner.

Find the open plaza at the foot of the glass tower, the lawn and the play area with the stage beside it. The tower is in front of you, twenty five storeys of glass. Now look for a lower, older building in one corner of the site. That's Dyemyoh Elementary School, built in reinforced concrete in nineteen twenty nine.

Take the school first. It closed when the number of children in central Fookoo oka fell away, and rather than clear the site, the city kept the nineteen twenty nine building and turned it into a centre for start ups. Inside, it still has its big central staircase. The plaza you're standing in was the schoolyard, and it's kept open as a garden. Have a look at the play equipment: it's modelled on the kohmah eenoo, the lion dogs that guard Shinto shrines.

The tower is Fookoo oka Dyemyoh Garden City, designed by Koomay Sekkay, and The Ritz Carlton takes the top floors, with its club and spa right at the summit. It opened in June twenty twenty three. The rooms look out either over Oh hoh ree Park or across Hahkahtah Bay.

Inside, the designers took their cue from Hahkahtah ohree, the yarn dyed silk weaving this city is known for, and worked local craftspeople's screens, textiles and paintings into the rooms. Even the terrace bar, called Bay, is meant to recall the ships in Hahkahtah Bay.

What I like about this corner is the deal it strikes. A luxury tower was always going to go up somewhere in Tenjin. This one paid for it by keeping the playground public and the old school standing.

Before you go, step round to the school building's entrance and look through the door at that staircase. Then walk a few minutes west, into the lanes of Dyemyoh proper, where the small shops and cafés are the neighbourhood the school once served.
