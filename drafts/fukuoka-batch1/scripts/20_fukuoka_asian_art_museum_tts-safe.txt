Fookoo oka Asian Art Museum

Most art museums tell the story of modern art from Paris or New York. This one tells it from Asia.

Find the Hahkahtah River rain building, beside the Hahkahtah River at the north end of the Kahwahbahtah arcade. The museum isn't at street level. It's high up in the building, so look for the signs and take the lift.

It opened in March nineteen ninety nine, built on a collection that the city had been gathering for twenty years at the Fookoo oka Art Museum. The aim was unusual: to collect, keep and show modern and contemporary art from across Asia, systematically, country by country. There are very few museums anywhere with the same purpose.

Have a look for some of the best known pieces. Fahng Lee jun's paintings of shaven headed young men from China. The Lwoh Brothers' I Love Beijing Tyen ahn men, all bright lacquer and pop imagery. Kim Chahng yol's painting of a single drop of water, from Korea, almost unbearably precise. And Tahwahn Doochahnee's Battle of Mahrah, from Thailand.

What's worth knowing is why Fookoo oka. The city has always looked across the sea to Korea and China rather than back to Tokyo. Boo sahn is closer than Osaka. A museum of Asian art here isn't an exotic idea. It's the city describing its own neighbourhood.

Before you leave, find a window and look out over the river towards Nahkahsoo. Then walk down to the water and along to Nahkahsoo, or south through the Kahwahbahtah arcade towards Kooshee dah Shrine.
