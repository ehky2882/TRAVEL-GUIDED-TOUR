Kahwahbahtah Shopping Arcade

Over a hundred and thirty shops sit under one roof here, and a towering festival float stands in the middle of them all year round.

Find the small square partway along the covered arcade, between Kooshee dah Shrine and the river. You'll know it by the float, a towering decorated display of figures from legend, with a little zen zye shop beside it.

The arcade runs for about four hundred metres, from Hahkahtah River rain at the north end to Canal City at the south, and it's shared by two traders' associations, Kahmee Kahwahbahtah and Kahwahbahtah Choo oh. Shops have traded along this street for more than a hundred and forty years.

Have a look at the float first. It's one of the decorated floats of the Hahkahtah Gee on Yahmah kahsah, the festival dedicated to Kooshee dah Shrine every July, and this one is kept on show here all year.

Then the zen zye. It's a hot, very sweet red bean soup with two rice cakes, grilled to order, and it has been billed as the sweetest zen zye in Japan. It closed once, and the traders reopened it because the neighbourhood asked them to. It's open from Friday to Sunday, and every day during the festival.

Along the arcade, look for Kahdohtah Chohcheen, a family lantern maker with around a hundred and twenty years behind it, where you can paint your own small lantern, and Mahsooyah, which sells Hahkahtah dolls by about sixty different artists.

What I like about Kahwahbahtah is that it's still doing what an old shopping street is meant to do. It sells you lanterns and dolls, yes, but it also keeps a festival float on show and brings a sweet shop back from the dead.

Kooshee dah Shrine, where the festival belongs, is just off the arcade. Or turn north to the river, where the Asian Art Museum sits on top of Hahkahtah River rain.
