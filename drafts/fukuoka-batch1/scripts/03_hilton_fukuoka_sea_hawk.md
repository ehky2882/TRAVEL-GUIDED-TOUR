## Clean script

**Hilton Fukuoka Sea Hawk**

On a map it looks like any other hotel. From the bay, it's meant to look like a ship.

Find a spot where you can take in the whole building, with the great dome of the baseball stadium close by. The hotel is the tall tower beside it, thirty-six storeys facing out to sea.

The architect was César Pelli, the Argentine-American behind some of the world's best-known towers, and the building is modelled on a great ship heading out into Hakata Bay. See if you can find the bow. It's a little literal, if you ask me, but on a clear evening it rather works.

It opened in April 1995 as the Sea Hawk Hotel and Resort, part of a waterfront development built around the dome, which is home to the Fukuoka SoftBank Hawks. Hence the name. It stands a hundred and forty-three metres tall, with more than a thousand rooms, which made it the largest Hilton in the Asia Pacific region when the brand took it over.

That takeover is part of the story. The original operator ran into money trouble, Japan Airlines' hotel arm took over the management in 2005, and Hilton followed in 2010. The building outlasted all of them.

What's worth knowing, standing here, is that this shoreline is reclaimed land. The hotel, the dome and the promenade were all part of one idea: that Fukuoka should turn and face the sea.

[beat]

If you've time, walk through the hotel's glass atrium, which rises several storeys over the restaurant. Then head west along the waterfront towards Momochi Seaside Park and its beach, with Fukuoka Tower rising behind it. The view back at the ship is best from there.
