Canal City Hahkahtah

They built a river inside a shopping centre, and called the whole thing a theatre.

Go down to the lowest level, to the water's edge. A curving canal runs through the middle of the complex, about a hundred and eighty metres long, lined with balconies, walkways and bridges in bold colours, climbing up on either side.

Canal City opened in April nineteen ninety six on the site of an old Kahnaybo textile factory. It was built by the developer Fookoo oka Jeeshoh, and designed by the American architect Jon Jerdee, who made his name turning shopping centres into places to wander. The concept was a theatre for the city.

Have a look at how it works. Shops, cinemas and theatres are stacked above you on several levels, and the canal pulls you round the curve so you keep walking and keep seeing more. There are no railings at the water's edge, and the canal is kept shallow, about seventy centimetres deep, so children can touch it.

Then the fountains. At regular intervals they burst up out of the canal, as high as twenty metres, with music and lights, and the balconies fill with people watching.

What's worth knowing, standing here, is how influential this was. Jerdee's idea of the shopping centre as a street, a stage and a day out was widely copied. Some people find it too much, and I understand why. But it changed what Fookoo oka thought a city centre could feel like.

Wait for a fountain show if you can. Then leave by the north side and walk up the Kahwahbahtah arcade towards Kooshee dah Shrine, the old way through Hahkahtah.
