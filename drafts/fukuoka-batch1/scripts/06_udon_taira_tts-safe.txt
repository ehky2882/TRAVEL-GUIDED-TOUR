Oodon Tie rah

Ramen gets the fame, but plenty of Hahkahtah people would choose a bowl of oodon.

Oodon Tie rah is a small shop on the ground floor of a block of flats in Soomee yoshee, south of Canal City. If it's lunchtime, the queue on the pavement will tell you you're in the right place.

The shop spent forty five years by Hahkahtah Station before a rebuilding scheme moved it here, and the queue came too. It's open at lunch only, from just after eleven until three, and it's closed on Sundays.

Order the gobboh ten oodon. Gobboh is burdock root, sliced and fried in tempura batter, and here it comes in crisp strips laid over the bowl. The noodles are the part that surprises people. Hahkahtah oodon is soft, flat and slightly chewy, nothing like the firm, springy noodles of Kahgahwah that most of Japan thinks of as proper oodon. The broth, made with dried sardines, is light and a little sweet.

If you're still hungry, add a kahsheewah ohneegeeree on the side. It's a rice ball cooked with chicken and burdock, and the two together are about as Hahkahtah as lunch gets.

The interesting bit is the softness. Visitors sometimes take it for overcooking. It isn't. It's a style built for a city of merchants who wanted something gentle and quick, and a shop like this one keeps it exactly as it was.

From here, Canal City is a ten minute walk north. And if you'd rather stay with the old Hahkahtah, carry on beyond it to the Kahwahbahtah arcade, where there's another local institution waiting: zen zye, a very sweet red bean soup with grilled rice cakes.
