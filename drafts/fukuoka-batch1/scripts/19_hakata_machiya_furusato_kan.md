## Clean script

**Hakata Machiya Furusato-kan**

The wooden house in front of you was a weaver's home and workshop, built in the Meiji years and carried here to be saved.

Find the corner across the street from Kushida Shrine where three buildings sit together. One of them is a traditional wooden townhouse. That's the machiya. Beside it are the exhibition building and a newer shop and café.

Start with the house. It was built in the middle of the Meiji era for a family of Hakata-ori weavers, the yarn-dyed silk the city is known for, and it was both their home and their workshop. It was later moved here and restored, and it's now protected by the city. Look at the narrow street front and the long depth behind it. That shape is how merchant towns like Hakata packed people in.

Next door, the exhibition building tells the story of old Hakata, its festivals, its crafts and its everyday life, and upstairs you'll find tools from merchant households and craftspeople working at traditional Hakata crafts.

You can also have a go yourself. Almost every day there's a chance to paint a Hakata doll, or a papier-mâché toy, or a spinning top, and there's a free guided walk round the neighbourhood every day.

And here's the thing. A great deal of the old town was lost to war and to rebuilding. A place like this keeps not just one house but a way of living in one.

[beat]

The newer building, opened in spring 2025, has a shop called hakatakara, which sells local food and crafts, and a café. Then cross the road to Kushida Shrine, or walk into the Kawabata arcade just behind it.
