Yahnahgee bahshee Rengoh Market

They call this lane Hahkahtah's kitchen, and it has been feeding the city for the best part of a century.

Find the entrance to the covered market, close to the Yahnahgee bridge over the Nahkah River. A narrow covered lane about a hundred metres long, lined with counters on both sides.

The market grew up in the early Shoh wah years, in the late twenties and thirties, and the story usually told is that it began with one man selling fish from a handcart. Others set up around him, and after the war they joined together into the market you see now.

Have a look at the counters first. Around thirty five businesses share the arcade: fishmongers, greengrocers, drink shops and places to eat. Professional cooks buy here as well as households, which is the surest sign a market is the real thing.

Some of them have a few seats and will feed you on the spot, which is the best way to see the place.

What I like about Yanagibashi is that it hasn't been prettied up for visitors. The business is still selling food to people who are going to cook it tonight. It's a working market that happens to welcome you, not a show.

Come in the morning, when the deliveries are being sorted and the arcade is at its busiest. Then walk down to the riverbank and follow the Nahkah River north. In about fifteen minutes you'll reach Nahkahsoo, where in the evening the street food stalls set up along the water.
