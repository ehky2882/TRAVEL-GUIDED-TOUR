Reck Coffee Yahkoo een Ekee mah eh

This café began as a van parked on a Yahkoo een street corner, and its owner went on to finish second in the world.

The shop is a five minute walk from Neeshee tetsoo Yahkoo een Station, at street level. Look for the Reck sign.

Reck Coffee started in two thousand and eight as a mobile coffee truck here in Yahkoo een, run by Yoshee kahzoo Eewahsay and Ohsahmoo Keetah zoh eh. In twenty ten they gave up the truck and opened this shop, their first proper café. It's still the original.

Eewahsay is the reason people travel here. He came third in the Japan Barista Championship in twenty eleven, won it in twenty fourteen and again in twenty fifteen, and in twenty sixteen finished second at the World Barista Championship.

Have a look at the menu. The house latte is the signature, and there are tarts, sablays and cakes. Since twenty eighteen the company has roasted its own beans, and it now has shops across Fookoo oka, in Tokyo and in Taiwan.

What I like about this place is that it has never lost the van's habits. It opens at eight and stays open until midnight, and later still at the weekend. Eewahsay has said that the goal isn't to keep expanding but to get good coffee into as many hands as possible, which is a very truck shaped ambition.

Yahkoo een is one of the most pleasant neighbourhoods in Fookoo oka to walk. From here, walk about ten minutes west to three bee Potters, the homeware shop, or south into Sheeroh gahnay.
