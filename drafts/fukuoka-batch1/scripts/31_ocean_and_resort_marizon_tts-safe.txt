Ocean and Resort Marry zon

You're standing on the sea. Not beside it, on it: this whole deck sits on piles driven into the floor of Hahkahtah Bay.

Walk out from the beach to the far end of the deck, where the ferry ticket office and landing are. The water should be on three sides of you, with the bay opening out to the north, and behind you, back towards land, the white sand of Mohmohchee beach and the tall glass spire of Fookoo oka Tower.

The land behind you is younger than it looks. It was reclaimed from the sea in the early eighties, and in nineteen eighty nine it hosted the Asia Pacific Exposition, the city's big coming out party. Marry zon opened that same year. The idea, first sketched in nineteen eighty six, was a promenade built out over the water, with shops and restaurants on it, and the city went into partnership with local companies to build it. When it opened, there was a wedding chapel, an Italian restaurant, a marine sports shop and a ferry landing.

Have a look at the deck itself. It was meant as the city's front porch onto the sea, and for a while it worked: Fookoo oka named it one of the treasures it wanted to hand on to the twenty first century. Then the shoppers drifted away. By two thousand and three the company that ran it was being wound up.

What saved it was weddings. A wedding company took it over, spent around seven hundred million yen on a full renovation in two thousand and four, and turned Marry zon into a wedding venue, with a cathedral style chapel and a chapel looking straight out to sea. Within a few years, weddings alone were bringing in four times what the whole complex had earned before. In twenty ten it was one of the first three places in Japan to win an Asian Townscape Award.

And here's the thing. Plenty of waterfront schemes from those years were simply pulled down when the money ran out. This one found a better reason to exist, and the public still gets the walk out over the water for free.

From here, the Oomee nahkah Line ferry crosses the bay to Oomee noh nahkah meechee in about twenty minutes, if you fancy a boat ride. Or walk back to the beach and up Fookoo oka Tower, where the view from the top shows the whole deck laid out on the water below you.
