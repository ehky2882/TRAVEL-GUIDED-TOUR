Tenjin Business Center

Look at the corners of this building. They look as if they're melting.

Stand on the corner of May jee dohree and look up at the tall office building in front of you. Glass, mostly, but the corners are broken up into small steps, like pixels on a screen.

This is the Tenjin Business Center, completed in twenty twenty one and designed by oh em ay, the international practice founded by Rem Coalhahs. The partner in charge was Shoh hay Sheegay mahtsoo, who was born in Fookoo oka. He called it a remarkable experience to build in his hometown.

Have a look at those pixelated corners. The architects meant them to break the big block down to a human scale and soften the line between the street and the offices. Some have compared them to an ice cube melting. At the bottom, the soffits under the stepped corners are lit up, so the base of the building glows at night.

It's the first building finished in the Tenjin Big Bang, the city's long plan to rebuild the centre of Tenjin. Height limits here had long been tight, with the airport so close, and relaxing them under a national special zone was what let new towers like this go up. In Sheegay mahtsoo's words, the Big Bang signifies a starting point.

What's worth knowing is that these offices house start ups, a big Japanese electronics company and a Google development team. Fookoo oka has set out to be Japan's start up city, and this building was its first statement in glass.

A second tower, the Tenjin Business Center Two, opened next door in August twenty twenty six, also by Sheegay mahtsoo. Walk round to compare the two. Then go down a level to the underground mall below Wahtahnahbay dohree, which is fifty years old this year.
