Tenjin Underground Mall

Underneath one of Fookoo oka's busiest streets is a little piece of nineteenth century Europe, built in nineteen seventy six.

Take any of the stairways down from Wahtahnahbay dohree into the mall. You'll find yourself in a long, dim passage, with stone and brick underfoot, an ornate patterned ceiling overhead, and brightly lit shopfronts on either side.

Tenjin Cheekah guy opened in September nineteen seventy six under Wahtahnahbay dohree, Tenjin's main street. It now runs for about six hundred metres, with more than a hundred and fifty shops, and it was extended north in nineteen ninety four and south in two thousand and five, when the Nahnah koomah subway line opened.

Have a look at the floor and the ceiling. The designers took their cue from the streets of nineteenth century Europe, with stone and brick paving in a southern European style and a ceiling of curling arabesque patterns.

Then notice the light. The concept was a theatre: the walkway is the audience, kept in shadow, and the shopfronts are the stage, brightly lit. When it opened, people complained it was too dark, so extra lights were added. Later, someone decided the original idea had been right, and they were taken away again.

What I like about it is that it's a fantasy, built at a time when Japan was copying Europe without embarrassment, and it has kept its nerve for fifty years. It would be easy to modernise it into a bright white shopping corridor. Nobody has.

This year the mall marks its fiftieth anniversary, and a monument has been unveiled to celebrate. Walk the whole length from north to south, and then come up at the far end near Tenjin Meenahmee, a short walk from the cafés of Hahroo yoshee and the river.
