## Clean script

**Island City Central Park Grin Grin**

The name means two things at once: green, green, and round and round.

Stand on the path beside the long pond in Island City Central Park, and look for three low, rounded shells rising from the grass, part covered in plants and part in glass. Walkways climb up and over them. That's Grin Grin.

It was designed by Toyo Ito, who later won the Pritzker Prize, and finished in 2005. It was the first building on Island City, an artificial island of about four hundred hectares in Hakata Bay, and it sits at the heart of a park of more than fifteen hectares.

Have a look at the shells. They're curving concrete, flowing from one into the next, strung along the pond like pearls. Patches of the roof are glazed in a scale-like pattern, letting light into the greenhouse below, where there are subtropical plants, and the rest is covered in planting, so the building disappears into the landscape.

Then go up. The walkways take you onto the roof, over the curves and down again, so you can walk on top of the building as if it were a hill.

What's worth knowing is the idea behind it. Ito wanted a building that is as much park as it is building, where you can't quite say where one ends and the other begins. Twenty years on, with the planting grown in, it has become exactly that.

[beat]

Inside there are workshops and gardening classes, and admission is a hundred yen. It's closed on Tuesdays. Afterwards, walk the length of the park, or cross back towards Kashii, where Nexus World holds six architects' visions of how to live.
