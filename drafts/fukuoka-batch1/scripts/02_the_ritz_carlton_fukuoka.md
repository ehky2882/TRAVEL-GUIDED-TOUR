## Clean script

**The Ritz-Carlton, Fukuoka**

There was a primary school on this spot from 1873 until 2014, and one of its buildings is still standing in the corner.

Find the open plaza at the foot of the glass tower, the lawn and the play area with the stage beside it. The tower is in front of you, twenty-five storeys of glass. Now look for a lower, older building in one corner of the site. That's Daimyo Elementary School, built in reinforced concrete in 1929.

Take the school first. It closed when the number of children in central Fukuoka fell away, and rather than clear the site, the city kept the 1929 building and turned it into a centre for start-ups. Inside, it still has its big central staircase. The plaza you're standing in was the schoolyard, and it's kept open as a garden. Have a look at the play equipment: it's modelled on the koma-inu, the lion-dogs that guard Shinto shrines.

The tower is Fukuoka Daimyo Garden City, designed by Kume Sekkei, and The Ritz-Carlton takes the top floors, with its club and spa right at the summit. It opened in June 2023. The rooms look out either over Ohori Park or across Hakata Bay.

Inside, the designers took their cue from Hakata-ori, the yarn-dyed silk weaving this city is known for, and worked local craftspeople's screens, textiles and paintings into the rooms. Even the terrace bar, called Bay, is meant to recall the ships in Hakata Bay.

What I like about this corner is the deal it strikes. A luxury tower was always going to go up somewhere in Tenjin. This one paid for it by keeping the playground public and the old school standing.

[beat]

Before you go, step round to the school building's entrance and look through the door at that staircase. Then walk a few minutes west, into the lanes of Daimyo proper, where the small shops and cafés are the neighbourhood the school once served.
