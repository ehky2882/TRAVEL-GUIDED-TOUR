Eesheemoorah Mahn say doh Main Store

If you've ever wondered why Japan has a day for giving marshmallows back, the answer is in this shop.

The main shop stands in Soozahkee mahchee, on the old Hahkahtah side of the river, at a corner the whole city knows in July.

It was founded on Christmas Day, nineteen oh five, by Zentahroh Eesheemoorah. His family were carpenters who built shrines and temples, but his health was poor, so he turned to making sweets fit to offer to the gods and the Buddha. He started out renting the ground floor of a house belonging to the family of Kahwahkahmee Ohtoh jeeroh, the actor and showman.

Have a look at the Tsooroo nohkoh. A soft white marshmallow wrapped round a sweet egg yolk bean paste, shaped like an egg. It began as a way of using up egg whites left over from another sweet made only with yolks, sold at first in real eggshells. When marshmallow making arrived from abroad, it took the shape you see now.

Now the story. In nineteen seventy seven the company president, Zengoh Eesheemoorah, read a letter in a magazine: a reader complained that men never gave anything back on Valentine's Day. So in nineteen seventy eight the shop proposed Marshmallow Day, on the fourteenth of March, when men would wrap the chocolate they'd received in marshmallow, a symbol of their own kindness, and give it back. Others claim the idea too, but in Hahkahtah this is White Day's origin.

Before you go, look outside. This corner is the turning point of the Hahkahtah Gee on Yahmah kahsah festival, where the racing floats swing round, and on the fifteenth of July the street is packed. Inside, ask for the Tsooroo nohkoh noh Kohnohkoh, made only here, with fresh marshmallow and a shelf life of thirty minutes.
