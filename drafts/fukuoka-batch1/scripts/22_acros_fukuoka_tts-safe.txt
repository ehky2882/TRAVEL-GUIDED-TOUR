Ackross Fookoo oka

From one side, this is an office building. From the other, it's a hill.

Stand on the lawn of Tenjin Central Park and face north. In front of you, the park doesn't stop at the building. It carries on up a long, stepped slope of trees and shrubs, terrace above terrace, all the way to the roof sixty metres above you.

The building is Ackross Fookoo oka, which stands for Asian Crossroads Over the Sea. It opened in April nineteen ninety five on the site of the old prefectural government office, and the idea came from the Argentine architect Ehmeeleeoh Ahmbahss, with Neehon Sekkay and Tahkay nahkah doing the detailed design. Look at what that achieves: the city got its big public building, and the park kept its green, laid over the building's back.

Have a look at the planting. It was designed to look like a natural mountain, and locals call it Ackross yahmah, Acros mountain. It now holds around two hundred species, and over thirty years the trees have grown up so that, from a distance, you'd struggle to see a building at all.

Inside are offices, shops and the Fookoo oka Symphony Hall, which seats nearly two thousand people.

And here's the thing. This was built in the mid nineties, long before green buildings were fashionable, and it now reads almost as a prediction. It's quite something to see what thirty years of growth has done.

You can climb it. The step garden is open every day except over New Year, free, from nine in the morning, though it closes in rain. At weekends the rooftop lookout opens too, with views across to Hahkahtah Bay. Afterwards, look for the old French style guest house at the river end of the park.
