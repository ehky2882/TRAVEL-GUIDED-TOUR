mahnoo coffee Hahroo yoshee

This little café is open until midnight, every day of the year, which tells you something about Hahroo yoshee.

The shop is on the ground floor of a tower on Wahtahnahbay dohree, on the Hahroo yoshee side of the Nahkah River. Inside, about eighteen seats, close together.

mahnoo coffee opened in Fookoo oka in two thousand and three, and it has grown with the city since, to a handful of shops, each with its own character. Hahroo yoshee is the late one. The neighbourhood is a mix of small bars, restaurants and flats, a few minutes from Nahkahsoo and the river, and the café stays open from nine in the morning to midnight to match it.

Have a look at the menu. Espresso and filter coffee, and the company has its own roastery café, Koojeerah, in Sheeroh gahnay, a little way south.

Then there's what happens to the leftovers. The company turns its spent coffee grounds and chaff, the papery skin that comes off the beans in roasting, into an organic fertiliser called Mahnoo ah. In twenty eighteen it made about six tonnes, and sold the lot. The firm calls its idea a cup of universe: the cup as a place where people, the city and culture meet.

That sounds grand for a café this size, but it's rather the point. A neighbourhood coffee shop can be part of how a city works, from the morning commute to what ends up back in the soil.

From here, cross the Hahroo yoshee Bridge towards Nahkahsoo and you'll reach the riverside street stalls in five minutes. Or walk a few minutes north to Hotel Eel Pahlahtsoh and its red stone front.
