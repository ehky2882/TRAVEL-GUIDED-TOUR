Kooshee dah Shrine

Every July, the festival that defines Hahkahtah is held in honour of this shrine, and one of its floats never leaves.

Come in through the main gate from the street, with the shrine's halls ahead of you. Look around the grounds for a towering festival float, crowded with painted warriors and gods.

Locals call this Oh kooshee dah sahn. It's the guardian shrine of old Hahkahtah, the merchant town, and tradition says it was founded in seven fifty seven. The buildings you see date from fifteen eighty seven, when the warlord Tohyohtohmee Heeday yohshee rebuilt Hahkahtah after years of war.

Have a look at the float first. It's a kahzahree yahmah, a decorated float of the Hahkahtah Gee on Yahmah kahsah, the festival held in honour of this shrine from the first to the fifteenth of July, which goes back about eight hundred years and is now on Unesco's list of intangible heritage. One is kept on show here all year round, so visitors never miss it entirely.

Then look up as you pass through the gate. On the ceiling is a large round board marked with the animals of the Chinese zodiac. It's an ay hoh bahn, and it shows the year's lucky direction.

The great ginkgo tree by the entrance is around a thousand years old, and it's protected as a natural monument by the prefecture.

What's worth knowing, standing here, is that the shrine is the city's living calendar. The Yahmah kahsah is held for it in July, the Dontahkoo procession sets off from here in May, and the Oh koonchee festival follows in October.

If you come in early February, look out for the huge smiling Ohtah fookoo mask at the gate for Setsoo boon. Otherwise, step out into the Kahwahbahtah arcade next door, where another float stands on display.
