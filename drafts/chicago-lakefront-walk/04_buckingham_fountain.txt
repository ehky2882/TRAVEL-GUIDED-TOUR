ATLAS — CHICAGO
Walk 3: The Lakefront — Millennium Park to Museum Campus
Segment 04 — Buckingham Fountain
Clean version

---

Wide, low, pink granite, four basins stacked like a wedding cake, and behind it nothing but lake.

Stand on the west side, looking east, so the water is beyond it. Notice what the fountain doesn't do: it doesn't rise up in front of the view. It spreads sideways instead, and the whole composition sits low enough that from here you can see straight over it to the water, and none of that is accidental. Edward Bennett designed it as the centerpiece of a park where a building was not permitted and a view was the entire point, and he solved the problem by making the monument out of the one material that can be enormous without blocking anything.

When it runs, the center jet goes a hundred and fifty feet straight up — which is the whole trick of the thing. It takes up as much room as a tower for twenty minutes an hour, and then puts every bit of it back.

It runs spring through fall and it's dry the rest of the year, which is worth knowing before you plan around it.

Kate Buckingham gave it to the city in 1927 in memory of her brother Clarence, who had been a trustee of the museum you just walked past. So the two things standing in this park — the one building that got permission and the one monument nobody had to give permission for — are connected by one family.

*[beat]*

And she did something most donors don't. Along with the fountain she left an endowment to pay for keeping it running, in perpetuity, on the reasonable assumption that a city which accepts a gift like this will eventually be tempted to stop maintaining it. Almost a century on, it still runs.

Now the long stretch. Keep south past Roosevelt — the next stop is Museum Campus, and the walk gets exposed from here.
