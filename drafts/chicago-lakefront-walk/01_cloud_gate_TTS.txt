Look down. Not at the sculpture — at the pavers you're standing on.

Under them is a waterproofing membrane, and under that, two multi-level parking garages and a working commuter rail line. Trains run in and out of a station beneath this plaza all day. Millennium Park is a garden on a lid, generally called the largest rooftop garden in the world, and every tree in it is planted in soil that stops a few feet down.

For a century and a half the strip you're on belonged to a railroad. In eighteen fifty-two the city handed the lakefront edge to the Illinois Central in exchange for the railroad building a breakwater to stop the lake from eating Michigan Avenue — a trade that put tracks and yards between downtown and the water and kept them there for four generations. The park opened in two thousand four. What you're walking on is the roof somebody finally built over that bargain.

Now the sculpture. Cloud Gate is sixty-six feet long and thirty-three feet high, seamless stainless steel, and ah nish kuh poor's team spent years grinding the welds out of it so the surface would read as one continuous skin. There isn't a joint on it you can find with your eye.

Stay a moment and think about what's under your feet rather than what's in front of them. Twenty-five acres of lawn, trees, plazas and one very heavy sculpture, all of it laid over a rail yard the city spent a hundred and fifty years apologizing for. The park didn't replace the trains. It covered them, and they're still running.
