ATLAS — CHICAGO
Walk 3: The Lakefront — Millennium Park to Museum Campus
Segment 05 — Museum Campus
Clean version

---

This is Museum Campus, and it's here because a court said it couldn't be somewhere else.

You should be on the plaza between the museums, with the columned limestone building to the west, the stadium beyond it, the aquarium ahead and the planetarium out on the point past that. Take a slow look around before any of the buildings. All of this is lake. Every square foot you can see from here was water inside living memory of the men who paid for these institutions — filled a stretch at a time under agreements with the city and the Army engineers, pushed out from the old shoreline until the ground was large enough to put something on.

Now the part that connects to everything behind you.

Marshall Field's estate wanted its natural history museum in Grant Park, at the center of it, and the city agreed. Montgomery Ward sued. He lost in the Chicago courts, appealed, and in October 1909 the Illinois Supreme Court overturned the judgment and held that nothing could be built on that park at all. The museum had nowhere to go — so it waited in a leftover fair building out in Jackson Park for a couple of decades while this ground was still being made, and finally opened here in 1921.

The aquarium followed. The planetarium followed. The stadium followed. A whole cultural district assembled a mile south of where it was supposed to be, on land that didn't exist when the argument started.

*[beat]*

So: a garden on a roof, a bandshell sized by sound, one museum that slipped through, and a whole campus of buildings that didn't — standing on made ground, because a park two-thirds of a mile north was held empty and stayed that way.

Stay out here a while if the lake lets you. It usually doesn't, or not for long, and there's nothing between this plaza and the water to slow the wind down — which is its own kind of argument for why somebody once thought it was worth going to court to keep the view.
