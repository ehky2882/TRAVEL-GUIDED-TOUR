ATLAS — CHICAGO
Walk 3: The Lakefront — Millennium Park to Museum Campus
Segment 02 — The Jay Pritzker Pavilion
Clean version

---

If there's a rehearsal on, you're hearing this stop before you look at it, and that's the right order.

You should be somewhere along the south or west edge of the great lawn, with the stainless steel bandshell ahead of you — the one that looks like a ribbon caught mid-unwrap — and a grid of steel pipes arching over the grass above your head. The lawn itself is open for events rather than for lounging, and the trellis has been under repair, so take the view from the edge and don't count on getting onto the grass.

Look up at the pipes first, because they are the part people mistake for decoration. That trellis is a mounting frame. Speakers are hung across it, out over the audience, so that a crowd sitting on open grass a hundred yards from the stage hears something closer to what a concert hall would give them. Outdoors, sound falls apart with distance and wanders off in the wind. The usual fix is to make it louder. The fix here was to stop sending it from the front and start dropping it from overhead.

So the interesting half of this building is the half that isn't a building. The bandshell is the photograph; the trellis is the machine. Frank Gehry designed both, and while the steel ribbons get all the attention, the thing doing the actual work is the ironwork overhead that nobody points a camera at.

*[beat]*

Notice how far out the trellis reaches. It doesn't stop at the edge of the stage or the edge of the seating — it keeps going, out over open grass, past where any wall would be. This building's real dimensions are the distance the sound has to travel, which is a strange way to size a building and the only way this one was ever going to work.

Head south out of the park and cross Monroe. Next stop is the east face of the Art Institute.
