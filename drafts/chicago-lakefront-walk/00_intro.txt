ATLAS — CHICAGO
Walk 3: The Lakefront — Millennium Park to Museum Campus
Segment 00 — Intro (Millennium Park, north end)
Clean version

---

None of the ground on this walk is ground.

Some of it is a roof over a railyard. Most of the rest is lake that got filled in, a shoreline pushed out into the water a piece at a time over about seventy years. The park you're standing in is younger than the buildings across the street from it, and the museums at the far end are sitting on land that did not exist when the men who paid for them were born.

You should be at the north end of Millennium Park, on Michigan Avenue, with the park dropping away east of you toward the lake. Five stops, running north to south, about a mile and a half end to end. Flat the whole way, all of it park path or sidewalk, and no highway to cross if you stay west of the drive.

Two honest notes. It takes longer than the distance suggests, because the last stretch south of the fountain is a long open run with nothing between you and the water. And the park is a working venue: the concert lawn is open for events rather than for wandering, the trellis over it has been under repair, and one thing or another is usually fenced. Nothing on this walk depends on getting into anything.

One question carries through all five stops: what was allowed to stand here, and what wasn't. The answer is stranger than you'd guess, and it's the reason the last stop is a mile from the first.

The sculpture on the plaza behind you is where we start.
