# 25 · Umeda Sky Building

**Category:** Observe · **Coordinates (WGS-84):** 34.705293, 135.490535 · **Length:** 319 words (about 128 s at 150 wpm)

## Script (clean)

Look up at the top. That bridge of steel joining the two towers was built on the ground, and then lifted into the sky.

Stand at the foot of the building, a little north-west of Osaka Station, and tip your head back. Forty floors each, about a hundred and seventy metres high, joined at the top by a huge ring of a structure, with a hole through the middle of it. Between the towers, look for glass tubes crossing the gap: those are escalators.

The architect was Hiroshi Hara, the same man who designed Kyoto Station a few years later. The project began in 1988 as something much bigger, called the City of Air, with four towers linked high above the ground. Then Japan's bubble economy burst. Two towers were built, and the building opened in March 1993.

Here's the clever part. Rather than build that ring at the top a hundred and seventy metres up, they built it on the ground, between the towers, and then lifted it up with hydraulic jacks. The glass escalators went up the same way. It was reportedly the first time anyone had done it.

Up top is the Floating Garden Observatory, with an open-air deck on the roof and the whole of Osaka laid out round you. And down in the basement, a street called Takimi Koji, built to look like the city in the early Showa era, all old shopfronts and lanterns.

Most skyscrapers are about being tall. This one is about the space between, the gap, and the hole through the top. Hara wanted a floating city, and the bit everyone remembers is the bit that seems to float.

*[beat]*

If you go up, take the glass escalator across the void. It's not for the faint-hearted. And before you leave, go down to Takimi Koji for a bowl of something hot in a street that pretends it's eighty years ago.
