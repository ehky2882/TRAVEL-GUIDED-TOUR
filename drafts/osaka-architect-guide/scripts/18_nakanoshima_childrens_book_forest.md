# 18 · Nakanoshima Children's Book Forest

**Category:** Visit · **Coordinates (WGS-84):** 34.693100, 135.506331 · **Length:** 300 words (about 120 s at 150 wpm)

## Script (clean)

Tadao Ando designed this library for children, and then he gave it to the city. He paid for the building himself.

The eastern tip of Nakanoshima island, in the park by the river. In front of you is a long, low building of bare concrete and glass, curved gently along its length. In front of it, on the paving, sits a very large green apple.

Start with the apple. Ando designed it himself, and it stands for youth: the idea that you should stay a bit green, a bit unripe, all your life. It's a cheerful thing to find outside a building by an architect best known for grey concrete and silence.

The library opened in July 2020. It was meant to open that March, and the pandemic held it back. Inside, the walls of all three floors are bookshelves, floor to ceiling, round a tall open atrium, with about twenty thousand books. You can sit and read anywhere: on the stairs, in nooks, on the steps of the atrium. Look up from the top of the stairs and you'll see light falling down through the whole height of the building.

What I love is the contrast. Most of Ando's famous buildings are about restraint: a slot of light, a blank wall, a pool of still water. This one is full to bursting, with colourful spines and children. It's the same architect, the same concrete. But here he's let other people fill the space. It's a rather generous building, in more ways than one.

*[beat]*

Entry is free, but numbers are limited. You can book a timed slot online, or try your luck at the door. Afterwards, the Museum of Oriental Ceramics is right next door, and a few minutes west along the island you'll reach the red-brick Central Public Hall.
