There's one rule here, and it's been the rule for longer than anyone can remember. You only dip once.

You should be on a side street in Sheen sekkye, a few minutes south of Tsoo ten kahkoo tower, outside the original branch of Dahroomah. A narrow counter, a hot fryer, steel trays of brown sauce along the bar, and a queue on the pavement most hours of the day.

It opened in nineteen twenty nine, on land that had been an amusement park called Luna Park, and back then it wasn't called Dahroomah at all. The founder, Yoshee eh Hyahkoo noh, called it Tahkoh heeshee and sold beef and potato on skewers, fried in batter, for one sen apiece. That's a hundredth of a yen. Food for workers, cheap and fast.

After the war, her son rebuilt the shop and renamed it. And he added the thing you'll notice in front of you: a bowl of raw cabbage, free.

Here's why the cabbage matters. The sauce trays are shared by everyone at the counter. Dip your skewer once, before you bite, and that's it. Put a bitten skewer back in and you've put it in everybody's sauce. So if you want more sauce, you use a leaf of cabbage as a scoop. The cabbage is the rule's way out. Rather elegant, when you think about it.

The shop nearly closed in two thousand and one. The third generation owner had decided to retire, and it was a regular customer, the actor Heeday kahzoo Ahkye, who begged him not to let it go. The business passed to Kahtsoo yah Oo eh yahmah, who still runs it, and there are now branches across Osaka and Kyohtoh.

When you've eaten, walk back up to Tsoo ten kahkoo and go to the top. Up there you'll find Billiken, a little smiling god of good luck. People rub the soles of his feet for fortune. After a plate of fried skewers, it seems only fair.
