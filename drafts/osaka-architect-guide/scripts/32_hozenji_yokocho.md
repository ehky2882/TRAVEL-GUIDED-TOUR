# 32 · Hozenji Yokocho

**Category:** Observe · **Coordinates (WGS-84):** 34.668185, 135.502671 · **Length:** 314 words (about 126 s at 150 wpm)

## Script (clean)

Splash a little water on the statue. Everyone does, which is why you can barely see his face any more.

A narrow stone-paved lane just south of Dotonbori, a couple of minutes from the neon. At its heart is a small temple yard, and in it a stone statue of the god Fudo, completely covered in green moss, with a basin of water and ladles in front.

This is the Mizukake Fudo, the water-splashing Fudo. People come to pray, scoop up a ladle of water and throw it over him. They've done it for so long, and so often, that he's grown a coat of thick moss, and his features have all but disappeared. In 1945 the air raids burned the temple's main hall to the ground. The statue survived.

Now look along the lanes. There are two of them, stone-paved, about eighty metres long and about three metres wide. Since the Meiji era people have been coming to the temple, and the food stalls that served them grew into this alley of small restaurants and bars.

Have a look at the signboards at the ends. The one at the west entrance was written by the comic actor Kanbi Fujiyama. The one at the east is by the storyteller Katsura Harudanji the third. Both are names Osaka loves.

In 2002 and again in 2003, fires tore through the lane. Under the building rules, a lane this narrow can't normally be rebuilt as it was. Tens of thousands of people signed in support, and it came back the same narrow width. That's the whole point of the place. Make it wider and you'd lose it.

*[beat]*

Come back after dark, especially if it's been raining. The lanterns come on, the stones shine, and it's as atmospheric as anywhere in the city. Then step back out to Dotonbori and its lights. The contrast is half the pleasure.
