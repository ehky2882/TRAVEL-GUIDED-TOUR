# 22 · VS.

**Category:** Visit · **Coordinates (WGS-84):** 34.705468, 135.493217 · **Length:** 326 words (about 130 s at 150 wpm)

## Script (clean)

For most of a century, the land around you was a railway freight yard. Now it's a park, and under part of it there's a space fifteen metres high.

Umekita Park, in the new Grand Green Osaka, a few minutes' walk north-west of Osaka Station. Look for a low pavilion in the park. That's VS., an art and event space that opened in September 2024.

The design was supervised by Tadao Ando, Osaka's most famous architect, and carried out by Nikken Sekkei, one of the country's biggest design firms. Most of it is underground. The largest room has a fifteen-metre ceiling, enormous for a gallery, and there's a wide underground hall for music and events.

It opened with an installation by Daito Manabe, the Japanese artist who works with light, sound and data. In 2025 it held an exhibition on Ando himself, called Youth, which included a full-size version of his Church on the Water from Hokkaido, built inside the hall.

Now, have a look at the park around you. The rest of it was designed by Nikken Sekkei and Mitsubishi Jisho Sekkei, with Ando's office supervising, and the great thin roof elsewhere in the park is by SANAA, the Tokyo partnership of Kazuyo Sejima and Ryue Nishizawa. It's still growing. The park is due to be finished in 2027, with a new wood planted on what was the freight yard.

The interesting bit is this. It was the last big plot of open land beside the busiest station in western Japan. Almost any city would have filled it with towers. There are towers, but the middle of it was given to grass, and the gallery was put underneath, so the park stays a park.

*[beat]*

Check what's on before you go in: it has no permanent collection, and it can change from week to week. Then walk the length of the park and look for that SANAA roof, so thin it barely seems to be there.
