The reception desk here is about two hundred metres off the ground. You'll want to start at the bottom, though.

Start in the ground floor entrance of Nahkah noh sheemah Festival Tower West, on the island of Nahkah noh sheemah, with the river a block away. The hotel takes the top eight floors of the tower, thirty three to forty, and its lobby is right at the very top. Before you go up, look for the video piece in the entrance by the artist Tahkah heeroh Mahtsoo oh: millions of tiny particles drifting as if there's no gravity. The colour shifts through the day, from white to blue to gold.

Now take the lift to the fortieth floor. When the doors open, the first thing you'll see is a sculpture by Koh hay Nahwah called Foo and Rye. That's the wind god and the thunder god, Foojeen and Rye jeen, the pair you'll find guarding temple gates all over Japan, usually with bulging eyes, a bag of wind and a ring of drums. Nahwah has turned them into clusters of soft, glistening spheres. Gods made of bubbles.

There are nearly four hundred works of art around the hotel, and most of them are about wind, water and light. And the reason is through the windows: the river, the bay, the mountains behind the city.

Here's the thing about that lobby. A normal hotel puts its public rooms at the bottom and sells the view by the night, to whoever books a room. Putting the lobby, the restaurants and the bar on the fortieth floor means the best view in the building belongs to anyone who walks in.

So if you're not staying, have a drink anyway. The Forty Sky Bar and Lounge sits next to the lobby, looking out across the city. And when you come back down, the tower's twin to the east holds Festival Hall, one of the city's great concert halls, while the museums at the western end of Nahkah noh sheemah are a short walk along the river.
