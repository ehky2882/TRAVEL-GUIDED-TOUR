# 27 · Maishima Incineration Plant

**Category:** Observe · **Coordinates (WGS-84):** 34.665621, 135.406508 · **Length:** 322 words (about 129 s at 150 wpm)

## Script (clean)

This is where Osaka burns its rubbish. It looks like a fairytale castle.

Out here on Maishima, an island in the bay at the western edge of the city, you're looking at a building striped in red and yellow, with golden onion domes, a chimney topped by a glittering ball, windows of every shape and trees growing out of the roof.

It was designed by Friedensreich Hundertwasser, the Viennese artist who hated straight lines. He believed there were no straight lines in nature, and none of identical things, so he tried to make buildings the same way. Look at the windows: no two quite alike. And look at the walls: the red and yellow stripes are meant to suggest the flames burning inside.

And they really are burning inside. This is a working plant that incinerates the city's waste and crushes bulky rubbish, and it reuses the steam from the burning to make energy. The trees on the roof and the grounds are part of the design too: they're planted with fruit trees and a pond for birds and insects, and the long-term plan is for the area round it to become a forest.

What's worth thinking about, standing here, is why. Waste plants are usually the buildings a city hides, behind fences on the outskirts. Osaka put this one in plain sight and asked an artist to dress it up. You could call that a gimmick. Or you could say that if a city has to burn its rubbish, it might as well make you look, and think about how much of it there is.

*[beat]*

You can go inside. Tours are free, Monday to Saturday, at ten, one and three, and last about an hour and a half, but you must book ahead. Otherwise, walk the green paths that loop round the plant. They're open to anyone, and from the far side you get the whole crazy skyline at once.
