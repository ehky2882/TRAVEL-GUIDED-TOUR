Nahkah noh sheemah Museum of Art, Osaka

Osaka first decided to build a museum of modern art in nineteen eighty three. This is it. It took nearly forty years.

In front of you is a great black box on the western end of Nahkah noh sheemah, between the rivers. Hardly any windows. Just a dark, almost sealed volume sitting on the ground. Ask anyone in Osaka for directions and they'll call it the one with the black outside.

The idea goes back to August nineteen eighty three, when a modern art museum was named as one of the projects for the city's hundredth anniversary. Then came committees, a preparation office, years of deliberation over where to put it, a decision in twenty thirteen to build here, and a design competition in twenty seventeen. Building work began in February twenty nineteen, and it finally opened in twenty twenty two.

The competition was won by the architect Kahtsoo heekoh Endoh, and his whole idea is inside. He calls it the passage, like the covered arcades of old Paris. A tall atrium runs right through the middle of the building, from the first floor to the fifth, with soft light falling from above, and it's open to anyone, ticket or not. Endoh described it as a museum like a city, where all sorts of people and activities cross.

Have a look at the furniture in the passage if you go in. The chairs, benches and sofas are by the designer Tye jee Foojee moree, in Hokkye doh oak.

What's worth noticing is the contrast. Outside, the building is closed and mysterious. Inside, it's as public as a station concourse. The black box doesn't keep people out. It makes them curious enough to come in.

Walk through the passage even if you're not seeing an exhibition. Then go next door. The National Museum of Art is almost entirely underground, and from up here, all you can see of it is a strange sculpture of steel.
