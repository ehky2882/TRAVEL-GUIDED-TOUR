Creative Center Osaka

Ships were built here. Now it's where Osaka's artists come to make things too big for anywhere else.

You should be outside the old Nahmoorah shipyard in Keetah kahgah yah, at the mouth of the Keezoo River, looking at a long run of industrial buildings: sheds, a four storey office block and open ground running down to the water.

This was the Osaka works of Nahmoorah Shipbuilding. When the company moved its shipbuilding elsewhere, the site went quiet, and so did the neighbourhood around it. The land belongs to Cheesheemah Tohchee, a local land company, and in two thousand and four it began turning the empty works and the empty houses round it into places for art and culture, starting with a suggestion from a theatre producer. Creative Center Osaka opened in October two thousand and five, and in two thousand and seven the government listed the shipyard as heritage of Japan's industrial modernisation.

Have a look at that office block. It's the shipyard's old general office, and its top two floors were the drafting rooms, where the ships were drawn. Now they're spaces for performances and exhibitions, along with a room painted completely black, called the Black Chamber.

One thing to know before you try the gate: this is private land, and it isn't open to wander in. It opens for events, and once a month there's a short guided tour.

And here's what's interesting. Most cities tidy their old industry away, or turn it into flats. Here a landowner decided to keep the rust and the scale, and to fill the old houses round it with artists' studios. It's a slow sort of regeneration, and twenty years on, it's still going.

So walk the streets round about. Keetah kahgah yah is full of large murals on the sides of houses and factories, and you'll find more of them almost every year. Start from the station and wander towards the river.
