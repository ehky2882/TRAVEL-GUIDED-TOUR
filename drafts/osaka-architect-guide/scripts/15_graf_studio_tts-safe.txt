Graf studio

Six friends started this in nineteen ninety three: a designer, a product designer, a carpenter, a furniture maker, an artist and a chef. That last one is the clue.

You should be on the western end of Nahkah noh sheemah, the long island between the rivers, outside a plain building with big windows on the ground floor. Through them, a shop of furniture and household things, and a kitchen. Upstairs, a gallery space.

The group first called itself decorative mode number three, and from nineteen ninety eight it became graff. The founder and leader is the designer Sheegekkee Hattoree. They've been based here on Nahkah noh sheemah since the early two thousands, and today they design spaces, products and graphics, make and sell furniture, and run food and music events. The ground floor has graff shop and graff kitchen. The first floor, which they call graff porch, holds exhibitions.

Have a look round the shop if it's open. Much of the furniture is their own, plain, solid and made to be lived with. The everyday objects sit beside it as if in somebody's house, which is the idea.

Here's what makes graff rather unusual. Most design studios keep the chef out of the room. graff put one in among the founders. Food isn't a sideline here, it's part of the design. A table isn't finished until somebody's eaten at it.

And they chose this end of the island before either of the big art museums arrived. Now those museums are its neighbours, and the area has become one of the most interesting corners of the city.

Those neighbours are worth the next half hour. The black box of the Nahkah noh sheemah Museum of Art is a couple of minutes away, and beside it, the National Museum of Art, which has put almost all of itself underground.
