# 29 · Ikoma Building

**Category:** Observe · **Coordinates (WGS-84):** 34.687507, 135.506364 · **Length:** 304 words (about 122 s at 150 wpm)

## Script (clean)

A watchmaker built this, so of course it has a clock. Look closely, though. The clock has no numbers.

Look up. On the corner of Sakaisuji and Hiranomachi, in Senba, the old merchant district, stands a narrow building of dark brick, with a clock tower on top and a rounded bay window running down the front.

It was finished in 1930 as the headquarters of the Ikoma watch company, which had been selling clocks and watches in Osaka since 1870. It was designed by the office of So Hyozo, one of the grand old men of Osaka architecture, and it's Art Deco, the style of the jazz age, all geometry and gleam.

Have a look at the details. The clock face on the tower is plain, with no numbers at all. Down the front, the bay window and the round windows are said to be modelled on a clock's pendulum. And at the windows, look for the stone eagles, perched and peering down at the street.

Inside, which you can see from the ground floor, there's an Italian marble staircase winding up beside an old hand-operated lift. During the war almost every metal fitting in the building was taken away and melted down. One thing survived: the company bell. It's in the lobby.

Senba was once lined with buildings like this, merchant houses and company offices with a bit of swagger. Most were bombed or pulled down. This one is still here and still in use, with a café on the ground floor. It's a little reminder of how handsome the business district was.

*[beat]*

From here, walk north up Sakaisuji for five minutes to Kitahama, the old stock-trading district, and Kitahama Retro, a tiny red-brick tearoom by the river. Or head west to Midosuji and the Gas Building, built three years after this.
