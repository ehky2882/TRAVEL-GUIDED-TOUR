Keetah hahmah Retro

Tea and scones, in a red brick building put up for stockbrokers in the last year of the Mayjee era. It shouldn't work. It does.

In front of you, on a street in Keetah hahmah by the Toh sah boree River, is a tiny two storey building of red brick, squeezed between much bigger neighbours. The windows and door are framed in cut granite, and through the glass you'll see cakes on a counter and a narrow stair going up.

It was built in nineteen twelve for a club of people in the share trade. Keetah hahmah was Osaka's financial district, and still is: the stock exchange is just round the corner. The style borrows from Britain, from the Glasgow school, with that mixture of sturdy brick and slightly whimsical detail.

After the war it became the head office of a building materials company, and then it sat empty, as old buildings in this district so often did. In nineteen ninety seven it reopened as a British tearoom, and it was registered as a cultural property soon after.

Go in if there's room, and climb to the first floor. It's small, and from the window you look out across the river towards Nahkah noh sheemah island. The afternoon tea comes on a tiered stand. The scones are the point.

Here's what I find rather lovely. Osaka could have kept this building as a museum of itself, a plaque on a locked door. Instead somebody put a kettle on. A building that's used is a building that's looked after, and the queue outside does more for it than any plaque would.

Before you go, walk a few minutes west to the Osaka Exchange building and look for the statue of Goh dye Toh moh ahtsoo out front, the man who helped found the exchange. Then cross Nahneewah bahshee bridge to Nahkah noh sheemah, where the red brick Central Public Hall is waiting.
