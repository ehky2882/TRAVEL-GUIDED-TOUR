Osaka Gas Building

This was the first office building on Midosuji. When it went up in 1933, the great boulevard was still being built, and it set the tone for the whole street.

Find a spot on the pavement of Midosuji, the wide tree-lined avenue, across the junction from the Gas Building. The bottom two floors are dark stone, almost black. Above them, everything is pale cream tile, with long horizontal bands of windows wrapping round the corner.

The architect was Takeo Yasui. It was built for the gas company, as its headquarters and as a place to show off the modern gas-powered life it was selling. That's why it looks the way it does: clean, horizontal and streamlined, the future in the nineteen thirties.

Now look to the north. In 1966 the building was extended in that direction by Shoichi Sano, from Yasui's own architecture practice, and he matched the old part so carefully that most people walking past have no idea there's a thirty-year gap between the two halves. Both are now registered cultural properties.

The building has long had a restaurant upstairs, the Gas Building Shokudo, with its period dining room restored.

A good building on a big avenue doesn't shout. It holds the corner, keeps to the line of the street and gets the details right. Midosuji was laid out to be Osaka's grand boulevard, and its buildings were long kept to a uniform height. This one showed the others how to behave.

[beat]

One thing to know: Osaka Gas has plans to renew the building over the coming years, keeping the old facades and most of the floor space, with a new tower going up next door. So take a good look while it's still exactly as it was. Then walk east along Hiranomachi for five minutes to find the Ikoma Building, another nineteen thirties survivor, with a clock tower and stone eagles.
