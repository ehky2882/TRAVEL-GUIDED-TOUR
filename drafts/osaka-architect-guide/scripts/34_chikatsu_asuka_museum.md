# 34 · Chikatsu Asuka Museum

**Category:** Observe · **Coordinates (WGS-84):** 34.503111, 135.643613 · **Length:** 309 words (about 124 s at 150 wpm)

## Script (clean)

Tadao Ando built this museum as a staircase. The building is the hillside, and you can climb all over it.

This is the Chikatsu Asuka Fudoki no Oka, a park of ancient burial mounds in the hills south-east of Osaka, about an hour from the city. In front of you, a great flight of wide concrete steps, climbing up and away from you. That's the roof of the museum.

The museum opened in 1994. Ando built it into the slope of the land, and set out its roof as one enormous, broad stair. It can work as an open-air theatre, and from the top you look out over the plum and cherry trees of the park.

The stair is the clue to what the museum is about. This part of Osaka is thick with kofun, the ancient keyhole-shaped and round burial mounds of the early Japanese rulers, and in this area alone there are more than two hundred, along with the tombs of four emperors. The park around you protects a cluster of them from the sixth century. So Ando made a building that is itself a mound, a piece of raised ground with something precious inside.

Inside, it's dark and quiet, and it tells the story of that age of mounds, when the first Japanese state was taking shape around here.

And here's the thing. Most museums ask you to look at the past. This one asks you to stand on it. You climb the building the way people once climbed the burial mounds around it, and from the top, the landscape suddenly makes sense.

*[beat]*

Before you leave, walk the paths through the park to the old tombs themselves. Some have their stone burial chambers exposed, and you can peer inside. And if you've come in late winter, you're just in time for the plum blossom.
