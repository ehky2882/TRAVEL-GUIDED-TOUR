Villa del bal bya nel lo

The man who left this villa to Italy had been to the North Pole and up Everest. He asked to end up in the garden.

At the tip of the la vay doh peninsula, find the loggia, the open gallery on the highest point of the garden, with lake on both sides of you. Its columns are smothered in creeping fig, and set into the floor at your feet is an inlaid compass rose.

There was a fran sis kan convent on this point from the thirteenth century. Then, at the end of the seventeen hundreds, Cardinal an jeh lo maria doo ree nee made it into a retreat for pleasure and for literature, and this loggia was his great idea, with a music room on one side and a library on the other.

The villa went through several owners after him, but the one who matters most arrived in nineteen seventy four. gwee doh mon tsee no was a Milanese businessman and an explorer. He led expeditions to the Arctic, including one to the North Pole in nineteen seventy one, and in nineteen seventy three he led the Italian expedition that put climbers on the summit of Everest. Up in the attic is his Museum of Expeditions: flags, photographs, the sledge from the North Pole, and around three hundred small in yoo it carvings, the largest collection of in yoo it ivories outside Greenland.

Now have a look at the trees. The plane trees by the jetty are pruned into candelabra. And the big holm oak was clipped into an umbrella so that mon tsee no could see mon tay len yo nay from his study window. The gardeners still do it, climbing up in harnesses.

mon tsee no died without heirs in nineteen eighty eight, and left the villa to the fy. If it looks familiar, that's because film crews love it. It's been a backdrop for Star Wars and for James Bond in Casino Royale.

Before you go, look for the old ice house among the rocks of the park. That's where he asked for his ashes to be laid, which seems about right for a man who spent so much of his life on the ice.
