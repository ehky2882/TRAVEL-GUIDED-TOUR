# Ponte delle Torri, Spoleto

*328 words, about 131 seconds at a natural pace.*

## Clean script

Two hundred and thirty metres long, eighty metres high, and Goethe said it looked like a second nature.

Stand at the end of the bridge near the Rocca, the great fortress on the hill above Spoleto, and look across. Nine tall stone arches stride over the gorge to the slopes of Monteluco, the wooded mountain opposite. A wall runs along the top on the side you're walking, about twelve metres high.

Nobody is quite sure when it was built. It probably took its present form at the end of the thirteen hundreds, and it may stand on the remains of something Roman, though nothing Roman obviously survives. It's often linked to the great building works that began after thirteen sixty-three, when Cardinal Albornoz started work on the fortress behind you.

Here's the gift: it isn't really a road bridge at all. It was an aqueduct. A channel along the top carried water from springs on Monteluco into the town, to the fortress and to the fountains of Spoleto. The high wall was built to protect it.

At the far end is a smaller fort, the Fortilizio dei Mulini, built to guard the crossing, and it worked as a mill until the eighteen hundreds. A couple of the piers rising from the valley floor are hollow, with rooms for guards inside.

[beat]

In October seventeen eighty-six, Goethe came this way and was enchanted. He wrote that it was the third work of the ancients he had seen that had the same grand spirit, a second nature working for human purposes. There's a plaque recording his visit.

After the earthquakes of twenty sixteen, the bridge was closed for eight years, and it reopened in December twenty twenty-four.

When you've crossed, keep going. At the far end, near the Fortilizio, a path called the Giro dei condotti follows the line of an old aqueduct along the slopes of Monteluco, past ancient hermitages, with the whole bridge and town opening up behind you.

## Fact-check notes

- Nine arches (Wikipedia); Goethe wrote ten. The script uses nine.
- The Gattapone attribution is only implied, so it isn't stated.
- Goethe's 'third work of the ancients' paraphrase is general knowledge of the Italian Journey passage; Wikipedia quotes 'seconda natura'. Check the wording.
- Reopened 7 December 2024 after 8 years (visitspoleto). Trial opening hours 6–18.
- 'The wall on the side you're walking' is approximate; the wall runs along the road on top.
