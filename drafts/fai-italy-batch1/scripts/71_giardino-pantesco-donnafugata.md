# Giardino Pantesco Donnafugata, Pantelleria

*315 words, about 126 seconds at a natural pace.*

## Clean script

A wall of black lava stone eleven metres across, four metres high in places, and inside it, one orange tree. That's all, and it's brilliant.

In the countryside at Khamma, on the island of Pantelleria, you'll find the round enclosure standing on its own in the vineyards: a ring of dry-stone wall, no mortar, with just one narrow way in.

Pantelleria sits out in the sea between Sicily and Africa. It's battered by wind, and it can go three hundred days without rain. An orange tree simply couldn't survive out in the open here. So the islanders came up with the giardino pantesco, the Pantelleria garden: a high stone wall around a single tree, to keep the wind off it.

Here's the clever part. The walls are thick and made of porous lava. As the air cools at night after a hot day, the stone pulls moisture out of the air and gives it back to the soil inside. Small slits let the rainwater in, and channels and a beaten earth floor gather what little falls. The wall is a shelter, a water collector and a microclimate, all at once, with no machinery at all.

[beat]

So this one tree, an orange of an old variety, has been kept alive for generations by nothing more than a wall and a bit of physics. I find that one of the most quietly impressive pieces of design anywhere in Italy.

The garden was given to the FAI in two thousand and eight by the Donnafugata winery, whose vineyards surround it. Visits are run with the winery, mainly in the summer months, and guided tours leave from the Donnafugata cellars here at Khamma. Book ahead.

When you step inside, stand still for a moment. Notice how the wind drops away and the air changes. That's the whole idea of the place, and you can feel it on your skin.

## Fact-check notes

- Dimensions are 11 m across and up to 4 m high (FAI IT).
- The microclimate mechanism (porous lava, day-night swing, slits, channels, beaten floor) and the 300 days of drought are from FAI.
- 'Between Sicily and Africa' is general geography.
- Donated in 2008 by the Donnafugata winery or family. The Rallo name isn't on FAI's pages, so it isn't used.
- The UNESCO alberello listing isn't on FAI's pages, so it's omitted.
- 'One of the most quietly impressive pieces of design' is the narrator's opinion.
- Visits follow the winery's hours (June to September); guided tours in July to September from the Khamma cellars.
