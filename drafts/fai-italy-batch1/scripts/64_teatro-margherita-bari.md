# Teatro Margherita, Bari

*333 words, about 133 seconds at a natural pace.*

## Clean script

This theatre stands on the sea because of a contract. Bari had promised not to build another theatre on city land. So somebody built one on the water.

From the seafront at Piazza Quattro Novembre, look at the pale building standing at the edge of the old port, with its dome and its rows of tall windows. Today the sea has been filled in around its base, but when it was built, it stood on pillars sunk into the water.

Here's the story. The city of Bari had an agreement with the Petruzzelli family, who were building the great opera house in town: the council would not put up any other theatre on municipal land, except over the sea. A company called Orfeo saw the gap, got a concession on the water, and in nineteen ten opened a wooden variety hall here, the Varietà Margherita. It burned down the following year.

The stone building that replaced it went up between nineteen twelve and nineteen fourteen, designed by Francesco De Giglio in the Liberty style, and it opened as the Kursaal Margherita in August nineteen fourteen. It was the first building in Bari made of reinforced concrete.

[beat]

So a loophole gave Bari a landmark. And the building has had several lives since. It showed films from the nineteen twenties. In nineteen forty-three the Allied army took it over and called it the Garrison Theatre, and it was damaged in the bombing raid that December. After the war it was a cinema again, until it closed around nineteen eighty.

After years of restoration, it reopened in December twenty eighteen, not as a theatre but as a centre for contemporary art. Its first show was an immersive Van Gogh exhibition, and nearly ninety-five thousand people came.

Before you go, walk round to the old harbour side and look back at the building. Try to imagine it on its stilts, with the water lapping right underneath, a theatre built exactly where nobody had thought to forbid one.

## Fact-check notes

- Single-source (Wikipedia); re-check before recording.
- The loophole: the agreement excepted 'costruzioni sul mare'; Orfeo got a concessione demaniale in 1912.
- Wooden Varietà Margherita: opened 5 October 1910, burned 22 July 1911. Stone building 1912–14, opened 22 August 1914.
- 'Damaged in the bombing raid that December' refers to 2 December 1943.
- Closure: the page gives 1979 (handed back) and 20 February 1980 (closed). The script says 'around 1980'.
- Reopened 6 December 2018 with Van Gogh Alive, 94,895 visitors.
