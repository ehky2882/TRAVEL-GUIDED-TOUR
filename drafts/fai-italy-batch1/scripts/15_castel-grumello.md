# Castel Grumello

*318 words, about 127 seconds at a natural pace.*

## Clean script

Two castles on one rock, and vineyards all the way down.

You're on the rocky spur above Montagna in Valtellina, with the castle ruins around you and the valley opening out below. Behind you rise the Rhaetian Alps. Ahead and beneath, the terraces of vines step down the mountainside.

Look at the ruins and you'll see there are two of them, side by side inside one wall. That's what makes Grumello unusual. It's a twin castle. The eastern half was military, built around a big square tower, with the remains of a second tower for keeping watch. The other half was for living in, with better cut stone and a fireplace, the part meant for comfort and for show.

It was begun at the end of the twelve hundreds or the start of the thirteen hundreds by Corrado de Piro, a Ghibelline who'd come up into the valley with his family after the wars between Milan and Como. It didn't go well for them. Within a few decades they had to sell it to their rivals. And in fifteen twenty-six it was destroyed by the Grey Leagues, the Swiss confederation of the Grisons, along with many of the fortresses of the Valtellina.

[beat]

So what you're looking at is the shell of an argument that was settled five hundred years ago, and the thing that's outlasted it is the farming. The name comes from the knoll it stands on, the grumo, and the terraces below have been growing Nebbiolo, the grape of the Alps, for centuries.

The castle was given away in nineteen ninety, restored, and opened to the public in two thousand and one.

There's a small restaurant just below the castle, with seasonal food from the valley. If you've the time, stop there, and then walk down among the terraces. You'll see how much patience it takes to farm a mountain, one dry-stone wall at a time.
