La Velarca

It's a house. It's a boat. And it was designed by the same people who gave Milan the Torre Velasca.

On the shore at Ossuccio, look for the long, low boat moored by the bank, with a timber cabin sitting on its deck like a small, very elegant shed. That's La Velarca, and the name is a little joke on the tower.

The hull is older than the house. It belonged to a working lake boat, the Corriera Tremezzina, nineteen metres long, which carried goods and passengers across Lake Como from nineteen eleven. In nineteen fifty-nine, the Milan architects BBPR, Banfi, Belgiojoso, Peressutti and Rogers, set a home on top of it. It was finished in nineteen sixty-one.

The idea came from Fiammetta and Emilio Norsa, who wanted somewhere on the lake to have their friends to stay. And what friends. Gio Ponti, the poet Eugenio Montale, the artist Lucio Fontana, Umberto Eco, and the architects themselves.

Have a look at the round tower of teak in the middle. That's the staircase, wrapped in a cylinder, and it ties the whole thing together. Bedrooms and a sitting room at the bow, a kitchen and bathrooms along the sides, a study and another bedroom at the stern.

What I like about it is how unserious it is, in the best way. The same studio was building a skyscraper in the middle of Milan, and on weekends they were fitting out a houseboat for their friends.

The family gave it to the FAI in twenty eleven. The hull was beyond repair, so it was rebuilt at the Riva boatyard, and the interiors were restored from old photographs before it came back to its original mooring here.

If you fancy a walk, the Greenway path along the shore takes you to Villa del Balbianello in about half an hour. It's a lovely way to go from one FAI house to another, by land, past the boats.
