# Rocca di Lonato and Casa del Podestà

*327 words, about 131 seconds at a natural pace.*

## Clean script

A senator bought a castle, a house and fifty thousand books, and then gave the lot away.

Up on the walls of the Rocca, look north-east. The fortress runs along a ridge of glacial moraine, almost a hundred and eighty metres long, and from the wall walk you can see out over the southern end of Lake Garda.

People have been fortifying this ridge since the tenth century. It passed from the lords of Verona to the Visconti of Milan, then the Gonzaga, and in fourteen forty-one to the Republic of Venice, which held Lonato for more than three hundred and fifty years. You'll notice it's really two castles in one: the Rocchetta at the top, and the larger main quarter below. Up in the Rocchetta, there's now a museum of birds, with around seven hundred specimens.

Now look down into the town, towards a house just below the fortress. That's the Casa del Podestà, built in the middle of the fourteen hundreds for the official who governed Lonato on behalf of Venice.

[beat]

Here's where the senator comes in. Ugo Da Como was a lawyer from Brescia, then a member of parliament, then a senator. In nineteen oh six he bought the Casa del Podestà at public auction, and had it restored by the leading architect of Brescia, Antonio Tagliaferri. Later he bought the Rocca too. And into the house went his library: around fifty thousand volumes, four hundred incunabula, the earliest printed books, and five hundred manuscripts going back to the twelfth century.

He lived there until his death in nineteen forty-one, and his foundation was created by royal decree the following year, so that none of it would be broken up. It's still a private foundation, and still open to the public.

When you come down from the walls, go into the Casa del Podestà and ask to see the library. It's one man's lifetime of reading, still on the shelves where he left it.

## Fact-check notes

- The tenth-century origin is Wikipedia-only.
- 'Lords of Verona' are the Scaligeri, per Wikipedia.
- Casa del Podestà purchase: official 1906, Wikipedia 1909. The script uses 1906.
- The bird museum location is the Casa del Capitano per the official site; the script says 'up in the Rocchetta'.
- 'Look north-east' for Lake Garda is my orientation; check it on site.
- Winter hours: the Rocca opens weekends only from November to February.
