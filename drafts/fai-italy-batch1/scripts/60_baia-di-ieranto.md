# Baia di Ieranto

*298 words, about 119 seconds at a natural pace.*

## Clean script

According to the Roman writer Pliny the Elder, this is where Ulysses met the Sirens. It's also where a steelworks once quarried limestone.

You've walked down from Nerano, and here's the bay: a deep green inlet at the very end of the Sorrento peninsula, the only one of its kind on this tip of land, and directly across the water, the great sea stacks of Capri, the Faraglioni.

Look up to your left, to the headland of Montalto, and you'll see an old stone watchtower. It's one of the defensive towers built along this coast in the fifteen hundreds, when Saracen pirates were a real and regular danger. Another stands out on Punta Campanella, the very tip of the peninsula.

The quieter history is the quarry. From the early nineteen hundreds, limestone was dug out of these cliffs, and from nineteen eighteen the quarry belonged to the steel company Ilva, part of what later became Italsider. Workers came all the way from Sardinia to cut the rock. It closed for good in nineteen fifty-two.

[beat]

And then the company did something unusual. Rather than sell the land for villas, it gave the whole bay away, in the late nineteen eighties, so it couldn't be built on. That's why it looks like this. The water belongs to the Punta Campanella marine reserve, and the land has been left to the olives, the scrub and the walkers.

There are no bars and no bins down here. Bring water, and take everything home with you.

If you've the energy for one more climb, go up to the Torre di Montalto. From there you see the whole story at once: the quarry scars healing over, the bay below, and Capri sitting out in the haze, exactly where the Sirens would have been singing.

## Fact-check notes

- The Pliny attribution is FAI's claim and wasn't checked against Pliny.
- 'The only inlet at the end of the Sorrento peninsula' follows FAI. Wikipedia notes it is geographically beyond Punta Campanella.
- Donation: FAI says Italsider 1987; Wikipedia says Ilva 1986. The script says 'late 1980s' and names both.
- The quarry closed in 1952 (Wikipedia).
- Montalto being 'to your left' is my orientation; check it on site.
- No bars or bins: from the FAI English page.
