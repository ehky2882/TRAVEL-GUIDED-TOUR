bat teh ree ah mee lee tah reh tal mo neh, pa low

For around two centuries, soldiers kept watch from this headland over one of the most beautiful stretches of sea in the Mediterranean.

You're at poon ta don dee eh go, between pa low and kos ta seh reh na, on the north east coast of sar din ee ah. In front of you is the mad da leh na archipelago, and just across the water the island of spar jee. The battery was built to guard the channel between spar jee and sar din ee ah.

Walk in and you'll see what's left of the military life here: a lookout tower, gun positions aimed out to sea, and the soldiers' barracks and service rooms, which have been restored so you can picture the daily routine of the men posted to this lonely spot.

tal mo neh was one link in a chain. Around fifty forts, small forts and batteries were built across the mad da leh na archipelago to defend it. Its importance grew after Italy was unified, when the royal navy made la mad da leh na one of its bases, and the battery became part of the watch over the fleet.

And then it simply stopped. After the Second World War, the peace treaty required Italy to dismantle its military batteries, and tal mo neh was abandoned for decades. Nature crept back in.

In two thousand and two the Region of sar din ee ah handed it over to be restored, and the work is still going on. Visits are by guided tour, on request, so arrange one before you come.

The walk out to the battery is part of the pleasure: half an hour or so along a path through the Mediterranean scrub, myrtle, bay and joo nip er, with the sea glittering below. When you reach the end, find the shelter that looks out over the water, and stand where the lookouts stood. You'll see exactly what they were guarding, and why you'd never tire of the view.
