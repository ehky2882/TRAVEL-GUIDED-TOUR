I Giganti della Sila

Some of these trees were already old when Napoleon was born. They're around three hundred and fifty years old, and up to forty-five metres tall.

You're in the Sila, the high plateau of Calabria, about fourteen hundred metres up, at the edge of a small wood near Croce di Magara. The path leads in among the trunks, enormous grey columns of laricio pine, the great native pine of Calabria, with mountain maples between them.

There are around sixty of them in a reserve of just five hectares, and they're here because a family left them alone. In sixteen thirty-one the Mollo barons bought this land, and in the sixteen hundreds the pines were planted. The family turned the estate into a farm with its own silk industry, and the Mollo hunting lodge, the Casino Mollo, stands nearby, with its mill and its silk works. Silk from here went as far as Marseille, London and Lyon.

Have a look at the bark low down on some of the trunks. For centuries, woodcutters known as piciari tapped these trees for resin, and some of the old wounds have grown into hollows big enough for people to have sheltered inside them.

[beat]

What I find moving here is how close it came. After the Second World War the estate was taken over by the state forestry, and the trees could easily have become timber. Instead, in nineteen eighty-seven, Paola Manes, the mother of the last owners, helped have it protected as a reserve. Since twenty sixteen the FAI has cared for the wood on behalf of the Sila National Park, and the Mollo sisters gave the Casino to the FAI in memory of their brother.

At the moment the site is closed until further notice, for forest maintenance and to restore the buildings, so check with the FAI before you travel.

When it's open, walk the path slowly, and somewhere in the middle, stop and look straight up. These trees were here before almost everything else you'll see in Calabria, and from underneath, you can see why people called them the giants.
