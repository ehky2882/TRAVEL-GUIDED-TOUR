Villa dei ves ko vee

Before pa lah dee oh, there was this. A bishop's house on a hilltop, built to behave like a Roman villa.

From the drive below, look up the slope through the vineyard. The villa sits on its terrace at the top of a small hill in the yoo gay nee an Hills, with a great open loggia running along its upper floor, and the countryside of the ven eh toh rolling away around it.

It was commissioned in the early fifteen hundreds by the bishops of pad yoo ah, and the man who made it happen was Cardinal fran ches ko pee zah nee, with the nobleman al vee zeh kor nah ro managing the works and the estate. The design came from the Veronese architect jo van nee maria fal ko net toh. When he died in fifteen thirty five, his pupil an dray ah da val leh built it, and the painter and architect joo lee oh ro mah no almost certainly had a hand in it too. The heavy rusticated stone of the ground floor may be his.

The model was the Roman house, built round a central courtyard, with a gallery above where you'd walk and talk. It was meant to run round all four sides. Only two survive, but you can still do the walking.

Inside, look for the work of a Flemish painter, lam bert soos triss, who arrived from Rome in fifteen forty one. He filled the rooms with myths and with landscapes full of Roman ruins: ah ree ad nee abandoned on nax oss, Orpheus and yoo rid i see, Apollo flaying the satyr mar see as. One room is named after a small boy eating grapes.

And here's the thing about these walls. In sixteen thirty they were whitewashed over, and the paintings were forgotten for more than three hundred years. They came back to light in the twentieth century, after the bishops sold the villa in nineteen sixty two. The new owner, vee tor ee oh ol cheh zeh, bought it on the advice of the great art historian ro bair toh lon ghee, and restored the frescoes. His family gave it away in two thousand and five, so that it could be opened to the public.

When you've seen the rooms, walk the length of the western loggia, where painted boys climb through trellises and fake Roman statues stand in painted niches. Then go down to the old stables, where the wine shop pours the wines of these hills.
