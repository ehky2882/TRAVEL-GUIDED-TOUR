Abbazia di Santa Maria di Cerrate

Greek monks once prayed here in the middle of an olive grove in Puglia. Then the pirates came, and the abbey became a farm.

In the open ground in front of the church, look at its plain stone face: a simple gabled front, a rose window, and a carved doorway in the honey-coloured stone of Lecce. Along one side runs a fourteenth-century portico. All around, olive trees.

The legend says it was founded after King Tancred of Hauteville, Count of Lecce, saw a vision of the Virgin while out hunting. The history is a little different. Between the end of the ten hundreds and the start of the eleven hundreds, the Norman Bohemond of Hauteville set up a monastery here for Greek monks, who followed the rule of Saint Basil. Southern Italy still had a strong Greek-speaking church, and this was one of its houses.

Go inside and look at the walls. The church holds a rare collection of Byzantine frescoes, and the FAI has restored them. Some were taken off the walls in earlier restorations and you'll find them in the little museum.

[beat]

And here's the turn in the story. In fifteen thirty-one the abbey passed to a hospital in Naples. Then, in seventeen eleven, Turkish pirates sacked it. After that it was abandoned as a monastery and turned into a working farm, a masseria, for pressing olive oil and drying tobacco. The monks' world became a farmer's world, and both of them are still here.

Look for the well in the courtyard, dated fifteen eighty-five. And then go down underground to the olive press, cut into the rock, where the oil was made in the cool and the dark.

The Province of Lecce entrusted the abbey to the FAI in twenty twelve. When you leave, take the old road outside for a few minutes. It follows the line of the ancient route between Brindisi, Lecce and Otranto, the road the monks would have known.
