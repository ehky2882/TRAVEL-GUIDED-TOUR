Ville Jacques koo ell, montee mannoo

These houses are trying very hard not to look like houses. That was the whole idea.

You're at montee mannoo, in the hills above abbyadoree, just outside porto chairvo. In front of you, among the granite boulders and the scrub, there are walls that curve instead of running straight, windows of odd shapes, and rooms that seem to have grown out of the rock rather than been built on it.

They're the work of Jacques koo ell, a French architect who never trained as one and who thought of himself as more of a sculptor. He was a friend of Picasso and dahlee, and he was known, a little unkindly, as the architect of billionaires. In nineteen sixty two, when the ahga kahn began building the kosta smeralda, koo ell was one of the architects asked to give it a look of its own, alongside looeejee vyettee and Michele boozeeree veechee. With his son savan he built the Hotel kahla dee volpay. Then he turned to this hillside, and in nineteen sixty eight he built villa la grotta, the cave, which he meant as his own home. Others followed around it.

Have a look at how little of it is flat. koo ell wanted houses shaped by the land, the sun and the wind, the way a shell or a burrow is shaped, so that you can't quite say where the inside stops and the outside begins.

What I find rather moving is where the story went next. For years the villas were shut away, after they were seized from organised crime. In May twenty twenty six they were handed to the town of artsakena, about thirty thousand square metres of buildings and land, and that October they opened to the public for the very first time. The plan now is a museum of the kosta smeralda itself, telling the story of the coast from the inside of one of its strangest houses.

They aren't open every day yet, so check before you come. And if you'd like to see the same hand on a grander scale, drive down to the coast at kahla dee volpay and look at the hotel, with its rough walls and towers, as if a village had been carved rather than built.
