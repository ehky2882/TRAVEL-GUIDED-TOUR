# San Giovanni degli Eremiti, Palermo

*327 words, about 131 seconds at a natural pace.*

## Clean script

Red domes above a garden full of birdsong. Inside, a Romanesque church. Outside, it could almost be in North Africa.

From the garden path leading up to the church, look up. Rising above the plain stone cubes of the building are the domes, rounded and painted a deep red. Around you, the green of the garden, and through it, the arches of a little cloister.

There was a monastery here long before this church. Tradition says Pope Gregory the Great founded one in the five hundreds, and it was destroyed by the Arabs in eight forty-two. Then, from eleven thirty-two, King Roger the Second, the Norman ruler of Sicily, rebuilt it on a royal scale and gave the monastery its title of royal. The church was finished in eleven thirty-six.

That's what makes it so striking. It's Norman, Romanesque, Christian, but built by craftsmen steeped in the Arab architecture of Sicily, so the outside reads like something from the Islamic world. Inside, the nave is divided into square bays, each with its own little dome.

Now about that colour. Those red domes are partly a nineteenth-century idea. Around eighteen eighty, the architect Giuseppe Patricolo stripped off later additions to bring back the medieval look, and he painted the domes red, based on old traces of red plaster he found. It's an interpretation, not a certainty.

[beat]

So you're looking at a medieval building, restored by a nineteenth-century architect to look the way he thought the Middle Ages looked. Which is, I think, part of the fun.

Since twenty fifteen, it's been part of the UNESCO World Heritage site of Arab-Norman Palermo, along with the cathedrals of Cefalù and Monreale.

Before you leave, step into the cloister. It's the best-preserved part of the old monastery, with pairs of slender columns, acanthus capitals and pointed arches around a garden. Look for the old Arab cistern there, too. Then sit for a minute. It's one of the quietest places in Palermo.

## Fact-check notes

- Single-source (Wikipedia); re-check before recording.
- Built 1132–1136 (infobox and timeline). The 'royal' title is from Wikipedia.
- Gregory the Great's 581 founding is a tradition; the 842 destruction 'da parte saracena' is rendered 'by the Arabs'.
- No mosque is mentioned, so it isn't claimed.
- Five domes: Wikipedia says five bays each with a small dome. The script avoids counting the exterior domes.
- The red colour is Patricolo's interpretation from traces of plaster.
- UNESCO since 3 July 2015.
