The story starts with a knight called gal gah no gwee dot tee, who gave up his life as a soldier. On Christmas Day, eleven eighty, so the tradition goes, he drove his sword into the ground on the hill of mon teh syeh pee, meaning to turn it into a cross. He lived there as a hermit until he died the following year.

After him came the sis ter shun monks, who began building this great abbey in twelve eighteen, and it was consecrated in twelve eighty eight. Then came the long decline. One account says that in fifteen fifty an abbot sold off the lead from the roof. The vaults fell in in seventeen eighty one, the bell tower collapsed in seventeen eighty six, and three years later the church was deconsecrated and left open to the weather.

And here's the thing. It's more beautiful as a ruin than it could ever have been with its roof on. The light comes straight in, and the building feels less like a church than like the memory of one.

Now walk up the hill, a few minutes away, to the chapel of mon teh syeh pee. It's a round building, with a dome made of rings of alternating coloured stone, built where gal gah no lived. In the middle of the floor is the rock, and sticking out of it, under a protective cover, the hilt of a sword and a stump of corroded blade.
