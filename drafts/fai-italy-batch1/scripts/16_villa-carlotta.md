# Villa Carlotta

*329 words, about 132 seconds at a natural pace.*

## Clean script

This villa has had a great many owners, and one wedding present. The wedding present is the one whose name stuck.

From the lakeside gates on the road at Tremezzo, look up. The villa sits above you on its terrace, with the garden spreading out on either side and the lake at your back.

It was built at the end of the sixteen hundreds for the Clerici family of Milan. In eighteen oh one it was bought by Giovanni Battista Sommariva, a politician of Napoleon's Cisalpine Republic and a very serious collector. He's the reason the inside is full of sculpture.

Have a look for three things. Canova's Palamedes, which has stood in its room since eighteen eighteen. A version of Canova's Cupid and Psyche, carved by his pupil Adamo Tadolini. And a long marble frieze by the Danish sculptor Thorvaldsen, The Entry of Alexander the Great into Babylon. Then, in the room named after him, Francesco Hayez's painting Romeo and Juliet's Last Kiss, from eighteen twenty-three, which is about as romantic as paint gets.

Now the name. In eighteen forty-three the villa was bought by Princess Marianne of Prussia, and she gave it to her daughter Charlotte when she married Duke Georg of Saxe-Meiningen. Charlotte died young, in eighteen fifty-five, but the villa kept her name. Carlotta is simply Charlotte in Italian.

[beat]

And here's the thing. Sommariva filled the house, but it was the duke who made the garden. He took a hollow that people called the valley of brambles, ran a stream through it, and turned it into a valley of ferns. The rhododendrons and azaleas came later, and they're what most people come for now.

Since nineteen twenty-seven the villa has been run by a public trust, and it's open to everyone.

If you're here in spring, mid-March to mid-April is when the rhododendrons are at their fullest. Whatever the season, though, find the Valley of Ferns. It's the duke's quiet answer to all that marble.

## Fact-check notes

- The Thorvaldsen frieze title is the official one, not 'Triumph of Alexander'.
- The Cupid and Psyche replica dates conflict on the official site (1818–20 vs 1819–24), so no date is given.
- The gift was made 'on the occasion of the wedding' (official site); 1847 and 1850 are Wikipedia-only, so no year is given.
- Ownership: the state took it in 1921 and the Ente Villa Carlotta was recognised in 1927. The script says 'since 1927, a public trust'.
