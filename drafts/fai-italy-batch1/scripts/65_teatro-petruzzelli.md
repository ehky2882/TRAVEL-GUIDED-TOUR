# Teatro Petruzzelli, Bari

*305 words, about 122 seconds at a natural pace.*

## Clean script

The fourth largest theatre in Italy burned down one October night in nineteen ninety-one. It took eighteen years to bring it back.

In the new town of Bari, stand across the street from the theatre and take in its great facade. Inside is a horseshoe of boxes in white and gold, around one of the biggest auditoriums in the country.

It was built by two brothers, Onofrio and Antonio Petruzzelli, merchants and shipowners, and designed by their brother-in-law, the engineer Angelo Cicciomessere. Building began in eighteen ninety-eight, and it opened on the fourteenth of February nineteen oh three, with Meyerbeer's opera Les Huguenots. It had electric light and heating from the start, and its interior was gilded with pure gold leaf.

For most of the twentieth century this was where Bari came to hear the greatest voices of the age. Beniamino Gigli, Renata Tebaldi, Mario Del Monaco, Alfredo Kraus, and later Pavarotti, Domingo and Carreras all sang here, and the great ballet companies danced here.

[beat]

Then, on the night of the twenty-sixth to the twenty-seventh of October nineteen ninety-one, after a performance of Norma, the theatre caught fire. The blaze was judged to be deliberate. When the dome collapsed, it smothered the flames and saved what was left, but the inside was gutted. There followed years of trials and arguments over who owned it.

It was rebuilt as it had been, with modern safety inside, and reopened in October two thousand and nine with Beethoven's Ninth Symphony. That December the opera season began again, with Turandot.

If you can, take one of the short guided tours, about half an hour round the foyer and the stalls. Or better still, buy a ticket for an evening and sit in it. A theatre that came this close to being lost should be heard, not just looked at.

## Fact-check notes

- 'The fourth largest in Italy' is from Wikipedia.
- The brothers' origin: Wikipedia says Bari, the foundation says of Trieste origin, so neither is stated.
- Construction start: Wikipedia says October 1898, the foundation says 23 May 1898. The script says 1898.
- Fire: the night of 26–27 October 1991, judged deliberate. The mafia angle is unsupported, so it's omitted.
- Reopened 4 October 2009 (Beethoven's Ninth); opera on 6 December 2009 (Turandot).
- 'Horseshoe of boxes' is descriptive; the foundation mentions white and gold.
