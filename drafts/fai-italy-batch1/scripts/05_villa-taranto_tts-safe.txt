Just inside the gates, the paths fan out ahead of you down the hillside towards Lake mad jaw ray, with the water glinting through the trees. Everything you're about to walk through was planted on purpose by one man.

He was Captain Neil mack eck ern, born in London in eighteen eighty four to a wealthy Scottish family, and botany was the great passion of his life. Around nineteen thirty he bought a villa here called la kro chet ta, and renamed it Villa ta ran toh, after an ancestor, Marshal mack donald, whom Napoleon had made Duke of ta ran toh. Then he spent the next ten years building a garden.

Have a look for the val let ta, the little ravine. In the winter of nineteen thirty eight, a hundred men dug it out by hand, and it's crossed by a dry stone footbridge thirty five metres long. The water for all the fountains and terraces comes up from the lake, through a system mack eck ern installed in nineteen thirty four.

In the greenhouse is the gift. The giant water lily victoria kroo zee ah na, grown from seed sent from Stockholm in nineteen fifty six. From the middle of June into October its leaves reach about two metres across, and each one can hold around ten kilos.

Here's what I love about this place. mack eck ern built it as a private paradise, and then, after the war, he opened the gates. The public has been coming in since nineteen fifty two, and these days the garden counts its plants in the thousands of species and varieties, gathered from all over the world.

He died in April nineteen sixty four, on the veranda, looking out over what he'd made. He's buried in the garden, in the small mausoleum chapel built the following year.
