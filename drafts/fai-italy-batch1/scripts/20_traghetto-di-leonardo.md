# Traghetto di Leonardo

*320 words, about 128 seconds at a natural pace.*

## Clean script

No engine, no fuel, and it's been taking people across this river for centuries.

At the landing in Imbersago, look out at the Adda. Stretched right across the water is a steel cable, and hanging from it is a wide wooden ferry, flat-decked, with a single ferryman aboard. On the far bank is Villa d'Adda, in another province.

Here's how it works. The ferry is tied to the cable, and the ferryman angles the boat against the current with its rudder. The river pushes on one side of the hull and simply shoves it across. He uses an iron pole to push off. It takes about five minutes, and it works because the river never stops.

It's called Leonardo's ferry, and he did know this river well. He stayed downstream at Vaprio d'Adda with the Melzi family in the early fifteen hundreds, and there's a drawing of his, now in the Royal Collection at Windsor, of the Adda with a ferry tied to a cable. But to be honest, he didn't invent it. Ferries like this were common all along the river, and there were five on the Adda at one time. This is the one that survived.

[beat]

What's documented is that a cable crossing ran here by sixteen seventy-eight. The boat you're looking at is younger. The old one was taken out of service as unsafe in nineteen ninety-one, and Venetian shipwrights built an exact copy, which began crossing in May nineteen ninety-four. Pope John the Twenty-Third used this ferry on his pilgrimages to the sanctuary up the hill.

Since the bridge at Brivio closed for works in twenty twenty-six, the ferry has gone back to being what it always was, a way to get to work.

So take the crossing, if it's running. Then, on the Imbersago side, walk up to the Santuario della Madonna del Bosco, the sanctuary the Pope was making for. It's worth the climb.

## Fact-check notes

- Leonardo's stay at Vaprio (1506–07) and the Windsor drawing are from Wikipedia and Euronews; both say he didn't invent the ferry.
- Wikipedia ties the Windsor drawing to the Vaprio/Canonica crossing. The script says only 'a ferry tied to a cable'.
- The current boat: the old one was blocked in December 1991 and the replica was inaugurated on 22 May 1994 (Wikipedia).
- The operator conflicts (municipal concession vs volunteers), so the operator isn't stated.
- Weekday running since the Brivio bridge closed in May 2026 is from Euronews; check it before recording.
- The Luoghi del Cuore link is unverified, so it isn't mentioned.
