Villa Fogazzaro Roi

Every Italian schoolchild reads a novel set in this house. The house kept the evidence.

You're in Oria, on the Italian shore of Lake Lugano, about ten kilometres from Lugano itself. The villa sits right on the water, with a hanging garden behind and a little terrace jutting out over the lake.

The writer was Antonio Fogazzaro, from Vicenza, who spent much of his life here. The house came down through his mother's family, and it became the setting for his best-known book, Piccolo mondo antico, The Little World of the Past, published in eighteen ninety-six. It's the story of Franco and Luisa Maironi, and much of it happens in these rooms.

That little terrace with the small cupola and the round iron table is Franco's terrace in the novel. The sweet olive in the hanging garden is in the book too. And the boat landing below the house is where the story reaches its saddest moment, when their little daughter Ombretta drowns.

Have a look for Fogazzaro's writing desk. He wrote notes all over it, some on the very edges of the wood, and one of them records the day he finished the book, the eleventh of August, eighteen ninety-five: finished in tears. That same year his own son Mariano died of typhus, and there's a note on the desk to him as well.

What I find rather moving is how little this place pretends. The rooms are small. There's a salon the family called Siberia, because it was impossible to heat. It's a real house that a great writer borrowed for a story, and then the story gave it back to us.

His great-nephew Giuseppe Roi, known as Boso, restored it and gave it to the FAI in two thousand and nine, so that nothing would change after he'd gone. Visits are by guided tour.

When you leave, stand out on the terrace for a moment and look across the water. That's the view Franco looked at, and the view Fogazzaro sat down to describe.
