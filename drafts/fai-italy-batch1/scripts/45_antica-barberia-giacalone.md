# Antica Barberia Giacalone, Genoa

*287 words, about 115 seconds at a natural pace.*

## Clean script

Ten square metres, coloured glass on every surface, and you can still get a shave.

You're on Vicolo dei Caprettari, one of the narrow caruggi of old Genoa. Look for the small shop window glittering in the alley. That's the barber's.

A barber called Giacalone opened it in eighteen eighty-two. In nineteen twenty-two his son Italo decided to redo the whole interior, and what he made is the shop you see now. The walls and the ceiling are covered with coloured glass and oval mirrors. There are Liberty lamps, and at the back the plain white tiles every proper barber's shop has. It's the size of a small bedroom, about ten square metres, and every one of them is decorated.

The Giacalone family kept it until nineteen ninety-two. When Italo died, the shop was at real risk of being lost. So there was a public appeal, the people of the city chipped in, and the shop was bought from his heirs.

[beat]

And then something rather sensible happened. Nobody turned the shop into a museum. It restored it and kept it working, so it's still a barber's, still cutting the hair of Genoese regulars and curious visitors, still doing the job it was built for in eighteen eighty-two.

I think that's the best kind of conservation. Not a room behind a rope, but a room that's still being used for the thing it's good at.

If you've got a quarter of an hour and a chin that needs attention, go in and ask for a shave. If not, stand at the window for a minute and watch the light bounce round that glass. It's one of the smallest treasures in the city, and one of the most cheerful.

## Fact-check notes

- The founder's first name isn't given.
- Art Déco (1922) and Liberty lamps are both FAI's terms.
- Bought by FAI from the heirs in 1992 via a public subscription promoted by the FAI Genoa delegation.
- Still working: the English page gives Tuesday to Saturday, 10:00–18:30. 'Ask for a shave' assumes shaves are offered; check it.
- No chairs or carved wood are mentioned in the sources, so they are omitted.
