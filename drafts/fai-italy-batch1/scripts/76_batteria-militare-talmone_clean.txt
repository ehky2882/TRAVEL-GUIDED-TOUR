Batteria Militare Talmone, Palau

For around two centuries, soldiers kept watch from this headland over one of the most beautiful stretches of sea in the Mediterranean.

You're at Punta Don Diego, between Palau and Costa Serena, on the north-east coast of Sardinia. In front of you is the Maddalena archipelago, and just across the water the island of Spargi. The battery was built to guard the channel between Spargi and Sardinia.

Walk in and you'll see what's left of the military life here: a lookout tower, gun positions aimed out to sea, and the soldiers' barracks and service rooms, which the FAI has restored so you can picture the daily routine of the men posted to this lonely spot.

Talmone was one link in a chain. Around fifty forts, small forts and batteries were built across the Maddalena archipelago to defend it. Its importance grew after Italy was unified, when the royal navy made La Maddalena one of its bases, and the battery became part of the watch over the fleet.

[beat]

And then it simply stopped. After the Second World War, the peace treaty required Italy to dismantle its military batteries, and Talmone was abandoned for decades. Nature crept back in.

In two thousand and two the Region of Sardinia entrusted it to the FAI, and the restoration is still going on. Visits are by guided tour, on request, so get in touch with the FAI to arrange one.

The walk out to the battery is part of the pleasure: half an hour or so along a path through the Mediterranean scrub, myrtle, bay and juniper, with the sea glittering below. When you reach the end, find the shelter that looks out over the water, and stand where the lookouts stood. You'll see exactly what they were guarding, and why you'd never tire of the view.
