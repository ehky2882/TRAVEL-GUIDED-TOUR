In November nineteen seventeen, the front line of the First World War came up this mountain. A century later, the cows are coming back.

You're on the high pastures of the mon tay grap pa massif, between the peaks of mon tay val deh ro ah, mon tay so la ro lo and mon tay fon tah na sek ka. Look around for the low stone buildings of the mal ga, the summer dairy farm, and then look out. On a clear day the view runs down the valley of the pyah veh all the way to the Venetian lagoon.

These hundred and fifty hectares of meadow and woodland were given to the fy in twenty fifteen by broo no and lee lee ah na kol lah vo, in memory of their parents, al doh kol lah vo and air mee nee ah sek ko.

But first, the war. After the defeat at ka po ret toh in the autumn of nineteen seventeen, Italy's line fell back to the Grappa and the pyah veh. On the twenty first and twenty second of November, the summit here was taken by the Austro German army, after it had been defended by the Alpine troops of the val leh ka mo nee ka battalion. If you walk the paths you'll still find trenches, tunnels and memorial stones. The ridge route that links them is called the al ta vee ah del yee eh roy, the High Way of the Heroes, and it leads to the great war memorial on the summit of chee ma grap pa.

What the fy is doing here is quietly ambitious. Not preserving a ruin, but bringing a working mountain farm back to life, with no mains water and no mains electricity. The old herdsman's house has been restored and opened as a place to tell the story of the mountain, with a film narrated by the actress mee keh la chess kon. The old stalls have been rebuilt to welcome visitors. And the pastures are meant for the boor lee na, a cow found nowhere but the ven eh toh, and close to disappearing.

Up here the dairy makes the local mountain cheeses, mor lak ko and bas tar doh.

Before you come, check what's open with the fy, because the work is still going on. And then walk a stretch of the al ta vee ah del yee eh roy. It's an extraordinary thing, to stand in a meadow full of cows and see a line of trenches running through it.
