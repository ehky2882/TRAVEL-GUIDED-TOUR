Kwai Chai Hong

Kwai Chai Hong means little ghost lane. The ghosts, it's said, were the children who used to play here.

You're at the entrance to Kwai Chai Hong, off Lorong Panggung, in Chinatown. In front of you is a narrow back lane between two rows of old shophouses, with murals painted on the walls.

For decades this was what back lanes usually are: the service side of a row of shops, used for drains, deliveries and rubbish. Then in 2018, a group of five local entrepreneurs who had formed a company called Bai Chuan Management took it on, with a grant from Think City. They restored ten shophouses and the lane behind them, and opened it to the public in 2019.

Have a look at the murals. They show everyday scenes of life in Chinatown in the 1960s, painted by five local artists, and they're placed where you'd have seen such things in real life. Some of them carry a code you can scan to hear a soundtrack or story.

The gift detail is in the history of the lane itself. Until 2018, one of the shops facing it was Ho Kow, a Hainanese coffee shop that had been in this spot since 1956. It moved round the corner the year before the lane reopened.

[beat]

What I like about this place is the idea behind it. Back lanes are where a city keeps its secrets, and where its ordinary life goes on. Kwai Chai Hong turns that hidden life into the main event, and asks you to look at the back of a building as carefully as the front.

When you've walked the lane, step round to Jalan Balai Polis and find Ho Kow, now on the main street. Order a coffee and a bowl of soft-boiled eggs, and you'll have the whole story.
