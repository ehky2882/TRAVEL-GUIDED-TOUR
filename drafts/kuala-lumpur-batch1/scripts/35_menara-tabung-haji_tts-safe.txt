Meh nah rah Tah boong Hah jee

This tower is a savings bank for one purpose only: getting to Mecca.

You're on Jah lahn Toon Rah zak, a little east of the Petronas Towers. Find the rounded tower with five tall white columns standing around it and rising up its sides. It looks less like an office building than an enormous drum.

Tah boong Hah jee is the pilgrims' fund. It was set up so that Malaysian Muslims could save, a little at a time, towards the cost of the hajj, the pilgrimage to Mecca. Its headquarters was designed by Hee jahs Kahs too ree, one of the most important Malaysian architects of his generation, and completed in the mid eighties.

Hee jahs later said this was one of the hardest buildings he ever designed, because the client wanted the form itself to express what the institution was for. So look at the five columns. They stand for the five pillars of Islam, the five duties of every Muslim, of which the hajj is one.

Then look at the shape of the whole. It's often said to recall a drum, the kind traditionally beaten to call pilgrims together for the journey.

And here's the thing. Most office towers in the eighties were trying to look like they belonged in New York. This one was trying to look like it belonged to its purpose. You can argue about whether symbolism makes good architecture, but there's no mistaking what this building is for.

Before you go, walk round to the other side for a clearer view of the columns against the tower. And keep this building in mind: Hee jahs Kahs too ree also designed the keh riss shaped Meh nah rah Toon Peh rak, the old Maybank tower. Seeing both is the quickest lesson in his thinking.
