Tugu Negara (National Monument)

If this sculpture reminds you of the flag-raising on Iwo Jima, there's a good reason.

You're at the National Monument, Tugu Negara, on Jalan Parlimen, in the Lake Gardens. In front of you, across a reflecting pool, is a large bronze sculpture of soldiers on a rocky base, with one of them holding up a flag.

After a visit to the United States in 1960, Tunku Abdul Rahman, Malaysia's first prime minister, saw the Marine Corps War Memorial outside Washington and wanted something similar for his own country. He met its sculptor, Felix de Weldon, and commissioned him. The result was unveiled in February 1966.

Have a look at the figures. There are seven of them. Five are standing: one holds the flag, one has a rifle, one a machine gun, and two are helping a wounded comrade. The two lying on the ground represent defeated communist insurgents. The monument commemorates those who died in the Second World War and in the Malayan Emergency.

The gift detail is in the stones. The rocks under the soldiers' feet were imported from Karlshamn, in Sweden.

The monument has a dark chapter of its own. On 26 August 1975, a bomb planted by the Malayan Communist Party exploded here and badly damaged the sculpture. It was restored and unveiled again in 1977.

[beat]

What's worth thinking about, standing here, is how strange this monument is. It's a Malaysian memorial made by an Austrian-American sculptor, modelled on an American war memorial, to remember a war against an enemy who then tried to blow it up. It tells you a great deal about the Cold War, and about the young nation trying to find its own way through it.

Before you leave, look for the older cenotaph nearby, which was moved here from its original site near the railway station in 1964. Then walk on to the Perdana Botanical Garden; the path down from here takes you into the shade.
