Per poo stah kah ahn Neh gah rah Malaysia National Library

The roof of this library is shaped like a hat, and that's meant as a compliment.

You're on Jah lahn Toon Rah zak, outside the National Library of Malaysia. Look for the building with a tall, peaked, tiled roof folded up into points, like a crown, and patterned all over.

The library moved into this building in February nineteen ninety three, after many years at Jah lahn Rah jah Lowt. It was designed by the firm Koom poo lahn Ah kee tek, and the idea behind it is simple: the roof is modelled on the teng koh lok, the folded cloth headdress worn by Malay men on formal occasions and by royalty.

Have a look at the roof up close. The tiles carry patterns inspired by song ket, the traditional Malay cloth woven with gold or silver thread. So the building is dressed, quite literally, in a headdress made of fabric.

Why a headdress? In Malay culture, the teng koh lok signifies respect for learning and for achievement. Putting it on a library is a way of saying that knowledge deserves to be crowned.

What's worth knowing is that this building belongs to a particular moment, in the eighties and nineties, when Malaysian architects were trying hard to find a national style. Some looked to Islamic geometry, as at Dye ah boo mee. Some looked to traditional houses. This library looked to dress, and to the pride of formal occasions. Whether you find it charming or a little literal, it's a building that knows exactly what it wants to say.

Before you leave, walk round the side of the building to see the roof from another angle. The points look quite different from the side, and the folded shape is much easier to read.
