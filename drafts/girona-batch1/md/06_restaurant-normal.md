# 06 · Restaurant Normal

*310 words · about 124 seconds at an unhurried pace*

## Clean script

The Roca brothers called this restaurant Normal, which is a fairly bold thing to do when you have three Michelin stars.

You are in the Plaça de l'Oli, the square of oil, in the old town. Find number one, a stone building from the seventeenth century, with the restaurant on the ground floor.

Normal opened on the ninth of July, twenty twenty one, as a celebration of everyday Catalan cooking, with Josep Roca directing it and Elisabet Nolla as head chef when it opened. The food is the kind of thing a Catalan family might cook at home, done very well.

The interior is worth a look on its own. It was designed by Andreu Carulla, a Girona designer who has worked with the brothers since two thousand and nine, and he made every piece of furniture for it. There are booths of French oak, and leather dining chairs based on the traditional lattice backed chairs of Spanish kitchens. Ceramic lamps were made by local potters. Then there's the detail I love: the countertops are terrazzo made from recycled glass from the brothers' own restaurant, El Celler. The signs are cut by hand into the limestone floor. Local ceramicists, carpenters and basket weavers all worked on it.

What's worth knowing is that this is a statement as much as a menu. After years of avant garde cooking, the most famous restaurant family in Spain opened a place devoted to the ordinary, and built it with craftspeople from the same corner of Catalonia. The furniture makes the same argument as the food. Normal, here, means made nearby, made carefully, and made to last.

[beat]

From the square, wander a few steps north into the streets around the cathedral. The stone walls you see there are the same stone that lines the dining room, and the old town is the restaurant's real reference.
