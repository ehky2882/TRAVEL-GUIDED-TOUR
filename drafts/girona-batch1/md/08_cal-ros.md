# 08 · Cal Ros

*316 words · about 126 seconds at an unhurried pace*

## Clean script

This is one of the oldest restaurant names in the city, under some of its oldest arches.

You are in the old town just behind the river, where Carrer de la Cort Reial meets the stone arcades known as the Voltes d'en Rosés. Look for Cal Ros at number nine, tucked under the arches. Take a moment with the stone around you, because the arches are the point.

This building has a long history of feeding people. It is described as an old inn and a meeting place for merchants, and by most accounts the name Cal Ros has been served here for more than a century. In April twenty twenty five it began a new chapter, when the Gascons family, who run the Michelin starred Els Tinars, opened it as Fonda Cal Ros. Marc Gascons runs the kitchen, and his sister Elena runs the dining room.

The idea is very simple and very Catalan. The food comes in courses meant for sharing. Tomatoes hang from the ceiling, ready to be rubbed onto bread. Wine, and even beer, arrives in a porrón, the glass jug with a long spout that you pour straight into your mouth from a height. The house butifarra, the Catalan sausage, is made from Duroc pork, and there are dishes on a blackboard that change with the market each week.

What I like is that the family describes it as reviving something they did back in the nineteen nineties, and keeping the porrón because it was part of how they grew up. In a city now famous for its tasting menus, this is the other half of Catalan eating: loud, generous, shared, and a bit messy.

[beat]

Before you go in, or after, walk the length of the arcade. The vaulted walkway runs along the backs of the river houses, and it shows you how merchants once did business out of the rain.
