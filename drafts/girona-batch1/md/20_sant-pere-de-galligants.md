# 20 · Església de Sant Pere de Galligants

*299 words · about 120 seconds at an unhurried pace*

## Clean script

This monastery was built outside the city walls, by a stream, and it has outlasted almost everything inside them.

You are at the north end of the old town, outside a plain Romanesque church with a squat bell tower, beside the little Galligants stream. Look for the round window high in the front. That is Sant Pere de Galligants, and today it houses the Girona branch of the Museum of Archaeology of Catalonia.

The monastery dates back to nine ninety two, when the count of Barcelona handed control of this little quarter to its abbot. The church you see was finished around eleven thirty one. It ran as a monastery for centuries until the monks left in the eighteen thirties. Later it became a museum, and in nineteen thirty one it was declared a national monument.

Go in and find the cloister. Its double columns carry more than fifty carved capitals, with lions, sirens, strange beasts and scenes from the life of Christ. They are close cousins of the carvings in the cathedral cloister up the hill. The archaeology collection fills the church and cloister around them, objects from prehistory to the Middle Ages laid out among the arches.

What's worth knowing is why the building looks so solid. Outside the walls a monastery was exposed, and it needed to look after itself. It's tempting to read that into the church, which feels rather like a small fortress. It has survived sieges, the loss of its monks and two centuries of changing use, and it has done so by being very, very sturdy.

[beat]

From the front door, follow the stream uphill and away from the town. Within a few minutes you are in the valley of Sant Daniel, all streams and woods, and the city simply disappears behind you.
