# 16 · Mercat del Lleó

If you want to know what Girona actually eats, rather than what it serves visitors, this is where you look.

You are on the west bank of the river, at the Plaça Calvet i Rubalcaba, outside the city's covered market. That is the Mercat del Lleó, the Lion Market.

It opened in the mid nineteen forties as the first municipal market in the city, and it has been feeding the town ever since. Inside you will find the produce of the region: fruit and vegetables from the market gardens, fish from the Costa Brava, cured meats and cheeses from the hills, and seasonal things like wild mushrooms in the autumn.

Have a look for the market bar when you go in. Bars inside the market have been serving traders and shoppers for most of its life, and they are the best place to eat something hot at the counter early in the day. This is the opposite of a tasting menu. You point at something, it is cooked, and you eat it standing up next to someone doing their weekly shop.

And here's the thing. Girona has become famous for its restaurants, but every one of the cooks you have heard of depends on what comes through markets like this one. The great kitchens rest on the stalls. If you want to understand the food scene here, a morning at the market will tell you more than a dinner.
