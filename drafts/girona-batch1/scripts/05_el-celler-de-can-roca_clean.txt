El Celler de Can Roca

On a quiet residential street on the edge of Girona sits a restaurant that has twice been named the best in the world.

You are in Taialà, a neighbourhood on the western edge of the city, on Can Sunyer street. Find the house at number forty-eight, set back behind its garden. There is no great fanfare at the gate, and that rather sets the tone.

The story starts about a hundred metres from here. In nineteen sixty seven the Roca parents opened a family restaurant called Can Roca, serving everyday food to the neighbourhood. In nineteen eighty six their three sons opened El Celler next door. Joan is the head chef, Josep the sommelier, and Jordi is in charge of desserts. The restaurant earned its first Michelin star in nineteen ninety five, its second in two thousand and two, and its third in two thousand and nine. In two thousand and thirteen, and again in two thousand and fifteen, it was ranked number one on the World's Fifty Best Restaurants list.

In two thousand and seven they moved into this house. Inside, the Barcelona interior designers Isabel López Vilalta and Sandra Tarruella, working with Ricard Trenchs, organised the dining room in a triangle around a small courtyard of silver birch trees, with glass walls on every side. So you eat around a little forest. The light comes through the leaves, and the seasons are part of the room.

And here's the thing. For all the stars, the family never left the street they grew up on. The parents' restaurant is still around the corner, still serving a set daily menu at ordinary prices. Few places in the world let you see the whole arc of a cooking family in a single stroll.

[beat]

If you have a few minutes, walk the short distance to the original Can Roca and look in. Same family, same neighbourhood, a very different bill, and it is the best way to understand where all of this came from.
