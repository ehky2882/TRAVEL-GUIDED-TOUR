la Fabrika

If you have ever wondered why there are so many people in Lycra drinking flat whites in this city, it begins here.

You are on Karreh duh la Yebbruh, a short lane in the old town, just up from the river. Find number three, and you will probably find a row of very expensive bicycles leaning outside before you find the door.

la Fabrika opened in twenty fifteen. It was the first specialty coffee shop in Jeerohna, set up by Christian Myer, a Canadian professional cyclist, and his wife Amber. They had moved here from Vancouver when Christian signed his first professional contract, because Jeerohna had quietly become a training base for racing cyclists from all over the world. The mountains are close, the roads are quiet, and the weather is kind for most of the year.

Have a look at the walls when you go in. They are hung with cycling photographs by the Belgian photographer Kristof Rahmon, and inside it feels cosy and fairly dark, a proper refuge after a long morning in the hills. The menu is substantial, because people who have just ridden a hundred kilometres are very hungry.

And here's the thing. This one small cafe helped to set the tone for a whole part of the city's life. Before it, Jeerohna was a historic town that happened to have cyclists. After it, cycling culture had a home in the old town, with coffee to match, and other places followed. It is a reminder that a city's character can be changed by one couple with a good idea.

When you leave, walk a few minutes to Espresso Mafia on Karreh duh la Kort Rayal. It belongs to the same couple and it is where they roast the coffee, so the two together tell you the whole story.
