Rokambolesk

A pastry chef with three Michelin stars opened an ice cream shop, and he did it with a sense of mischief.

You are on Karreh duh Santa Klara, on the west bank of the Oonyar, right by the red iron bridge. Find number fifty. There will very likely be a queue, and that is a fair sign you are in the right place.

Rokambolesk opened in April twenty twelve. Jordee Rokka, the pastry chef of El Suhyay duh Kan Rokka, created it with Ahleh Reevas. The idea began as a dessert trolley. The plan was to take the restaurant's little cart of sweets out onto the streets of Jeerohna. In the end they gave it a permanent home instead.

Have a look at the interior. It was designed by the Barcelona studio of Sandra Tarrooayya as a sort of theatre, with tubes and taps that make the counter look rather like a laboratory. That's no accident. Everything is developed in Jordee Rokka's own pastry workshop, where they mix old techniques with new ones. Some of the toppings and shapes come straight from his restaurant desserts.

And here's the thing. A shop like this is the easiest way to taste what El Suhyay is about. You don't need a reservation months ahead or a serious bill, just a few minutes in a queue on a pavement by the river. It is the most democratic thing the family makes.

With your ice cream in hand, step onto the red iron bridge a few paces away. From the middle you have the river houses on both sides and the cathedral above, which makes it one of the best spots in town to stand still and eat.
