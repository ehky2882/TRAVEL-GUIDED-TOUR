Kasa Massoh

Among all the coloured houses on the river, the white one is where Jeerohna's great architect was born.

You are on Karreh duh less Bayustereeus, in the old town, outside number twenty nine. On the street side it is fairly modest. To see it properly, though, you need its river front, so either picture it from the bridges or go in, because the house runs right through to the water. It's the white house in the row of colours on the Oonyar.

This is the birthplace of Rafa el Massoh, who lived from eighteen eighty to nineteen thirty five and was the leading architect of Nowsenteezmuh in Jeerohna. That was the Catalan movement of the early twentieth century, which turned away from the swirling, extravagant lines of Moderneezmuh towards something calmer, more classical and more Mediterranean. The house was made by joining four houses that his family bought between the mid nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, and what you see today follows the last changes he made, in nineteen nineteen.

Inside, the furniture and decoration of the period are still in place, with views right over the river and the city. Since two thousand and six it has belonged to the city council, and it's run by the Rafa el Massoh Foundation. Visits are by guided tour and must be booked ahead.

And here's the thing. Almost every one of those river houses is a private home, seen only from the outside. This is the one chance to stand at a window, looking out over the Oonyar, from inside the most famous view in Jeerohna.

Afterwards, look for Massoh's other work around the city. The Fareenaira Tusheedor, his flour mill on Karreh duh Santa Ayoozhenya on the west bank, is the most striking, and it is a good fifteen minutes' walk away.
