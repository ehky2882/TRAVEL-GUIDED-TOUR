ATLAS — CHICAGO
W5: PILSEN — EIGHTEENTH STREET, EAST TO WEST
Segment 01 — Casa Aztlan
Position: Eighteenth and Racine, looking south
---

The mural on this building had a title. Hay Cultura en Nuestra Comunidad. There Is Culture in Our Community.

You should be at Eighteenth and Racine, looking south down Racine at the three-story brick building a few doors along — the one carrying painted figures across its front.

It opened in 1905 as Howell Neighborhood House, a settlement house for the Czechs and Poles and Italians packed into these blocks: English classes, a clinic, somewhere to leave your children. By 1970 the families here were largely Mexican, many of them displaced from the Near West Side by an expressway and a university campus. The settlement house took a new name that year: Casa Aztlan. Aztlan is the northern homeland the Mexica described migrating out of, centuries earlier — so a community center on Racine Avenue took the name of a place out of a migration account.

Ray Patlan painted the interior in 1970, while he was still an art student, and the front between 1971 and 1973, working with about forty neighborhood teenagers on a city youth-jobs program. Patlan was born in Chicago; his parents came from Guanajuato. The faces they put up here — Cuauhtemoc, Benito Juarez, Frida Kahlo — belonged to people who had never set foot in Illinois, and in one case had been dead four hundred years. None of the painters had seen any of them either; they were working from pictures of pictures.

[beat]

Nothing on this wall originated on this wall. The iconography came north, the name came out of a migration story, the building came out of a Protestant settlement movement, and a Chicago-born art student put the three together on one facade. Faithful reproduction was never the assignment.

The center closed in 2013 and the building holds apartments now. The front was painted over in 2017 and repainted later that year by Patlan and the muralist Roberto Valadez. Patlan died in 2024.

Walk west — barely a block, and Eighteenth crosses Allport. Stop where you can see the limestone building on the corner and the church facing it.
