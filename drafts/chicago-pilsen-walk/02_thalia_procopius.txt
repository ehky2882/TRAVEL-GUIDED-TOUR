ATLAS — CHICAGO
W5: PILSEN — EIGHTEENTH STREET, EAST TO WEST
Segment 02 — Thalia Hall and Saint Procopius
Position: Eighteenth and Allport
---

The first Catholic church on this corner was not built on this corner.

You're at Eighteenth and Allport. The heavy limestone building with the corner turret and the storefronts underneath is Thalia Hall. The church is across Eighteenth Street: Saint Procopius.

In the summer of 1875 a committee of Czech residents bought three lots here for thirty-six hundred dollars. A Methodist congregation at Nineteenth and Halsted wanted to put up something in brick and offered its old frame church for two thousand dollars. The committee bought it, had the whole building moved four blocks west and a block over to this corner, using the upper floor for worship and the lower for a school. First Mass, Easter Sunday, 1876. The brick church came in the 1880s, and Czech Benedictines took the parish soon after.

Thalia Hall went up in 1892 for John Dusek, who kept a saloon. Faber and Pagels designed it, and by most accounts they modeled the theater inside on the Old Opera House in Prague. The name comes from Thalia, the Greek muse of comedy. The shops at street level and the flats above them were not an afterthought — halls like this were built with rent attached, because the rent is what kept the theater lit. In 1895 a Shakespearean actor from Prague staged Romeo and Juliet in that room, in Czech.

Not everyone here was in the church. Czech freethinkers in Chicago had organized their own congregation in 1870 — it held weddings and namings, ran schools, and set up gymnastic clubs, the sokols, two of which stood within a few blocks of here. The Benedictine press at Saint Procopius argued with them in print for years. Both sides had crossed the same ocean out of the same country.

[beat]

What came over on those ships was not only people and not only money. It was a floor plan, a Greek muse, a quarrel about God, and a church that somebody else had finished with.

Keep west on Eighteenth about five blocks, to Paulina and the elevated station.
