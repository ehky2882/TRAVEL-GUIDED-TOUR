ATLAS — CHICAGO
W5: PILSEN — EIGHTEENTH STREET, EAST TO WEST
Segment 00 — Intro
Position: Eighteenth and Halsted, facing west
---

There is a city in western Bohemia, four thousand miles east of this corner, that Czechs call Plzen and Germans call Pilsen.

Chicago took the German version, and took it secondhand. Sometime in the 1870s a Bohemian man opened a restaurant on this street and named it, in Czech, At the City of Plzen — a nostalgic name, the kind you give a place when you are not going home. Neighbors started calling the whole district by the restaurant's name, and the post office made it permanent by christening its local branch Pilsen Station.

Nothing on this street arrived intact. Over the next mile you'll pass a Catholic church that was bought used and dragged here, an opera house built out of somebody's memory of Prague, an Aztec calendar stone rendered in house paint on an elevated platform, and a museum whose stated frame is art without borders. All of it came from somewhere else, and all of it changed on the way. The changing is the part worth walking for.

You want the corner of Eighteenth and Halsted, facing west. From here the street runs straight, and every stop sits on it or within a block of it — a little over a mile, flat the whole way, four stops.

Walk west to Racine. Stop at the corner and look south; the brick building with the figures painted across its front is a few doors along.
