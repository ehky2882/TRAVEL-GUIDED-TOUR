bo vo lo is Venetian for snail. Step into this courtyard, look up, and you'll see why.

kor tay kon ta ree nee del bo vo lo, in San Marco, tucked behind kampo ma neen, a few minutes north of the ree al toe route. It is easy to miss. Look for the little signposted lane leading into a small courtyard. When you get there, there it is in front of you: a tall cylinder of white arches spiralling up the side of an old brick pa lat so.

The pa lat so was built in the fifteenth century for a branch of the kon ta ree nee family. In fourteen ninety nine they added this outside staircase to it. The design is usually credited to jor jo spa ven toe, though some guides name jo vah nee kan dee, who worked on the pa lat so itself.

Have a look at how it climbs. The arches step up in a continuous spiral, round and round, until they open into a lo ja at the very top. It is part Gothic and part Renaissance, the moment in Venice when one style was turning into the next.

The interesting bit is why it is on the outside. Staircases were usually hidden inside the building. This one was put out here, where the whole courtyard could see it, for one reason: to show off. The kon ta ree nee were one of the great families of Venice, and this was their statement.

Orson Welles saw the theatre in it. In his nineteen fifty two film of Othello, he used the pa lat so as the house of bra ban tee oh, Desdemona's father. The staircase features prominently.

The staircase was restored and reopened to the public in twenty sixteen, and you can climb it. Go up to the lo ja at the top. The view over the roofs of San Marco is quiet and close, rather than grand. You look down onto terraces, chimneys and washing, with the domes of the basilica just beyond.
