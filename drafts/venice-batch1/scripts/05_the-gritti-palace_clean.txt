The Gritti Palace

A doge lived here. Centuries later, Ernest Hemingway treated it rather like home.

Campo Santa Maria del Giglio, just off the busy route from San Marco to the Accademia. The hotel entrance is on this side, but the palazzo really faces the other way, onto the Grand Canal, with the great white dome of the Salute directly across the water. If you can, walk through to the waterside terrace, or look from the traghetto landing nearby.

It was built in fourteen seventy-five for the Pisani family. In fifteen twenty-five it became the private residence of Andrea Gritti, Doge of Venice. His name stuck to the building long after he had gone.

It became a hotel in the eighteen nineties, and it learned to look after writers. Hemingway was a regular, and the presidential suite is named after him. There are suites for Somerset Maugham, and for John Ruskin, who wrote The Stones of Venice. The whole building was carefully restored in twenty thirteen.

Here's what I love about this spot. Look across at the Salute and think about who has sat here doing exactly what you're doing. The view has hardly changed. And the gondola ferry still carries people across the canal a few steps from here, as it has for centuries.

[beat]

If the terrace is open, it is one of the best places in Venice to sit at dusk, even if you aren't staying. If not, look for the traghetto, the standing ferry gondola, at the landing by the campo. For a couple of euros you can cross the Grand Canal standing up, the way Venetians do.
