It was built to sell typewriters. It ended up as a calling card for an entire company, in what its owner called the most beautiful square in the world.

Piazza San Marco, north side, under the arcades of the pro koo rah tee eh vek ee eh, about halfway along the square. Look for a narrow shopfront with dark wood, glass and the name olly vetty. Through the window you'll see a staircase that seems to float.

In nineteen fifty seven, Adriano olly vetty, the industrialist behind Italy's most celebrated typewriters, asked karlo scarpa to design a showroom here. olly vetty didn't want a shop. He wanted, as he put it, a calling card in the most beautiful square in the world. Scarpa finished it in nineteen fifty eight.

Now have a look at that staircase. Its steps are slabs of pale ow ree zee na stone, staggered, each one appearing to hang in the air. It is the heart of the room, and the reason you'll remember it.

At the entrance is a bronze sculpture by al bair toe vee ah nee, Nude in the Sun, suspended over a basin of black marble with a film of water across it. And under your feet, a mosaic of small glass tiles, set so that the pattern stays visible even when high water comes in from the square. In this square, it often does.

What's worth knowing is that Scarpa never treated anything as minor. A door handle, a light fitting or the edge of a step each got as much thought as the whole. Even the door is a slab of stone, made to disappear into the wall.

The shop is cared for by FAI, the Italian national trust, and it is open to visitors. Inside are typewriters and calculating machines from the nineteen fifties and sixties. Have a look at one of the old typewriters and try to see it the way olly vetty did: as something as carefully designed as the room around it.
