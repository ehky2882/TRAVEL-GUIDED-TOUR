# Fondazione Giorgio Cini

**Visit** · Google Maps: *Fondazione Giorgio Cini* · 45.429283, 12.342414  
~301 words · about 2:00 at a natural pace

---

This whole island was rescued by a father in memory of his son.

You're on San Giorgio Maggiore, the island directly across the water from San Marco. Stand on the quay with Palladio's white church to one side and the long monastery buildings behind it. The church is the part everyone sees from the Riva. The monastery, which most visitors never enter, is the Fondazione Giorgio Cini.

For centuries this was a powerful Benedictine monastery. Then came Napoleon. The monks were driven out, and the island was used by the military and left to decay for almost a hundred and fifty years.

In nineteen fifty-one, the industrialist Vittorio Cini set up a foundation to restore the whole complex. He named it after his son Giorgio, who had been killed in a plane crash in nineteen forty-nine. The idea was to bring the island back to life as a centre for scholarship and culture, and it has been one ever since.

Have a look at what was saved. There are two cloisters, one of them by Palladio. There is Palladio's refectory, the monks' great dining hall, for which Paolo Veronese painted his enormous Wedding at Cana. The original now hangs in the Louvre, a souvenir of Napoleon's visit. And there is a library and a grand staircase by Baldassare Longhena, the architect of the Salute.

What's worth knowing is that the foundation's spaces are visited only on guided tours. So the island stays quiet even when San Marco, five minutes across the water, is at its busiest.

[beat]

Book the guided tour if you can. If you have time afterwards, look for Le Stanze del Vetro, the foundation's glass gallery, also on the island. Then take the lift up the church bell tower next door for the best view in Venice.
