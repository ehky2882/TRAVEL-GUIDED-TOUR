# Aman Venice

**Stay** · Google Maps: *Aman Venice* · 45.436702, 12.331960  
~353 words · about 2:21 at a natural pace

---

There's a curious thing about this palazzo. It is a luxury hotel, but the family who own it still live upstairs.

The best view is from the water, but from the land you'll find the entrance on Calle Tiepolo, in San Polo, a few minutes' walk from the Rialto Bridge. This is Palazzo Papadopoli. Its front faces the Grand Canal, and two pointed obelisks stand on its roof. In the old Republic, obelisks like that were the mark of a family that had produced a great naval commander.

It was built in the mid sixteenth century by the architect Gian Giacomo de' Grigi for the Coccina family, merchants who had come to Venice from Bergamo. In the eighteenth century it passed to a branch of the Tiepolo family. In eighteen sixty-four it was bought by the Papadopoli, who added gardens and a ballroom. And in nineteen twenty-two it passed by inheritance to the Arrivabene Valenti Gonzaga. Count Gilberto and Countess Bianca still live in the private apartments at the top.

Have a look at those gardens if you can catch a glimpse of them. Private gardens are rare in a city built on water, and this palazzo has its own.

Inside, almost every room has frescoes or reliefs. The best known are eighteenth-century frescoes from the circle of the Tiepolo, the great Venetian family of painters, which now hang over the heads of guests in a games room. The staircase is from eighteen seventy-four. The walls of one room are covered in leather brought from Córdoba generations ago.

What's worth knowing, standing here, is that the hotel was laid over the house rather than carved out of it. Aman opened here in twenty thirteen, and the family stayed. Venice is full of palazzi that were emptied and sold. This one is still lived in.

[beat]

From here, walk to the Rialto Bridge and stand at the top of it, looking down the Grand Canal. Pick out the two obelisks on the roofline. Once you know what they mean, you'll start spotting them on other palazzi all the way along.
