Most of the masks for sale in Venice are made far from here. These are made in the back room.

kal lay day lay bot teh gay, in dor so doo ro, between the akka deh mee a and the university at car foss ka ree. The shop is the one with the window crowded with faces: gilded, painted, beaked, feathered, and some of them rather unsettling. Through the doorway, if it is a quiet moment, you'll see the workshop behind.

car ma kah na has been making masks here since the nineteen eighties. It is still a family business, and every mask is made by hand from pap yay mash ay, the same material Venetian mask makers were using centuries ago. They say no two are decorated alike.

The gift detail is the film. When Stanley Kubrick was making Eyes Wide Shut, the masks for its famous masked ball were bought in Venice. car ma kah na made some of them. It was one of several workshops the film's buyers went to, and its pieces were chosen for the production.

Now have a look at the shapes in the window. The long beaked one is the plague doctor. The beak was once said to hold herbs against the smell of disease. The plain white face that covers everything down to the chin is the bow ta. It was the mask that let a Venetian go anywhere, do anything and be no one, and it was so widely worn that the Republic made rules about it.

What's worth knowing is that the mask in Venice was never really about the party. It was about being anonymous in a city where everyone knew everyone. Carnival was only the most famous excuse.

If you have an hour or two, ask about a workshop. You can make and paint your own mask here, which is a far better souvenir than one off a stall. Otherwise, walk five minutes to kampo santa mar geh ree ta, the liveliest student square in the city.
