# The Iuav Gate at the Tolentini

**Observe** · Google Maps: *Università Iuav di Venezia - Tolentini* · 45.437770, 12.321834  
~328 words · about 2:11 at a natural pace

---

Most people walk straight past this doorway. Have a proper look and you'll see that the old gate isn't standing up. It is lying down, in the water.

Campazzo dei Tolentini, in Santa Croce, a few minutes from Piazzale Roma. This is the entrance to Iuav, the architecture university of Venice. Facing you is a modern entrance in concrete and stone, set into an old wall,. Look down at your feet, just in front of it.

The building behind was a convent, designed by Vincenzo Scamozzi for the Theatine fathers and finished around sixteen oh two. Napoleon closed it in eighteen ten. Over the next century and a half it was a barracks, an office and a refuge for flood victims. In nineteen fifty-eight it was given to the university.

The entrance is the work of Carlo Scarpa, who was Venice's greatest modern architect and taught here for decades. He began thinking about it in the late nineteen sixties, but he died in nineteen seventy-eight before it was built. His student and colleague Sergio Los completed it from Scarpa's design, and it was inaugurated in nineteen eighty-five.

The gift detail is the old portal. Rather than throw away the original stone gateway, the arched doorframe was taken down and laid flat on the ground at the entrance, where it now holds a shallow pool of water. You walk past the old door to reach the new one.

What's worth knowing, standing here, is that this is a lesson for every student who walks through it. In Venice you don't sweep the past away. You lay it down gently and build next to it.

[beat]

Before you go, look for the Latin carved into the entrance: verum ipsum factum, roughly, the truth is in the making. It is a motto from the philosopher Giambattista Vico, and it is Scarpa's whole approach in three words. Then walk back to the Grand Canal and compare it with Calatrava's bridge.
