# Fortuny Showroom and Factory

Behind this wall, for a hundred years, a factory has been printing fabric by a method that nobody outside it has ever been allowed to see.

Fondamenta San Biagio, at the western end of the island of Giudecca. Just along the waterfront to the west is the Molino Stucky, the great brick flour mill, now a hotel. In front of you is the long, low Fortuny building, in what was once Giudecca's industrial quarter. The showroom and the garden are open to visitors on certain days. The factory floor is not.

Mariano Fortuny y Madrazo was born in Granada in eighteen seventy-one, the son of a painter. He grew up in Paris and then Venice, where he and his wife and partner, Henriette Negrin, ran a studio in a Gothic palazzo. He was a painter, photographer, stage designer and inventor. And he made fabrics that looked as if they had come out of Renaissance paintings.

In the summer of nineteen twenty-two he opened this factory. It is still where the fabrics are made.

The gift detail is the secret. Fortuny's will held that the techniques behind his fabrics were never to be revealed publicly, and a century on they still haven't been. What comes out of this building looks like silk damask or old brocade. It is actually printed on cotton, by a process only the factory knows.

The second hero of the story is Elsie McNeill Lee, an interior designer from New York. She saw Fortuny's fabrics in a Paris museum in nineteen twenty-seven, became his sole distributor, and after his death ran the company for more than forty years. The garden here, made on the grounds of a former convent, became hers. She even put in a swimming pool.

[beat]

If the showroom is open, go in and look at the fabric closely. Try to work out how it was printed. Nobody will tell you. Otherwise, walk east along the Giudecca waterfront for the view back across to the Zattere. And if you'd like the rest of the story, the Fortuny museum is back across the water, in the palazzo where he lived.
