# Basilica di San Giorgio Maggiore

**Observe** · Google Maps: *Basilica di San Giorgio Maggiore* · 45.429389, 12.343279  
~337 words · about 2:14 at a natural pace

---

You've almost certainly seen this church already, from across the water. Now see it the other way round.

Stand on the little quay in front of the basilica, on the island of San Giorgio Maggiore. San Marco and the Doge's Palace are straight across the water. Behind you is the white front of the church, its columns and triangular pediments facing the city like a stage set.

The church is by Andrea Palladio, the architect from Vicenza whose villas were copied across the English countryside for two hundred years. He began it in fifteen sixty-six, but he died in fifteen eighty, before the front was built. It went up after his death, between about sixteen oh seven and sixteen eleven, under Simone Sorella, following Palladio's design.

Now have a look at the front. It is really two temple fronts laid over one another: a tall one for the nave, framed by four huge columns, and a wider, lower one behind it for the side aisles. It is Palladio's solution to an old problem: how to put the face of a Roman temple on a Christian church, which is a different shape.

Inside, go up to the high altar and look for Tintoretto's Last Supper, painted in the last years of his life, in the fifteen nineties. The table runs off at a steep diagonal, the room is lit by a single blazing lamp, and the angels swirl through the smoke.

What's worth knowing is how carefully this was placed. The white front stands exactly opposite the Piazzetta, so the view from San Marco and the view back are designed as a pair. Venice was looking at itself in a mirror.

[beat]

Take the lift up the bell tower. It costs a few euros, and the view from the top takes in San Marco, the Giudecca, the Lido and on a clear day the Alps. It is, to my mind, the best view in Venice, and the queue is nothing like the one at San Marco's own tower.
