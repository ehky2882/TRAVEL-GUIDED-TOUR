The gree tee Palace

A doge lived here. Centuries later, Ernest Hemingway treated it rather like home.

The hotel entrance is on this side, but the pa lat so really faces the other way, onto the Grand Canal, with the great white dome of the sa loo tay directly across the water. If you can, walk through to the waterside terrace, or look from the tra get toe landing nearby.

It was built in fourteen seventy five for the pee zah nee family. In fifteen twenty five it became the private residence of an dray a gree tee, dohj of Venice. His name stuck to the building long after he had gone.

It became a hotel in the eighteen nineties, and it learned to look after writers. Hemingway was a regular, and the presidential suite is named after him. There are suites for Somerset Maugham, and for John Ruskin, who wrote The Stones of Venice. The whole building was carefully restored in twenty thirteen.

Here's what I love about this spot. Look across at the sa loo tay and think about who has sat here doing exactly what you're doing. The view has hardly changed. And the gondola ferry still carries people across the canal a few steps from here, as it has for centuries.

If the terrace is open, it is one of the best places in Venice to sit at dusk, even if you aren't staying. If not, look for the tra get toe, the standing ferry gondola, at the landing by the kampo. For a couple of euros you can cross the Grand Canal standing up, the way Venetians do.
