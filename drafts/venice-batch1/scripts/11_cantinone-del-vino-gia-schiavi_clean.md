# Cantinone del Vino già Schiavi

~305 words · about 2:01 at a natural pace

---

Take your glass outside, turn round, and watch gondolas being made. That is the deal at this wine bar, and it has been for generations.

Fondamenta Nani, in Dorsoduro, beside the Rio di San Trovaso. The wine shop is the one with bottles stacked to the ceiling and people standing outside with a glass. Now turn to face the canal. On the far bank is a cluster of wooden buildings that look as if they belong in the mountains rather than in Venice. That is the Squero di San Trovaso, one of the very few gondola yards still working in the city.

The bar has been here since the late eighteen hundreds. Già Schiavi means formerly Schiavi, after a previous owner. In nineteen forty-five it was taken over by Sisto Gastaldi. In nineteen sixty his son Lino met Alessandra De Respinis, and she is still behind the counter, with their children. Venetians also call the place il Bottegon, the big shop.

The gift detail is Alessandra's crostini. She makes them fresh every day, small toasts with dozens of different toppings. Many people will tell you they are the best in Venice. You eat them standing up, with an ombra, the small glass of wine Venetians drink at the bar.

The interesting bit is the view. Gondola builders traditionally came from the mountains, which is why the squero looks like an Alpine chalet. The two businesses sit across the water from each other, both of them family trades that most of the city has lost.

[beat]

Stand on the far side of the canal for a minute and look into the squero if the doors are open. You may see a gondola upturned on the slipway. Then follow the canal down to the Zattere, a couple of minutes away, for the sun on the Giudecca Canal.
