Ca' d'Oro alla Vedova

Ask a Venetian where to find the best meatball in the city, and there's a fair chance they'll send you down this alley.

Turn off the Strada Nova, the wide, busy shopping street in Cannaregio, close to the Ca' d'Oro water bus stop, and follow the narrow passage to its end. The doorway at the far end, with old lamps glowing yellow inside, is the trattoria. Venetians simply call it the Vedova: the Widow.

The name is a family story. When the owner's father died, his mother was left a widow, and the regulars started saying, let's meet at the widow's, when they wanted an ombra. An ombra is a small glass of wine, and it is the Venetian way to start an evening. The nickname stuck so firmly that it is now part of the official name.

Inside, it looks the way you hope an old bacaro will look: copper pots hanging from the ceiling, old furniture, low yellow light. The place goes back more than a century, and it shows.

Now have a look at the counter. That is where the polpette sit: fried meatballs with a crisp, dark crust and a dense, savoury centre, served hot. Around them are the other cicchetti, the small bar snacks of Venice. Sardines in saor, sweet and sour with onions. Baccalà, the salt cod. Grilled cuttlefish, octopus salad.

What's worth knowing is the difference between the counter and the tables. The tables are mostly visitors, and you'll want to book. The counter is where the neighbourhood comes for a meatball and a glass and is gone again in ten minutes. If you want to understand Venice, stand at the counter.

[beat]

When you come back out onto the Strada Nova, walk a few steps towards the Ca' d'Oro stop and look for the lane down to the Grand Canal. The Ca' d'Oro itself, the Golden House, has one of the loveliest Gothic fronts on the canal, and it is best seen from the water.
