osteh ree a allay test yeh ray

The name of this restaurant means headboards, as in beds. There is a perfectly good reason for that, and it has nothing to do with sleeping.

kal lay del mondo no vo, in kass tello, between kampo santa maria for mo za and the ree al toe. You are looking for a very small dining room behind a modest front. If the door is open and the room is full, which it usually is, you are in the right place.

When the current owners took it over, the sign above the door still read dah mario. While they were clearing out the old furniture, they found a set of headboards from the nineteen thirties. Rather than throw them away, they hung them on the walls, and the osteh ree a became allay test yeh ray: at the headboards.

That was in nineteen ninety three. The owners are two friends: bruno ga van yeen, who cooks, and luka dee vee ta, who runs the room and the wine. They have run it together for more than thirty years. There are only around twenty seats, so the room fills with a single sitting and stays full.

There is no fixed menu to speak of. It is fish, nearly all of it, bought at the ree al toe market that morning and cooked with a light hand. What's on depends on what the lagoon and the Adriatic gave up that day.

What's worth knowing, standing outside, is that a place this small can only survive if the locals keep coming back. Twenty seats is a very small margin for error. That it has lasted three decades in the busiest part of Venice tells you more than any review.

You'll need to book, well ahead. If you've arrived without a table, walk two minutes to kampo santa maria for mo za, one of the broadest squares in this part of the city. The kweh ree nee stam pah lee a, with karlo scarpa's garden, is just across the canal on its far side.
