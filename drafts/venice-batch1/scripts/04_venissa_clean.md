# Venissa

**Stay** · Google Maps: *Venissa* · 45.488515, 12.410955  
~319 words · about 2:07 at a natural pace

---

This vineyard is surrounded by water and protected by a medieval wall. What grows here is a grape that was very nearly lost.

You're on Mazzorbo, the quiet island joined to Burano by a long wooden footbridge. Facing you, behind old brick walls, are rows of vines. Above them stands a lone bell tower, the last fragment of a medieval monastery that once stood here. The low buildings beside it hold a restaurant and a handful of rooms.

The grape is called Dorona, from the Venetian for golden, because of its colour. It is a native lagoon variety, a natural cross of two older grapes, and it was once served at the banquets of the doges. Then in nineteen sixty-six a great flood swept salt water across the lagoon. It wiped out almost all the farming here, and Dorona was thought to be gone for good.

The Prosecco winemaker Gianluca Bisol went looking for it. He found a few surviving vines in a small garden on Torcello, the neighbouring island, and DNA tests confirmed what they were. They were replanted inside these walls, and the estate took the name Venissa.

The gift detail is the bottle. It is made on Murano, and gold leaf, hammered by a Venetian family of goldbeaters, is applied by hand and fired into the glass. Every bottle is numbered, and only a few thousand are made each year.

The interesting bit is this. Most of the lagoon is now just a backdrop. This place is an argument that the lagoon can still be farmed. The same argument runs through the restaurant, which holds a Michelin star and cooks largely from these islands.

[beat]

When you're ready, walk back over the wooden bridge to Burano. Stop halfway and look back at the bell tower over the vines. Then carry on into Burano, where the painted houses start almost the moment you step off the bridge.
