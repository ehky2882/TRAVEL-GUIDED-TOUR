Al Timon

At aperitivo time, the best seat on this canal isn't a seat at all. It is a boat.

The Fondamenta degli Ormesini, in Cannaregio, a long, lively waterside walk in the north of the city. The bar is on the fondamenta itself. Moored against the bank in front of it is an old wooden boat, with people sitting on its deck holding glasses of wine and plates of cicchetti.

The boat is a bragozzo, a traditional flat-bottomed fishing boat of the northern Adriatic, now earning its keep as an extra terrace. When the evening is warm, the regulars spill out of the bar, along the edge of the canal and onto the boat.

Al Timon means at the helm, or the tiller. It started as a bacaro serving cicchetti, the small snacks you eat standing at the counter, and it still does. But it has become just as well known for steak, grilled over the coals, which is not what you expect in a city of fish.

Here's what I love about this stretch. The Ormesini has become one of the places where young Venetians actually go out, and Al Timon is often credited with leading the way. In a city where the centre empties of locals after dark, the northern canals are still a neighbourhood.

[beat]

From here, carry on along the Ormesini and over the bridge onto the Fondamenta della Misericordia, which continues in the same spirit. Or head into the old Ghetto, just a couple of minutes south. It is one of the oldest Jewish quarters in Europe, and it is quiet and rather moving at dusk.
