# RAAS Devigarh

Look up. That's an eighteenth-century fort on top of the hill, and since nineteen ninety-nine it has been a hotel.

You should be at the foot of the hill in the village of Delwara, some twenty-five kilometres north of Udaipur. Above you, the palace rises in tiers of pale stone, with the Aravalli hills all round. RAAS Devigarh is the name now. For most of its life it was simply the Delwara Fort Palace.

Its story starts with a battle. In fifteen seventy-six, after the battle of Haldighati, the principality of Delwara was given to Rajrana Sajja Singh and his brother, Ajja, for their service to Maharana Pratap of Mewar. The palace you see was completed in seventeen sixty, under a later Rajrana, Sajja Singh the Second. It was built with marble quarried nearby.

Then it fell quiet. When it was finally restored, the work took a team of seven hundred and fifty people almost fifteen years. They went back to the same locally mined marble. That's why the patches and the originals sit so comfortably together.

And here's what's worth knowing. Inside, the restorers didn't try to recreate a maharaja's fantasy. The rooms are largely plain marble, almost minimal, set against the old mirror-work ante-chambers and murals of Marwari horses. It was a bold choice for a palace hotel, and it makes the old parts look more remarkable, not less.

*[beat]*

So the fort that guarded a valley now looks after guests. Rather a gentle retirement, if you ask me.

If you have an hour, walk down into Delwara itself. The hotel sends guests out on walks from its gates, and from the village lanes you get the best view of the fort above.
