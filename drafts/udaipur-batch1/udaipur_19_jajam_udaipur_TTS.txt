Jaajum

In rural Raajasthaan, a jaajum is a huge block printed cotton floor spread. Village councils sat on one to settle disputes, and families rolled them out for weddings and festivals, so everyone could sit down together. It's a good name for a co working space.

You're in Madhoobun, a quiet residential neighbourhood east of Fatteh Saagar. Jaajum is in an old house that's been converted into a café and shared workspace. Look for laptops on the tables, a coffee machine, and very likely a dog asleep somewhere it shouldn't be.

Have a look at how the house has been used. It still feels like a house rather than an office. There are quiet corners for heads down work, and shared rooms where the conversation happens. It mixes traditional Indian touches with what you'd actually need for a working day: decent internet and a chair you can sit in for hours.

Then the coffee and the baking, which regulars rate rather highly. It's all home baked: chocolate chip cookies, and a millet cake that's very much of this region. Raajasthaan grows more millet than any other state in India.

What I like about Jaajum is that it's built for the people who live in Oodaypoor, not for the people passing through. On a weekday you'll find locals working, and the talk at the next table is as likely to be about cycling or cinema as about deadlines. That's rather the point of a jaajum. Nobody gets a better seat than anyone else.

If you're in town for a few days, come back at the weekend. The menu changes, and so does the crowd.
