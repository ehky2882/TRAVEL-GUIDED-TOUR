# Third Space

White marble screens wrap this building like lace. Behind them, children are climbing walls, building gadgets and putting on shows.

You're at Pahada, on the north-eastern side of Udaipur, at Third Space. If you're facing the entrance, you should see a low building wrapped in white marble jali, perforated stone screens. Here and there little boxes project out of the screens, like windows that have stepped forward.

Have a look at the screens first. Jali is one of the oldest ideas in Rajasthani building. It's a stone lattice that lets breeze in, keeps the glare out, and gives privacy without walls. The architects, Studio Saar, cut these with modern machinery. They then reused the offcuts and marble waste inside, as flooring and wall infill, so little was thrown away.

Now those projecting boxes. They're gokhra, a traditional overhanging niche that works as a wind-catcher. It pulls air into the building, and inside it makes a small private seat. Together with an evaporative cooling tower, they keep the interior comfortable without leaning on air-conditioning.

The plan is borrowed too. Studio Saar called it the Haveli of Curiosity, after the old courtyard mansions, with a pinwheel of cloisters round a central court. Overhead, bamboo sails shade the roof. And there's a sunken, shaded space modelled on a stepwell, where people sit out of the heat.

*[beat]*

And here's the thing. A third space is the place that isn't home and isn't school or work. It's the place you go to simply be among people. The charity behind it, Dharohar, built one using the city's oldest building ideas. It's a very good argument that those ideas were never just for palaces.

Before you leave, look across the road. The centre's programmes reach into fifty hectares of reforested jungle on the other side, and it's worth asking at the desk whether you can walk a little way in.
