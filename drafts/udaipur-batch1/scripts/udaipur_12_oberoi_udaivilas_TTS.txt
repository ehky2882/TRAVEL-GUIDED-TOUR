The Oberoi Ooday vilaas

There's a hotel on Lake Pichohla that suggests you arrive by boat. Having seen the approach, I'd say they're right.

If you've come across the water, you'll have stepped off at the jetty on the western shore. In front of you are low pavilions and domes set in long gardens. Behind you, across the lake, the City Palace fills the far shore. That's The Oberoi Ooday vilaas.

Have a look at the domes first. The hotel was built new, and it opened in two thousand and two, but the domes are borrowed from the old palaces of Mayvaar. By one count there are thirty seven of them, the one over the lobby painted inside with gold leaf, and they're linked by covered walkways. Walk underneath them and you're in shade almost the whole way, which in a Raajasthaanee summer is not decoration but survival.

Then look at the size of the place. The grounds cover around thirty acres of gardens running along the lakeshore. The hotel says this was once royal hunting land. That would explain why there's so much room, and why the planting feels closer to a park than to a garden.

And finally, the view the whole thing is built around: across the water to the City Palace, with Jag Mandir, the island palace, out on the lake to the south.

What's worth thinking about is the trick it pulls off. Ooday vilaas isn't old, but it was built to sit politely beside buildings that are. It faces the real palace. It doesn't compete with it.

If you're not staying, the best way to see it is from the water. The boats from the city ghaats pass right along this shore, and from the lake, the domes line up exactly as the architects intended.
