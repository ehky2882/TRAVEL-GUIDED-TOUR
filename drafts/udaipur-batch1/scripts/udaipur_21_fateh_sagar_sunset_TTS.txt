Sunset on the Fatteh Saagar Paal

Every evening, half of Oodaypoor comes to this embankment to watch the sky change colour. It's well worth joining them.

You're on the Paal, the long stone embankment along the eastern side of Fatteh Saagar Lake. The water is in front of you, with the Araavalee hills behind it and the sun dropping towards them. Behind you are the food stalls and the traffic. Out on the lake are the islands.

Let's start with what you're standing on. This embankment is the dam that makes the lake. The first lake here was built in the seventeenth century, but its earth dam was washed away. In eighteen eighty nine Mahaaraana Fatteh Singh rebuilt it in stone. He named the new dam the Connaught Bund, after Queen Victoria's son, who happened to be visiting. The lake took the Mahaaraana's own name. Locals simply call the embankment the Paal.

Now the islands. The biggest one, with gardens and a café, is Nayroo Park, and boats go out to it from here. Another has a white domed building on it, and that's not a pavilion. It's the Oodaypoor Solar Observatory, one of India's research observatories for studying the sun. It's built on the water because the lake keeps the air above it steady, which makes for sharper pictures of the sun.

There's something rather nice in that. Scientists picked this lake to watch the sun by day. Every evening, the rest of the city comes to the same lake to watch it set.

Before you go, do what everyone else on the Paal is doing and eat something. Street food is part of the ritual here, so follow the longest queue. Then walk the full length of the embankment as the lights come on across the water.
