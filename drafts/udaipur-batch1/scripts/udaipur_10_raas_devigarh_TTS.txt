Raas Dayveegur

Look up. That's an eighteenth century fort on top of the hill, and since nineteen ninety nine it has been a hotel.

You should be at the foot of the hill in the village of Delvaara, some twenty five kilometres north of Oodaypoor. Above you, the palace rises in tiers of pale stone, with the Araavalee hills all round. RAAS Dayveegur is the name now. For most of its life it was simply the Delvaara Fort Palace.

Its story starts with a battle. In fifteen seventy six, after the battle of Haldee ghaatee, the principality of Delvaara was given to Raajraana Sujja Singh and his brother, Ujja, for their service to Mahaaraana Pratap of Mayvaar. The palace you see was completed in seventeen sixty, under a later Raajraana, Sujja Singh the Second. It was built with marble quarried nearby.

Then it fell quiet. When it was finally restored, the work took a team of seven hundred and fifty people almost fifteen years. They went back to the same locally mined marble. That's why the patches and the originals sit so comfortably together.

And here's what's worth knowing. Inside, the restorers didn't try to recreate a maharaja's fantasy. The rooms are largely plain marble, almost minimal, set against the old mirror work ante chambers and murals of Maarvaaree horses. It was a bold choice for a palace hotel, and it makes the old parts look more remarkable, not less.

So the fort that guarded a valley now looks after guests. Rather a gentle retirement, if you ask me.

If you have an hour, walk down into Delvaara itself. The hotel sends guests out on walks from its gates, and from the village lanes you get the best view of the fort above.
