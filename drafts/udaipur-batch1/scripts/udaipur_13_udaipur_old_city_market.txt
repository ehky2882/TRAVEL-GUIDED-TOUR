Old City Market — Udaipur, India
Vantage: Acharya Nanesh Marg, Old City · 24.582386, 73.689884
---

Nothing in Udaipur's old city runs in a straight line. Lanes narrow to the width of a scooter, open into a little square, then narrow again.

You're on the edge of Mochiwada, east of the clock tower, in the knot of streets that make up the old bazaars. There'll be shopfronts open to the lane on both sides, goods hung from every hook and awning, and an auto-rickshaw trying to get past you. Step in to a doorway and let it.

Start with the name of the neighbourhood. A mochi is a cobbler, and Mochiwada is the cobblers' quarter. Old Indian cities were often laid out like this, trade by trade, a street of shoemakers here and a street of metalworkers there. The names stuck around long after the neat divisions blurred.

Then look at what's actually for sale. A lot of it is made by hand, and some of it has clearly been around a while: brass, textiles, carved wood, silver, and leather shoes with turned-up toes, the traditional mojari. In places like this the line between antique and simply old is rather generous. Bargaining is expected, and it's done with good humour.

[beat]

Here's what I love about these lanes. They aren't laid out for visitors. They're laid out for the people who've lived and traded here for generations. That's exactly why they're so pleasant to get lost in. You never quite know what's round the next bend, and neither, sometimes, does the auto-rickshaw.

Head west and you'll come out at the Ghanta Ghar, the clock tower. From there it's a short climb up to the Jagdish Temple and the City Palace.
