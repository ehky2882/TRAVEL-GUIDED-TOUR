Kaalad vaas Laal Havaylee

Every morning at six, the clock tower across the street chimes. For the man who now runs this house, that sound has been his alarm since childhood.

You're on Ganesh Ghaatee, in the thick of the old city, a few minutes' walk from the City Palace. Find the Ghunta Ghur, Oodaypoor's clock tower, and turn to face the old stone house that looks onto it. That's Kaalad vaas Laal Havaylee. Laal means red, and the name is fair warning.

Look at the facade first. It's Sajjangur quarry stone, named for the hill west of the city where the Monsoon Palace stands. When the house was restored, it was cleaned and repaired by hand. The restoration took six years, working with the late architect Veeral Moonshee. The builders avoided cement entirely. They used lime based plaster and mortar, the way the house was first built, because lime lets old walls breathe in this climate.

The house itself is around three hundred years old. It belongs to the Chaavra family of Varsoda, who for generations ran the village of Kaalad vaas for the Mahaaraanas. That's where the name comes from. Paraakram Singh Chaavra, who grew up running up and down these stairs, decided to restore it rather than let it crumble.

Inside, there are just seven suites. The rooms work through every imaginable shade of red, scarlet and saffron.

The interesting bit is that this is a family house first and a hotel second. The family still runs it day to day. That's increasingly rare in an old city where plenty of havaylees have changed hands, or been left to fall down.

Before you move on, stand under the clock tower for a moment and look along the lanes in each direction. The old bazaars start right here.
