Kaladwas Lal Haveli — Udaipur, India
Vantage: Ganesh Ghati Marg · 24.581698, 73.685069
---

Every morning at six, the clock tower across the street chimes. For the man who now runs this house, that sound has been his alarm since childhood.

You're on Ganesh Ghati, in the thick of the old city, a few minutes' walk from the City Palace. Find the Ghanta Ghar, Udaipur's clock tower, and turn to face the old stone house that looks onto it. That's Kaladwas Lal Haveli. Lal means red, and the name is fair warning.

Look at the facade first. It's Sajjangarh quarry stone, named for the hill west of the city where the Monsoon Palace stands. When the house was restored, it was cleaned and repaired by hand. The restoration took six years, working with the late architect Viral Munshi. The builders avoided cement entirely. They used lime-based plaster and mortar, the way the house was first built, because lime lets old walls breathe in this climate.

The house itself is around three hundred years old. It belongs to the Chawra family of Varsoda, who for generations ran the village of Kaladwas for the Maharanas. That's where the name comes from. Parakram Singh Chawra, who grew up running up and down these stairs, decided to restore it rather than let it crumble.

Inside, there are just seven suites. The rooms work through every imaginable shade of red, scarlet and saffron.

[beat]

The interesting bit is that this is a family house first and a hotel second. The family still runs it day to day. That's increasingly rare in an old city where plenty of havelis have changed hands, or been left to fall down.

Before you move on, stand under the clock tower for a moment and look along the lanes in each direction. The old bazaars start right here.
