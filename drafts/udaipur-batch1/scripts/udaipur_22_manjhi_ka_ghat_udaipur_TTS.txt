Maanjee Ka Ghaat

If you've seen one photograph of Oodaypoor, it was very probably taken from about here.

You should be on the steps of Maanjee Ka Ghaat, also called Ambraai Ghaat, on the western shore of Lake Pichohla. Facing across the water, you'll see the long frontage of the City Palace on the far shore. Down to your right are the white walls of the Lake Palace, out on the water. Straight across from you, at the water's edge, is Gangore Ghaat.

Start with the steps you're on. A ghaat is a flight of steps down to water, for bathing, washing, landing boats, or simply sitting. Oodaypoor's lakes are lined with them. This one's old name was Maanjee Raaj ka Ghaat, and it's now kept up by the city, with marble edging and wide seats. It's one of the easiest places in town to do nothing at all for an hour.

Then the lake. It was here before the city. Mahaaraana Ooday Singh the Second enlarged it after founding Oodaypoor, flooding a village called Pichohlee in the process. That's where the name comes from.

And then the view itself. From this side, the palace and the old city stack up together, roof above roof, with the temple spires behind. You're far enough away to see it whole and close enough to pick out the windows.

Here's the thing about this spot. From the palace side, you're inside Oodaypoor's history. From here, you're simply looking at it, which is how most of its visitors have always seen it. Sometimes the view from outside is the better one.

Stay for the hour after sunset, when the palace lights come on and double in the water. Then walk north along the shore to the Chaand pole bridge, for one more angle on the same view.
