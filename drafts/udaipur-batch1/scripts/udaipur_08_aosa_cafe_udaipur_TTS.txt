Ah osa Café

You'll probably smell it before you see it. Fresh bread, butter, and coffee being roasted somewhere behind the counter.

Ah osa is in Fattehpoora, in the Punchvatee neighbourhood, a short walk from you eye tee Circle. Look for a café that calls itself a bakehouse and roastery. Inside, it's rustic and contemporary at once, with a garden seating area further back.

Have a look at the counter first. Ah osa roasts its own coffee, which not every café in a city this size bothers to do. It means the beans haven't travelled far before they reach your cup. Then look at the pastry case, which does a lot of the persuading. Croissants are the thing here, including one made into a puneer tikka sandwich. That's a French pastry wrapped round a north Indian grill, and it's rather better than it has any right to be.

Find a table out in the garden, if you can. It's quieter back there, with planting all round and the noise of the junction a long way off, though it's only a few metres.

The interesting thing about Ah osa is that it's doing something very old in a new way. Oodaypoor has always been a city of tea, and of the chai stall on the corner. A café roasting its own coffee and baking its own croissants tells you the city's tastes are widening, and that someone thought there was a market for it here.

Ninkaasee, the colourful tapas bar, is just a few steps along. And if you'd rather have tea, Tuppree Nivaas is about ten minutes' walk north.
