Ninkasi — Udaipur, India
Vantage: Fatehpura, Panchwati · 24.597686, 73.688422
---

Ninkasi was the Sumerian goddess of beer. Nearly four thousand years ago someone wrote her a hymn, and the hymn is also a recipe.

You're in Fatehpura, in the Panchwati neighbourhood, just off the busy junction near UIT Circle. Step inside and you'll know you've arrived. There's colour on every surface, gold on the ceilings, and nothing whatsoever left plain.

Look at the bar area first. The designers, an architecture practice called Studio Rust, put large paintings in saturated yellows and reds into its corners. They're a nod to Rajasthani folk art, under exposed ceiling beams. Then go further in, to the lounge. The ceilings there are painted in red and gold geometric patterns, borrowed from traditional palace frescoes. The doorways are framed in golden arches that echo the jharokha, the projecting carved balcony you'll see all over this city's old havelis.

So you have an ancient Mesopotamian goddess, a Rajput palace and a tapas menu, all in one room. By rights it shouldn't work. The trick is that nobody here is pretending to be subtle. The place calls itself maximalist, and it means it.

[beat]

What's worth noticing is where the ideas come from. That gold on the ceiling, and the arch round the door, are the same ones you'd see in the City Palace. Here they've been moved out of the royal court and into somewhere you can sit with a cocktail.

When you leave, the café a few steps along is Aosa, a bakery and coffee roaster. If you're here in the morning rather than the evening, it makes a very civilised start to the day.
