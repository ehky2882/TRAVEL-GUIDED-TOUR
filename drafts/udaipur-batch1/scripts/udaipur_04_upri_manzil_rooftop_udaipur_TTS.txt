Oopree Manzil Rooftop

Oopree Manzil means, quite simply, the upper floor. In this part of Oodaypoor, the upper floor is where everybody wants to be.

You're near Hanoomaan Ghaat, on the western shore of Lake Pichohla, just south of the Chaand pole bridge. Climb to the rooftop restaurant and face east, across the water. The long white mass on the far shore is the City Palace. Out on the water, to your right, is the Lake Palace, which seems to float.

Start with the lake. It's older than the city around it. The story goes that Mahaaraana Ooday Singh the Second enlarged it after he founded Oodaypoor in the fifteen fifties. In doing so he flooded a village called Pichohlee, which gave the lake its name. So under the water you're looking at, there's a village.

Now the food, which the restaurant keeps deliberately simple. It's local dishes and familiar ones, and the menu changes every few months. Regulars point you to the gutteh, little gram flour dumplings in a spiced yoghurt curry, a Raajasthaanee staple. Nobody comes up here for theatre on the plate. The theatre is across the water.

And here's the thing about this side of the lake. The famous postcard of Oodaypoor, with the palace walls rising out of the water, is taken from roughly this shore. Over there you're inside the history. Over here you get to look at it.

Come back after dark if you can. The palace lights come up one floor at a time and stretch out across the water. From the Chaand pole bridge, just to your north, you can watch the whole shoreline light up.
