Page Break at Manuscript

The best view of the City Palace isn't always from the lake. Sometimes it's from a rooftop two minutes up a side street.

This is Bhatiyaanee Chohutta, the lane that runs off City Palace Road into the old city. The building you want is Manuscript, a hotel in the old Jhilvaara Haveli, and Page Break is the restaurant at the top of it. If you're on the roof, turn towards the long, pale wall of the palace. That's the view people come for.

Have a look at the building on your way up. It was built in nineteen ten as a residence for the Jhilvaara family. By Oodaypoor standards that makes it fairly young, but it kept the old vocabulary. There's carved stone jaalee latticework, hand carved pillars and pointed arches, and the restorers left them in place. The new interiors are deliberately quiet, all neutral tones and rust, so the old carving does the talking.

Then there's the name. A hotel called Manuscript, with a restaurant called Page Break. Someone clearly enjoyed themselves. And it suits a building in this part of town, where every house has a few chapters behind it.

The interesting thing about this rooftop is what it tells you about the old city. From the street, these lanes feel closed in. You get walls, doorways and the odd scooter. Up here you realise the whole neighbourhood is built to look at the palace. Every roof turns the same way.

When you come back down, walk up to the top of City Palace Road. The Jugdeesh Temple is just there, up a steep flight of steps, and it's worth the climb.
