The Climb to Karnee Maata

There's a cable car to the top of this hill. This is the other way up.

You're at the foot of Muchla Mugra, the steep hill that rises east of Lake Pichohla, where the path begins. It's best walked early, before the heat. Above you, at the top, is the temple of Karnee Maata. Most visitors ride up from Doodh Talaai on the ropeway. You're going to earn it.

As you climb, keep turning round. It's the best reason to walk rather than ride. First you'll see the rooftops of the old city close below you. Then, as you gain height, the palace on its ridge, the shine of Lake Pichohla, and finally the hills ringing the whole valley. On a winter morning there's often mist sitting in the low ground, and the city comes out of it a roof at a time.

At the top is the temple of Karnee Maata, a form of the goddess Doorga, much loved across Raajasthaan. Just to be clear, this isn't the famous Karnee Maata temple at Daysh noke, out in the desert near Beekaneer, the one with thousands of sacred rats. This one is quieter. What you'll notice first is the incense drifting down the path to meet you.

And here's what I find rather lovely about this climb. The ropeway gets you the same view in a few minutes. Walking, you watch the city slowly assemble itself below you, street by street. By the time you reach the top, you understand how Oodaypoor fits together.

At the summit, find a spot facing the lake and wait for the sun to clear the hills. Then, if your legs have had enough, take the ropeway down. Nobody will tell.
