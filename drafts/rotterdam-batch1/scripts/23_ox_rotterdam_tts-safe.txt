Ox Rotterdam

You won't find the door to this restaurant by looking at the street, because the directions only arrive by email once you have booked.

Skeekahda one eighty nine is the address, and the building above is the Skeeblock, a former office block with a long, plain facade facing the busy road, a few minutes' walk from Rotterdam Sentrahl. Ox lives in the basement, hidden away beneath all of it, which is exactly how it likes to be found.

Ox is a Chinese restaurant in the speakeasy style, drawing on dishes from across China and Malaysia, served as small plates to share. Expect things like oysters, cold and hot small dishes, and dumplings filled with pork, prawn, shiitake and eel, with cocktails inspired by Chinese culture and a long list of natural wines alongside.

The building above is a story of its own, because the local architects Zus turned it into a laboratory for the city, full of small businesses, with a rooftop field billed as the first urban farming roof in Europe.

Running out from it is the Lookhtsingel, a bright yellow wooden footbridge about four hundred metres long, which links three districts that busy roads and railway lines had cut apart. It was paid for partly by the public, with more than eight thousand planks sold at twenty five euros each, every one carrying the buyer's name.

My favourite thing about the Skeeblock is that almost everything interesting happens above or below eye level, with a farm on the roof, a public bridge in the air and a hidden dining room in the basement.

Before your reservation, walk up onto the Lookhtsingel and read a few of the names on the planks, then cross it towards the Hofplain. The bridge gives you a view of this part of the city that very few streets can.
