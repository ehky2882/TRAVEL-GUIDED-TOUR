CityHub Rotterdam

This hotel gives you a smaller room than almost anywhere in town, and quietly bets that you will spend most of your stay outside it anyway.

Witte de Withstraat runs between the Museumpark side of town and the old harbour basins, and it is the street for galleries, bars and late dinners. Partway along, three neighbouring buildings with a single entrance between them make up CityHub, and through the glass a bright communal space shows where a conventional reception desk would normally be.

The hubs themselves are the first surprise: instead of typical rooms, guests sleep in compact sleeping units, each with its own lighting and connectivity, and the whole place is designed for travellers who live through their phones, from the digital check-in to the self-service bar.

The interior is by the Amsterdam design studio Modijefsky, completed in 2018, and the clever trick is in the joins. Three older buildings were stitched together while keeping their individual character, with traces of the original walls left visible, and a long linear chandelier of light runs through all three to guide you around. The colours are worth noticing too, shifting floor by floor and growing lighter the higher you go, as the daylight increases.

The bathrooms add a small joke of their own, with mixed tiles and playful colour schemes deliberately chosen to ignore the usual pink and blue conventions. What CityHub really sells, if you ask me, is not the bed but the street: by shrinking the private space, it pushes its guests out into one of the liveliest strips in Rotterdam, which is exactly where the designers seem to think they belong.

[beat]

Walk the length of Witte de Withstraat in either direction, and look out for Kunstinstituut Melly, the contemporary art space a short way along. In the evening the terraces fill up, and the whole street turns into the communal lounge this hotel was imagining.
