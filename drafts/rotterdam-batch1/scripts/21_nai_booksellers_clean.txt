NAI Booksellers

A city that had to rebuild itself almost from scratch was always going to need a very good architecture bookshop, and this is where Rotterdam keeps one.

Head into the lobby of Het Nieuwe Instituut on the Museumpark, and the bookshop is right there, open to anyone, with no museum ticket needed. Before you go in, look back at the building itself: a long, low run of glass and dark structure beside a pond, with a raised archive wing standing on columns above the street behind.

NAI Booksellers was founded in 1993, at what was then the Netherlands Architecture Institute, and it specialises in architecture, urban planning, design and landscape architecture. The shelves carry everything from big monographs to academic texts and city guides, from Dutch publishers and from all over the world.

The building around it opened the same year, designed by the architect Jo Coenen and opened by Queen Beatrix, and since 2013 it has been Het Nieuwe Instituut, the national institute for architecture, design and digital culture. Its archive holds the national collection for Dutch architecture and urban planning, more than eighteen kilometres of drawings, models and papers, including the personal archives of many Dutch architects.

So the books on these shelves are about buildings, while the drawings behind a great many real Dutch buildings are kept under the same roof.

The setting, to my mind, could hardly be more fitting, since Rotterdam is the city where modern Dutch architecture has had the most room to experiment, and here is the shop that sends those ideas back out into the world, one book at a time.

[beat]

Leave time to walk round to the Sonneveld House next door, a beautifully preserved 1930s family home that belongs to the institute. And if you are here on a Thursday, the shop stays open into the evening, which suits a slow browse rather well.
