Bar Alaska

The little river at the end of this street gave Rotterdam its name, and these days it gives this bar its terrace.

Where the Kerkhoflahn meets the water of the Rotta, in the old neighbourhood of Krohswike, the corner building with tables spilling out towards the river is Bar Alaska. With the bar at your back, the Rotta runs to your left and right, narrow and slow, with boats moored along its edges and the city centre somewhere off downstream.

The bar opened in twenty twenty three, from the same people behind Cafay Marsay and Bar Bilbao, and they describe it as a tribute to Rotterdam and Krohswike, inspired by the small international eating houses of Berlin. In practice that means cold beer, cocktails and decent wine, with a kitchen that changes its offerings rather than sticking to a fixed pub menu.

The food is where the neighbourhood really shows itself, with meatball sandwiches made with meat from a local butcher, a Krohswike fish soup served with bread, and on Sundays the oysters have been known to go for a single euro each, which is reason enough to plan a weekend around. Look out too for the Cape Verdean evenings with live music, a nod to the city's large Cape Verdean community.

And the river is what holds it all together. Back in the thirteenth century a dam was thrown across the Rotta, and the settlement that grew beside it became Rotterdam, so a drink on this terrace puts you beside the very water that started the whole city, now doing nothing more demanding than reflecting the evening.

If you have time before or after, walk the path along the Rotta in either direction, because it is one of the gentlest walks in the city. The bar also teams up with a local boat company for food trips along the river, if you fancy seeing it from the water.
