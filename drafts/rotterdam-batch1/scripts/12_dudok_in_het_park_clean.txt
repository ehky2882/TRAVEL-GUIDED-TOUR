Dudok in het Park

This house is older than the park around it, and for a long time it stood at the edge of the polders, well outside the city.

Between the trees at the edge of Het Park, a pale, symmetrical house with tall windows and a decorated doorway faces out over the lawns, and that is the old Heerenhuys De Heuvel, home today to Dudok in het Park. If the Euromast is visible above the treetops, you are in the right corner of the park.

Nobody knows the exact building date of the house, but it went up around 1750, and it appears on a map of 1784 as the main building of a country estate called De Heuvel, which simply means the hill. One of its residents was Jan Jacob Elsevier, a former mayor of Rotterdam, and the estate belonged to that world of wealthy merchants who kept a house in the green country just beyond the town walls.

Eventually the city caught up with it, when Rotterdam bought the property in 1875, and over the following years the park laid out by the landscape architect Zocher was joined to the old estate, which is why the house now sits so comfortably among the lawns.

The modern chapter is a rescue, because a heritage foundation acquired the building in 1995 and restored it, removing later steel fire escapes, and it reopened as Dudok in het Park in 2017, after earlier lives as a café and a restaurant.

Seen this way, the house is a small lesson in how cities grow: a country retreat built to escape the town ends up as a quiet café in the middle of it.

Settle in with a coffee if you have the time, and then follow the paths down towards the Euromast and the river, where the park opens out onto the water and the ships passing by.
