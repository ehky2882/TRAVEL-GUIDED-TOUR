Fenix

Cargo on its way across the world once filled this warehouse, and now it holds the stories of people who made that same journey.

The long brick warehouse on the Kahtendrekht waterfront, with a gleaming, twisting structure bursting out through its roof, is Fenix. Look up and you will see it: a double spiral of polished steel and wood, climbing from inside the building to a viewing platform high above the harbour.

The warehouse was built in nineteen twenty three as the San Francisco warehouse, around three hundred metres long and once said to be the largest in the world, and it stored goods for the Holland America Line, whose ships carried millions of migrants from this harbour across the Atlantic, which makes it a fitting home for a museum about migration.

The spiral is called the Tornado, designed by the Chinese studio Mad, and it is two helical staircases spliced together, built with help from a rollercoaster specialist and suspended so that it needs as few supports as possible. Climb it and the river, the bridges and the skyline turn slowly around you.

Inside, two installations are well worth finding: The Family of Migrants gathers around two hundred photographs of departures, journeys and arrivals from dozens of countries, and the Suitcase Labyrinth asks you to find your way through two thousand donated suitcases, each carrying a story of someone who left home.

Put simply, Fenix turns a building made for goods into a building about people, giving the travellers the space this warehouse once gave to their cargo.

Climb the Tornado all the way to the top if it is open, and look out over the water towards the Vilhelmeenapeer, where the old Holland America Line headquarters still stands. From up there, the route those migrants took begins to make sense.
