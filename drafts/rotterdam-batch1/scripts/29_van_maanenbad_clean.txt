Van Maanenbad

The roof over this swimming pool was built to slide open, so on a warm day the indoor pool could become an outdoor one.

From the Van Maanenstraat entrance, look up above the doors, where the original iron letters still spell out Sportfondsenbad. Behind the solid brick front, out of sight from the street, the outdoor pool and sunbathing lawns stretch away.

That word above the door tells the story, because the Sportfondsen movement was founded in 1923 by Han Bierenbroodspot, who came up with a savings scheme that let ordinary people help fund new swimming pools, and this one, opened in 1937 as the Sportfondsenbad Rotterdam-Noord, was built that way.

Inside, the main pool is twenty-five metres long, and the gabled roof above it splits into two halves that slide apart on rails, so swimmers could look up at the open sky, which was a remarkably modern idea for the 1930s.

The outdoor pool is fifty metres long, and today it is the only outdoor pool in central Rotterdam, so on a summer afternoon it is about as close to a beach as the city centre gets. The architects were De Roos and Overeijnder, and the whole complex has been a national monument since 2002.

Carefully restored and reopened in 2015, it remains a serious lap-swimming pool, with no slides or wave machines in sight.

The way it was paid for is what moves me most, since it was not a gift from a city or a rich patron, but the result of ordinary people saving together for something they wanted to share, and it is still doing exactly that job nearly ninety years later.

Bring your swimming things if you can, and try the outdoor pool on a fine day, or else walk on into the streets of Blijdorp and Bergpolder around you, where the 1930s architecture makes this whole neighbourhood feel of a piece with the pool.
