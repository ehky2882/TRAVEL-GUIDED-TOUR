Supernova Hotel

Two buildings that had run out of reasons to exist, an empty childcare centre and a tired old hotel, were stitched together to make this one.

The 's-Gravendijkwal is the wide boulevard on the western side of the centre, with the approach road to the Maastunnel running down its middle. Supernova is at number sixty-eight, where the bright, eclectic front of the hotel stands out among the older brick buildings on this stretch.

The building tells you a lot about how Rotterdam likes to work. The architects, Zondervan Architectuur, took the vacant childcare centre and the outdated hotel beside it, stripped them both right back and joined them into one, and the result won the Entree Award for best hotel design in 2019.

Inside, the interior was designed with the creative agency Containr Affairs, and the idea is that the hotel should reflect the many cultures that make up Rotterdam. There are thirty-eight rooms, and no two are the same, ranging from compact micro-lofts with beds up a level to family rooms, with vintage furniture, pieces by local artists and here and there a record player.

The public spaces are the real draw, with a vinyl lounge, a cocktail bar, a small concept store and a garden, and everything is meant to feel playful and lived-in rather than polished.

In its own small way, Supernova sums up a city that would rather rework what it has than start over. Two tired buildings, given new purpose, have turned into one of the more characterful places to stay in town, which feels like a fair summary of modern Rotterdam itself.

On your way out, follow the boulevard south towards the river, where the Maastunnel takes cars, cyclists and pedestrians underneath the water. The cyclists' tunnel, with its long wooden escalators, is well worth a look.
