Portugalia Tasca

In Portugal a tasca is a small, cosy neighbourhood restaurant where families gather around honest food, and this one has brought the idea to a quiet lane in the middle of Amsterdam.

Bakkersstraht is a short street between the busy Rembrantplain and the river Amstel, and Portugalia Tasca is at number twelve. The noise of the square sits at one end and the water at the other, with this small restaurant tucked in between, open every day from midday until late.

The menu reads like a tour of Portuguese home cooking. There is bakalyow a brahsh, shredded salt cod with eggs, olives and fine strips of fried potato; caldo verde, the kale, potato and sausage soup; and polvo a lagarayroo, slow cooked octopus finished with garlic and plenty of olive oil.

For something quicker, try the pashtaysh de bakalyow, crispy cod fritters that the restaurant recommends as the perfect start to any Portuguese meal, or a praygoo noo powng, a steak sandwich with garlic butter, which is about as satisfying as lunch can get.

The Portuguese link to this part of town is far older than the restaurant. In the seventeenth century, many Jewish families who had fled Portugal settled in Amsterdam and helped build its trade, and their great synagogue, the Portuguese Synagogue, still stands not far from here across the Amstel. A plate of salt cod in this neighbourhood is, in its own small way, part of a very long conversation between the two countries.

With lunch done, walk down to the end of the street and along the Amstel, where the bridges and the houseboats line up beautifully towards the Mahgera Brug. It is one of the best short walks in the city, and the right pace after a long lunch.
