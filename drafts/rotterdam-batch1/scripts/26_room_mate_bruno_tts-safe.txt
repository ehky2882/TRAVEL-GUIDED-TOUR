Room Mate Bruno

Painted on the walls of this old warehouse are the names of islands half a world away.

With your back to the Rinehahven, look up at the brick warehouse on the Vilhelmeenakahda, with its green loading doors and old hoisting beams still in place. This is the Pakhicemaysteren building, and Room Mate Bruno occupies much of it, with a food hall on the ground floor and rooms above.

The building's name comes from Pakhicemaysteren van de Tay, the company founded in eighteen eighteen that took over the warehousing work of the old Dutch East India Company in Rotterdam. This warehouse was first built in eighteen ninety one, burnt down in nineteen thirty seven, and was rebuilt on its original wooden piles, storing tea and other goods from the colonial trade.

Look for the painted lettering on the facade naming Celebes, Borneo, Java and Sumatra. They are a reminder of where the cargo came from, and of a colonial history the Netherlands is still working through.

The building stood empty from the nineteen eighties until restoration began in twenty fifteen, when the architects restored the old fabric and added a light court and extra floors. The hotel inside has two hundred and seventeen rooms, and the designers drew on spices, Spanish warmth and the bold primary colours of the Dutch de Stile movement, so the interiors are about as far from a sober warehouse as you can get.

The story here is one of reuse with the past left visible. Instead of scrubbing the warehouse clean of its history, the restoration kept the names, the doors and the beams, so the building wears its old life on the outside while doing something entirely new within.

Step into the food hall on the ground floor before you go, then cross the Rinehahven bridge to Kahtendrekht, the old sailors' quarter, where the waterfront is lined with places to eat and the views back across the harbour are hard to beat.
