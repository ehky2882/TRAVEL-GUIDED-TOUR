Toren Towin

The Dutch word ontmooten means to meet, and if you split it with a hyphen it also hints at being free of things you must do, which is the joke this cafay is built on.

Across the Lisplain from the pond, in the Liskwarteer of Rotterdam Nort, Toren Towin occupies part of the Church of the Resurrection, a post war church building completed in nineteen sixty one. Look for the entrance and the south facing terrace, with its planted garden beds where neighbours grow things together.

The church has housed a Protestant congregation for decades, and in twenty twenty three a local resident, Ahree Yan Bahn, set out to turn an unused part of it into a meeting place for the whole neighbourhood. Toren Towin opened that summer, run by a non profit foundation with nearly fifty volunteers, and its name simply means tower garden.

The terrace beds out front are looked after by people from the area, and the harvest is shared out among the neighbourhood, so the vegetables on the plate and the people at the next table may well have something in common.

The cafay keeps short hours, usually from Wednesday to Saturday, and serves coffee roasted on Goray Overflakkay, an island in the south of the province, with breakfast and lunch in a homely setting. There are children's workshops, weekend breakfasts, live music and lunches for local networks, all designed to get people who live a few doors apart to finally say hello.

And here is the real point: in a big city it is easy to live surrounded by people you never meet, and Toren Towin treats that as a problem worth solving with coffee, vegetables and an open door. The church building, built for one kind of gathering, has found room for another.

The opening hours are limited, so it is worth checking them before you come, and afterwards take a slow walk round the Lisvyver, the pond in front of the church, which is one of the quietest corners of Rotterdam Nort.
