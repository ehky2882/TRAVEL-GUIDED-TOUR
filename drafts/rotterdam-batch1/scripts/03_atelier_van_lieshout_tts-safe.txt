Atelier van Leeshowt

Some artists rent a studio, but Yoop van Leeshowt set up an entire workshop in the old port and at one point declared a small independent state there.

Kyleuhvekh, out in the former harbour district of Mairva Feerhahvens, puts low warehouses and open yards all around you. The long former port sheds housing Brutus, the space van Leeshowt founded, are the ones to look for, and in the garden and yard you may find large works waiting outside, depending on what has been made, shipped out or brought home that season.

Atelier van Leeshowt was founded in Rotterdam in nineteen ninety five, and its output is deliberately hard to pin down: sculpture, furniture, machines, buildings and installations, often circling the same big themes of power, self sufficiency, life and death. In two thousand and one, the year Rotterdam was European Capital of Culture, the studio launched ay vee el veel in the harbour, a free state with its own flag, constitution and currency.

The work has a talent for causing a stir. A sculpture called Domestikahtor, two buildings locked together in an unmistakably intimate pose, was withdrawn from a Louvre exhibition in the Tweeleree Garden in twenty seventeen because it stood near a playground, and it found a home instead outside the Pompidou Centre.

Brutus states its own purpose with refreshing bluntness, offering around six thousand square metres for art that is too big, too unwieldy, too noisy or too difficult for a conventional museum, and that list tells you why it sits out here rather than in a polished gallery district: work made at an industrial scale needs a place that still feels industrial.

Check the opening days before you set off, because the exhibition spaces keep shorter hours than the garden. Then walk on towards the water afterwards, where the old harbour basins of this district are slowly being remade, and you can see the next chapter of the port arriving one warehouse at a time.
