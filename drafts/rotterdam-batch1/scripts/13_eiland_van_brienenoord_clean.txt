Eiland van Brienenoord

Directly above you, one of the busiest motorway bridges in the country carries traffic over the river, and directly below it, Highland cattle are grazing.

Cross either of the two small bridges onto the island, and stop once you are on the main path with the great span of the Van Brienenoord Bridge overhead. The Nieuwe Maas wraps around you on all sides, with willows and poplars along the paths and river traffic moving steadily past.

The island has had a long working life as a base for salmon fishing and shipbuilding, and allotment gardens have been here since the Second World War. Look out for an old flooded dock where sections of the Rotterdam metro tunnel were built, with rusting machine parts still lying about.

The newest chapter is the tidal park, completed in 2023. The city added new bridges and nearly two kilometres of nature-friendly shoreline so that the river could move in and out properly, and the result won a special prize at the Gulden Feniks awards. Look for the branching tidal creek near the bridge, where the water rises and falls by about a metre and a half, driven by tides arriving from the North Sea more than thirty kilometres away.

The grazing is done by a small herd of Scottish Highland cattle, here since 2021 to keep the vegetation in check, and they are wild animals, so give them plenty of room.

The interesting bit is this: Rotterdam has spent centuries pushing the river into straight lines and hard edges, and here, under one of its busiest roads, it has deliberately let the tide back in. The rough nature around you was not left over, but invited back.

Follow the walking route round the island, which is about five kilometres, and find one of the small sandy beaches on the far side. Sitting there, with the barges going by and the bridge humming overhead, is a very Rotterdam kind of peace.
