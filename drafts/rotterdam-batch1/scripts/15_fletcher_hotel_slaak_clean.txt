Fletcher Boutique Hotel Slaak

For the first decades of its life this building smelled of ink, because it was where a big part of Rotterdam's news was printed.

On the Slaak, in Kralingen-West, the solid post-war building with its long rows of windows and a lobby lined in strips of natural stone is now the Fletcher Boutique Hotel Slaak. Pause on the pavement opposite and take in the full width of it, because the proportions belong to the 1950s, when this was a working industrial building rather than a place to stay.

It went up between 1952 and 1954, during the long reconstruction after the bombing of 1940, as a printing house for the social democratic newspaper Voorwaarts and the socialist daily Het Vrije Volk. The architect was Jo Vegter, working with Pieter Arend Leupen, and the building became a national monument in 2010 for its part in Rotterdam's recovery.

The stonework in the lobby is worth a look, because the style is a very Dutch post-war blend of plain, functional design softened with natural materials, so the building does its job without ever looking cold.

After the presses stopped, the building had several lives, as offices and for a while as a place for art exhibitions, before it was renovated and opened as a hotel in 2017.

There is a continuity underneath all this change that I rather like. In the 1950s this building helped a battered city follow its own rebuilding day by day, through the papers rolling off its presses, and today it houses people who have come to see the city that rebuilding produced.

[beat]

From the hotel, walk south-west towards the Oostplein, where the old windmill De Noord still stands at the edge of the reconstruction city. It is a good stretch for seeing how differently the older and newer halves of Rotterdam grew.
