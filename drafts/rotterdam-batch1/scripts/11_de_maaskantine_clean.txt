de Maaskantine

Rotterdam spends most of its time treating the river as a motorway for ships, but on this particular stretch of shore it is mainly something to sit beside.

Down on the Esch, the green spit of land in Rotterdam East where the city thins out into parks and allotments, the bright, painted canteen with a strip of sand in front of it is de Maaskantine. With your back to the bar, the Nieuwe Maas is right ahead of you, wide and grey-green, with barges sliding past on their way to and from the port.

The name is a small Dutch pun, joining the Maas, the river, to kantine, a canteen, and the idea is exactly that unfussy. It is billed as beach, bar and barbecue, with food inspired by the American East Coast: chicken wings and cauliflower wings, burgers, bread with dips, and vegan bitterballen for anyone who wants the classic Dutch bar snack without the meat.

Take a look at the building itself too, because it is covered in colourful artwork by the Rotterdam artist Jelmer Konjo, which gives the whole place the look of a beach hut that has wandered into a comic strip. On summer evenings there are open-air films, from cult classics to blockbusters, along with quiz nights and game nights.

Here the city's usual relationship with its river is quietly turned on its head. For centuries the Maas has been about trade, tonnage and getting cargo somewhere else, and here, a short ride from the centre, it becomes simply a view to enjoy with your feet in the sand and a drink in your hand.

[beat]

This is very much a warm-weather place, so it pays to look up the opening days before making the trip. Afterwards, follow the riverside paths east along the Esch, where the view opens towards the bridges and the water traffic heading upstream.
