Bakehouse

French patisserie, Dutch strawberry cream cake and Moroccan shebakeeya all share the same counter here, and the counter is open from six in the morning.

Halfway along the Tweeda Middellandstraht, at numbers eighteen to twenty two, the wide bakery front with its counters of bread and pastry is the one you are after. This is one of the busiest shopping streets in Rotterdam West, lined with grocers, butchers and bakers whose owners and customers have roots all over the world, and Bakehouse sits right in the middle of that mix.

The bread comes first, because that is the backbone of the place, with loaves and rolls baked fresh every day and filled sandwiches made up for people heading to work. The shop calls itself the world baker of Rotterdam, and the range behind the glass explains the title: croissants and French pastries alongside the honey soaked, sesame covered shebakeeya that turn up on Moroccan tables, especially around Ramadan.

The coffee corner is the second thing to notice. Plenty of customers never sit down, but there is room to stay for breakfast or lunch, and the long hours, starting at dawn and running into the evening, mean the bakery quietly serves the whole rhythm of the street, from the early shift to the after school rush.

There is one more detail worth knowing, which is that the bakery offers learning and work places for young people, so the trade behind the counter is being passed on as well as sold. The deeper point is that a neighbourhood bakery here does not have to choose a single tradition, because the people buying the bread never had to either.

Pick something from each end of the counter if you can, one familiar and one not, and eat them as you walk. The Middellandstraht runs on in both directions, and almost every shopfront along it has another version of the same quiet story.
