Hay Rotterdam

Good design should be affordable, according to the company behind this shop, and it has found a home inside a building that looks assembled from glass building blocks.

Look for the corner where the Maint meets the Hahkseveer, and you will find Hay on the ground floor of the Timmerhice. Above the shop, the building steps up and back in a stack of glass boxes, while behind it a wing from the nineteen fifties survives, folded right into the newer structure.

Hay itself was founded in Denmark in two thousand and two by Rolf Hay, and it built its name on furniture, lighting and accessories that are well designed, practical and priced within reach. The Rotterdam store was the brand's second in the Netherlands, after Amsterdam, and when it opened it was billed as the longest design supermarket in the city, complete with a minimarket of colourful small things that are very hard to leave without.

The building itself, the Timmerhice, was completed in twenty fifteen by oh em ay, the Rotterdam practice founded by Rem Kohlhahs, and its glass extension is made of cubes measuring about seven metres by seven by three and a half, repeated across fifteen storeys. The effect is pixelated, breaking a very large building into something that feels almost light on its feet.

There is a neat match between the two, since Hay makes design small enough to take home, and the Timmerhice breaks architecture down into small, repeatable pieces, so both are really about scale and about making big ideas feel approachable.

Cross the street afterwards and look at how the old nineteen fifties wing meets the new glass, because the two are literally joined, the older facade now sitting inside the newer building. Then head along the Maint, one of the city's best streets for independent shops.
