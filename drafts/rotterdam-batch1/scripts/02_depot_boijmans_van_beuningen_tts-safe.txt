Depot Boymans van Burningen

Every big museum has a back room where most of its collection waits out of sight, and this is the first one in the world built so that you can walk straight in.

That mirrored bowl rising out of the Museumpark lawn is the Depot, and it is worth stepping back far enough to catch your own reflection in it. The building narrows towards the ground and widens towards the top, so the trees, the paths and the passing cyclists all bend across its surface like a fairground mirror.

Count the panels if you have the patience: there are one thousand six hundred and sixty four of them, covering a building almost forty metres tall, designed by Weenee Mahs of em vee ar dee vee and opened in November twenty twenty one. The mirrors are a kind of good manners, since a storage block this size could easily have loomed over the park, and instead it hands the park back to you.

Inside are more than one hundred and fifty thousand objects from the collection of Museum Boymans van Burningen, and here is the lovely oddity: they are arranged by the climate they need rather than by period or artist. Paintings, metal, plastics and photographs each live in their own conditions, so an old master can end up a corridor away from a piece of modern furniture, with conservators at work in restoration studios inside the building.

The real argument of this building is about who gets to see what. Museums have always shown a small fraction of what they own, and the Depot turns the hidden back room into the main event, so storage itself becomes the exhibition.

Take the lift right to the top before you go, where a small forest of birch trees and pines grows on the roof beside the restaurant. From up there the city spreads out in every direction, and the building underneath you, for once, is the only thing you cannot see.
