Fletcher Boutique Hotel Slahk

For the first decades of its life this building smelled of ink, because it was where a big part of Rotterdam's news was printed.

On the Slahk, in Krahlingen West, the solid post war building with its long rows of windows and a lobby lined in strips of natural stone is now the Fletcher Boutique Hotel Slahk. Pause on the pavement opposite and take in the full width of it, because the proportions belong to the nineteen fifties, when this was a working industrial building rather than a place to stay.

It went up between nineteen fifty two and nineteen fifty four, during the long reconstruction after the bombing of nineteen forty, as a printing house for the social democratic newspaper Fohrvahrts and the socialist daily Het Vryeh Folk. The architect was Yo Fekhter, working with Peeter Ahrent Lurpen, and the building became a national monument in twenty ten for its part in Rotterdam's recovery.

The stonework in the lobby is worth a look, because the style is a very Dutch post war blend of plain, functional design softened with natural materials, so the building does its job without ever looking cold.

After the presses stopped, the building had several lives, as offices and for a while as a place for art exhibitions, before it was renovated and opened as a hotel in twenty seventeen.

There is a continuity underneath all this change that I rather like. In the nineteen fifties this building helped a battered city follow its own rebuilding day by day, through the papers rolling off its presses, and today it houses people who have come to see the city that rebuilding produced.

From the hotel, walk south west towards the Ohstplain, where the old windmill de Nort still stands at the edge of the reconstruction city. It is a good stretch for seeing how differently the older and newer halves of Rotterdam grew.
