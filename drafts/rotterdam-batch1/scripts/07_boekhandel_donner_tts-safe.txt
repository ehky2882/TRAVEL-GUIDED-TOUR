Bookhandel Donner

This bank was begun during the war, finished after liberation, and rescued many years later by the people who wanted to keep reading in it.

Coalsingel one nineteen, with its red brick, pale stone and a copper roof gone green, is the building to look at, ideally from a few steps back on the pavement. Above the central entrance, carved figures stand in the stonework, and the name over the door now belongs to Donner, one of the best loved bookshops in the Netherlands.

Have a look at those carvings first, because they are by the sculptor Hayman and show the port, shipping, fishing, farming and livestock, the whole economy a Rotterdam bank of the nineteen forties depended on. Higher up, around a round window, the architect placed figures for war, death and madness on one side and peace, fertility and prosperity on the other, a statement in stone from people who knew exactly what they had lived through.

The bank was designed by Mertens, and its first foundation pile went in during the spring of nineteen forty one, in a city centre that had been flattened the year before. Work stopped in nineteen forty two and only resumed after liberation, so the building is a sort of promise kept late.

Donner's own story turns out to be just as stubborn as the building's. The shop dates back to nineteen twelve, and when it was dragged into bankruptcy in twenty fourteen, five employees set out to save it, with some seventeen hundred and fifty book lovers buying shares. In twenty eighteen it moved in here, which means a bank built on hope in wartime now holds a bookshop owned in part by its readers.

Go inside and climb the main staircase, where a large window of sandblasted glass by the designer Copeer lights the way up. Then take your time, because the high ceilings of the old banking hall make it very easy to lose an afternoon among the shelves.
