Bertmans

Breakfast runs straight into lunch here, lunch slides into dinner, and the menu changes with the seasons rather than the clock.

Find the Karel Doormanstraat side of the block, a short walk from the Schouwburgplein, and the restaurant with the big windows and the terrace out front is Bertmans. To one side you have the shopping streets of the post-war centre, and to the other the open square with its theatre and cinema, so you are standing right at the meeting point of the city's shopping and its evenings out.

The menu is unusually honest about what it is for, since Bertmans calls itself an all-day restaurant, built around fresh, seasonal and largely organic ingredients with flavours borrowed from kitchens around the world, and a large share of the dishes are vegan or vegetarian without making a sermon of it.

The signature I would point you to is the buckwheat pancake, made with almond milk and served with apple, maple syrup and a cashew vanilla cream, which manages to feel indulgent and virtuous at the same time. There is avocado and poached egg on spelt toast for the traditionalists, and on weekday mornings an early breakfast deal for people on their way somewhere.

This part of Rotterdam was rebuilt after the bombing of 1940 with speed and efficiency in mind, and the nearby Lijnbaan, opened in 1953, was among the first purpose-built pedestrian shopping streets in Europe. A place that asks you to slow down and eat what the season offers sits rather nicely against that backdrop, like a long lunch in a city designed for errands.

[beat]

Once you have eaten, step out onto the Schouwburgplein and look up at the tall red lamp-posts that lean over the square like a row of harbour cranes. Then wander the Lijnbaan, where the low shops and the flats behind them still show how the reconstruction planners imagined everyday life.
