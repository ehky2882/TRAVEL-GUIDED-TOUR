Markthal

The people who live in this building look down through their windows onto a food market, which is about as Rotterdam a solution as you could wish for.

Inside the arch, with the stalls at ground level and that vast painted ceiling curving overhead, is the place to begin. Look straight up and the colours of fruit, flowers, fish and insects stretch away above you, and along the sides of the arch you can make out rows of windows looking in.

The mural is called Horn des Oaverfloots, the Horn of Plenty, created by the artists Arno Koonen and Iris Roskam, and it covers around eleven thousand square metres. The giant produce is a nod to Dutch still life painting of the seventeenth century, blown up to the scale of a cathedral.

The arch was designed by em vee ar dee vee and opened by Queen Maksima on the first of October twenty fourteen; it rises about forty metres, and those windows belong to two hundred and twenty eight apartments wrapped into the curve. The glass walls at each end are hung from a net of pre stressed steel cables, so the hall stays open while the weather stays out.

Down at ground level there are around a hundred fresh food stalls, with restaurants and shops along the edges.

And here's the clever part: the city needed a covered market, the old outdoor market had to meet stricter hygiene rules, and the planners wanted more people living in the centre. Rather than building three separate things, em vee ar dee vee wrapped the homes around the market, so the building solves its problems by stacking them.

Down at the lower level, find the time stairs, where objects found during the excavation are displayed in the order they were dug up. Then step outside onto the Binnenrotta, where the open air market still sets up on Tuesdays and Saturdays.
