Restaurant Frits

You will not be handed a menu at this restaurant, because the decision about dinner has already been made for you, and it changes every week.

Peet Hines plain, in historic Delfs hahven, is the little square where the old harbour district begins. Restaurant Frits is at number twenty five, an intimate dining room in one of the older buildings, with the gables of old Delfs hahven gathering just beyond the square.

Frits has been a tabla dote restaurant since two thousand and five, run by Ilya and Robbert, and the format is beautifully simple: everyone eats the same five course dinner, created fresh each week, with optional wine pairings or a non alcoholic version poured alongside. Allergies and dietary needs are handled with a so called shadow menu, as long as you let them know ahead of time.

Reviewers have described the room as more French than France, with patterned wallpaper, chandeliers and a wooden floor that creaks companionably as you cross it. It is open in the evenings from Thursday to Saturday, so this is a place you book rather than stumble into.

The square has history too, since Peet Hine, the naval commander who famously captured a Spanish silver fleet in sixteen twenty eight, was born in Delfs hahven, and a statue of him by the sculptor Graven was unveiled here in eighteen seventy with the king present.

In a city of endless choice, Frits offers the pleasure of giving the choice away. There is something rather freeing about sitting down and simply trusting the kitchen, and the weekly change means regulars never quite eat the same evening twice.

Before or after dinner, walk a few minutes into the heart of Delfs hahven, along the old harbour, where the Pilgrim Fathers' church and the brewery buildings line the water. It is one of the few parts of Rotterdam that looks much as it did centuries ago.
