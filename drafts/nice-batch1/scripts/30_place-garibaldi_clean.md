# Place Garibaldi

This square was built as a front door for travellers arriving from Turin.

You should be standing in Place Garibaldi, the big arcaded square on the northern edge of the old town. All around you are the buildings painted in ochre and yellow, with matching arcades at street level, and in the middle, in a little garden, a bronze statue on a high pedestal. That's Garibaldi.

The square was begun in seventeen seventy-three, when Nice still belonged to the kingdom of Sardinia, ruled from Turin. The architect, Antoine Spinelli, was from Turin too, and he designed it on the Turin model, with uniform facades, arcades to walk under in the shade, and strict symmetry. It was meant to be the grand entrance to the city at the end of the road from Turin. Over the years it's been Place Royale, Place Victor-Emmanuel, Place Napoléon, depending on who was in charge.

It got its current name in eighteen seventy, after Giuseppe Garibaldi, who was born in Nice, in eighteen oh seven, and went on to become one of the heroes of the unification of Italy. The statue went up in eighteen ninety-one.

The gift detail is the irony in that. In eighteen sixty, Nice was handed over to France, and Garibaldi was furious about it. His own birthplace had been traded away. And yet here he stands, in the middle of a French city's grandest square, built in the Italian style, on the old road to Italy.

[beat]

So this is one of the best places in Nice to feel what the city was before it was French. Not a Riviera resort, but an Italian port with its back to the mountains.

If you've got a few minutes, find the chapel of the Blue Penitents, on the square itself. Then head east down to the port. And if you're hungry, the oysters at the Café de Turin, under these same arcades, are hard to beat.
