# Confiserie Florian

You can eat a violet here. You can eat a rose petal, too.

You should be on the Quai Papacino, along the west side of the old port, near where it meets Rue Robilant. Look for the shopfront with Florian over the door, and the window full of fruit glazed until it shines. That's the confiserie.

The Florian name has been tied to this harbour for about a century. A chocolate maker called Florian worked on the port here from the nineteen-twenties. The confectionery family that now runs it began up in the hills near Grasse, in the Gorges du Loup, and took over this shop in nineteen seventy-four.

The gift detail is the flowers. Grasse, an hour inland, is the perfume capital of the world, surrounded by fields of rose and jasmine. The violets come from the village of Tourrettes, in the same hills. Here, the flowers are crystallised, coated in sugar until they keep, and they still taste faintly of their scent. It's like eating a perfume bottle, in the nicest possible way.

The fruit is the other half of the business. Clementines, lemons from Menton, oranges, cherries. A big piece of fruit can soak for up to three months, with the syrup changed every couple of days, before it's ready.

[beat]

What I find rather wonderful is that nothing about this is new. People along this coast have been sugaring their fruit and flowers for centuries because they had too much of both. What began as a way of not wasting the harvest has become one of the prettiest windows on the port.

If you have a little time, ask inside about a tour. They've long offered free visits of the workshop, with tastings. And on the way out, look at the boats in front of you, the little wooden ones painted in bright colours. They're called pointus, and they're as Niçois as anything in the shop.
