# Oh tel windsor

Nobody planned for this hotel to become an art gallery. It happened one room at a time.

You should be on roo dal pot so, a quiet street a couple of blocks back from the prom nahd day zong lay. The building in front of you, number eleven, is a nineteenth century building in what the hotel calls the Eiffel School style. That's the Windsor.

The reh dol fee family has run it for around eighty years. In nineteen eighty nine, bernard reh dol fee did something no sensible hotelier would do. He handed one room, number eighty three, to the artist zho el doo kor wah and let him do what he liked with it. Then he did it again. And again.

Today most of the rooms are the work of a different contemporary artist, and no two are alike. Some of the names will be familiar if you know French art. Ben, the Neece artist famous for his handwritten slogans, is here. So is the British cartoonist Glen Baxter, and the video artist pyeh reek so ran. Every year a new artist takes over the lobby.

The other surprise is behind the building. There's a tropical garden, thick with palms and flowering plants, a heated pool, and a giant rubber tree that breakfast is served under in summer. At night, an installation by mow ro ben etty called lah loona is reflected in the water.

What I find rather lovely about the Windsor is that it's the opposite of the grand seafront palaces a few streets away. They spent fortunes to look impressive. This one spent its imagination instead, and it has ended up with a collection a small museum would envy.

If you can, step into the bar for a drink. The installation in there is called bar bah ree, by nee ko lah rubinstein, and it's the quickest way to see what the fuss is about.
