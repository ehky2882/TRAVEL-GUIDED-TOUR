Cours Saleya

Matisse lived on the top floors of the building at the end of this market for seventeen years.

You should be standing in the Cours Saleya, the long open square in the old town, just behind the seafront. Down the middle are the market stalls, with their striped awnings. Find the eastern end, where the square closes off, and look for the tall yellow building with rows of windows facing straight down the market. That's the one.

The market has been here since the eighteen-sixties, when the wholesale, flower and produce markets were all brought together on this stretch. These days the flowers and fruit and vegetables are out from Tuesday to Sunday. On Mondays, the whole square turns over to antiques and the flea market.

Look along the seaward side of the square at the long, low row of buildings. Those are the Ponchettes. They were built in the eighteenth century, after the old city walls came down, and they're backed onto by a raised terrace where you can walk along the roof line above the sea.

Now back to that yellow building, the Palais Caïs de Pierlas, at number one Place Charles-Félix. Matisse moved in in nineteen twenty-one and stayed until nineteen thirty-eight, on the third and fourth floors. From his windows he could see the market on one side and the sea on the other. A lot of the light-filled interiors he painted in those years, with their patterned fabrics and open windows, were painted up there.

[beat]

So when you see a Matisse interior in a museum somewhere, the shutters open onto the Mediterranean, there's a decent chance you're looking at this square, seen from that window.

Before you go, buy something from a stall, a bunch of flowers or a handful of olives. Then walk up onto the terrace above the Ponchettes and look down on the market. It's the view Matisse had, give or take a few storeys.
