# Tête Carrée

This is a sculpture of a head, and people go to work inside it.

You should be on the edge of the Promenade du Paillon, just behind the MAMAC, near the Louis Nucéra library. In front of you is a giant figure, a bust whose head is a cube, faceless, square and enormous. That's the Tête Carrée, the square head.

It's the work of the Nice artist Sacha Sosno, who was associated with the New Realists, the group of artists around Yves Klein. Sosno built his career on what he called obliteration, hiding part of a figure to make you look harder at the rest. Here, he obliterated the face entirely. He worked with the architects Yves Bayard and Francis Chapus, and it was inaugurated in two thousand and two.

Now for the gift detail. The head isn't solid. It's a building. The cube, fourteen metres on each side, holds seven floors of offices for the city's library network, set on a vast aluminium neck and shoulders. The whole thing stands about thirty metres high. It's often called the first inhabited monumental sculpture in the world.

It isn't open to the public, unfortunately. The librarians get the view.

[beat]

What I like about it is the joke. A library is a building full of other people's heads, and Sosno made a head to put the library's brains in. And he left off the face, so that every passer-by fills in an expression of their own.

If you've time, walk round it slowly. From one side it reads as a sculpture and from another as a tower block, and the change happens in a few steps. Then go into the library itself, next door, which is open to everyone.
