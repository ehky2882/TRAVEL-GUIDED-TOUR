# Café de Turin

The arcades over your head were built by an architect from Turin, which is why the café underneath them is named after his city.

You should be standing on Place Garibaldi, under the arcades. Look for the café's terrace and its counter piled with ice and shellfish. The man in the apron opening oysters at the front has the most important job here.

Café de Turin has been an institution in Nice for more than a hundred years, and it does one thing. Seafood platters, all sizes. Oysters, clams, prawns, crab, mussels. In season, it's also one of the places to try oursins, the local sea urchins, broken open and eaten raw with a spoon. They taste of nothing so much as the sea itself.

The name isn't decoration. When this square was laid out in the seventeen-seventies, Nice belonged to the House of Savoy, ruled from Turin. The square was designed as a grand gateway at the end of the road from Turin, with matching arcades on the Turin model so that people could walk in the shade. For most of its life, Nice faced north towards the Alps and Italy, not towards Paris.

[beat]

So eating oysters here is a small piece of time travel. The arcades are Piedmontese, the statue in the middle is the Italian hero Garibaldi, who was born in this city, and the café is named after a capital Nice no longer answers to.

If you've got a few minutes afterwards, head south-east out of the square, towards the port. Walk a couple of blocks down to the water and you'll see the boats that supply a good deal of what's on these platters.
