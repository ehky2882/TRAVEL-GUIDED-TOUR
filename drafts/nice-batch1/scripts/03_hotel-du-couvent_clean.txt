Hôtel du Couvent

For nearly four hundred years, this was a place for people who'd given up the world. Now it's a hotel.

You should be in the upper part of the old town, at the corner of Rue Honoré Ugo, where the lanes start climbing towards the Castle Hill. The long building with the old stone walls and shuttered windows is the Hôtel du Couvent. Look for the terraces rising up the slope behind it, green with trees.

It was built as a convent in sixteen oh four, by nuns of the Order of Saint Clare. In the early nineteenth century the Visitation sisters moved in, and they stayed until the early nineteen-eighties. 

The man who brought it back, Valéry Grégo, spent roughly ten years on it, and the approach is what makes it worth a stop. The architects, Studio Mumbai with Studio Méditerranée, called it frugal. Timber and stone found on the site were reused. Some of the desks in the rooms are made from four-hundred-year-old beams pulled out of the convent's own roof.

Then there's the water. Up the hill in Cimiez are the ruins of the Roman baths of the ancient town of Cemenelum. The hotel has built its own version, a run of pools that get warmer as you go, fed down through terraced gardens with more than three hundred kinds of plant.

[beat]

So the convent ends up doing very nearly what it always did. It grows its own herbs, bakes its own bread with flour milled on site, and sends most guests away a bit calmer than they arrived. The vows have gone. The rhythm hasn't.

If you're passing on a Saturday morning, look in. There's a farmers' market in the courtyard, and it's the easiest way to see the cloister without booking a room.
