# Oh tel lah pay rooz

This hotel doesn't sit at the foot of the cliff. It's built into it.

Find yourself on the kay rowba kapeyoo, at the far eastern end of the bay, where the prom nahd day zong lay runs out of room and the Castle Hill drops straight into the sea. The building pressed against the rock face in front of you is La pay rooz. Look up from the entrance and you'll see that the hotel climbs the hill in terraces, the bare limestone showing between them.

The name comes from zhon fron swah duh lah pay rooz, the eighteenth century French navigator who sailed off to map the Pacific and was never seen again. Fitting, for a building that spends its whole life staring at the horizon. The hotel says it has been carved into this rock since nineteen thirty six.

Two details make the place. The first is the garden. Behind the front door there's a patio planted with lemon trees, some of them a century old, with jasmine climbing the walls. In the middle of a city it smells like a farmhouse in the hills. The second is the pool, which is cut into the rock itself, so you swim with the cliff at your back and the whole bay in front of you.

What I love about this spot is the trick of it. From the Promenade, this end of the bay looks like a dead end, all rock and no room. Somebody looked at that cliff and saw a hotel. Climb high enough and every window has the view that the rest of the seafront pays a fortune for.

From here, you don't need to be a guest to get the view. Carry on along the quay for a minute, past the war memorial cut into the same cliff, and take the steps or the lift up the Castle Hill. At the top, look back down at La pay rooz. From above, you'll finally see how much of it is hiding in the rock.
