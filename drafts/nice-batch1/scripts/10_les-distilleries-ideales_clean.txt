Les Distilleries Idéales

This bar looks as if it's been here for a century. It hasn't.

You should be on Rue de la Préfecture, in the old town, at the corner where the bar's terrace spills out onto the lane. Look for the old-fashioned lettering over the windows, the dark wood frontage and, after six in the evening, a crowd of locals with drinks in hand.

The name means the ideal distilleries, and the room is dressed for it. Inside there's a Belle Époque and industrial look, all wood, metal and old bottles, the sort of décor that makes you assume your great-grandfather drank here. In fact it's been on this corner for a couple of decades, which by old-town standards is practically yesterday.

That doesn't make it a fake. It makes it rather good at something Nice has always done. This whole city is a bit of a stage set. The seafront hotels were built to look like palaces, and the Promenade itself began life so the English winter visitors could have somewhere to stroll. A bar dressed to look old fits right in.

What's actually in the glasses is simple. There are around eighteen beers on tap, a reasonable list of wines, and boards of charcuterie to go with them. And the crowd is mostly from Nice, not from tour groups, which is the best review any bar in this part of town can have.

[beat]

So treat it as what it is, the neighbourhood's front room. The apéritif hour between six and eight is the moment to come.

When you've finished your drink, walk a couple of minutes towards the sea. You'll come out onto the Cours Saleya, and if the market is packing up, you'll catch the last of the flower stalls being folded away.
