# Ka fay doo see kleest

The cyclists here are about to ride up mountains. The coffee is part of the training.

You should be on the kay day docks, along the east side of the old port. Look for the kaffay and shop at number sixteen, with bicycles leaning outside and riders in Lycra standing about with espresso cups in hand. That's kaffay doo see kleest.

It's a clothing brand first. It was founded in Neece in two thousand and nine, and the story goes that it was dreamt up in a traditional kaffay in the hills above the coast, the kind of place riders stop at halfway up a climb. In twenty fifteen it moved down here, to a big space on the port, and became a shop, a kaffay and something like a clubhouse at once.

The reason it's here, and not in Paris, is outside the door. Neece has the sea on one side and the Alps on the other, and the roads that link them are some of the most famous in cycling. Professional riders live along this coast because they can train on serious climbs within half an hour of their front door. The kol dez, behind the city, is one of the famous climbs of the Paris to Neece race. The routes start right here.

What I like about this place is that it gets something right about Neece that the postcards miss. People come here for the beach. The locals, quite a lot of them, come here for the hills.

If you're not riding, order a coffee and watch the groups set off. Then walk to the end of the quay and look up at the hills behind the port. Every one of those switchbacks has someone on a bicycle somewhere on it.
