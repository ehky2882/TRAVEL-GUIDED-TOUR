# Palais Lascaris

The lane outside is barely wide enough for two people to pass. The staircase inside was built for a procession.

You should be on Rue Droite, the narrow street running through the middle of the old town. Look for the tall facade with a carved doorway, stone balconies and the words Palais Lascaris. From out here it barely looks like a palace. That's the old town for you.

It was built in the middle of the seventeenth century for the Lascaris-Vintimille family, one of the great noble houses of the county of Nice. It stayed in the family until the time of the French Revolution, and then passed through other hands. The city bought it in nineteen forty-two, restored it, and opened it as a museum in nineteen seventy.

Go through the door and the scale changes completely. A grand staircase rises in front of you, with carved balustrades and marble statues set in niches. Upstairs, the state rooms have ceilings painted with scenes from mythology, framed in stucco from the end of the seventeenth century. And on the ground floor, there's an eighteenth-century pharmacy.

The gift detail is what lives in those rooms now. The palace holds the second-largest public collection of historic musical instruments in France, harpsichords, guitars, horns, much of it left to the city by a collector in nineteen-oh-four.

[beat]

What I love about this place is the surprise. In a city of wide promenades and seafront palaces, one of the finest Baroque interiors in Nice is hidden behind a doorway on a lane you could touch both sides of.

When you leave, carry on along Rue Droite and take any turn uphill. Within a couple of minutes you'll be on the lower steps of the Castle Hill. Look back down at the rooftops, and try to pick out the Lascaris from above.
