# Moulin à Huile d'Olive Alziari

Look for the blue and yellow tins. They've become the unofficial flag of Niçois olive oil.

You should be on Rue Saint-François-de-Paule, a few steps from the opera house. The little shop with its shelves of painted tins is Alziari. If the door's open, you'll smell it before you see it.

The business goes back to eighteen sixty-eight, when a man named César Martin bought a ruined old mill in the Madeleine district, in the hills on the west of the city, and rebuilt it. In nineteen hundred his son-in-law, Nicolas Alziari, took it over and gave it his name. The mill is still working today, the oldest of its kind still running in Nice.

The shop came later. Nicolas's daughter Pauline opened it here at some point between the wars, and it's been the house's best-known address ever since. The tins came with it, in that azure blue and sunflower yellow, decorated in the Provençal style.

The local olive is called the Cailletier, small and dark, and it's the only variety grown in this area. It's also the olive that ends up, brined and black, in a salade niçoise.

[beat]

And here's the gift detail. Nicolas Alziari's idea was to blend, to mix oils from different olives to get a flavour he liked, rather than rely on any single one. That's why a tin from here never tastes quite like anybody else's. A family recipe for oil, of all things, kept going for more than a century.

When you've bought your tin, walk on to the Cours Saleya, a few minutes away. In autumn and winter, look out for the market stalls selling the season's olives. That's where all of this begins.
