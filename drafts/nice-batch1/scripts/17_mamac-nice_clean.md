# MAMAC, Musée d'Art Moderne et d'Art Contemporain

Four marble towers, linked by glass bridges, standing on top of a river you can't see.

You should be on Place Yves Klein, on the edge of the long park called the Promenade du Paillon, looking at a large white building made of four square towers. They're clad in smooth white marble from Carrara, and they're joined high up by glazed walkways. That's the museum of modern and contemporary art, which everyone here calls the MAMAC.

It was designed by the architects Yves Bayard and Henri Vidal and opened in nineteen ninety. The four-legged form was a deliberate choice. The building stands astride the course of the Paillon, the river that once ran through the middle of Nice and has since been covered over, so it acts as a sort of grand gateway between the old town and the newer city.

The square in front is named after Yves Klein, and that's the clue to what's inside. Klein was born in Nice in nineteen twenty-eight and became famous for a single, impossibly deep blue that he patented. He and the artists around him, the New Realists, many of them from Nice, are the heart of the museum's collection. Klein has a room of his own.

A word of warning. The museum is closed for a long renovation and isn't expected to reopen until twenty twenty-eight, so for now this is a building to look at rather than walk into.

[beat]

Which turns out to be no bad thing. From out here you can see what Bayard and Vidal were up to. They didn't put the art in a box. They built a set of white frames against the Mediterranean sky, and in this light, the white is doing what Klein's blue did. It makes you look.

Before you leave, walk round the back of the building towards the Tête Carrée, the giant square head next door. It's by Sacha Sosno, and it's part of the same story.
