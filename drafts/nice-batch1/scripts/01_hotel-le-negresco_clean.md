# Hôtel Le Negresco

The man who built this hotel trained as a sweet-maker.

Stand on the sea side of the Promenade des Anglais and look inland. The long white wedding cake of a building in front of you is the Negresco. You'll know it by the dome on its corner, pink, with a green cap, and by the doormen out front in eighteenth-century livery and hats with red plumes.

Henri Negresco was born Alexandru Negrescu, the son of a Romanian innkeeper. He trained as a confectioner in Bucharest, worked his way across Paris and the Riviera, and ended up running the municipal casino here. Then he did what every hotel manager dreams of. He built his own. It opened just before the First World War, designed by Édouard-Jean Niermans, the architect of choice for Belle Époque pleasure palaces, and Niermans gave it that dome.

Here's the bit the brochures sometimes get wrong. You may hear that the great glass roof inside was made by Gustave Eiffel. It wasn't. Eiffel never worked here. The building is Niermans from top to bottom.

Inside, under that roof, hangs a Baccarat chandelier of more than sixteen thousand crystals. It was ordered by Tsar Nicholas the Second. The Russian Revolution got in the way, the Tsar never took delivery, and it ended up here instead.

[beat]

And that's rather the story of the whole building. Within a couple of years of opening, the war turned it into a hospital, the rich stopped coming, and Negresco lost the lot. He died in Paris at fifty-two. The hotel he dreamed up has outlived him by more than a century, and since nineteen fifty-seven, when the Augier family bought it, it has filled up with paintings and sculpture as if it were a private museum with beds.

If you've got a few minutes, and you're dressed for it, ask at the door about seeing the Salon Royal. The chandelier is worth the detour. Then come back out and look along the seafront at the dome once more. It's still the most recognisable roofline on the whole Baie des Anges.
