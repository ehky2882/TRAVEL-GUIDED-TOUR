# Mah mak, the museum of modern and contemporary art

Four marble towers, linked by glass bridges, standing on top of a river you can't see.

You should be on plass eve klyne, on the edge of the long park called the prom nahd doo pie yon, looking at a large white building made of four square towers. They're clad in smooth white marble from Carrara, and they're joined high up by glazed walkways. That's the museum of modern and contemporary art, which everyone here calls the mah mak.

It was designed by the architects eve bye ar and on ree vee dal and opened in nineteen ninety. The four legged form was a deliberate choice. The building stands astride the course of the pie yon, the river that once ran through the middle of Neece and has since been covered over, so it acts as a sort of grand gateway between the old town and the newer city.

The square in front is named after eve klyne, and that's the clue to what's inside. klyne was born in Neece in nineteen twenty eight and became famous for a single, impossibly deep blue that he patented. He and the artists around him, the New Realists, many of them from Neece, are the heart of the museum's collection. klyne has a room of his own.

A word of warning. The museum is closed for a long renovation and isn't expected to reopen until twenty twenty eight, so for now this is a building to look at rather than walk into.

Which turns out to be no bad thing. From out here you can see what Bayard and Vidal were up to. They didn't put the art in a box. They built a set of white frames against the Mediterranean sky, and in this light, the white is doing what klyne's blue did. It makes you look.

Before you leave, walk round the back of the building towards the tet ka ray, the giant square head next door. It's by sasha soz no, and it's part of the same story.
