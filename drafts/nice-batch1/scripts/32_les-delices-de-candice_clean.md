# Les Délices de Candice

Despite the name, there's nothing to eat in here.

Rue de la Préfecture, in the old town, a few steps from the Palais de Justice square. Find number seventeen, a small shopfront with a window full of rings, bracelets and necklaces. Délices means delights, and in a city famous for its sweet shops you'd be forgiven for expecting chocolates. What you get instead is jewellery.

Everything in the window is made by one person, Candice, in a small workshop at the back of the shop. The gift detail is what she makes it from. Each piece starts with something old, original pieces from roughly the nineteen-twenties to the nineteen-seventies, which she combines with brass, semi-precious stones and crystal. Nothing is quite a copy of anything else, and the skull rings have something of a following.

Now, the room itself has a longer story than the jewellery. For a hundred and sixty-seven years, number seventeen belonged to Maison Bestagno, an umbrella maker. It was founded in eighteen fifty, ten years before Nice became French, by a man named Felipe Colombo, who walked here from Lake Maggiore in the Italian Alps and sold umbrellas door to door. After him it passed down the family, nephew to nephew, brother to brother. Until nineteen seventy, the umbrellas were made by hand on the floor above. Belmondo and Brigitte Bardot bought them here, and the shop made the umbrellas for Prince Albert's wedding in Monaco. The last of the family, Gino Bestagno, retired at the end of twenty seventeen with nobody to take over, and the shop was let to another artisan.

[beat]

And that's what I like about this doorway. In most old towns, a closing like that means a souvenir shop. Here, a room that spent a century and a half with somebody making things at the back has, once again, somebody making things at the back. Different objects, same habit.

Before you go, look up at the four floors above the shop. The Bestagno family bought the whole building in eighteen fifty. Then walk a few doors further along Rue de la Préfecture to Les Distilleries Idéales, if the hour is right for a drink.
