PHILADELPHIA — W1 — THE FIFTH SQUARE — 12 — WASHINGTON SQUARE — THE PUBLISHERS' SQUARE
Walk 1 · segment 12 · register AMERICAN
Vantage: the Walnut Street edge of the square, the Curtis Building and the Athenaeum in view
Spines: none in this segment (S1 is W2's; the square is counted, not drawn) · docket 3, 7 (the ground nodded to, not re-told)

The square's standalone stop is about what is under it. This one is about what went out from it, because for a hundred years the buildings around this park printed much of what the country read.

The long white building on the north side with the frieze along its top is the Curtis Building, finished in nineteen ten for the Curtis Publishing Company, which sent two magazines with circulations in the millions out of this block every week and every month. Behind you on the south side, the brownstone with the Greek Revival front is the ath eh nee um, a subscription library that has lent books to its members since eighteen fourteen. Around the other edges, in buildings that have mostly since turned into apartments and offices, were lip in kot, the medical publishers Lea and feb ih ger and double you bee saunders, and a half dozen smaller houses, close enough together that an author could walk a manuscript from one to the next in an afternoon. The trade has largely gone elsewhere and left the square with the buildings.

It is worth one sentence on the ground, because this walk is about to pass over a good deal of it: this park was a burial ground before it was a garden, and the people in it, soldiers of both sides, the dead of the fever year, Black Philadelphians denied a churchyard, are stated and honored in the standalone stop, and this walk lowers its voice as it crosses them and does not say more. Leave by the southwest corner, onto Sixth Street, and walk south. Three short blocks: Spruce, Pine, lom bard.
