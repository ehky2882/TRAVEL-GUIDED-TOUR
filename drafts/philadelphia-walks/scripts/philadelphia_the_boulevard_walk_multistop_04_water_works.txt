PHILADELPHIA — WALK 3: THE BOULEVARD — SEGMENT 22: THE WATER WORKS — WHAT THE BOULEVARD WAS AIMED AT
Walk 3 · segment 22 · register AMERICAN
Vantage: the Water Works terrace above the dam, facing the river
---
Stand at the rail and look at the water first, not the buildings. The dam runs across the river in a long low curve, and the river slides over its lip in a sheet. Above the dam the water is flat and wide and barely moving. Below it the river is a river again, brown and quick, heading for the city.

The buildings behind you went up between 1812 and 1822, columns and pediments on a stone base, and they housed machinery. The engineer in charge was set the job of getting river water up to the reservoir on the hill you have just come down from, and his first answer was steam. His second, after the steam engines proved expensive and twice blew up, was to build the dam, raise the river into a pool, and let the pool turn waterwheels that lifted the river to the top of the hill. From 1822 the river pumped itself, and the city came out on Sundays to look at it doing so, and kept coming after the wheels stopped in 1909. Inside the buildings now is an exhibit on the city's water, with hours this walk does not vouch for; the terrace is always open, and the terrace is what you came for.

*[beat]*

This is the last thing the walk has to say about the two rivers, and it has been walking toward it since the circle. The Delaware, on the far side of the city, got the work, the wharves and the trade and later the highway, and this river got the picture. But look at what the picture is made of. The flat water upstream, the stillness that the rowers use and the boathouses are lit to be reflected in, is not what the river looked like before 1821. It is the pool behind Graff's dam. The city built a dam to raise water for a pump, found it had made a lake, and spent the next century deciding the lake was worth looking at, until it laid a boulevard a mile long to arrive at the top of this hill and look down at it. The picture was made by the work. The whole street you have just walked was aimed, in the end, at a pool behind a dam that was built for plumbing.

Leave the terrace at its upstream end and take the path along the river, keeping the water on your left, under the trees. The road beside you is Kelly Drive. In a few hundred yards a line of boathouses begins, and the walk ends in front of them.
