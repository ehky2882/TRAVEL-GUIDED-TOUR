philadelphia — walk 4: brick — segment c1: the arch street blocks
Walk 4 · segment C1 · register AMERICAN
Vantage: Arch Street, west from Third toward Fourth
---
On the first plan this street was Mulberry. Arch is a nickname that stuck: near the river, Front Street was carried over it on an arch, so that boats and carts could pass beneath, and the street took the bridge's name after the bridge was gone.

The brick here is later than anything you have seen so far and bigger in every way. These are the four- and five story fronts of the eighteen hundreds, when this end of Arch sold cloth and hardware to the rest of the country, and the ground floors were showrooms and the upper floors were storage. The bond is mostly common; by then nobody sorted headers for a warehouse. What the builders did instead was stretch the material: taller windows, arched in brick; cornices in brick stepped out course by course; entire fronts in a single unbroken color because the brickyards, by the eighteen hundreds, were an industry and not a kiln behind a house. Two hundred years after the Alley, the lot was still the unit. It had simply grown.

Keep to the left hand sidewalk, because what comes next is on that side. Ahead at Fourth, set back behind a brick wall of its own, is a meeting house: the same material as the church you started at with every decoration left off, and the leaving off was a decision. Beyond it, at Fifth, is the burial ground, and in the corner of the burial ground, a few feet from the sidewalk, is the grave the whole city knows, which the walk will reach without naming. The next segment takes both. Carry on to the wall.
