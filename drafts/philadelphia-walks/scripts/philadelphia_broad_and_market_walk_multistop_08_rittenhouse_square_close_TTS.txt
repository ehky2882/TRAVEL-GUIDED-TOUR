Walk 2 · segment 09 · register AMERICAN
Vantage: The northeast corner of Rittenhouse Square, at Eighteenth and Walnut, then the diagonal to the center
---
Enter at the corner and take the diagonal path, not the one along the edge.

The diagonal is what the design is for. The square was relaid in nineteen thirteen so that people crossing it on their way somewhere would pass through its center instead of skirting it, and the center is a small oval with a reflecting pool and a bronze goat. You are crossing it the way the architect intended, from the busy corner to the quiet one.

Look at what rings the square as you go: a brownstone church of the eighteen fifties on the far corner of the Walnut Street side, a music school behind you in a pair of old mansions, apartment houses, a hotel. A famous study of nineteen sixty one credited exactly that mix for keeping the square in use at every hour of the day, and you can test the claim from any bench.

Sit down on one. You have walked from the northeastern square to the southwestern one, through the center, along a street named for the thing that moved. When the plan was laid out, this ground was pasture at the far corner of a city nobody had finished, and the city's middle was a mile and a half east of here, among the sheds. Then the sheds came out, the hall went up on the center square, the towers went up past it, and the money and the people kept coming this way until the quiet square in the far corner became the one everybody wanted to live beside.

Nobody won the argument about the middle. The city just kept moving it west, and when the middle reached this square it stopped arguing and sat down.
