PHILADELPHIA — W1 — THE FIFTH SQUARE — 00 — INTRO AT WASHINGTON SQUARE
Walk 1 · segment 00 · register AMERICAN
Vantage: Washington Square, the Walnut Street edge, facing east
Spines: none (docket 1 — the Lenape statement lives here and nowhere else)
---
Face east. Six blocks that way is a river you cannot see from here, and this walk ends standing beside it. Between here and there the city runs out: the grid of streets laid down in sixteen eighty two reaches Front Street and stops, and past Front Street there is a highway in a trench and then the del a wair. The walk is about a mile, flat, and it takes the long way around, south through some of the oldest residential blocks in the city and then east, because the straight way is not the interesting one.

One thing has to be said first, and this is the place to say it. Before there were squares or streets, this was leh nah pay hoh king, the country of the leh nah pay, who fished this river and farmed its banks for longer than anyone has counted. The founder bought his land from them in a series of deeds, and his sons took a great deal more in seventeen thirty seven with a document the leh nah pay have called a fraud ever since. There are leh nah pay nations in Oklahoma, Wisconsin, and Ontario, and the leh nah pay Nation of Pennsylvania is here. That is the ground under every stop on this walk. It will not be repeated; it belongs at the beginning.

The square you are standing in is one of five laid out at the founding, the southeastern one, and it has a standalone stop of its own that tells what lies under the grass. This walk takes it differently. Cross to the Walnut Street side and look at the buildings.
