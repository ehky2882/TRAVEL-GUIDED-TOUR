PHILADELPHIA — WALK 3: THE BOULEVARD — SEGMENT 07: THE STEPS — AIMED FROM THE TOP
Walk 3 · segment 07 · register AMERICAN
Vantage: first the plaza at the foot of the museum steps, then the top landing, facing back down the boulevard
---
The oval is a traffic circle with a park inside it, and the only honest way across is on foot at the signals. Cross the boulevard's lanes at the light where the street meets the oval, cross onto the grass, pass the equestrian fountain at its center, and cross again at the far signal to reach the plaza at the foot of the steps. Wait for every light; the traffic here moves as though the museum were a highway exit, which in a sense it is.

The hill the museum stands on is called Fairmount, and for a century it was a reservoir: the city's drinking water sat in basins on top of it, pumped up from the river on the far side, and ran downhill from here to every tap in the grid. The basins were drained for the museum. The building you are about to climb toward stands where the water stood, and that fact comes up again at the bottom of the hill.

Climb the steps. At the top, set back from the edge on a pedestal, is the statue the city moved up here in 2026, after decades at the foot of the steps where a bronze of Joe Frazier, a champion who fought out of this city, now stands in its place. The statue has its own history and this walk does not run it; the steps are the reason to stand here.

Turn around. From the top you are looking straight back down the boulevard: a mile of straight street to the tower, the circle you started at halfway along it, the matched pair framing the circle, the trees in their rows. Everything on it was placed to be seen from here, and the museum was placed to be seen from everything on it. The building was set on the hill to be the thing at the end of the street, the street was laid to arrive at the building, and neither makes sense alone. The museum opened in 1928, three wings around a court open to the boulevard, in a stone the color of sand, with its whole face toward the city.

It turned its back on the river. That is the side worth seeing now. From the top landing, walk left, around the museum's north wing, to the terrace behind it. Below you, on the riverbank, is a row of small classical buildings with a dam running beside them across the water. A stair drops to them from the terrace; if the stair is closed, the drive that loops behind the museum reaches the same ground, more slowly. Go down to them.
