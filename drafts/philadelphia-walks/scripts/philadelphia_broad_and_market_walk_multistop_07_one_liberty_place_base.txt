PHILADELPHIA — WALK 2: BROAD AND MARKET — SEGMENT 30: ONE LIBERTY PLACE, AT THE BASE
Walk 2 · segment 30 · register AMERICAN
Vantage: The corner of Sixteenth and Market, at the foot of the tower, then the far corner
---
From here you cannot see the top. That is the thing about a very tall building at its own front door: it is a lobby, a revolving door, and a granite wall, and the part that changed the city is more than nine hundred feet above your neck.

Cross to the far corner, on the south side of Market, and look up anyway. The tower steps back as it rises, in stages, and ends in a crown of blue glass and a spire, a shape the architect took openly from a New York skyscraper of the 1930s and did not apologize for. It opened in 1987. Twenty minutes ago, in the courtyard, you were told what that date meant. This is the building that did it, and from down here you would never know — it meets the sidewalk like any office building on any block, and keeps its drama for the sky.

Its twin, a little shorter, stands behind it to the south, and the two share a block and a glass shopping rotunda between them. Beyond them, west and north, are the towers that followed once the rule was gone, each one a little farther from City Hall than the last.

From this corner, walk south on Sixteenth Street three blocks to Walnut, turn right, and walk two blocks west to where the trees begin. That is the northeast corner of the last square.
