PHILADELPHIA — WALK 3: THE BOULEVARD — SEGMENT 24: THE RODIN GATE — THE ONE THE BOULEVARD MADE
Walk 3 · segment 24 · register AMERICAN
Vantage: the boulevard sidewalk at the Rodin Museum's gateway, looking through it to the garden and the pavilion
---
Look back the way you came before you look through the gate. Nearly everything on this boulevard is older than the boulevard. The library's collection, the natural history academy a block off the circle, the science institute, the Barnes you just passed: each was founded somewhere else, in a rented room or a house or a suburb, decades or a century before this street existed, and each moved here when the street gave it an address. The boulevard is a shelf. The city built it to hold things it had already founded.

*[beat]*

This gate is the exception. Nothing was founded here first and carried over; there was no institution waiting for a better address. A Philadelphian who had made his money in movie houses began buying the sculptor's work in the twenties and commissioned a building and a garden for it on this spot, so the two were conceived together, for this street, with nothing to move in from anywhere. The boulevard, which everywhere else received, here produced. It is the one building on the street that the street made rather than housed, the founding and the building arriving in the same moment and the same place, which in this city almost never happens.

The gateway you are standing at was modeled on the front of a small eighteenth-century château that Rodin had bought and had re-erected, stone by stone, at his house outside Paris. He liked old fronts. His seated figure waits beneath this one, elbow on knee, as it does in a dozen cities, and behind it the garden runs back in clipped hedges to a long pool and a pavilion the color of the museum on the hill. The garden is free and open in daylight; the building behind it has hours and a ticket, and the walk does not go in. Walk the length of the pool if you like; the figures along it are bronze and were meant for weather.

When you have had enough of the garden, go back to the sidewalk and keep the direction you had. Ahead of you the boulevard opens out into an oval with a fountain at its center and the museum behind it on its hill. Getting across the oval is the next thing to say, and it needs saying plainly.
