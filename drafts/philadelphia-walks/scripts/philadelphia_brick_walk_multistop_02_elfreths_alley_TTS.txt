philadelphia — walk 4: brick — segment 04: el freths alley
Walk 4 · segment 04 · register AMERICAN
Vantage: the mouth of the Alley at Second Street, looking east
---
The lot on the corner is being made into a small park, named for the woman who formed the Alley's association in nineteen thirty four and kept the street standing. If there is still a fence around it, that is what the fence is for.

Now look down the Alley. The founding plan had no street here. Around seventeen oh three two men who owned the lots on either side cut a cart path between them to reach the river, and then did what owners do with frontage, which is sell it, in pieces. The pieces are the houses. Thirty two of them, built from the seventeen twenties into the eighteen thirties, two stories and three, some the width of a single room, none quite the width of its neighbor. Follow the fronts with your eye and you can see the money change from one to the next: here a run of Flemish bond on a house whose owner could afford to sort his bricks, there plain common bond on one whose owner could not, and the cornice on one house a little deeper than the cornice beside it. The pavement is brick too, laid on edge, and the cartway is still only wide enough for the cart.

Every other street this old in an American city is a church or a palace or a public building, one owner building for everybody to look at. This one is thirty two owners, each building for themselves and nobody building for the street, and the street came out whole anyway. That is what the brick is for. A brick is the size of one hand so that one person can lay it; a house like these is the size one family can own; an alley is as many of those as fit between two streets. The unit of this city was never the block. It was the lot, and the lot was the width of a house, and the house was made of a material you could buy by the hundred and lay yourself.

This is the last thing the walk has to say about the small house owned, and it is only this: that the city which built a church with the kiln's best bricks built this with the kiln's plain run, by the same hands, in the same bond, and that both are still standing on the ground they were sold.

Follow the Alley to its far end at Front Street; halfway along, a passage on the left opens into a small court with three houses in it, the Alley's own alley. Come back the same way to Second. Head south to Arch, and round the corner to your right.
