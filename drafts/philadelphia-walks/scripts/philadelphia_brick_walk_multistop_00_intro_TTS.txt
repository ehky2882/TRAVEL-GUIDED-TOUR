philadelphia — walk 4: brick — segment 00: at the gate
Walk 4 · segment 00 · register AMERICAN
Vantage: Second Street sidewalk at the Christ Church churchyard gate, north of Market
---
Stand at the gate and look up. You will not see it. The steeple this church is known for is directly over your head, and from here it is a wedge of white against the sky, foreshortened to almost nothing, so that the one spot in the city from which it cannot be seen is the spot you are standing on. It was never meant to be looked at from here. It went up in seventeen fifty four to be seen from the river, by ships coming up the del a wair, and for a long time afterward it was the mark they steered by: a white spike over a low town of red. This walk is about the red.

It is called Brick, because every stop on it is made of the same stuff, and because the question it asks at each one is who the brick belonged to. The founding plan gave this city straight streets and square corners; it did not give it a material. What the first Philadelphians had was clay, a great deal of it, under their feet and along the riverbanks, and a habit brought from England of firing it and laying it in patterns. So the city built itself out of its own ground. This church did it with glazed headers and a tower. The alley two blocks north did it with whatever a carpenter could afford. The meeting house on Arch Street did it on principle. The printer's grave is walled with it. And at the far end, where the walk comes out at the river, you will be standing on the one surface in the route that is not brick, with a town that is at your back.

The route is about a mile and a half, all of it flat, and it doubles back on itself once: north up Second Street to the Alley, west along Arch to the burial ground at Fifth, south to Market and east again to the court behind it, then north to Race Street and under the highway to the pier. Six stops. Three of them you can walk into; three you take from the sidewalk, which on this walk is the better side anyway, because brick is a thing you see from outside.

For now, step back across Second Street until the tower has room to come into view, and let it. Then come back to the gate, because the first stop is what the tower stands on.
