PHILADELPHIA — WALK 4: BRICK — SEGMENT 20: RACE STREET PIER
Walk 4 · segment 20 · register AMERICAN
Vantage: Race Street under the highway, then the pier's end beneath the bridge
---
Race Street goes under the highway here, and the walk says so plainly: it is an underpass, a short one, with sidewalks on both sides, lit along its length, with arrows on the wall pointing one way to the city and the other to the river, and a screen overhead that shows the river's surface, turned into shifting light, before you have reached the water itself. Then the street comes out into daylight again at the riverside road, and across it is the pier.

The pier opened in 2011 on the piles of a municipal pier from the eighteen nineties, and it reaches out under the bridge, so that for its full length the bridge is over you, and at the end it is not. Go to the end. The river in front of you is the one this city worked from, and it still does, down toward the bend to your right, where the cranes at Penn's Landing are a park being built over the highway. Most of what moves on it is working traffic. The bridge above you opened in 1926 and has its own stop and its own story; here it is a roof.

When you are ready, look back the way you came, toward the city. Between you and it is the highway, and then the warehouses of Front Street, and then, behind those, the low town the walk has been in all this while. If the rooftops allow, the white steeple you stood under at the start is the one thing above the roofline that is not new. From here it is doing what it was built to do, and you are seeing it the way the river saw it: a white line on a red base.

Everything behind you today was made of the ground it stands on, dug up and fired and laid a hand's width at a time, by people who each owned the width they laid. The church owned its wall and glazed it. The Alley owned itself in thirty-two pieces. The upholsterer rented, and then everyone chipped in and bought. The meeting house laid its brick plain and the burial ground laid its brick around a stone, and the printer built five houses to let and one to live in, and only the five are left. That is the walk, and the pier is where it stops. The brick does not come out this far. The river was the one thing in this city nobody could own, and the walk ends at the edge of it, with its back to the brick.
