PHILADELPHIA — WALK 2: BROAD AND MARKET — SEGMENT 00: FRANKLIN SQUARE (INTRO)
Walk 2 · segment 00 · register AMERICAN
Vantage: The center of Franklin Square, beside the fountain, facing west
---
Stand near the fountain and face west. Everything this walk does, it does in that direction.

This is a walk about a disagreement. For more than three centuries this city has been arguing with itself about where its middle is, and the argument has been conducted mostly on one street, which runs a block south of here and is called Market. You will reach it, leave it, and come back to it twice. The walk is about a mile and a half, flat the whole way, and it ends in a garden square a good deal like this one, on the far side of the city's center.

Here is what makes the disagreement strange. The city had a middle before it had a city. In 1682, William Penn's surveyor drew the plan on paper — a grid between two rivers with five open squares on it, one in each quarter and one dead center — and the drawing was finished before most of the ground it described had been cleared. You are standing in the northeastern square. The walk passes through the center one, which is now under City Hall, and ends in the southwestern one. Three of the five, in order, heading west.

*[beat]*

So the middle was never in doubt on paper. The plan put it at the crossing of the two widest streets, and the city eventually built its hall there to settle the matter. What the plan could not do was make people live where it said. They settled by the river, a mile east of here, and spent nearly two centuries walking west toward the center they had been promised — and then, having reached it, they kept going. This walk follows them.

Leave the fountain and walk to the square's northeast corner, where a roadway rises over your head.
