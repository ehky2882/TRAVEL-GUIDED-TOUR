philadelphia — walk 4: brick — segment 05: christ church
Walk 4 · segment 05 · register AMERICAN
Vantage: the churchyard gate and the Second Street wall
---
The wall in front of you is the dressed version of everything you will see today. Look at it closely, because the rest of the walk is a step down from here, and the step is deliberate.

The bricks are laid in what masons call Flemish bond: a long side, then a short end, then a long side, alternating in every course, so that each short end sits over the center of the long one below it. The short ends are glazed. They were the bricks that sat nearest the fire in the kiln, and the heat fused their faces to a dark blue black, and the bricklayers set them end out, so that the wall checkers in the light. It is the most you can do with a brick without carving it. The church, built between seventeen twenty seven and seventeen forty four as the parish of the Church of England in a Quaker town, wanted that much done.

Then it wanted more, and in seventeen fifty four the tower and steeple went up at the Second Street end, paid for by lotteries because the parish could not raise the money any other way. The brick stops where the wood begins; above the belfry it is all timber and paint, which is why from the river it reads as a white line standing on a red base. The standalone stop for this church tells the story of the steeple. This walk is only interested in the base.

Walk the length of the Second Street wall and back. Notice how the pattern never breaks, not around the arched windows, not at the corners, where the bond turns and keeps going. A brick wall this long with no mistake in it was a statement of means. It said that this congregation could afford the kiln's best bricks and the hours it took to sort them.

The church is open on most days, and if the doors are open you may go in later, on your own time; the walk's business with this building is finished at the wall. Head north on Second. Two short blocks, past Arch, and on your right, between the corner lot and the next building up, a street opens that the city never planned.
