PHILADELPHIA — WALK 3: THE BOULEVARD — SEGMENT 00: LOGAN CIRCLE — WHERE THE GRID GIVES WAY
Walk 3 · segment 00 · register AMERICAN
Vantage: the south edge of Logan Circle, where the boulevard enters from the southeast, facing the fountain with the City Hall tower behind you
---
Every street you have walked in this city so far met the next one at a right angle. That stops here. Stand on the south edge of this circle, with the fountain in front of you and the tower of City Hall over your shoulder, and the ground you are standing on belongs to two different ideas about how a city should be laid out, and this is the one place where they touch.

The first idea is the one from 1682, the plan of straight streets crossing straight streets with five open squares set into it, and this was the northwestern square. For two centuries it sat in the grid like the others, square-cornered. The second idea arrived around 1900, when the city decided it wanted a boulevard: a wide diagonal running from the tower behind you to the hill ahead, lined with institutions, the kind of street this city had seen in pictures of other cities and never built for itself. A diagonal cannot be put through a grid without breaking something, and what it broke was this square. The boulevard came in from the southeast and went out to the northwest, and the square was rounded off into a circle so that traffic could get around what was left. The shape under your feet is the join: a circle where a square was, because a diagonal needed to pass.

This walk follows the diagonal from the hinge to its far end. It runs about one point four miles. It is flat until the oval in front of the museum, and then it drops, down the hill to the river by a stair or a drive, and along the bank to the boathouses. Allow an hour and a quarter.

What the boulevard was for is the question the walk keeps asking. The short answer, the one the city gave at the time, was culture, a street to hold the museums and libraries a serious city was supposed to own. The longer answer is at the bottom of the hill, where the street runs out, and it has to do with water. The walk gets there last.

Face the fountain and walk toward it, with the brownstone front of the cathedral on your right. The square, and what it was before anyone thought of a circle, comes next.
