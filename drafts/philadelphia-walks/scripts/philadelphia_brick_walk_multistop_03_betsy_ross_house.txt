PHILADELPHIA — WALK 4: BRICK — SEGMENT 14: BETSY ROSS HOUSE
Walk 4 · segment 14 · register AMERICAN
Vantage: the north sidewalk of Arch Street, opposite 239
---
Across the street, the small one. Two and a half stories of brick with a gable turned to the street, a house of about 1740, one room to each floor and a winding stair from the cellar to the roof. Houses this size were called bandboxes, for their size, and they were what a working family in this city rented when it could not yet buy. The house puts an upholsterer in it as a tenant from 1773 to 1785, and she, and worked on the ground floor and lived above it.

The claim about the flag is the house's. A grandson told it in public in 1870, when the people who could have confirmed it were gone, and the house has told it ever since, and this walk repeats it as the house's and adds nothing to either side. What the walk can say for itself is what the house is: a tradeswoman's rented shop, of the plainest brick on this street, in common bond, with the one luxury of a pent roof over the ground floor to keep the rain off the door. If you have come from the Alley, you have just seen a dozen of these. This is the one that got famous, and the fame has not changed the brick.

It did change the ownership. In the eighteen nineties a committee bought the house with a public subscription taken in dimes, so that the building passed from one family to, in a sense, everyone, and in the nineteen thirties it was rebuilt to look the way its restorers believed it had looked. The gap between the two numbers, 1740 and the nineteen thirties, is where most of what you are looking at was decided.

The courtyard beside it is open to anyone when the house is, and you do not need a ticket for it. It is brick-paved, and the upholsterer and her third husband have been buried there since 1975, when they were moved from a cemetery across the city. Go in if you like. Otherwise keep west on Arch, across Third. The block and a half ahead is the connective, and it holds more brick in one stretch than anything else on the walk.
