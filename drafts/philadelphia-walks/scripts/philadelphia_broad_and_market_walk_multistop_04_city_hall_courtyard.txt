PHILADELPHIA — WALK 2: BROAD AND MARKET — SEGMENT 06: CITY HALL, THE COURTYARD
Walk 2 · segment 06 · register AMERICAN
Vantage: Inside the City Hall courtyard, entered by the east portal, standing near the center
---
This courtyard is the center square: the fifth of the five, the one the plan reserved in 1682 for public buildings and the city took nearly two centuries to use. There is a compass set into the paving to mark the spot. If a stage or a market stall is standing on it when you arrive, take the center on faith, because the building around you is not subtle about where it is.

Four portals, one in each face, and you came in through the east one. The courtyard was designed as a passage as much as a room. The city's two widest streets meet here, and the building sits on their crossing like a knot, so that to cross the center of Philadelphia on foot you go through its government rather than around it. People do, all day; watch for a minute and you will see the courtyard used as a shortcut by nearly everyone who enters it. The tower itself has been closed to visitors for maintenance since early 2025, and this walk assumes it still is.

Step back toward the south portal and look up past the north face. On top of the tower is the bronze figure of the city's founder, and the brim of his hat is the height the whole skyline stayed beneath until 1987, by an understanding that was never a law — you will pass the building that broke it in about twenty minutes.

*[beat]*

Something else has to be said in this courtyard, once, because this is where the city governs itself. In 1985, decisions made in this building ended with the city's own forces destroying a house on Osage Avenue in West Philadelphia with people inside it, and the fire took the block around it; the city's formal apology came thirty-five years later, in 2020.

Cross the courtyard and leave by the west portal, into the park.
