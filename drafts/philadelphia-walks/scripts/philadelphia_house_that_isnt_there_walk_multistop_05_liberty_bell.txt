PHILADELPHIA — WALK 5: THE HOUSE THAT ISN'T THERE — SEGMENT 02: LIBERTY BELL
Walk 5 · segment 02 · register AMERICAN
Vantage: the north sidewalk of Chestnut Street at the south glass wall of the Liberty Bell Center
---
Come up to the glass. The bell is a few steps inside it, at the south end of a long low building, hung from its yoke with the State House at your back, so that from in there the window frames both. This is the only vantage on the walk where you are looking at a thing and the building it was made for at the same time, and they are on opposite sides of the street.

It was cast in London in 1752 for the State House tower, cracked on the first test ring, was melted down and recast twice by a local foundry, and hung in the steeple for the better part of a century ringing for whatever the assembly wanted rung. The name it has now was given to it in the eighteen thirties by people who wanted the country to end slavery and had read the verse cast on its shoulder as a promise. The crack as you see it was finished in 1846, rung for a holiday, and that was the last time it rang.

*[beat]*

So far that is the story the bell always gets, in one order or another, and the walk is not here to tell it again. It is here for what happened in 1976. That year, for the Bicentennial, the bell was carried out of the building it had hung in for two hundred and twenty years and set down in a pavilion across the street, and in 2003 it was moved again, into this one. That is the re-meaning the story leaves out. A tower bell is part of a building. A relic is a thing you move. The day the bell crossed Chestnut Street it stopped being the State House's and became its own, a thing with an address instead of a place, and the glass behind it was angled so that the building it had left would stay in view behind it, like a house in the background of a portrait of someone who no longer lives there.

You do not need to go in. If you want to, the door is at the far north end of the building, on Market Street, the line is for security and not for a ticket, and it moves. From the glass you can see it well enough.

Go up Sixth Street, north, keeping the glass building on your right. At the corner of Market, where the sidewalk opens out and there is no wall at all, that is the last stop.
