PHILADELPHIA — WALK 5: THE HOUSE THAT ISN'T THERE — SEGMENT 16: SECOND BANK OF THE UNITED STATES
Walk 5 · segment 16 · register AMERICAN
Vantage: the north sidewalk of Chestnut Street at the 400 block, facing the portico
---
Line up with the center of the portico before you read anything on it. Eight columns, no ornament, Pennsylvania marble gone the color of old paper, a pediment with nothing in it. The architect — the same man who later put the steeple back on the State House, and the walk will not name him twice — won a competition in 1818 with a design that asked for as plain a building as the stone would allow, and he got it. It opened in 1824 as the headquarters of the Second Bank of the United States, the country's central bank, and it held that job for twelve years.

Then the job ended. The bank's charter ran out in 1836 after Andrew Jackson refused to renew it, and the building did what this city's good buildings tend to do, which is find other work. It was a private bank briefly, then the federal Custom House for ninety years, then, for the Bicentennial, a gallery for the portraits the park owns, more than a hundred and fifty faces from the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, many of them painted by one Philadelphia family. The gallery is dark now. It has been closed since the spring of 2025 with no reopening date, and the door at the top of the stairs is locked.

That is not a loss for this walk, and it is worth saying why. A building between jobs is a building you can see. Nobody is going in or out; the columns have nothing to do but stand there, so you can take them for what they are, a very large, very calm claim, made in stone in the eighteen twenties, that the republic would keep its money somewhere that looked permanent. The money moved out inside twelve years. The claim has been standing on Chestnut Street for two hundred years, hired out to one tenant after another, and it has never once appeared to be for rent. On this route it is the building that proves the rule the walk is heading toward: what a government builds, it leaves, and what it leaves, somebody else has to decide what to do with.

Carry on west. The next building is the one with the steeple, and you will reach it in a block; stay on this side of the street so that the whole north face comes at you at once.
