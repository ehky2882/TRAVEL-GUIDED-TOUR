PHILADELPHIA — W1 — THE FIFTH SQUARE — 18 — HEAD HOUSE SQUARE — THE SHED END-ON
Walk 1 · segment 18 · register AMERICAN
Vantage: Pine Street at Second, looking south down the length of the Shambles toward Lombard
Spines: none in this segment · locked 4 · S5 is W4's (the shed is a neighborhood thing here, not a brick argument)
---
Seen from this end, the market is a roof on legs and nothing else. It runs a full block south down the middle of Second Street, open on both sides, brick piers and a pitched roof, with the Head House, the little firehouse with the cupola, standing at the far end like a cap on a bottle. The standalone stop gives the dates and the people who paid for it. From here the thing to see is what it does to the street.

A city market in the eighteenth century was not a building you went into but a street you went down, and this is one of the few places in the country where that arrangement still stands in the open. The stalls were under the roof, the carts stood in the roadway on either side, and the houses facing the square sold from their ground floors, so that the whole block worked as one room with the sky for a ceiling. Most sheds like it in the city came down in the nineteenth century. This one was left, partly because it stood at the edge of things, and partly because by the time anyone thought to remove it the neighborhood around it was being saved instead.

Walk the length of it, under the roof, to the Head House at the Lombard end. Then come back up the east sidewalk to Pine and continue east, two blocks, to Front Street, where the grid ends and the ground drops away.
