philadelphia — walk 5: the house that isn't there — segment 01: independence hall
Walk 5 · segment 01 · register AMERICAN
Vantage: the north sidewalk of Chestnut Street between Fifth and Sixth, facing the north front
---
Pull up across the street from it, on the north side of chess nut, and look at the door. That is the door. The State House of the Province of Pennsylvania was begun in seventeen thirty two and finished, more or less, in seventeen fifty three, by a lawyer who drew it and a master builder who raised it; the walk calls them the builders and leaves it there. The assembly of the colony sat in the room to the left of the hall as you face it, and when a continental congress needed somewhere to sit in seventeen seventy five, the assembly moved upstairs and let them have the room. The men who went in through that door in the summer of seventeen seventy six came out having declared a country. Many of them enslaved people; that is stated here once, plainly, and the walk returns to it at its last stop.

Behind the building is a square, and on the eighth of July, four days after the vote, the Declaration was read aloud to the city there, from a platform in the yard, not from this door. The two faces have kept those jobs. The street side is where the work went in; the square is where the result came out. If you go in, mornings before half past ten you walk in and look around on your own; after that it is a guided tour, free, with a ticket reserved online for a dollar, you will enter from the square side, through security on Fifth Street, which is to say from the side the news came out of.

Eleven years after the Declaration, the same room, the same tables, and a different set of men wrote the Constitution, and the two documents share a room in a way they share almost nothing else. One is a departure. The other is a set of rules for staying. The building did not change between them. The colony's assembly still owned it, still met upstairs when the room was wanted, and still, in seventeen eighty seven, had to be asked.

Face west. The small brick building at the far end of the row, at the corner of Sixth, is where the government went when it had a country to run and nowhere of its own to run it from. Head for the corner; it is one short block.
