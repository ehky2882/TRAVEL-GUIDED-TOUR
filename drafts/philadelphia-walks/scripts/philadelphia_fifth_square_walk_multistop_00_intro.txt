PHILADELPHIA — W1 — THE FIFTH SQUARE — 00 — INTRO AT WASHINGTON SQUARE
Walk 1 · segment 00 · register AMERICAN
Vantage: Washington Square, the Walnut Street edge, facing east
Spines: none (docket 1 — the Lenape statement lives here and nowhere else)
---
Face east. Six blocks that way is a river you cannot see from here, and this walk ends standing beside it. Between here and there the city runs out: the grid of streets laid down in 1682 reaches Front Street and stops, and past Front Street there is a highway in a trench and then the Delaware. The walk is about a mile, flat, and it takes the long way around, south through some of the oldest residential blocks in the city and then east, because the straight way is not the interesting one.

One thing has to be said first, and this is the place to say it. Before there were squares or streets, this was Lenapehoking, the country of the Lenape, who fished this river and farmed its banks for longer than anyone has counted. The founder bought his land from them in a series of deeds, and his sons took a great deal more in 1737 with a document the Lenape have called a fraud ever since. There are Lenape nations in Oklahoma, Wisconsin, and Ontario, and the Lenape Nation of Pennsylvania is here. That is the ground under every stop on this walk. It will not be repeated; it belongs at the beginning.

The square you are standing in is one of five laid out at the founding, the southeastern one, and it has a standalone stop of its own that tells what lies under the grass. This walk takes it differently. Cross to the Walnut Street side and look at the buildings.
