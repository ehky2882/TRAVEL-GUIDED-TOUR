philadelphia, walk 3: the boulevard, segment 23: boat house row, the held water
Walk 3 · segment 23 · register AMERICAN
Vantage: Kelly Drive, on the river side, at the downstream end of Boathouse Row
---
Up close the row stops being a row. It is a short street of boathouses, ten of them, no two alike, each belonging to a club that stores its shells on racks inside and launches them down ramps to the water. The earliest date from the eighteen sixties. They are brick and stone and shingle, with turrets and porches and whatever ornament was fashionable the decade each was built, and from this side you are seeing them the way their members do, from the door, not the way the city does, lit and reflected from the far bank at night. The lights are on the river faces. They went up in nineteen seventy nine for a papal visit and were replaced in twenty twenty four with a system that can be any color the occasion wants.

The clubs have raced each other on this stretch since the eighteen fifties, and the governing body they set up to do it is still in charge of the course. The course is possible for one reason. Without the dam you stood above a few minutes ago, the river here would be tidal, a stretch of mud and current twice a day, and no one would keep a boat on it. The city slowed the river so that it could pump it. The rowers inherited the slowness, the painters and photographers inherited it after them, and the boulevard on the hill was the last inheritor, a street built to deliver people to the edge of a stillness that a water engineer made by accident.

Watch a boat go out, if one does. Eight oars, one voice, the shell sitting on water so flat it hardly registers the hull. Then look upstream at how far the flatness goes, around the bend and out of sight, all of it the same pool, all of it held by the stone you stood above an hour ago.

The walk ends here. Behind you, the drive climbs back to the museum and the boulevard, and the circle you started at is a mile and a half back the way you came, or a short ride. Before you go, look once more at the water in front of the ramps. The city held it so that it could be looked at, and the held water, under the boats, keeps leaving over the dam, a sheet at a time.
