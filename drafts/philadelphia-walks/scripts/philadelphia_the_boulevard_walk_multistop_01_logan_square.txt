PHILADELPHIA — WALK 3: THE BOULEVARD — SEGMENT 11: LOGAN SQUARE — THE SQUARE BEFORE THE CIRCLE
Walk 3 · segment 11 · register AMERICAN
Vantage: the rim of the Swann Fountain, facing east toward the cathedral
---
Before it had a fountain, or a name, or a circle cut into it, this was Northwest Square, and the city used it for what it did not want near its houses. The poor and the unclaimed were buried here. Public hangings were carried out here, the last of them in the eighteen twenties. In 1825, the city gave the ground a name, after a colonial official who had been secretary to the founder, fenced it and planted it, and let the grass grow over what was underneath. The turn from gallows ground to garden square took about a generation, which is roughly how long it takes a city to forget on purpose.

The fountain arrived a century later, in 1924: three reclining bronze figures, each standing for a river, with water thrown up between them when the season allows. The city shuts it off in the cold months; if you have come to a dry basin, the figures are easier to see, which is a fair trade.

Across the circle to the east is the cathedral, brownstone, with four columns across its front and a dome behind, built between 1846 and 1864 as the seat of the archdiocese. The walk looks at it from here and goes no closer. The windows begin high in the walls and the front is more fortress than invitation; the effect is a building that keeps to itself while the traffic goes around it.

To the north, two buildings face the circle with the same front, a library and what was built as a courthouse, finished fourteen years apart to a single design. The men who laid out the boulevard wanted the circle framed, and a frame needs two matching sides, so the second building was made to agree with the first across a gap of years. That is the boulevard's habit, and you will see it again at the hill: not one building but a pair, or a set, arranged so the street reads as a whole.

Leave the circle past the library, on the right-hand sidewalk of the boulevard as you face the hill. In two long blocks you will pass a low stone museum set back behind a pool of water, the Barnes Foundation, founded in a suburb in 1922 and moved here in 2012. Beyond it, at the next corner, is a gate with a small garden behind it. Stop at the gate.
