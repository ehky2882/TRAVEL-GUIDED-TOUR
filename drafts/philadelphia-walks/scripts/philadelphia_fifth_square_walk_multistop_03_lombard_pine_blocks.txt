PHILADELPHIA — W1 — THE FIFTH SQUARE — C1 — CONNECTIVE — THE LOMBARD AND PINE BLOCKS
Walk 1 · segment C1 · register AMERICAN
Vantage: walking east on Lombard, then north one block to Pine at Fourth, then east on Pine toward Second
Spines: none · locked 4 (residents never) · Brumbaugh link, once
---
These four blocks are the part of the city most often photographed as its oldest, and much of what you are passing is not old. Society Hill was a working neighborhood in decline by the nineteen fifties, and the redevelopment authority bought it block by block, tore out what it judged beyond saving, and sold the eighteenth-century houses to buyers who agreed to restore them, with three concrete towers by the architect I. M. Pei set down at the river end to pay for it. The brick you see is a mixture of what survived and what was rebuilt to look as if it had, and the restoration architect who did much of the careful work here, G. Edwin Brumbaugh, is the same one who restored the market sheds you are walking toward. Turn north at Fourth, then east on Pine. The street narrows and the sidewalks turn to brick. Two blocks ahead, where Pine meets Second, the roof of the Shambles closes off the street.
