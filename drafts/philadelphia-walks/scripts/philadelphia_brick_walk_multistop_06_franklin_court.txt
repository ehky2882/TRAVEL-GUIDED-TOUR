PHILADELPHIA — WALK 4: BRICK — SEGMENT 10: FRANKLIN COURT
Walk 4 · segment 10 · register AMERICAN
Vantage: inside the court, beside the steel frames
---
The five houses you have just come through were rentals. He built them in 1786 and 1787, on the Market Street frontage he owned, to be let out for income, and he ran the passage through the center of them so that he could reach his own house from the street. The passage is brick. The court is brick. The pavement you are standing on is the pattern the walk has been laid in.

His house stood where the steel stands. It was built from 1763 to 1765 while he was mostly abroad, to his instructions by letter, and he lived in it in his last years, and in 1812 his heirs had it pulled down for the value of the lot. What is here instead went up in 1976: a frame of painted steel, raised by the architects to the outline of the house, to the height of its roof and the width of its walls, with nothing filled in. The brick of the court runs right up to it and under it. You can stand inside the shape of the house and still be outdoors.

Benjamin Franklin printed here, and from this city he started the lending library, the fire company, the hospital, and the academy that became the university, and he also started the learned society that still meets a few blocks south. Every one of them is still running. He is at the burial ground you have just come from. The small house owned was one of the two things this city made. The other is the founded thing.

A founded thing is a thing built to be run by other people. That is its whole design. He started the library and left it to its members, the fire company to its firemen, the hospital to its managers, and each of them was alive the day he died because it had never depended on him being there. A house is the opposite. A house is the one thing a person builds to come back to. He came back to this one, and it was the one thing he built in this city that could not be run without him, and it is the one thing that did not last.

*[beat]*

The steel is the shape of the only thing he kept for himself. Everything he gave away is still in use.

Step back out through the passage to Market Street and head east, to Second. Then north, two blocks, to Race, and turn toward the river. The highway is in front of you. The last segment takes you under it.
