philadelphia — walk 4: brick — segment 28: the meeting house and the burial ground
Walk 4 · segment 28 · register AMERICAN
Vantage: the south sidewalk of Arch Street from Fourth to the Fifth Street corner
---
The meeting house is first. It stands back from the street behind a low brick wall on ground the city's founder set aside in seventeen oh one for a Quaker burial ground, and the building went up on top of the graves in eighteen oh four, in brick, with a center block and two wings and nothing on any of them that is not structural. The lintels are plain. The cornice is a few courses of brick stepped out and stopped. Where the church you began at glazed its headers to make the wall shine, this wall was laid so that it would not. The standalone stop for this place makes the case for the plain wall; the walk needs only the fact of it. The meeting house is a working one, with meetings on their own days. The walk looks at it from here and goes no closer.

Now go on to the corner at Fifth. The burial ground belongs to the church where the walk started, and has since seventeen nineteen, and it is enclosed in a brick wall. Most of what is in it you cannot see from the sidewalk. One grave you can. At the corner, the wall has an opening cut in it with an iron grille, made in eighteen fifty eight because people kept asking to look, and through it, a few feet in, is a flat marble slab laid level with the grass. The printer is under it, with his wife. His name and hers and the year are cut on the stone, and that is all the stone says; he wrote the inscription himself and kept it to that.

There will be coins on it. People have been leaving them for a long time, and the churchyard gathers them up and puts them toward its upkeep.

Of all the brick on this walk, this is the one place where the material is around the thing rather than the thing itself. The wall was built to keep the ground; the opening was cut so the ground could be looked at. A city that made itself out of brick left the printer under a slab of something else, and then built the brick around him and left a gap at eye level.

Head south on Fifth, one block, to Market. Turn east. Halfway along the next block but one, on the south side of Market, five brick houses stand in a row with an archway through the center of them. Duck through it. The court behind is the next stop.
