Start with a sound you can't hear from out here. A full choir, in the room behind this wall, performing a play that people crossed state lines to see.

You're on Auburn Avenue in front of Big Bethel A.M.E., a Romanesque church in rough-cut stone — arched openings, a corner tower that rises well above anything near it. This is an active congregation, so this is an exterior stop; the sidewalk is the vantage.

Find the steeple.

Two words up there, in light: Jesus Saves. They read from a long way off and they have been part of this skyline for generations. Now the timing.

The congregation goes back to 1847, when enslaved men and women were permitted to worship at a church called Union Church — which makes this, by most accounts, the oldest Black congregation in Atlanta. They took their own name in the eighteen fifties, joined the AME denomination after emancipation, and built. An earlier building of theirs housed a school for Black children and, in 1881, the beginnings of Morris Brown College.

Then, in 1923, the church burned.

What went up afterward is what you're looking at — designed by the architect John Anderson Lankford and built by Alexander Hamilton, both of them Black professionals working in a segregated city. And on top of it, facing outward, in light, a two-word statement of belief. Much of the money was raised by the congregation itself: members wrote a religious folk drama called Heaven Bound, sold tickets to it, and kept performing it year after year, for decades, in and out of the Depression. The room behind that stone has since held Presidents, and Nelson Mandela, and Mary McLeod Bethune, and it is still a working sanctuary on a Sunday morning.

[beat]

Think about what that takes. Your building is gone. The money is your own congregation's money. You could rebuild quietly and low and nobody on earth would blame you. Instead you put up a tower, and on the tower you put two words in light, aimed at the entire city. That isn't decoration. It's a position, taken at the worst possible moment, in the most visible way available.
