This bridge gets photographed constantly, and almost none of the reason is the bridge.

Get yourself somewhere near the middle of the span, on the sidewalk, facing southwest. Downtown is directly ahead of you — the whole cluster of it, stacked and unobstructed, close enough to pick out individual towers. Under your feet, the interstate, running in a trench wide enough that it takes a moment to look across.

The view first, then what's holding it up.

The view is genuinely good, and it's good for one specific reason: nothing is in the way. Most cities can't hand you a clean frontal sightline to their own skyline from half a mile out, because most cities have several hundred buildings in between. Atlanta has a ditch.

The ditch is the Downtown Connector, where two interstates run through the middle of town as a single road, and it was cut through occupied blocks in the nineteen fifties and sixties. The gap it opened is the gap you're photographing. This bridge is simply the place where a sidewalk happens to cross that gap at the right angle.

The rest of the fame arrived later, and it arrived through a camera. The pilot of a zombie series shot its empty-Atlanta sequence up here in twenty ten, and the shot has been re-staged by strangers ever since — most of them, understandably, with no particular interest in what's underneath them.

So this is a viewing platform that nobody designed as one, aimed at a skyline that is only this visible because a neighborhood isn't.

The city has finally conceded what the bridge is actually for. There's work underway to widen the walking area, add a protected bike lane, put in planters and some seating — around six hundred thousand dollars, timed to land before the World Cup summer. If it's done by the time you get here, what you'll mostly notice is that you're no longer standing in traffic to take the picture.

Either way, give it a few minutes. The light gets better the later you leave it, and the trucks below never stop, and after a while you start hearing the view instead of just looking at it.
