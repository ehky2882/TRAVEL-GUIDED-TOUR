Clear Creek runs under this neighborhood in a pipe, and has for well over a century, and when enough rain falls the pipe stops behaving like one and the ward floods. The city's answer, drawn up in the early nineties, was a storage tunnel — dig a hole nobody would ever see and pipe the overflow into it. Roughly forty million dollars.

What got built instead is what you're sitting in. Same job: the pond takes on up to four million gallons and lets it out slowly. Around twenty-three million dollars, which left something like seventeen million on the table and produced seventeen acres of park. The first phase opened in twenty eleven.

That's the whole argument of this place, and it isn't a sentimental one: the nicer answer was also the cheaper one. Cities are rarely handed that choice, and this one nearly didn't take it.

One more thing about this ground. In nineteen seventeen a fire ran north through here and burned a large piece of the ward out, and the city rebuilt without ever making a story of it. Atlanta has a myth about the other fire; it never built one out of this. What it eventually put here, a century on, was a hole dug on purpose to hold water.
