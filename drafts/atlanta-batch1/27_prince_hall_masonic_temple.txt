A beauty shop on the first floor, a radio station above it, and above that an office with no window in it — same building, same decade, one address.

You're looking at the Prince Hall Masonic Temple — a tall brick block on Auburn Avenue, stone trim, a Masonic emblem cut above the entrance. It went up between 1937 and 1940, built by Georgia's Black Masons under the leadership of John Wesley Dobbs, whose name is on the street one block north.

Count the floors.

The beauty shop was a Madam C.J. Walker salon — the national brand, franchised, Black women's money moving through Black women's businesses. Above it was WERD, which at the end of the nineteen forties became the first radio station in the country owned by an African American, after a professor and bank president named Jesse Blayton bought it and replaced the entire staff. And higher up, from 1957, the Southern Christian Leadership Conference kept its headquarters, with Martin Luther King Jr. working out of a small interior office for years.

So: cosmetics, broadcasting, a fraternal order, and a civil rights organization, stacked vertically, paying rent to one another. That isn't a charming coincidence. A movement needs a landlord who will rent to it, a printer who will print for it, and a station that will put it on the air — and on this street all three existed, and two of them were in this building.

It nearly didn't survive. The temple sat empty and deteriorating for years; one preservation group called it among the most significant unprotected buildings in the region. Restoration finished in early 2026, and on the eleventh of February, 2026, it was formally brought into the Martin Luther King Jr. National Historical Park. Public programming began that spring. It is the newest piece of the park and the schedule is still settling, so check before you count on getting inside.

Look up at the top floors once more. Whatever you picture when you picture the movement — the marches, the cameras, the pulpit — a great deal of the actual work was mimeographs, phone bills, and a windowless room above a hair salon.
