Five hundred and one Auburn Avenue is a two-story wooden house on a street of two-story wooden houses. That is the most important thing about it.

You should be on the sidewalk on the north side of Auburn, east of Ebenezer and the King Center — the street numbers climb as you walk away from downtown. Look for the yellow house with the porch that runs across the front and turns the corner. Queen Anne, built in 1895, low iron fence out front. The rooms inside are ranger-led only, free, first come first served, fifteen people at a time, and they go fast; tickets come from the temporary visitor center up at Fire Station Number Six. If you didn't get one, you haven't missed the stop. The block is the stop.

Start with the porch.

A white family built this house. In 1909 a Baptist preacher named Adam Daniel Williams bought it, and his daughter and her husband moved in with him, and on the fifteenth of January, 1929, at noon, their second child — Martin Luther King Jr. — was born upstairs in the front bedroom. He lived here until he was twelve.

Now widen out. Look left, look right. Nearly every house on this block is about the same size, about the same age, built for about the same kind of household — a teacher, a mail carrier, a pharmacist, a preacher. When the federal government moved to preserve this place in 1980, it did not buy the house and put a fence around it. It took the whole block.

That decision is the argument. A single preserved house tells you a great man came from somewhere. A preserved street tells you what that somewhere actually was: a working Black middle class with its own mortgages, its own front porches, and its own church a couple of minutes down the sidewalk.

[beat]

The porches here are all doing the same thing, which is facing each other. That's not really architecture, it's a way of living on a street: you sat out front in the evening, and the street sat out front with you, and everybody's business was conducted about fifteen feet from everybody else's. The government could have bought one house and put a shrine in it. It bought the neighbors.
