ATLANTA — STOP 28
GRAVES HALL, MOREHOUSE COLLEGE
Clean version

One building on this hill had to be the dormitory, the library, the laboratory, the classrooms, the kitchen, the dining room and the chapel, all at once — because for a while it was the only building the college had.

You want to be on Westview Drive, at the front edge of the Morehouse campus, looking up the slope. Find the oldest building on the rise: dark red brick, a squared tower at one corner, terra cotta trim around the windows. That's Graves Hall. It went up in 1889.

Don't start with the tower.

The college was founded in 1867 in Augusta, to train preachers and teachers. It moved to Atlanta in 1879, and in 1885 it bought fourteen acres out here in the West End. Four years later it had this. The building carries the name of Samuel Graves, the president who raised the money for it.

The ground came with something on it: the ridge you're looking up at had been part of the line the Confederate army dug around the city in 1864. The college built on top of it.

Preachers and teachers sounds like a narrow ambition until you notice it was the whole of the available ground. Those were the two professions a Black man in Georgia could hold without a white employer's permission. Train a preacher and you have a man with a building, a budget and a congregation that answers to nobody downtown. Train a teacher and you have a room full of children every morning. In 1906 the college made John Hope its president, the first Black man to hold the job; from 1940 to 1967 Benjamin Mays ran the place and was explicit that its purpose was to produce leaders.

So the crowding in this building isn't a charming detail about a poor start. It's the shape of the arithmetic. When the institutions across town are closed to you, you don't get to build a campus one specialized building at a time — you build one building, make it do seven jobs, and train people for the two positions nobody can fire them out of.

*[beat]*

Graves Hall still houses students, a bit over a hundred of them, in an honors residence. Whoever has the room up in the tower is sleeping in the middle of the oldest thing on this campus, which is a respectable place to be getting your reading done.
