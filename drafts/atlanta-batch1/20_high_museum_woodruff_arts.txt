On the third of June, 1962, a chartered jet carrying a tour group home from a month of European museums crashed on takeoff at Orly. One hundred and six of the dead were Atlantans — the core of the city's art association, the people who ran it, funded it, and had spent decades trying to build Atlanta a museum worth the name. Thirty-three children lost both parents on the same afternoon.

You're on the Peachtree Street sidewalk in front of the Woodruff Arts Center, with the white porcelain-paneled museum to your north and the darker concrete of the theater and the symphony hall beside it.

What the city did next is why this campus exists. The Memorial Arts Center broke ground on the fourth anniversary, to the day, and opened in 1968 — one complex holding a museum, a symphony, a theater and a school, built as a memorial to the people who had died trying to get a museum built. The French government sent a casting of Rodin's The Shade as a gesture of sympathy. It stands out here on the grounds: a bronze figure bent forward, head turned down and away. Walk over to it.

The museum's name comes from further back still. In 1926 a woman named Harriet High handed the art association her family's house on Peachtree to use as a gallery, and the institution has carried her surname ever since — which is why Atlanta's art museum is named for a person almost nobody can tell you anything else about.

The building you actually see came later. Richard Meier's white museum opened in 1983, and Renzo Piano's addition in 2005 more than doubled it — which is why this complex reads as three or four different arguments about what a museum should look like, all standing next to each other and not agreeing.

Most American cities got their art museum from one wealthy family, or one industrialist's second thoughts, or a world's fair. Atlanta got this one because a plane went off a runway in France, and a city decided the response was to finish what those people had started. That's an unusual thing to build a cultural district out of, and it's why the word Memorial was in the original name.
