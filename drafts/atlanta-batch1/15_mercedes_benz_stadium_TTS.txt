The building that stood on this ground before this one lasted twenty-five years.

Stand on the plaza on the north side of Mercedes-Benz Stadium, Northside Drive behind you. Look for the open lawn along the side of the building — about thirteen acres of grass, with a walking path around the edge of it. That lawn is a footprint.

Two minutes, facing north.

The Georgia Dome opened in nineteen ninety-two, held two Super Bowls and part of an Olympic Games, and was brought down with explosives in November of twenty seventeen, three months after this stadium opened beside it. The park on its footprint opened the following year.

Before the Dome, this was Lightning. A Black working-class neighborhood, roughly between Vine City and downtown, and it came apart in stages rather than all at once: the Georgia World Congress Center began acquiring blocks here in nineteen seventy-three, and the Dome finished the job about twenty years later. There is a historical marker on this site for the Georgia Dome. It does not mention Lightning.

The last pieces to go were two churches. Friendship Baptist, founded in eighteen sixty-two and standing on Mitchell Street since the eighteen seventies, sold for just under twenty million dollars. Mount Vernon Baptist sold for something close to fifteen. Both buildings came down in twenty fourteen. Both congregations are still meeting, in newer buildings, a few miles from where you're standing.

Which is the shape of this whole site. The ground gets cleared for events, and each time, what goes up is larger, costs more and lasts less time than the thing it replaced. The neighborhood outlasted the dome that replaced it by decades. In twenty twenty-six this stadium held eight World Cup matches, thirty years after the games that rebuilt the far end of downtown — the same instinct, thirty years apart, at the two ends of the same downtown.

The lawn is the honest part of the site, if you want one. It's the only piece of this ground not holding anything up, and on a weekday afternoon it's mostly people eating lunch on grass that used to be a street with an address.
