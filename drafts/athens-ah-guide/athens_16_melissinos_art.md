# Melissinos Art – The Poet Sandal Maker

**Atlas · Athens · 16 · Shop** — single-stop tour  
**Address:** Tzireon 16, Makrygianni  
**Pin (WGS-84):** 37.968378, 23.730970  
**Length:** 352 words, about 2:21 spoken

---

The Beatles, Jackie Onassis and Maria Callas all came to the same Athens sandal maker. So did a great many poets, because he was one.

You should be on Tzireon Street in Makrygianni, the quiet streets just behind the Acropolis Museum, outside number sixteen. This is Melissinos Art, the Poet Sandal Maker. A word of warning if you've an old guidebook: the workshop used to be down in Monastiraki, and it has moved here.

The family has been making shoes since nineteen twenty. The legend of the place, though, is Stavros Melissinos, who took over the family workshop in the early nineteen fifties and had an idea nobody else had: to bring back the sandals of ancient Greece as something people would actually wear. It worked. His workshop became one of the sights of Athens, visited by The Beatles, Jackie Onassis, Sophia Loren, Rudolf Nureyev and Maria Callas.

Here's the gift detail. He was a real poet, too, and his verses covered the walls of the workshop. In nineteen fifty nine he published his Athenian Rubaiyat, with lines like this one, in his son's translation: I will build a myriad palaces in me, as I live from day to day. Stavros died in twenty twenty five, in his nineties.

Today the workshop is run by his son, Pantelis, who is a painter and a poet as well as a sandal maker. The sandals are made from natural, vegetable tanned leather, based on ancient designs and his own, and each pair is fitted to your foot here in the shop. There are no online sales, and they're sold nowhere else at all.

*[beat]*

What I find rather moving is that the business has never really been about shoes. It's about a family deciding that a craft, a poem and a pair of feet could all belong in the same small room. Three generations on, they still do.

From here, the Acropolis Museum is only a few minutes' walk. And if you've bought a pair, there's no better place to try them out than the old stone paths up Philopappou Hill, a short walk away.
