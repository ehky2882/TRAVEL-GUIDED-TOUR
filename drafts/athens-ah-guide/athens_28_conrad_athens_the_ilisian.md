# Conrad Athens The Ilisian (former Athens Hilton)

For sixty years everyone called this the Hilton. Look for the drawings carved into its walls before you look at the new name.

You should be on Vasilissis Sofias Avenue, across from a long, white slab of a hotel, gently curved, rising above the traffic with its rows of balconies. Until recently this was the Athens Hilton. Since April twenty twenty six, it has been Conrad Athens The Ilisian, named after the old Ilissos river and the Ilisia neighbourhood around it.

It opened in nineteen sixty three, designed by a team of Greek architects: Emmanouil Vourekas, Prokopis Vasileiadis, Antonis Georgiadis and Spyros Staikos. For a generation it was the most modern building in Athens, the place where the city first felt international: the bar, the pool, the jet set.

Now look for the broad walls without windows. Those great flat surfaces aren't blank. They're covered in huge, linear figures cut into Pentelic marble, the same marble as the Parthenon. They're by the painter Yannis Moralis, who was commissioned in nineteen fifty nine and carved around six hundred and twenty square metres of relief across the building. That's the gift detail: one of the largest works of Greek modern art, and it's on the outside of a hotel.

The recent restoration was led by Aeter Architects, with interiors by the New York studio AvroKO, and the hotel now shares the building with private residences.

*[beat]*

Here's the thing to take away. A building like this could easily have been pulled down for something taller. Instead, it's been kept, cleaned, and taken seriously as a piece of the city's history. Athens is learning to love its nineteen sixties. This is the proof.

Before you go, look along the avenue towards the National Gallery. Across from it stands The Runner, a striding figure built of stacked sheets of glass by the sculptor Costas Varotsos. Come back at dusk and you'll see why it's so loved.
