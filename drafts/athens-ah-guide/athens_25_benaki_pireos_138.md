# Benaki Museum – Pireos 138

**Atlas · Athens · 25 · Visit** — single-stop tour  
**Address:** Pireos 138  
**Pin (WGS-84):** 37.974474, 23.708603  
**Length:** 303 words, about 2:01 spoken

---

The Benaki is the grand old private museum of Athens. This is the part of it that moved into a factory.

You should be on Pireos Street, the long, straight road that once carried the industry of Athens down to the port of Piraeus, outside number one hundred and thirty eight. The building in front of you is plain and modern, with nothing of the mansion about it. That's the point.

The main Benaki Museum is a neoclassical house in Kolonaki, full of jewellery, costume and centuries of Greek history. In the early two thousands, the museum needed space for large exhibitions, and it found it out here, in a former industrial building of the nineteen sixties. The conversion kept the original plan of the building and added a floor on top, and the new Benaki opened here in two thousand and four.

Go inside and you'll notice the main idea straight away. The building turns its back on the busy street and opens inwards, around a big central courtyard. All the light and life of the place faces in, which, on a road as loud as Pireos, is rather sensible.

The exhibitions change, so check what's on before you come. These are the big temporary shows, the kind that would never fit in a nineteenth century house.

*[beat]*

What's worth noticing is the street itself. Pireos was the industrial spine of the city: factories, warehouses, the gasworks at Gazi. In the last few decades it's become a line of cultural buildings instead. The Benaki was one of the first to make that move.

From here, walk up Pireos towards the centre and you'll come to Gazi, where the brick chimneys of the old gasworks still stand. It's now a cultural centre called Technopolis, and on a summer evening the whole neighbourhood comes out.
