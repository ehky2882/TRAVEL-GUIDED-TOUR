# Brettos

**Atlas · Athens · 12 · Drink** — single-stop tour  
**Address:** Kydathineon 41, Plaka  
**Pin (WGS-84):** 37.971709, 23.730525  
**Length:** 297 words, about 1:59 spoken

---

Look past the bar to the back wall. Floor to ceiling, lit from behind, hundreds of coloured bottles. That's Brettos.

You should be on Kydathineon Street, the main pedestrian lane through Plaka, outside a narrow, old-fashioned bar with that glowing wall of bottles visible from the street. If you can see red, green, amber and blue glass all lit up like a church window, you're in the right place.

Brettos has been here since nineteen oh nine, when a man called Michail Brettos began distilling and selling his own ouzo on this street. It's generally described as the oldest distillery in Athens, and it still makes its own ouzo and brandy. The founder's old still, I'm told, is kept on show inside.

Have a look at that wall properly. Those bottles are the house liqueurs, around fifty flavours of them, from peppermint to watermelon, and lit from behind they turn the whole room into something like stained glass. People come just to photograph it, and you can hardly blame them.

You sit at old wooden barrels, and you choose from a long, slightly encyclopaedic list. In winter, try the rakomelo: hot raki with honey and cinnamon. Or tell the bartender whether you're a sweet person or a sour person, and let them decide the rest.

*[beat]*

What's worth knowing, standing here, is that before cocktail bars, this was how Athens drank. Little neighbourhood distilleries, making their own spirit and selling it by the glass to whoever walked in. Brettos is what's left of that world, and it's survived by simply carrying on.

From here, the lanes above Kydathineon climb towards the Acropolis rock. Follow them uphill for ten minutes and you'll reach Anafiotika, a tiny island village of whitewashed houses, hidden in the middle of the city.
