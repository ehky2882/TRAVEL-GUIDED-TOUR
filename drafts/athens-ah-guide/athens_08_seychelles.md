# Seychelles

**Atlas · Athens · 08 · Dine** — single-stop tour  
**Address:** Kerameikou 49, Avdi Square, Metaxourgeio  
**Pin (WGS-84):** 37.982258, 23.720289  
**Length:** 310 words, about 2:04 spoken

---

It used to be a café called the Bahamas. The new owners kept the furniture and simply changed islands.

You should be in Metaxourgeio, at the corner of Kerameikou Street and the little Avdi Square. The restaurant fills the ground floor of a building about a hundred years old, with big windows onto the street and, in good weather, tables spilling out onto the square.

Have a look through the window first. When Seychelles took over, they kept the bones of the old Bahamas café: the retro tiles, the worn fittings, even an old refrigerator. Nothing was ripped out to make it look new. The kitchen is in the same room as the diners, so you eat with the cooks working right beside you, and it's loud, cheerful, and very much a neighbourhood room.

The food changes with the season and often with the day. Reviewers mention chickpeas with feta and mint, crisp tomato and courgette fritters, and wide ribbons of pappardelle with kavourmas, a spiced preserved meat. It's Greek home cooking with a sharp eye, and it's not expensive. Do book, though. Athenians know about it.

*[beat]*

Here's the interesting bit. Metaxourgeio means silk mill, after a factory that worked here in the eighteen hundreds and closed in eighteen seventy five; the old mill building has since become the city's Municipal Art Gallery. For a long stretch of the last century this was one of the parts of Athens people stopped coming to. Places like Seychelles are a big part of why they've started coming back: no grand makeover, just an old corner, kept as it was, and fed well.

If you've got time before or after, walk south down Kerameikou Street towards the ancient cemetery of Kerameikos, ten minutes or so on foot. You'll pass from a corner café to the graves of classical Athens in a single street.
