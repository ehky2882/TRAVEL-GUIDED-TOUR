# Taf Coffee

**Atlas · Athens · 15 · Drink** — single-stop tour  
**Address:** Emmanouil Benaki 7–9, Exarchia  
**Pin (WGS-84):** 37.983609, 23.730948  
**Length:** 230 words, about 1:32 spoken

---

If you want to know where Athens' serious coffee scene started, this doorway is a good guess.

You should be on Emmanouil Benaki Street, on the edge of Exarchia, a few blocks up from the main shopping streets. Taf is at numbers seven to nine: a café that is also a coffee roaster, so it should smell right before you even reach the door.

Taf began as a family coffee roasting business back in the nineteen nineties. In two thousand and nine, Yannis Taloumis opened this café, and it became one of the places that taught Athens about speciality coffee: beans chosen and roasted in house, with care over where they came from and how they're brewed.

Have an espresso and ask about the house blend, called Rosebud. You can buy the beans to take home, too.

*[beat]*

And here's the thing about Athens and coffee. This is a city that already drank coffee constantly, sweet Greek coffee in little cups and the frothy iced frappé in summer. Persuading it to slow down and care about the bean was no small job. Places like Taf did it.

From here, walk five minutes north, up to Exarchia Square. On the corner there's an apartment block called the Blue Building, from the early nineteen thirties, one of the finest of its kind in Athens. It's worth seeing with a coffee still in hand.
