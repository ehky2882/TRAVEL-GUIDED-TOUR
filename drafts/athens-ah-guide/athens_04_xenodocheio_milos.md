# Xenodocheio Milos

**Atlas · Athens · 04 · Stay** — single-stop tour  
**Address:** Kolokotroni 3–5 (corner of Voulis), Syntagma  
**Pin (WGS-84):** 37.977135, 23.733426  
**Length:** 325 words, about 2:10 spoken

---

The name on the door simply means hotel. It's the Greek word for it, written out in full.

You should be on Kolokotroni Street, at the corner with Voulis, with the Old Parliament building across the road behind you. In front of you is a neoclassical building in two matching halves, with tall shuttered windows, stone details, and something newer and glassier sitting on the very top.

That top floor is the clue. This is two twin buildings, both listed, built side by side in the old style, and joined much later by a glass addition across the roof. They belong to the Army Share Fund, a military pension fund, which leased them out for twenty years. After a long restoration, the hotel opened at the start of twenty twenty two.

Now, Milos. If you know restaurants, you may know the name. Estiatorio Milos is the seafood restaurant group started by the Greek chef Costas Spiliadis, with branches in seven cities around the world. Their style is famously plain: very good fish, very little interference. This is the group's first hotel, and the restaurant on the ground floor is, in a sense, the founder coming home.

The forty or so rooms have the high ceilings and marble bathrooms you'd hope for in a building like this, and some of the terraces look straight up at Lycabettus.

*[beat]*

What I like is the honesty of the name. Xenodocheio: a house for strangers. And the idea they've hung it on is philoxenia, the very old Greek obligation to treat a stranger as a friend. You can argue about whether a five-star hotel quite manages that. But it's a good word to have over the door.

Before you go, turn round and cross to the Old Parliament. In front of it stands the bronze of Theodoros Kolokotronis on horseback, the hero of the War of Independence this very street is named after. Worth a minute of your time.
