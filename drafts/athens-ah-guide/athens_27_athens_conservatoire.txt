ATLAS · ATHENS · 27 · Athens Conservatoire
---

This building was meant to be the first piece of a modern acropolis for Athens. It's the only piece that ever got built, and it was never finished.

You should be at the corner of Rigillis Street and Vasileos Konstantinou Avenue, looking at a long, severe building of raw concrete stretched out along the avenue. This is the Athens Conservatoire, the city's old music school, in a building by Ioannis Despotopoulos.

Despotopoulos was born in Smyrna in nineteen oh three, and he walked out of his architecture course in Athens to go and study at the Bauhaus in Weimar, under Walter Gropius. In nineteen fifty nine he won the competition for something enormous: an Athens Cultural Centre, stretching from Vasilissis Sofias Avenue to here, and from Rigillis Street to the National Gallery. An opera house for eighteen hundred people, a theatre, a new Byzantine Museum, an extension to the National Gallery, a great public square.

Now, the gift detail. Of all that, only this building was ever begun. Construction started in the late nineteen sixties and stopped in nineteen seventy six, when the money ran out. The music school moved in anyway, into an unfinished building, and it has stayed there ever since, rehearsing and performing in a work in progress.

It's now a listed modern monument, and there have been plans, more than once, to finish it at last.

[beat]

What I find so affecting about it is this. Most cities show you their ambitions. Here, you can see the ambition and its interruption at the same time. It's a Bauhaus dream for Athens, paused mid sentence, and somehow all the more honest for it.

Before you go, look next door along Vasilissis Sofias. The Byzantine and Christian Museum stands there, one of the buildings Despotopoulos had hoped to replace. And a little further along the avenue is the former Hilton, the other great modern landmark of this stretch.
