# BOSTON · 18 — Quincy Market
T2 · single stop · B1b · clean · 28 Sept 2026
---

The ground under your feet was harbor water once, part of the town's own dock.

You should be at the east end of a long granite building with a columned portico, looking back along its length. A copper dome rises from the middle of the roof, and two long brick-and-granite rows, the North and South Markets, run parallel on either side. At the far end, past all of it, sits the smaller brick hall with the cupola, Faneuil Hall. Behind you, the harbor is about three blocks away.

If you picture the water coming back, start where you are. For well over a century, the Town Dock reached in from the harbor toward that far hall, with wharves pushing out around it. In the 1820s the city filled the dock, built over the wharves between it and Long Wharf, laid out new streets on top, and put this market down the middle.

The man behind it was the mayor, Josiah Quincy, who drove the project through in a couple of years, and the market opened on the twenty-sixth of August, 1826. It sold meat and produce for well over a century, and by the middle of the twentieth it had faded along with the rest of the waterfront.

And it came back. Restored, it reopened as a place for eating and strolling on the twenty-sixth of August, 1976, with Mayor Kevin White presiding, one hundred and fifty years to the day after the first opening. Its two hundredth anniversary fell on the twenty-sixth of August, 2026.

*[beat]*

That's the thing to carry down the length of this building. Boston grew by moving its edges into the water, all around the peninsula, but here you can pace it out. The dock gave the market its shape, long and narrow, reaching inland from the harbor, and then it disappeared underneath the thing it shaped.

As you walk toward that far hall, you're walking up the old dock toward the town, the way a ship's crew once would have, with the harbor falling away behind you and the market running on either side where the water used to be.
