# BOSTON · 21 — Charles Street and the Flat of the Hill

The ground under Charles Street used to be the top of the hill in front of you.

You should be on Charles Street at the foot of Mount Vernon Street, looking uphill. Mount Vernon climbs away from you, brick on both sides, toward the top of Beacon Hill. Charles Street runs left and right along the bottom, a flat line of shops and restaurants. The Charles River is a couple of blocks behind you, out of sight past the buildings at your back.

If you look up Mount Vernon Street toward the crest, you're looking at a hill that used to be taller. In the seventeen nineties the western slope was pasture, and it belonged to John Singleton kop lee, the portrait painter, who by then was living in London. His agent sold it to a group of investors who called themselves the Mount Vernon Proprietors. The painter decided he had sold too cheaply and tried to undo the deal from across the Atlantic. He failed, and the Proprietors got to work.

Their plan was to lower the hill so that houses could be built on gentler ground, and in seventeen ninety nine they started cutting. The earth had to go somewhere, and the obvious place was the river, which in those days came right up to the foot of the slope, about where you're standing. So the Proprietors built a railroad. Tracks ran from near the summit, close to where lewis burg Square sits now, down to the water's edge. Loaded cars rolled downhill on their own weight, and as they went, a rope pulled the empty ones back up. No engine, just gravity, and the hill going downhill one carload at a time.

Look down at your feet, then back up the slope. Charles Street isn't at the bottom of Beacon Hill so much as it's made of it. From eighteen oh three the street was laid out along the new edge, and the flat you're standing on is earth that used to sit up at the crest. The hill didn't just get shorter. It moved west and lay down.

The river kept its distance after that. Next time you walk down Mount Vernon Street toward Charles, feel the grade ease off near the bottom. That easing is where the hill ends and the old top of the hill begins.
