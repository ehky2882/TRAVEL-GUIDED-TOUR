# BOSTON · 24 — The Greenway at the North End
T2 · single stop · B3 · clean · 28 Sept 2026
---
Traffic is passing under you right now.

You should be at the North End Parks, under the long pergola, facing the entrance to Hanover Street across the lawn and the fountains. This is the Rose Kennedy Greenway, the ribbon of parks that runs from here south through downtown, and this stretch is its front porch: the pergola on one side, the neighborhood on the other, and a thin strip of green between them.

The green is the top of a tunnel. The interstate runs beneath the lawn, and the cars are down there in the dark. Nothing about the surface tells you. The fountains run, the swings under the pergola move, and all of it rests on a roof.

That roof is the reason for the story. In the nineteen fifties Boston built an elevated highway along this line, the Central Artery, a steel deck raised over the streets to carry traffic past the center of town. It took a strip of the North End's edge with it and then stood on that strip for half a century. From where you are, it would have been a wall of green-painted steel between the neighborhood and the rest of the city, with no easy way through.

*[beat]*

City Hall Plaza, across the street behind you, is Boston's first attempt at starting over, and this is its second, and the two attempts don't match. The plaza built in the open. Here the city didn't remove the mistake. It sank it. The highway went underground through the nineteen nineties and into the two thousands, the last of the elevated deck came down by 2004, and the parks on top opened in October 2008. The traffic is exactly where it was, one level lower. What changed is what's on top of it.

That's a different kind of fixing. The first correction made room by removing what was there. The second put a neighborhood's front yard back and left the problem running underneath, out of sight. Hanover Street now opens straight onto a lawn where for two generations it opened onto a highway pier.

Somewhere under the middle of the lawn is the centerline of the road below, and nothing on the grass marks it. That's the whole idea of the place.
