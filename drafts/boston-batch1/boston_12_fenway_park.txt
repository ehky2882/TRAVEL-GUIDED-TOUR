# BOSTON · 12 — Fenway Park
T1 · single stop · B-Outer · clean · 28 Sept 2026
---

The thing people come here for is on the other side of this wall, and from the street you can't see it.

You should be on the Jersey Street sidewalk, with the brick front of the ballpark beside you and the gates shut. Across the street the stands sell caps and sausages on game days and sit shuttered otherwise. What you're looking at is a plain brick face, arched windows, green steel behind. It looks like a warehouse that learned to sell tickets.

*[beat]*

The brick has been here since 1912. It opened on the twentieth of April, and the newspapers barely noticed, because the Titanic had gone down five days earlier. The architect, James McLaughlin, built a working building, not a monument. The park took its name from the neighborhood, and the neighborhood from the Fens, the wet ground the city had drained and planted a generation before.

Inside, the shape is the story. The lot was hemmed in by streets that don't meet square, so the field came out lopsided. Left field is short, and to keep cheap home runs off it the owners raised a wall thirty-seven feet tall. It carried advertisements for years, went green in 1947, and has been the Green Monster since. It runs along Lansdowne Street, behind the park, where you can walk its length and see nothing but its back.

*[beat]*

By the league's own count this is the oldest ballpark still in use in the majors, and it looks it, on purpose. The seats are narrow. The posts that hold up the roof block the view. The scoreboard in the wall is still changed by hand, from inside it. The owners who arrived in 2002 spent a decade repairing it, and the old argument about tearing it down was settled by not doing it.

Two things belong on the record. In 1959 the club became the last in the major leagues to put a Black player on the field. And there's a story fans tell, about a curse laid on the team when it sold Babe Ruth to New York in 1920, and the drought that followed. That's a legend. The record says the team won again in 2004, and the story has been in retirement since.

*[beat]*

The wall is the reason to come back with a ticket. From the street, Fenway keeps its famous thing on the inside, and shows you brick.
