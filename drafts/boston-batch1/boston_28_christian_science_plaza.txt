# BOSTON · 28 — Christian Science Plaza
T2 · single stop · B5b · clean · 28 Sept 2026
---

The ground you're on belongs to a church.

You should be at the Huntington Avenue edge of the plaza, where a long sheet of water begins, looking along it toward a great stone dome at the far end. The water is the reflecting pool, far longer than it is wide, with the dome and a row of columns lying upside down in it on a still day. To the left a plain concrete tower goes up twenty-eight stories. To the right, low concrete buildings close the space. Everything in view is one property.

*[beat]*

This is the world headquarters of the First Church of Christ, Scientist, founded in Boston in 1879, and the dome belongs to the building its members call the Mother Church. The first church went up here in 1894, a modest stone building with a steeple, tucked against the big one; it was outgrown almost at once, and the domed extension was finished in 1906, built to seat thousands. For sixty years the two churches faced ordinary city streets.

Then, in the 1960s, the church bought the blocks around it and hired the office of I. M. Pei, where an architect named Araldo Cossutta drew the plan you're looking at: the tower, the low buildings, the pool, and a plaza large enough that the churches stopped being buildings on a street and became the far end of a view. The work finished in 1972. The church owns all of it, pavement included, and keeps it open to anyone who wants to cross it, sit by the water, or bring children to the fountain that sprays up out of the paving elsewhere on the plaza.

*[beat]*

The pool was rebuilt in 2021. Its floor was made shallower, to hold less water and lose less of it, and the whole thing was cut back by sixteen feet at this end so that the plaza could have a proper entrance where you're standing. Underneath it is a parking garage, which is why it needed new waterproofing, and one reason a sheet of water could be laid flat across a city block at all.

In 2011 the city made the plaza a landmark, so the church can't change the outside without permission. It is a private space the public got attached to, and now the public has a say in it.
