# BOSTON · 06 — Paul Revere House
T1 · single stop · B3 · TTS-safe · 28 Sept 2026
---
This house looks older today than it did when Paul Revere lived in it.

You should be in North Square, facing a dark wooden house with small paned windows and an upper story that juts out a little over the ground floor. That's the Paul Revere House. The square is small and lopsided, more a widening of the streets than a plan.

Straight ahead of you is a timber frame put up around sixteen eighty, on a lot where the minister Increase math er's house had burned in the town's great fire four years before. A merchant built it. By the mid seventeen hundreds the front roofline had been raised for a third floor, and that was the house Revere bought in seventeen seventy, for his wife, five children and his mother. He kept it for thirty years.

After him the house came down in the world. It took in sailors as lodgers, then became a tenement, with shops cut into the ground floor: at one time or another a candy store, a cigar factory, an Italian bank. In nineteen oh two a great grandson of Revere's bought it to save it, and the restorers took the third floor off to bring back the house of sixteen eighty. So the building in front of you is the house Revere bought, returned to a moment before he ever saw it. Most of the frame is the real thing. The shape is a choice.

The night he's remembered for got the same treatment. On April eighteenth, seventeen seventy five, Revere left this house to carry a warning out of Boston, and the version most people learn has him galloping through the dark shouting that the British are coming. He almost certainly didn't. People here still thought of themselves as British. The troops in town were the Regulars, and a warning about the British would have told nobody anything. He wasn't alone, either. William Dawes rode out by another road with the same news.

What the record does offer is a Lexington sergeant's memory of Revere telling him the Regulars were coming out. It was also written down about fifty years after the fact. The legend is loud and the record is quieter, but even the record is somebody's recollection.

The house and the story have both been pared back to what people came here hoping to find. Underneath, the timber is old and the ride really happened. The polish is later.
