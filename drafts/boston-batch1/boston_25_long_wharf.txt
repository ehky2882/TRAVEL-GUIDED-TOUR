# BOSTON · 25 — Long Wharf
T2 · single stop · B3 · clean · 28 Sept 2026
---
The best explanation of where you're standing is at the far end of the street behind you.

You should be out at the end of Long Wharf, on or near the big compass rose set into the paving in granite and bronze, with the harbor on three sides. Turn your back to the water and look up State Street. It runs straight inland from the foot of the wharf, and at the top of it, small among the towers, is the building this wharf was built to reach.

That's the Old State House, the Town House of the seventeen hundreds. Long Wharf was laid out between 1710 and 1721 as the seaward extension of the street that led to it, then called King Street. A ship could tie up here and its cargo could go, in a straight line, from the hold to the warehouses to the heart of the town. That line is still there. You're at one end of it.

What happened next is the part the eye misreads. This looks like a pier that got cut short. It's the opposite. The water on either side of its inner half was filled, block by block, through the eighteen hundreds, the filled ground was built on, and the landward stretch stopped being a wharf and became the last blocks of State Street. The city didn't shorten the wharf. It walked out along the wharf and made the wharf into street.

*[beat]*

One morning in October 1768 the line ran in the other direction. Two regiments of British troops came ashore here, and the Fourteenth marched up King Street, drums going, to the Town House at the top of your sightline, to be quartered in a town that hadn't asked for them. The argument that began with that march ended, years later, in the street you're looking up.

The same line served the wharf's darker trade. Ships from here carried enslaved people, and the town's newspapers advertised their sale.

The compass under your feet came in the nineteen seventies, when the wharf was rebuilt as a place to walk rather than work, and the ferries still leave from beside it. Keep your eye on the old building up the street a moment longer. The last stretch of street between you and it was harbor once, and the wharf is what the harbor was filled out to meet.
