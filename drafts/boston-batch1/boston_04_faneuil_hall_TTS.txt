# BOSTON · 04 — Faneuil Hall
T1 · single stop · B1b · TTS-safe · 28 Sept 2026
---

A copper grasshopper has been keeping an eye on this square since seventeen forty two, from the top of the cupola, turning whichever way the wind tells it to.

You should be in Dock Square, the open space on the city side of a long brick hall with arched windows, three floors high, a small white cupola rising from the roof. That's fan yul Hall. The granite markets run on behind it toward the water, but this stop stays with the hall, and with the gold speck at the very top.

The grasshopper, because it's been here longest. It's hammered copper under gold leaf, with glass eyes, and it was modeled on the grasshopper that sat above the Royal Exchange in London, the sign of a merchant city. An earthquake knocked it down in seventeen fifty five, and its maker, Shem drown, put it back up with his son. Years later the son tucked a note inside its belly, written in the grasshopper's own voice, promising to do its job and admitting it would always vary with the wind.

Look down to the building under it. The ground floor was a market, and it still sells things; the big room upstairs was for meetings. That arrangement was the point, and it was controversial. Bostonians had fought for years over whether a town market should exist at all, and a crowd had torn the last one down. When a merchant offered to build this one as a gift, the town meeting accepted by three hundred sixty seven votes to three hundred sixty. The hall burned in seventeen sixty one and was rebuilt, then enlarged in eighteen oh six to twice its width, with a third floor on top.

The merchant who gave it, Peter fan yul, built his fortune in part on the slave trade.

This is where Boston did its shopping and its arguing under one roof, one floor apart. The protests against British taxes in the seventeen sixties and seventeen seventies were held upstairs, over the stalls, by a town that had argued just as hard about the stalls themselves. Boston never kept its commerce and its politics in separate buildings, and this hall is the proof in brick.

The grasshopper is still doing what the note promised, holding its post and swinging with every change in the air, which makes it a fair emblem for the room beneath it.
