# BOSTON · 29 — Chinatown Gate
T2 · single stop · B-Outer · clean · 28 Sept 2026
---

The gate in front of you marks an edge the neighborhood behind it never drew.

You should be on the Greenway side of Surface Road, looking west across the traffic at the gate, with Beach Street running behind it into Chinatown. Two marble lions sit at its feet; above them the roofs step up in tiers, green tile on red posts, lanterns under the beams. To your right is a narrow park with a stream cut through stone; to your left the roads bend toward the highway ramps. The gate faces the roads, not the neighborhood.

*[beat]*

The gate is painted steel over concrete, put up in 1982, a gift from the government of Taiwan. The neighborhood it announces is a century older, and nobody planned it.

Boston keeps setting a second city down beside the first; you've heard it on Beacon Hill and in the North End. The others grew, or were laid out. This one arrived. In 1870 a shoe factory in North Adams, at the far end of the state, brought in Chinese workers from California to break a strike. When the contracts ran out some came east to Boston, and others followed, driven from the West by mobs and by law. Shut out of nearly every trade but laundry, they opened laundries, and on Sundays laundrymen from across the region came to these few blocks for supplies and a day among people who knew them. In 1882 Congress barred nearly all Chinese immigration, and for sixty years most of the men already here could not send for wives or children. It held anyway, on low streets by the rail yards where the Irish had lived before them, then Syrians and Lebanese, ground nobody with a choice was fighting for.

*[beat]*

Then the roads came. In 1954 the Central Artery took the neighborhood's eastern edge. In 1963 the Turnpike extension took the southern one, and the east side of Hudson Street with it; owners learned by letter that the state now held their houses and would collect the rent. A medical center grew into what the roads left. When the Artery went underground, this park was built on the tunnel's roof, and the Hudson Street block came back as housing.

*[beat]*

So the gate stands on a border the highways cut. A city within the city, come from somewhere else, put its front door where the cutting stopped, and turned it to face the traffic.
