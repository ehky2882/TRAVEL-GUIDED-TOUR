# BOSTON · 26 — Commonwealth Avenue Mall
T2 · single stop · B5 · TTS-safe · 28 Sept 2026
---

This is somebody's idea of Paris.

You should be at the Arlington Street end of the Mall, just across from the Public Garden, looking west down the center of Commonwealth Avenue. A wide strip of lawn and path runs away from you between two rows of big trees, traffic on either side and brownstone houses beyond, block after block, until it fades at Massachusetts Avenue. Near you, on a granite pedestal, is a figure of Alexander Hamilton. He was the first statue here, in eighteen sixty five, when there was little behind him yet but bare lots.

The plan was drawn in eighteen fifty six by an architect named Arthur gill man, who had looked hard at the boulevards then being cut through Paris and wanted one. He got it. The avenue was laid out from eighteen fifty eight on, ahead of the houses, over what was still water on the older maps. The cross streets were named in alphabetical order, Arlington, Berkeley, klar en dun, Dartmouth, and so on to the letter H, which tells you how it was made. Nobody names a neighborhood alphabetically after the fact.

Beacon Hill, behind you and to the right, is the older Boston. It grew. Its streets bend because a hill was in the way, and its houses went up one at a time as families could afford them. This avenue is the opposite kind of city: ruled and lettered before anyone lived on it, a whole district ordered like a suit. And the families who moved in, block by block as each block was ready, were largely the same families from the hill, who wanted rooms the hill couldn't give them. The counter city here wasn't a rival crowd. It was the old crowd, in a bigger house.

There are nine statues and memorials between here and the far end. They were never in gill man's plan. He wanted an unbroken avenue of trees, and the statues arrived one donor at a time. Every one of them faces the way you came, east, toward the Garden and the old town. Every one but a Viking. layf erik sun, near the far end, looks west.

The trees were elms once, and they arched over the path like a roof. The elms mostly died in the last century, and the canopy now is a mix of whatever will grow. The lines are the same. gill man drew them on water, and they held.
