# BOSTON · 08 — Bunker Hill Monument
T1 · single stop · B4 · TTS-safe · 28 Sept 2026
---
The battle this monument remembers was lost.

You should be in Monument Square in Charlestown, on the grass at the foot of the obelisk, near the bronze of William Prescott with his sword out. The square is a ring of brick rowhouses around a green hill, and the hill is the point.

Rising in front of you is a plain granite shaft with no figures on it, put up to mark a June morning in seventeen seventy five. The night before, militia from the towns around Boston had dug a fort into the top of this rise. The British landed on the flats below and came up the slope, were twice thrown back, and took it on the third try when the defenders ran out of powder. Charlestown burned that afternoon, set alight by British fire from across the river. By dark the British held the hill and the Americans held the road back to Cambridge.

The name is the one thing to get straight. This is Breed's Hill. Bunker Hill is the higher ground behind it, where the militia had been told to dig, and the battle has carried the other hill's name since the week it was fought.

The monument took longer than the war. The cornerstone went in on the fiftieth anniversary, in eighteen twenty five, with Lafayette on hand, and the granite came from kwin zee. The money ran out with the shaft partway up, and it stood unfinished for years, until a fair run by the women of Boston in eighteen forty raised the balance. It was dedicated in eighteen forty three.

Then there's the line. Nearly everyone arrives knowing that somebody ordered the men not to fire until they could see the whites of the enemy's eyes. Nobody who wrote about the battle at the time mentions it. It first appears attached to this hill in print decades later, in accounts pieced together from old soldiers' memories, and some of those memories give it to Prescott, some to Putnam, and the phrase itself is a soldiers' saying older than the war. Boston's memory of seventeen seventy five keeps taking that shape. The line everyone knows shows up after most of the people who could have heard it are gone.

The monument was finished by a fair. The famous words were finished by memory. The battle was over by nightfall, and lost, and the country decided that was worth a column of granite.
