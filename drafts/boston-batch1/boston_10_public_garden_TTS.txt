# BOSTON · 10 — The Public Garden
T1 · single stop · B5 · TTS-safe · 28 Sept 2026
---

Everything on the far side of this garden is younger than the garden.

You should be at the middle of the small bridge over the lagoon, facing west, with the Arlington Street gate ahead and the Common behind. The bridge is iron over granite, from eighteen sixty seven, at the water's narrowest point. On either side the lagoon widens into a long bent shape with an island, and from spring to early fall the Swan Boats move across it slowly, a big white bird at the back of each one hiding the pedals.

Before any of this, the ground under you was salt marsh, sometimes called Round Marsh, at the western edge of the Common, with the open water of the Back Bay past it. People dug clams here and shot snipe. Then in eighteen twenty one a dam went across the bay along the line of Beacon Street, to turn the tides into mill power. The mills mostly didn't come. The stink did. Cut off from the tide, the marsh became mud that never dried, and three years later the city voted to keep it public and start raising it.

This was the first piece of the bay the town took for itself west of the Common, and it did it the way it did everything that century: the high ground came down and the low ground came up. The marsh went under carted earth. For years the result was a bare lot that smelled.

In eighteen thirty seven seventeen Bostonians petitioned to put a botanical garden on it, and the city leased them about twenty acres. The name came a year later, the winding water in eighteen fifty nine, and the eighteen sixties brought the bridge, the iron fence, the fountains and Washington on his horse at the Arlington gate. The Swan Boats came in eighteen seventy seven, built by a shipbuilder named Robert paj it, whose family still runs them. The family's story is that he got the idea from an opera in which the hero crosses a river in a boat drawn by a swan.

Then the ducks. Off the bridge to your right, toward Beacon Street, a mother duck in bronze leads eight ducklings along a strip of cobbles. They arrived in nineteen eighty seven, and people dress them: team jerseys, raincoats, whatever the week calls for.

The neighborhood behind Arlington Street came later, on the same principle, at many times the size. This garden was the rehearsal.
