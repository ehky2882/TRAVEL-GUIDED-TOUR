# BOSTON · 09 — USS Constitution and the Charlestown Navy Yard
T1 · single stop · B4 · clean · 28 Sept 2026
---
The ship outlived the shipyard.

You should be on Pier One in the Charlestown Navy Yard, with the black hull and the masts of USS Constitution alongside you, and the long granite basin of Dry Dock One a short way along the pier.

The ship has been tied up in this yard, on and off, since 1897. She was launched in October 1797 from Edmund Hartt's yard on the North End shore, across the water behind you, one of the Navy's first six frigates. In the War of 1812 she beat three British warships in single combat and came out of that war with the nickname she still carries, Old Ironsides. By the Navy's own count she is the oldest ship it keeps in commission. Sailors crew her, and she goes out into the harbor a few times a year under tow.

The yard around her opened in 1800, one of the Navy's first six. It built ships by the hundred, with its own ropewalk and forge and dry docks, and was busiest in the nineteen forties. In 1974 the Navy closed it. The ship stayed.

*[beat]*

Look along the pier at the dry dock. It was begun in 1827 and finished in 1833, a basin of Quincy granite that could be sealed off from the harbor and drained, and the first ship that ever went into it was this one. The engineer was Loammi Baldwin, a Charlestown man who had also had a hand in the obelisk on the hill behind you, raised out of the same stone. Every twenty years or so since, Constitution has gone back into that basin, and each time some of her is taken out and replaced: planks, copper, knees, masts. Most of what you're looking at has been renewed in this yard. The shape and the name are old. The wood is a running argument between the ship and the salt water, and the yard has been settling it since before it had a dry dock to settle it in.

A navy yard exists to keep ships going, and this one kept hundreds going and then closed. The one ship it can't finish keeping is the one that was already old when the yard was new. The dock still floods for her. The yard is a neighborhood now, and the job it was built for has one ship left and no end date.
