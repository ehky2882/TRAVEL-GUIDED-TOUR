# Library & Learning Center

A building that leans out over its own front door at a startling angle, and somehow doesn't fall over. That's Zaha Hadid.

You're on the campus of the WU, Vienna's University of Economics and Business, on the edge of the Prater park. In front of you is the campus's central building: a huge, sculpted block with black and white bands wrapped around it and a great slanting front that leans forward over the plaza. That's the Library and Learning Center.

It was designed by Zaha Hadid Architects and opened in October twenty thirteen, the centrepiece of a new campus of seven buildings for around twenty-four thousand students. Hadid took the most ordinary part of a university, the library, and gave it the most dramatic form on the site.

Now look at the front. The façade leans outward at around thirty-five degrees, and the upper part, where the main library sits, cantilevers over the square, giving the readers inside long views across the campus and the park. The surface is made of panels of fibre-reinforced concrete, cut with narrow, canyon-like slots of glass.

If you can, go in. The interior is a tall, canyon-like atrium, with ramps and walkways crossing through it, so you can see the whole life of the building at once: people climbing, reading, meeting.

And here's the thing worth knowing, standing underneath. Not long after it opened, a couple of the concrete cladding panels came loose and fell. Nobody was hurt, the panels were replaced, and the building carried on. It's a useful reminder that even the most futuristic architecture is still made of heavy things held up by fixings.

[beat]

Before you go, walk a full circle round the campus. Each of the other buildings is by a different practice from around the world, and they make a deliberately mismatched set. Then step out into the Prater's long chestnut avenue, the Hauptallee, a few minutes away.
