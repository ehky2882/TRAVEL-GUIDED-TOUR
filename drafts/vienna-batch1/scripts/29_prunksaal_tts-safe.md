# State Hall of the Austrian National Library

The first time you walk into this room, the usual reaction is to stop dead and look up. Allow yourself that.

You're on yo zefs plats, a quiet square inside the hofe boorg, the old imperial palace. In front of you is the long baroque front of the former court library, with the bronze equestrian statue of Emperor Joseph the Second in the middle of the square. The entrance to the State Hall is from the square itself.

The hall was built between seventeen twenty three and seventeen twenty six for Emperor Charles the Sixth. It was designed by yo hahn bairn hard fisher fon air lakh, the great architect of Viennese baroque, and when he died, his son, yo zef eh mah noo el, finished it.

Inside, the numbers do a lot of the work. It's almost eighty metres long and twenty metres high, lined with about two hundred thousand old books, with galleries running round above them. The court painter Daniel Gran covered the ceiling and the great central dome with frescoes, completed in seventeen thirty. In the dome the emperor is shown being raised to glory, surrounded by allegories of everything a ruler hoped to be known for.

Under the dome stands a marble statue of Charles the Sixth himself, dressed as Hercules, patron of the Muses. And around the oval centre of the hall is the library of Prince Eugene of Savoy, the military commander and collector, about fifteen thousand volumes in their original bindings. Near them are four great Venetian globes, each more than a metre across.

What's worth knowing is that this was never just a library. It was an argument about power, made with books. Knowledge, collected, ordered and housed by an emperor.
