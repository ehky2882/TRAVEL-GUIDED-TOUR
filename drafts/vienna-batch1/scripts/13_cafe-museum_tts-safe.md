# Caffay Museum

In April eighteen ninety nine, a young critic who hated ornament got his first big job. This is it.

You're at the corner of opern gasser and freed rikh shtrasser, a few minutes from the State Opera. Look across freed rikh shtrasser and you'll see the white cube and golden dome of the Secession. Behind you, on the corner, is a large caffay with big windows on two sides. That's Caffay Museum.

The building itself dates from eighteen seventy two. The interior was given to adolf lohs, who was then better known for his writing than his buildings. It was his first major commission, and he treated it as a manifesto. Smooth walls, brass strips, marble tables, bentwood chairs, mirrors, and bare light bulbs. No garlands, no gilding. A plain room for people who had better things to look at.

And here's the joke. lohs despised the Secession, the gold domed temple of ornament across the road. Yet the Secession artists adopted his caffay as their local. Klimt, sheela, ko kosh ka, Otto Wagner, karl krowss and robert moo zil all drank coffee here.

lohs's room didn't last. In nineteen thirty and thirty one, yo zef tsotty, a pupil of yo zef hoffman, remodelled it with cosy red booths. In two thousand and three it was rebuilt to look like lohs's original, a reconstruction that critics found neither authentic nor comfortable. Since it reopened in twenty ten, the look has gone back towards tsotty: red velvet benches and round globe lamps.

What's worth knowing is that this is a room the city has argued over for a hundred and twenty five years. Few caffays can say that.

When you leave, cross freed rikh shtrasser to the Secession and read the gold lettering over its door. Then look back at the caffay's plain corner, and you'll be standing exactly between the two sides of the argument.
