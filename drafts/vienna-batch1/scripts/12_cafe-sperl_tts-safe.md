# caffay shperl

If you want to know what a Viennese coffee house was like in eighteen eighty, this is still one of the best answers.

You're on goompen dorfer shtrasser, at the corner of lay har gasser, a few minutes' walk from the nash markt. In front of you is a broad corner caffay, and through the glass, with luck, the green of billiard tables. That's caffay shperl.

It opened in eighteen eighty as Caffay ron akher, and was bought five years later by the shperl family, whose name it has kept ever since. The interior was designed in the ring shtrasser style by vil helm yel ee nek and anton grohss, and it has survived remarkably intact: parquet floors, marble tables, bentwood toe net chairs, a crystal chandelier, and the carom billiard tables you can see from here. The last major renovation, in nineteen eighty three, was done carefully, and the whole room is now protected.

Think about who sat here. Officers from the military academy round the corner. The composer Franz lay har, whose name is on the side street. And between eighteen ninety five and the Second World War, a regular table of artists, the hah gen geh zell shaft, while yo zef hoffman and kolo man moser met here with friends in the years before they founded the Vienna Secession.

And here's the thing. Nobody comes to caffay shperl for novelty. They come because it doesn't change. A coffee here comes on a small silver tray with a glass of water, and nobody will rush you if you sit with it all afternoon.

When you leave, walk two minutes down towards the nash markt and look at the two buildings by Otto Wagner on the linker veen tsy leh, numbers thirty eight and forty. One has gilded medallions; the other is covered in painted majolica roses. They're the best argument going for crossing the road.
