Yppenplatz

If the Naschmarkt is Vienna's market for visitors, this is the one for the people who live here.

You're on Yppenplatz, in Ottakring, the sixteenth district, just outside the old line of the Gürtel. Around you is a square lined with cafés and restaurants, with market pavilions and stalls on one side. Running off it, south along Brunnengasse, is a long street of market stalls. That's the Brunnenmarkt, and the square is its upper end.

The market goes back to the late eighteenth century, when this was the village of Neulerchenfeld, outside the city walls. Food sold inside Vienna was taxed; out here it wasn't, so traders and customers came out to the villages. The square had its own market by the end of the nineteenth century, and over time the two grew together.

Today the Brunnenmarkt runs for nearly a kilometre and has around a hundred and seventy stalls. It's often described as one of the longest permanent street markets in Europe, and it's certainly the most international market in Vienna, with a large share of Turkish and Balkan traders among many others.

Now look round the square itself. Over the past twenty years, Yppenplatz has become one of the city's liveliest corners, with bakeries, cafés and restaurants spilling outside on fine days. The interesting bit is that the market never left. The new places have settled in around it rather than replacing it.

Saturday is the day to come. In the morning, farmers from around Vienna set up on Yppenplatz with honey, cheese, vegetables and wine, right beside the regular stalls.

Before you go, walk the whole length of the Brunnenmarkt, from here down to Thaliastraße, and back. Buy something you can't name, ask the stallholder how to cook it, and you'll have done the Brunnenmarkt properly.
