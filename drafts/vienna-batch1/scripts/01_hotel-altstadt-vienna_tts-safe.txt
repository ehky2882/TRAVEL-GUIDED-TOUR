A hotel is usually one person's idea, repeated sixty times. This one is sixty two ideas, and almost none of them agree.

Instead of fitting out sixty two identical rooms, he handed them to different hands. No two rooms here are alike, and they're not meant to be.

Then there's the art. Up the stairs and along the corridors you'll find Warhol, bri gitta koh vants, pra khen skee, atter zay, heln vine. Some of it is on loan from museums, some belongs to vee zen tahl himself. It isn't hung like a gallery. It's hung like a house where someone collects rather more than the walls can comfortably hold.

What I like, as an architect, is that the building doesn't try to be anything. The old house does the old house job, high ceilings and deep windows, and lets the rooms argue with each other inside it. It's a very Viennese idea: a calm facade, and a great deal going on behind it.
