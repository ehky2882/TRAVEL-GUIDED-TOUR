Church of St Charles Borromeo, Central Cemetery

In the middle of a city of the dead, there's a church with a sky-blue dome. Vienna's relationship with its cemeteries is famously affectionate, and this is its centrepiece.

You're in the Central Cemetery, the Zentralfriedhof, in Simmering, at the far south-east of Vienna. From the main gate, walk straight ahead up the long central avenue. At the end, raised up on a terrace with broad steps, is a great domed church in pale stone. That's the Church of St Charles Borromeo.

It was designed by the architect Max Hegele, a pupil of Otto Wagner, and built between nineteen hundred and eight and nineteen hundred and eleven. Hegele had his design worked out years earlier, by eighteen ninety-nine, but construction took almost a decade to begin. It's considered his masterpiece, and one of the finest Jugendstil churches in Vienna, alongside Wagner's own Kirche am Steinhof.

Locals often call it the Lueger church, because the crypt beneath it holds the tomb of Karl Lueger, mayor of Vienna at the turn of the twentieth century. He's a deeply controversial figure today, as an openly antisemitic politician, and that's part of what you're standing in front of too.

Now climb the steps and go in, if it's open. The interior is a complete work of art, painted and decorated by a team of artists, and the dome is painted a deep sky blue. Coming in from the grey of the cemetery, it's quite something.

The three flights of steps leading up to the church are often said to represent three paths to God: scripture, the contemplation of nature, and the contemplation of the good and beautiful.
