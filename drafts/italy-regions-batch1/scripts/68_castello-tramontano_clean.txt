Castello Tramontano, Matera

The man who built this castle was murdered before it was finished, and the street where it happened is still called the Street of Redemption.

You're on the Lapillo hill, above the old centre of Matera. Ahead of you is the castle: a big round keep in the middle, two lower round towers either side of it, all crenellated and pierced with slits for weapons, standing alone above the town.

It was begun in fifteen oh one by Count Giovan Carlo Tramontano, who had been given the county of Matera a few years earlier. He was deep in debt, and he paid for his building, and his debts, by taxing the people of Matera. The castle cost twenty-five thousand ducats, a fortune, and it was meant less to defend the town than to keep it under control.

Look at the style, too. It's Aragonese, the military architecture of the Spanish kings who then ruled southern Italy. The two side towers were once joined by a bridge, long since gone. And a walkway that was meant to link the castle to the older Norman fortress was never built at all.

Because on the twenty-ninth of December, fifteen fourteen, the count walked out of the cathedral and was killed in a side street just beside it. The people of Matera had planned it, meeting in secret behind a boulder they came to call the stone of bad counsel. The street where he died was later given the name Via del Riscatto, which means the street of redemption, or of ransom, depending on how you like your history.

The castle was never finished.

It's been under restoration, along with the park around it, for some years, so check what's open before you come. Either way, walk round the moat, where the old tufa walls have been repaired, and then go down into the town and find Via del Riscatto beside the cathedral. It's a short walk, from the count's ambition to the place it ended.
