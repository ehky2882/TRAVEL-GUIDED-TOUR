Hospital de los Venerables

This was a retirement home for old priests, and it's one of the most beautiful Baroque buildings in Seville.

You're in the Plaza de los Venerables, in the heart of Santa Cruz. The building in front of you, with its Baroque doorway and its church behind, fills one side of the little square.

It was founded in sixteen seventy-five by Justino de Neve, a canon of the cathedral, as a home for elderly, poor and infirm priests, the venerables of the name. Neve was also a friend and patron of the painter Murillo, and you can feel that circle of taste and money in every part of the building. Work began under Juan Domínguez and was finished in sixteen ninety-seven by Leonardo de Figueroa, the greatest Sevillian architect of his day.

Go in and have a look at the courtyard. It's one of the loveliest in the city. In the centre is a fountain set down inside a little round sunken basin, reached by tiled circular steps. You step down into it, and the water is at your feet. It's a simple trick, and it makes the whole courtyard feel cooler and quieter.

Then go into the church. The walls and vaults are covered in paintings by Juan de Valdés Leal and his son Lucas Valdés, one of the richest painted interiors of the Sevillian Baroque.

The Focus foundation restored the building between nineteen eighty-seven and ninety-one, and in two thousand and seven it bought a painting of Santa Rufina by Diego Velázquez, Seville's greatest painter, for more than twelve million euros. It hangs here now in the Velázquez Centre.

And here's the thing. Velázquez was born in this city and left it in his twenties for the court in Madrid. His home town has very few of his paintings. This little building, among the orange trees, is where he finally came home.
