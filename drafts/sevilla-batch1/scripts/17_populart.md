## Script (clean)

Some of the tiles in this little shop are older than the house they're sitting in.

Pasaje de Vila, a short passage in the Barrio de Santa Cruz, a few minutes from the cathedral. Populart is the small shop full of ceramics, at number four. It's easy to walk past. Don't.

The shop was founded in nineteen seventy-seven by Laura Salcines, an expert in traditional crafts, and it has been a quiet treasure ever since. It sells pottery, ironwork, embroidery and engravings. But mostly, it sells tiles.

Have a look at what's on the shelves, because this is the difference. Much of what you'll see in Seville's tourist shops was made last month. Many of the tiles here have been rescued from buildings across the region, from houses being renovated, demolished or modernised. Some go back a century or two. Some are seventeenth-century. Each one was made for a particular wall in a particular house.

Alongside them are old rustic bowls, pots and plates, the everyday kitchen ware of Andalusia a hundred years ago, plain and beautiful, the kind of thing that was simply used until it broke.

And here's the thing. Seville has been building with ceramic for more than five hundred years, and every renovation puts some of that history in a skip. A shop like this is a small rescue operation. Buy one old tile, and you're carrying home a fragment of a real Sevillian house.

*[beat]*

From the passage, walk on into the Plaza de Doña Elvira and the little streets around it. Look at the doorways and the bases of the walls as you go. Many of the tiles still in place are the cousins of the ones you've just seen in the shop.
