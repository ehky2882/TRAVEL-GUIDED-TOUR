## Script (clean)

Most of this arts centre is underground, in a space that was meant to be a car park.

You're at the foot of the Torre Sevilla, on the Isla de la Cartuja, the tallest building in Andalusia. At its base, look for a low, glowing canopy that seems to float over a set of entrances and ramps going down. That's CaixaForum. Almost everything else is beneath your feet.

The tower above you is by César Pelli, the architect of the Petronas Towers in Kuala Lumpur. The arts centre is by Guillermo Vázquez Consuegra, the Sevillian architect who also built the navigation pavilion just along the road. It opened in twenty seventeen.

It's a story with a twist. In two thousand and nine, Vázquez Consuegra won a competition to put CaixaForum somewhere quite different: the Reales Atarazanas, the vast medieval shipyards in the centre of the city. That project never happened. Instead, the arts centre came here, into a concrete structure under the tower that had been built for parking.

Have a look at the canopy. It's made of panels of stabilised aluminium foam, a strange, spongy-looking material, very light, a bit like pumice. It covers the entrances, and through a big skylight it catches the light and pours it down into the lobby underground.

That's the interesting bit. The architect was handed the least promising space imaginable, a concrete basement, and the job was to make people want to go down into it. The answer was to give the city a roof and to send the daylight down after them.

*[beat]*

From here it's a short walk to the Pabellón de la Navegación, by the same architect, twenty-five years earlier. Compare the two. The earlier building is a great timber hull, built to be seen. The later one is almost invisible.
