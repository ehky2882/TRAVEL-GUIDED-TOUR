Kah yeh Beh tees

The best view of Seville is from the other side of the river. This street has it.

Kah yeh Beh tees runs along the Tree ah nah bank of the Gwah dahl kee veer, from the Pwen teh deh Tree ah nah south towards the Plaza deh Koo bah. You should be on the riverside of the street, with a row of brightly painted houses behind you, and the river and the old city spread out in front.

The name is Roman. Bye tis was what the Romans called the Gwah dahl kee veer, and the city council gave the street that name in eighteen fifty nine. Before that it had simply been the river street: kah yeh del Ree oh, Veh rah del Ree oh, Oh ree yah del Ree oh. Different words, same idea.

For most of its history this bank was hard to live on. The river flooded. From seventeen eighty seven a great embankment was built against it, which is why the street now sits well above the water. Once the iron bridge opened in eighteen fifty two, the street filled up with houses and became the face of Tree ah nah.

Now look across the river. From left to right, you should be able to pick out the white and ochre ring of the Mah ess trahn sah bullring, the Hee rahl dah above the rooftops, the church of the Hospital deh lah Kah ree dahd, and the Toh reh del Oh roh. On a clear day, look south for the towers of the Plaza deh Ess pahn yah.

And here's the thing. Seville built its monuments facing the river, as a show for anyone arriving by boat. Tree ah nah, the working quarter of sailors and potters, got the best seat in the house, and kept it.

On a summer evening in late July, this end of the street fills with stalls and lights for the Veh lah deh Santa Ana, Tree ah nah's own festival, which may go back to the Middle Ages. Any other time, just find a bar with a riverside table and wait for the sun to go down behind you and light up the city.
