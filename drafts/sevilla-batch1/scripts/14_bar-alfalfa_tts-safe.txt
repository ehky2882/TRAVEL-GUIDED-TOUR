Bar Alfalfa

This tiny bar sits on the corner of a street named after a candle, and the candle once caught a king.

You're at the top of the Plaza deh lah Alfalfa, on the corner of Kah yeh Kahn dee leh hoh. Bar Alfalfa is the very small bar on the corner, with barely room to turn round inside. People spill out onto the pavement with their glasses. If it's evening, you'll probably have to wait.

The bar is run by Italian owners, and you can taste it. Alongside the traditional Sevillian tapas, the sal moh reh hoh and kroh keh tahs, there are carpaccios, bruschetta and baked provolone. It's small, friendly and busy, and it's as much a neighbourhood bar as a restaurant.

But it's the street name that I want you to notice. Kahn dee leh hoh means a little oil lamp. The legend goes like this. One night in the fourteenth century, two men fought a duel here, and one was killed. An old woman watched from her window by the light of her candle. When the authorities asked her what she saw, she said the killer had walked away with his knees creaking, cracking with every step. Everyone in Seville knew whose knees creaked like that: King Peh droh the First.

The king had promised that the killer's head would be displayed in the street where the crime happened. So he kept his word, in his own way. A stone bust of the king himself was set up in a niche. The story was being told in print by the seventeenth century, and the bust has been replaced more than once.

Walk a few steps along Kah yeh Kahn dee leh hoh and turn into Kah yeh Kah beh sah del Ray Don Peh droh, the street of King Peh droh's Head. Look up, and you'll find him there, in his niche, still keeping an eye on the crossroads.
