Ess lah vah

One of the most famous tapas in Seville is a single egg yolk. It's served here.

You're on Kah yeh Ess lah vah, in the San Loh ren soh quarter, right beside the church square. Ess lah vah has high tables outside that the neighbours guard rather fiercely at lunchtime. If there's a small crowd outside with drinks in hand, you've probably found it.

Seeks toh Toh var opened it in nineteen eighty eight. In those days San Loh ren soh was a quiet parish of churches and old houses, a long way from any tourist trail, and Ess lah vah was a neighbourhood bar. Within a few years it had changed what people in this city expected a tapa to be.

Start with the dish that did it. An egg yolk, very gently cooked, set on a soft sponge made with mushrooms, in a dark, glossy sauce of caramelised mushroom and sherry. It sounds simple. It wins prizes. Burst the yolk and let it run into the sauce.

Then there's the one with the best name: Oon see gah roh pah rah Beh ker, a cigar for Beh ker. Goos tah voh Ah dol foh Beh ker was Seville's great Romantic poet, and this is a thin, crisp roll filled with cuttlefish, seaweed and squid ink, finished with a dusting of something grey that is meant to look like cigar ash. It's a joke you can eat.

And that's the interesting bit. For a long time, tapas meant a saucer of olives or a slice of ham. Ess lah vah kept the bar, the noise and the prices, and changed only what was on the plate. Plenty of clever tapas bars across Spain owe it a little something.

When you've eaten, walk round the corner into the Plaza deh San Loh ren soh. On one side is the basilica of Heh soos del Grahn Poh dare, home to one of the most venerated images in Seville. If the doors are open, step in. The silence inside is quite a contrast to the bar.
