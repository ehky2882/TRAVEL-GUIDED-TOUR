## Script (clean)

The most famous thing on the menu here is an omelette with whisky in it. Stay with me.

You're on Calle Adolfo Rodríguez Jurado, a short street in the Arenal, a few minutes from the Puerta de Jerez and the cathedral. Bodeguita Casablanca is a small corner bar.

It opened in two thousand and five, and it's run by two cousins, Antonio and Tomás Casablanca, both from families long in the city's hospitality trade. The cooking is what they call traditional Andalusian: fried fish, octopus, scrambled eggs with wild mushrooms, the things this city has always eaten at a bar.

And then there's the tortilla al whisky. Spanish potato omelette is the most ordinary dish in the country, made in every home. Here it's served in a sauce made with whisky, and it has become the house signature, copied by other kitchens since. Order it, and something fried to go with it. The puntillitas, tiny fried squid with garlic and baby broad beans, are a good choice.

What makes a place like this work is the counter. Have a look at it: the regulars standing, the plates going over the top, the orders called out across the room. It's quick, sociable and loud. You don't come for the room; you come for the room's company.

That's the interesting bit. Nobody would put this bar in an architecture guide for its building. But an architect will tell you that a good bar counter is one of the best-designed public spaces in Spain. It gets strangers talking.

*[beat]*

From here it's a short walk down to the river and the Torre del Oro, the twelve-sided watchtower the Almohads built in the early thirteenth century to guard the port. Go and stand at its foot.
