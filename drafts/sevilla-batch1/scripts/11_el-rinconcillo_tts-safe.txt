El Reen kon see yo

This bar opened in sixteen seventy. Your bill will still be written in chalk.

You're at the corner of Kah yeh Heh roh nah and Kah yeh Ah lon dee gah, a few minutes east of the Seh tahs. El Reen kon see yo is the old corner house with its name above the door. Across the street is the brick church of Santa Kah tah lee nah. If you can see the church, you're in the right place.

Sixteen seventy makes this the oldest bar in Seville. In eighteen fifty eight the Deh Rweh dah family bought it, and they have run it ever since. In eighteen ninety seven they bought the house next door, on Ah lon dee gah, and for decades it was a little grocer's shop. When grocers like it began to disappear in the nineteen sixties, the two houses were joined into the tavern you see today.

Step inside and have a look at the counter. It's mahogany, and the waiters chalk your order straight onto the wood in front of you. When you pay, they wipe it off. That's the whole accounting system, and it has outlived every computer ever invented.

Then look at the walls. The tiles run from the seventeenth to the nineteenth centuries, the brick is laid in the old Moorish manner, and the carved shelves behind the bar are stacked with bottles, tins and jars. Spanish film crews have used this room more than once, because nothing in it needs dressing.

And here's the thing. The family's great achievement is not what they have added, but what they have refused to change. In a city that has been redecorated by every passing fashion, this room is a sort of time capsule that still serves lunch.

Before you go in, or after you come out, cross the street to Santa Kah tah lee nah. It's a medieval church in the Moo deh har style, partly built on the site of an older mosque, and it reopened after a long restoration. Look up at its brick tower.
