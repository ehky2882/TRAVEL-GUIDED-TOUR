## Script (clean)

One of the most famous tapas in Seville is a single egg yolk. It's served here.

You're on Calle Eslava, in the San Lorenzo quarter, right beside the church square. Eslava has high tables outside that the neighbours guard rather fiercely at lunchtime. If there's a small crowd outside with drinks in hand, you've probably found it.

Sixto Tovar opened it in nineteen eighty-eight. In those days San Lorenzo was a quiet parish of churches and old houses, a long way from any tourist trail, and Eslava was a neighbourhood bar. Within a few years it had changed what people in this city expected a tapa to be.

Start with the dish that did it. An egg yolk, very gently cooked, set on a soft sponge made with mushrooms, in a dark, glossy sauce of caramelised mushroom and sherry. It sounds simple. It wins prizes. Burst the yolk and let it run into the sauce.

Then there's the one with the best name: Un cigarro para Bécquer, a cigar for Bécquer. Gustavo Adolfo Bécquer was Seville's great Romantic poet, and this is a thin, crisp roll filled with cuttlefish, seaweed and squid ink, finished with a dusting of something grey that is meant to look like cigar ash. It's a joke you can eat.

And that's the interesting bit. For a long time, tapas meant a saucer of olives or a slice of ham. Eslava kept the bar, the noise and the prices, and changed only what was on the plate. Plenty of clever tapas bars across Spain owe it a little something.

*[beat]*

When you've eaten, walk round the corner into the Plaza de San Lorenzo. On one side is the basilica of Jesús del Gran Poder, home to one of the most venerated images in Seville. If the doors are open, step in. The silence inside is quite a contrast to the bar.
