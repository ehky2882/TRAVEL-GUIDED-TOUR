## Script (clean)

This hotel belongs to the city. Not to a chain, not to a duke. Seville owns it, and has done since the day it opened.

The building in front of you, on Calle San Fernando, is a long palace of red brick, white plaster and glazed tile, with a run of arched windows and towers at its corners. Next door, behind the railings, is the old Royal Tobacco Factory, now the university. If you can see both, you're in the right spot.

It was built for the Ibero-American Exposition of nineteen twenty-nine, the great fair that remade the south of the city. The design came out of a competition, and the winner was a Sevillian architect, José Espiau y Muñoz. Building began in nineteen sixteen and took twelve years. On the twenty-eighth of April, nineteen twenty-nine, King Alfonso the Thirteenth and Queen Victoria Eugenia sat down to the opening banquet.

Have a look at the materials, because they're the whole argument. Brick, plaster, timber and ceramic. These are the humble things Seville had always built with, here put to work on a grand hotel. The style is called Neo-Mudéjar: it borrows from the medieval craftsmen, Muslim and Christian, who built the Alcázar, and dresses a modern building in their clothes.

Step inside to the central patio if you can. It was first modelled on the courtyard of the Hospital de los Venerables, a Baroque almshouse in Santa Cruz, and then redesigned at the king's own request. The tiles climb the walls; the lamps hang from carved ceilings.

And here's the thing. Its name has not always been safe. When the king left Spain in nineteen thirty-one and the Republic was declared, the hotel was quietly renamed the Andalucía Palace. The name came back later. The building outlasted the politics, which is rather what buildings in this city tend to do.

*[beat]*

When you leave, cross to the Tobacco Factory next door. In the eighteenth century it was one of the largest industrial buildings in Europe, and it's where the opera puts Carmen to work, rolling cigars. Walk through its courtyards; the university doesn't mind.
