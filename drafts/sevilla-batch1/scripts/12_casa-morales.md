## Script (clean)

Behind the bar here, huge earthenware jars line the walls. Each one held nearly four thousand litres of wine.

You're on Calle García de Vinuesa, in the Arenal, one street from the cathedral. Casa Morales is the old shopfront with its name on the front, opening onto a narrow bar. Look past the counter into the back room and you'll see the jars, called tinajas, lining the walls like sleeping giants.

The bodega was opened in eighteen fifty by Leocadio Morales Prieto. It's still run by his great-granddaughter, Reyes Morales, who gave up a career as a pharmacist to come back to the family business. The street used to be called Calle del Mar, the street of the sea, because it led down towards the river and the port.

For most of its life this wasn't really a place to eat at all. It was a wine business. The family sold wine from Valdepeñas, in La Mancha, by the litre, and the tinajas in the back held the stock. When the rules on selling wine in bulk changed in the nineteen eighties, that trade ended. The house wines are still aged and bottled in Valdepeñas.

The food came late. Reyes began serving little sandwiches, montaditos, in Holy Week of nineteen ninety-two, the first food ever sold in this bar. Today there's a kitchen in the old warehouse, but the jars stayed. Nobody would dream of moving them.

That's the interesting bit. You're drinking inside the working machinery of a nineteenth-century wine shop. The jars aren't decoration. They're the original equipment, simply left where they stood.

When you leave, head along the street towards the cathedral. In a couple of minutes you're under its walls. Find the Puerta del Perdón on Calle Alemanes, the gateway of the old mosque that stood here before the cathedral was built.
