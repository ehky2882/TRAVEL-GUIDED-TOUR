# 15 · Mercado de Feria

- **Coordinates (WGS-84):** 37.399474, -5.991191
- **Length:** 310 words, about 120 seconds at a natural pace

## Script (clean)

This is the oldest market in Seville. There's been trading on this spot since the thirteenth century.

Plaza de Calderón de la Barca, at the back of the market, just off Calle Feria. The market is in two buildings with a small alley between them, and right beside it stands a heavy medieval church wall: Omnium Sanctorum, one of the oldest parish churches in the city.

The market has its roots in the Middle Ages, but its own history usually starts in seventeen nineteen. The halls were extended in the eighteen thirties and again in nineteen twenty-nine, and renovated in the nineteen eighties. It's a building that has been added to rather than replaced, which is very Seville.

Have a look at the stalls first. Fish, meat, fruit and vegetables, as you'd expect, but the fish hall is now down to a handful of family stalls. The rest of the market has slowly turned into somewhere to eat and drink, and on a Saturday morning it's one of the most enjoyable places in the city.

Now the bars. On the south side is La Cantina, known for some of the best fried fish in Seville. Its tables stand right up against the wall of Omnium Sanctorum, so you eat your fried fish in the shadow of a medieval church. At the north end, La Almadraba serves tuna from the coast of Cádiz.

What I like about it is the mixture. Old ladies with shopping trolleys; young people drinking vermouth; a fishmonger shouting prices. Nobody has tidied it up for visitors.

*[beat]*

If you're here on a Thursday morning, walk up Calle Feria. For centuries a flea market called El Jueves, simply "Thursday", has spread along this street every week. It's a jumble of old tools, books, postcards and furniture, and nothing seems to have changed in years.
