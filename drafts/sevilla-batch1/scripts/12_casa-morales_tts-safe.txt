Kah sah Moh rah less

Behind the bar here, huge earthenware jars line the walls. Each one held nearly four thousand litres of wine.

You're on Kah yeh Gar see ah deh Vee nweh sah, in the Ah reh nahl, one street from the cathedral. Kah sah Moh rah less is the old shopfront with its name on the front, opening onto a narrow bar. Look past the counter into the back room and you'll see the jars, called tee nah hahs, lining the walls like sleeping giants.

The bodega was opened in eighteen fifty by Leh oh kah dee oh Moh rah less Pree eh toh. It's still run by his great granddaughter, Ray ess Moh rah less, who gave up a career as a pharmacist to come back to the family business. The street used to be called Kah yeh del Mar, the street of the sea, because it led down towards the river and the port.

For most of its life this wasn't really a place to eat at all. It was a wine business. The family sold wine from Vahl deh pen yahs, in Lah Mancha, by the litre, and the tee nah hahs in the back held the stock. When the rules on selling wine in bulk changed in the nineteen eighties, that trade ended. The house wines are still aged and bottled in Vahl deh pen yahs.

The food came late. Ray ess began serving little sandwiches, mon tah dee tohs, in Holy Week of nineteen ninety two, the first food ever sold in this bar. Today there's a kitchen in the old warehouse, but the jars stayed. Nobody would dream of moving them.

That's the interesting bit. You're drinking inside the working machinery of a nineteenth century wine shop. The jars aren't decoration. They're the original equipment, simply left where they stood.

When you leave, head along the street towards the cathedral. In a couple of minutes you're under its walls. Find the Pwair tah del Pair don on Kah yeh Ah leh mah ness, the gateway of the old mosque that stood here before the cathedral was built.
