## Script (clean)

You're about to do your shopping on top of the Spanish Inquisition.

You're beside the Mercado de Triana, at the Triana end of the Puente de Triana, just to your left as you come off the bridge. The market is the tiled building at the foot of Calle San Jorge, with the river behind it.

This site was once the Castillo de San Jorge, the Castle of Saint George. There was a fortress here guarding the river crossing long before Ferdinand the Third took Seville in twelve forty-eight. Afterwards he handed it to the Order of Saint George, which gave it the name.

Then, in fourteen eighty-one, the castle became the headquarters of the Spanish Inquisition, the first tribunal of its kind in Castile. It stayed, with interruptions, for three hundred years. Many thousands of people passed through its cells and courtrooms. What finally drove the Inquisition out was the river. After the great floods of the seventeen eighties, the castle was no longer usable.

The castle was demolished in the first years of the eighteen hundreds, and in eighteen twenty-three a market was built over its ruins. Its foundations stayed buried until the market was rebuilt in the nineteen nineties, when the castle came to light again.

Now have a look inside. Stalls of fruit, ham, fish and spices, little bars and a cooking school, among a great deal of decorative tiling. You can buy a coffee and eat a slice of tortilla standing in the middle of it all.

That's the interesting bit. The Inquisition is long gone, and the place where it sat is now one of the friendliest rooms in Seville, full of noise and food. History here isn't sealed off behind glass. It's under the floor of the fishmonger's.

*[beat]*

Go to the back of the market, where the castle remains are open to visitors when the site is open. Then step outside into the Callejón de la Inquisición, the narrow alley that was once part of the fortifications. It runs down beside the market to the river.
