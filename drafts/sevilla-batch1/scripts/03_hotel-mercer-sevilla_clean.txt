Hotel Mercer Sevilla

The architects who converted this house also designed Seville's main railway station. Here they were given twelve bedrooms.

You're on Calle Castelar, in the Arenal, the old riverside quarter between the cathedral and the bullring. The Mercer is the nineteenth-century house with the classical front, tall windows and a grand doorway. It's easy to walk past, so check the name by the door.

The house went up around eighteen eighty for a well-off Sevillian family. It's a type you'll see all over the old city: a symmetrical front on the street, and behind it a typical Andalusian central courtyard, with a marble staircase rising from it to the bedrooms.

In two thousand and sixteen it was converted by Cruz y Ortiz, the Sevillian practice behind Santa Justa station and, much later, the football stadium of Atlético Madrid. Their touch here is so light you could miss it. They kept the façade, the patio and the staircase. Then they made one decisive move: the house had a second, smaller courtyard at the back, a little off to one side, and they shifted it onto the central line of the building, so that the whole plan reads as one calm, symmetrical whole.

The rooms have ceilings around four and a half metres high, which in a Seville summer is not a luxury but a cooling system. And on the roof they put a small pool lined in stainless steel, with a bar looking out over the Maestranza bullring, the cathedral and the river.

That's the interesting bit. A firm that builds stations and stadiums chose to leave almost no fingerprint at all. Sometimes the best work in an old house is the thing you decide not to do.

Walk to the end of Calle Castelar and turn right towards the river. In two minutes you'll reach the Maestranza bullring, whose whitewashed and ochre front you just saw from the roof. Its curve is famously not quite a circle.
