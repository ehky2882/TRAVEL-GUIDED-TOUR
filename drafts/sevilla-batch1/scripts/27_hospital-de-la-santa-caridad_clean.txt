Hospital de la Santa Caridad

The man who built this hospital asked to be buried under its doorstep, so that everyone who came in would walk over him.

You're on Calle Temprado, in the Arenal, between the river and the Maestranza theatre. In front of you is a whitewashed church front, framed in stone, with large tile panels set into it, and a bell tower above. That's the church of San Jorge, part of the Hospital de la Santa Caridad, the Holy Charity. The church and hospital have been under restoration, so if the doors are shut, this tour works perfectly well from the street.

The man behind it was Miguel de Mañara. He joined the Brotherhood of the Holy Charity in sixteen sixty-two, became its head the next year, and spent the rest of his fortune and his life caring for the sick and the dying poor, and burying the dead nobody else would bury. There's an old legend that his wild youth inspired the story of Don Juan. It's almost certainly not true, but you can see why it stuck.

Have a look at the tile panels on the front. They show Saint George and Saint James, and the three virtues of Faith, Hope and Charity. Charity was the whole point of this building.

The church opened in sixteen seventy-four. Inside are two of the most startling paintings in Spain, by Juan de Valdés Leal: one shows Death snuffing out a candle, the other a rotting bishop and a knight in their coffins. Mañara ordered them as a warning at the door. Murillo painted six works of mercy for the walls. Four of them were taken by Napoleon's Marshal Soult in eighteen ten, and they are now in London, Ottawa, Washington and Saint Petersburg.

And Mañara got his wish. He's buried under a stone slab at the church entrance, and his own words are cut into it: here lie the bones and ashes of the worst man there has ever been in the world.

Walk down to the river and the Paseo de Cristóbal Colón; the Torre del Oro is two minutes to your left. And if you're ever in the National Gallery in London, look for Murillo's healing of the paralytic. It was painted for the walls of this church.
