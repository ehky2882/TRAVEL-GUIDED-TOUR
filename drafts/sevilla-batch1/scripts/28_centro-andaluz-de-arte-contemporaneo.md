# 28 · Centro Andaluz de Arte Contemporáneo

## Script (clean)

Christopher Columbus spent time in this monastery. Later, an Englishman turned it into a pottery factory. Now it's full of contemporary art.

You're on the Isla de la Cartuja, the long island across the river from the old city. In front of you is the old monastery of Santa María de las Cuevas, with its church, cloisters and gardens. Look for a cluster of strange brick towers shaped like enormous bottles, and a tall factory chimney. Those are the kilns.

The monastery belonged to Carthusian monks, and it was one of the great religious houses of Seville. Columbus was close to the monks here, and for some years after his death his remains rested in the monastery before being taken across the Atlantic to Santo Domingo.

Then the story changes completely. In the eighteen thirties the Spanish state seized the property of the monasteries, and in eighteen thirty-nine an English merchant, Charles Pickman, bought this one. In eighteen forty-one he started making ceramics here. The Pickman factory turned out tableware for well over a century, until the nineteen eighties. La Cartuja china is still made today, in a factory outside the city.

Have a look at those bottle kilns. They're the most surprising thing on this island: industrial chimneys and furnaces rising out of a medieval monastery, the cloister and the kiln side by side.

The whole complex was restored for the World Expo of nineteen ninety-two, which was held on this island. And in nineteen ninety-seven the Andalusian Centre for Contemporary Art moved in.

And here's the thing. Most old buildings in Seville have been restored to look like one moment in their history. This one shows you all of them at once: monks, Columbus, factory workers and artists. Nothing has been tidied away.

*[beat]*

When you've been round, walk down to the riverbank on the eastern side of the island. From there the whole old city is spread out across the water, from the Giralda to the bridges, and you can see how close the monks were to the city, and how far.
