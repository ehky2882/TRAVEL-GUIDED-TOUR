El Rinconcillo

This bar opened in sixteen seventy. Your bill will still be written in chalk.

You're at the corner of Calle Gerona and Calle Alhóndiga, a few minutes east of the Setas. El Rinconcillo is the old corner house with its name above the door. Across the street is the brick church of Santa Catalina. If you can see the church, you're in the right place.

Sixteen seventy makes this the oldest bar in Seville. In eighteen fifty-eight the De Rueda family bought it, and they have run it ever since. In eighteen ninety-seven they bought the house next door, on Alhóndiga, and for decades it was a little grocer's shop. When grocers like it began to disappear in the nineteen sixties, the two houses were joined into the tavern you see today.

Step inside and have a look at the counter. It's mahogany, and the waiters chalk your order straight onto the wood in front of you. When you pay, they wipe it off. That's the whole accounting system, and it has outlived every computer ever invented.

Then look at the walls. The tiles run from the seventeenth to the nineteenth centuries, the brick is laid in the old Moorish manner, and the carved shelves behind the bar are stacked with bottles, tins and jars. Spanish film crews have used this room more than once, because nothing in it needs dressing.

And here's the thing. The family's great achievement is not what they have added, but what they have refused to change. In a city that has been redecorated by every passing fashion, this room is a sort of time capsule that still serves lunch.

Before you go in, or after you come out, cross the street to Santa Catalina. It's a medieval church in the Mudéjar style, partly built on the site of an older mosque, and it reopened after a long restoration. Look up at its brick tower.
