## Script (clean)

The name is an old Castilian word for an apron. It's a modest name for a kitchen that first won a Michelin star in two thousand and nine.

You're on Calle Alcalde José de la Bandera, a quiet residential street in Nervión, just east of the old city walls. Abantal sits behind a discreet front on the ground floor. If you're not eating tonight, there's not much to see from the pavement, and that's rather the point.

Abantal opened in the early two thousands, and its chef, Julio Fernández Quintero, was trained in Seville. The star arrived in two thousand and nine, and for years Abantal was one of the very few restaurants in the city to hold one.

The cooking is Andalusian, but contemporary. The house philosophy is a phrase about knowing where you come from in order to understand where you're going. In practice that means the ingredients of this region, olive oil, the fish of the Andalusian coast, sherry, cooked in a modern way and served as a tasting menu.

The dining room is designed to step out of the way. It's open, bright and warm, without any of the gilded drama you might expect. In a city where so much of the beauty is old and ornate, there's something bracing about a room that is simply clean and calm.

And here's the thing. Seville's food culture is built on the bar, the standing, the sharing and the noise. A tasting menu is the opposite of all that: you sit, you wait, you are told a story. Abantal's achievement has been to make that slower meal feel Sevillian too.

*[beat]*

From here, walk a minute north to Calle Luis Montoto. Along it stand the surviving arches of the Caños de Carmona, the old aqueduct that for centuries brought water into the city. Follow them towards the old town.
