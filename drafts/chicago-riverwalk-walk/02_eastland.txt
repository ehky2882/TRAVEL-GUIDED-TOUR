WALK 1: THE RIVERWALK
Segment 02 — The Eastland (south bank, LaSalle to Clark)

You should be on the wide stone steps between the LaSalle Street bridge and the Clark Street bridge, water in front of you. The marker is set in the ground up at the corner of LaSalle and Wacker.

On a Saturday morning in July 1915, the excursion steamer Eastland was tied up at the dock along this stretch, loading more than two thousand people. Factory workers and their families from the Western Electric plant out in Cicero, going across the lake for the annual company picnic.

The ship never left the dock. It rolled onto its side while still tied up, a short distance from the wall you're standing on. It remains the worst maritime disaster in the history of the Great Lakes.

For most of the century afterward, almost nothing here said so. The first memorial went up in 1990 and was stolen. The plaque that replaced it is what's at the corner now, and that single marker is still the whole of it.

*[beat]*

What there is instead is the anniversary. Every July twenty-fourth, descendants come down to this stretch of the Riverwalk. There's a moment of silence. Someone leans over the railing and drops flowers into the water. It's a small thing, kept up by the families, and it has happened every year for longer than most of the people who come to it have been alive.

Stay as long as you like. When you're ready, keep east, under Clark and under Dearborn, until two round concrete towers stand up on the far bank: Marina City.
