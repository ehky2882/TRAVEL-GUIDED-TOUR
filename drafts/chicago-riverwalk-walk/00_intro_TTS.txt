A mile and a quarter, a staircase at every bridge, and a river that has been running the wrong way on purpose since nineteen hundred.

You're at the top of the Lake Street stairway, at the west end of the Chicago Riverwalk. Below you the path runs east along the south bank of the river, all the way to Lake Michigan. It's free, it's open from early morning until late, and it passes underneath every bridge instead of making you climb back up to the street, which is the whole trick of the thing.

The honest version: getting down and getting out is stairs — Franklin, Wells, luh sal, Clark, State, Wabash, Michigan. Between fall and spring most of the boats and the food and the noise go away. Twenty minutes if you march it. We're going to take considerably longer.

One idea holds this walk together. Every stop on it is a decision about water: which way it goes, how high it sits, what gets built on top of it, who is allowed to cross it and when. None of that is natural. All of it was decided by somebody, usually in an argument, and most of those decisions are still being enforced this morning.

We start at the fork where the city began and finish at the gate where the lake is let in.

Go down the stairs, put the river on your left, and walk east until a long limestone building fills the far bank. First stop: Wolf Point and the Merchandise Mart.
