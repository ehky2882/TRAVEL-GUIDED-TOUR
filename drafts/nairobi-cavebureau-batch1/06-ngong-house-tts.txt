Ngong House

Nairobi's suburbs have their share of grand colonial houses. This place put its bedrooms up the trees.

Ngong House sits on Ndohvoo Road, in the leafy Karen and Langahta side of the city — a gated plot of acacias and flowering trees. Look up from the garden and you'll see them: little wooden houses perched high on stilts, roofed in thatch, each with a balcony turned towards the hills. If you're among trees with your neck craned, wondering how anybody gets their luggage up there, you've found it.

Have a look at how they're made. Hand built timber, thatch on top, raised well clear of the ground — part safari lodge, part childhood den. The cave bureau guide calls it a "rustic getaway", and that's exactly the register. Reviewers have called it eccentric and quirky, which, in a world of identical hotel rooms, is rather the point.

The view they're facing is the Ngong Hills, the long, knuckled ridge that lines the city's south western horizon. You'll know the name, and the hills, from Karen Blixen — "I had a farm in Africa, at the foot of the Ngong Hills." Her old coffee farm is only a few kilometres from here. When she went back to Denmark, the land was carved into plots, and those plots became the suburb that still carries her name.

The property is said to have begun as a hunter's lodge, before a former Belgian diplomat turned it into a small hotel; for years, guests talked about him hosting drinks by the fire most evenings. Ownership and room counts have shifted with time, so check the details if you're booking.

And here's what I like about it. Most of Nairobi's grand houses were built to look out over the land as though they owned it. These are built to sit up in it, among the branches, like the birds.

If you're nearby, the Giraffe Centre is only about a kilometre away — and between the two you'll have spent a morning at eye level with things that usually live a long way above you. Before you leave, though, find a spot in the garden at dusk, and watch the Ngong Hills turn blue.
