## Script (clean)

Giraffe Centre

You're about to be eye to eye with the tallest animal on earth. Mind your head.

The Giraffe Centre is on Duma Road in Langata, in the south-western suburbs. You'll find a raised wooden platform with a feeding deck at roughly giraffe height. If a very long neck has just swung towards you and a dark purple tongue is reaching for your hand, you're in the right place.

Have a look at the giraffes first. These are Rothschild's giraffes, a subspecies found only in East Africa. You can tell them by their legs: below the knee they're plain white, as if they're wearing stockings. When this centre began, they were in serious trouble. In the late nineteen seventies, only about a hundred and thirty were left in western Kenya, on a ranch that was being divided up for settlement.

That's when Jock and Betty Leslie Melville stepped in. He was a Kenyan of British descent; she was American. In nineteen seventy-nine they founded the African Fund for Endangered Wildlife, and brought two young giraffes, Daisy and Marlon, to their home here in Langata to raise them. From there they started breeding giraffes, and moving herds to safe parks around the country. Today there are more than three hundred Rothschild's giraffes breeding in Kenyan national parks.

Now, the feeding. You'll be handed pellets. Hold one between your fingertips, or lay it on your flat palm. And be warned: the cave_bureau guide's advice is not a joke — "careful, it can headbutt you." A giraffe swinging that head round can deliver a proper knock. Respect the neck.

[beat]

And here's the part that's easy to miss. This isn't really a tourist attraction. It was built as an education centre, and its main purpose is to teach Kenyan schoolchildren about their own wildlife. Every one of those visitors with a pellet in their hand is part of the plan.

When you've finished, look out through the trees for the grand old ivy-covered house that shares this land. That was the Leslie Melvilles' home, where Daisy and Marlon grew up. It's now a hotel, Giraffe Manor, where the giraffes are known to put their heads through the windows at breakfast.
