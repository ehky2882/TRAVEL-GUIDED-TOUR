## Script (clean)

Ngara

Look at the upper floors. That's where Ngara's history is.

You're on Ngara Road, just north of the central business district, outside the Ngara branch of Soma Nami Books — the one with the bright mural round its entrance. Either side of you, the street is lined with two-storey buildings: shops on the ground floor, verandas and living quarters above. Some are freshly painted; some look exactly as they have for seventy years. If you can see both at once, you're standing in the middle of the story.

Ngara was laid out by colonial planners as an Asian district, for the Indian families who came with the railway and stayed. By the nineteen fifties, these two-storey buildings lined the streets. After independence, the segregation ended, African families moved in, and over the decades many Indian families left for the suburbs or abroad. Have a look for Amar Sons, the fabric and tailoring shop — run by the same family since the nineteen fifties, its windows still full of saris and embroidered suits.

In later years Ngara got a harder reputation: garages, grime, and a place where, as one local put it, you could easily sell parts from a stolen car. And then something changed. A development company called NBST, founded in twenty twenty, began buying, restoring and leasing buildings along this stretch under the name Visit Ngara. Its early plan was to take fourteen neighbouring properties on Ngara Road and turn them into an arts district. The bookshop arrived. Above it, a lounge with a rooftop terrace, in what was once a dentist's surgery. A fashion house. Murals. A launch party in twenty twenty-four. And the parties kept coming.

[beat]

The cave_bureau guide calls this "an experiment in gentrification worth visiting", and that's exactly the right phrase. The developers say nobody was evicted and that many of the upper floors were empty. One writer, at the launch party, overheard a guest say: "I can't help wondering if we are gentrifying Ngara." The honest answer is that it's still being decided — on this street, right now.

Before you go, walk a little way along the road in both directions, beyond the new paintwork. Notice where the restored stretch stops and the older Ngara carries on. That edge is the experiment.
