Maasai Market, City Centre

This market doesn't have a building. It has a timetable.

On Saturdays, it sets up in the car park beside the courts in the city centre, behind the Hilton and a short walk from the Kenyatta International Conference Centre. If it's a Saturday morning and you're looking at rows of colour laid out on cloths across the tarmac, you're in the right place. If it's any other day, you may be looking at an empty car park — which is rather the point of this tour.

The Maasai Market moves. Through the week it rotates round the city: different days, different car parks and shopping centres, packing up each evening and reappearing somewhere else the next morning. The cave_bureau guide puts it simply: "scattered across the city on different dates and times." The days and places have changed over the years, so check before you go. But the Saturday city-centre market is traditionally the biggest and busiest of the lot.

Have a look at the beadwork first. Collars, bracelets and belts, worked in tiny glass beads in bold reds, blues, whites and greens. Then the shukas — the checked and striped cloths, often red, that Maasai men wear as wraps. Then the carvings, the soapstone, the baskets, the sandals cut from old tyres. Much of it comes from makers right across Kenya, not just Maasai, so ask who made what. People are generally happy to tell you.

Bargaining is expected, and it's meant to be friendly. Start low, smile, and settle somewhere you're both pleased with.

[beat]

Here's what I find rather lovely about it. We usually think of a market as a place — a hall, a square, a roof. This one is closer to a habit. A whole market that the city carries round with it, setting itself up and taking itself down, week after week. Architecture made of cloth, tables and trust.

When you've done the stalls, walk round the corner to the Kenyatta International Conference Centre and take the lift up to its rooftop. From up there, you can look back down on the market — a patchwork of colour laid across the tarmac, which by nightfall will have vanished entirely.
