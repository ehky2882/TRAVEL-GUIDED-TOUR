The Mist, The Mall Westlands

The best room in this shopping centre is the one underneath it.

The Mall, on Ring Road Parklands in Westlands. It's bluntly named and, from the outside, perfectly ordinary — a shopping centre that opened in nineteen ninety. Head for the basement. The Mist doesn't look like much by day: bare grey concrete walls and columns, exposed pipes, a high ceiling. If you've found a room that looks like a car park with ambitions, you're in the right place.

On club nights, that concrete box turns into one of Nairobi's most important rooms for electronic music. The Mist calls itself an underground venue, and it means it literally. The line ups run across ambient, techno, house, jungle, drum and bass and Afro tech — the experimental end of things. One writer at an early event described the bass reverberating through the thick walls "like an acoustic container — frequencies trapped in a space". That's the gift of a basement. The concrete holds the sound, and the sound holds the room.

Now here's the story of the building around it, because it's the part I find most hopeful. Over the years The Mall lost its lustre. Big brands moved out; then Covid emptied it further. The owner, Beekee Kangwahna, decided to offer the empty spaces to arts collectives at low rent. They came. A virtual reality studio. A music innovation hub, Santooree East Africa, that turned a vacant hair salon into a studio. A skate park on the upper roof. A festival of experimental music, Keelehleh. And in the basement, The Mist, a home, as its owners put it, for "the misfits, the adventurous, the ones willing to explore frontiers".

So what you're standing in is a shopping centre that stopped trying to sell you things and started letting people make things. The Irish Times held it up as a model for Dublin. Not bad, for a basement.

If you've come in daylight, take the stairs all the way up instead. The roof holds the skate park and, on event days, an open air bar — and from up there you can see how far the culture has climbed through the building. Then come back on a Friday night, and go down.
