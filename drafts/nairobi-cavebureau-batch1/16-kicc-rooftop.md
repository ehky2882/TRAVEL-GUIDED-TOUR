## Script (clean)

Kenyatta International Conference Centre Rooftop

Up here, you can see where the city ends. Which is rather a lot to ask of most rooftops.

You're on the roof of the Kenyatta International Conference Centre, the tall cylindrical tower on City Square, in the heart of Nairobi. You'll have come up in the lift and paid a small fee on the way, and now you're on the open platform at the top — once the building's helipad — with a low wall all round and the whole city at your feet. If the wind has picked up and you can see in every direction, you've made it.

Start with the building itself. It was completed in the early nineteen seventies, the work of the Norwegian architect Karl Henrik Nøstvik with the Kenyan architect David Mutiso — Kenya's first African chief government architect — and it rises thirty-two storeys. For years, it was the tallest building in the city. Look down into the courtyard and you'll see its low amphitheatre under a cone-shaped roof — often said to echo a traditional hut — and the statue of Jomo Kenyatta, the first president, seated in front.

Now turn round slowly. To the west, the green of Uhuru Park and Central Park. To the south, past the railway, Nairobi National Park — on a good day you can make out the open grassland. And if the sky is really clear, look north-east for Mount Kenya, the second-highest mountain in Africa, floating over the haze.

[beat]

The cave_bureau guide simply calls this the best view of Nairobi City. They're right. But I think the reason it's the best view isn't the height. It's that from up here, you can see what they spend their whole guide arguing: the city is held in place by green. The park, the forests, the open plains. Nairobi isn't really surrounded by countryside. It's surrounded by wilderness.

Before you head down, find the city's main shopping streets below you and trace them back towards the station. That grid grew from the railway depot that was set down here in eighteen ninety-nine. Everything else you can see grew out of it in little more than a century.
