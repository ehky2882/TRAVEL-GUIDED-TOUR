## Script (clean)

K'Osewe Ranalo Foods

It all started with five kilos of kebabs.

Kimathi Street, in the heart of the city centre, a short walk from Nation Centre. You're looking for K'Osewe, which you'll also see written as Ranalo Foods — two names for one restaurant, and Nairobi uses them interchangeably. Politicians, office workers and tourists have been squeezing through its doors on this street for years.

Here's the story. In the early nineteen seventies, a young man called William Osewe sold a five-kilogramme batch of mshikaki — skewered meat, Kenya's answer to the shish kebab — to his neighbours in Kaloleni, in Nairobi's Eastlands. The hawking grew into an eatery under a tree in Kaloleni. That became Ranalo Foods at the Kenya Railways central station. Then a move to the old Cameo Cinema on Kenyatta Avenue. And finally, here, on Kimathi Street. From a tree to a cinema to one of the best-known restaurant names in the capital. Rather a good journey.

What you're here for is Luo cooking, from the shores of Lake Victoria in western Kenya. The classic order is a whole tilapia, fried, served with brown ugali — the maize staple, made with a darker flour — and greens such as sukuma wiki, which is kale, more or less. Fish from the lake, eaten in the middle of the city. Use your hands; everybody else does.

Now, it hasn't all been plain sailing. In the last few years the Osewe business has had a rough time in the courts and with the banks, and one branch, in Upper Hill, was auctioned off. It's worth knowing, because it makes the place feel less like an institution carved in stone and more like what it actually is: a family business, built from nothing, still trying to keep its lights on.

And that's why the architects at cave_bureau put it on their list. Not for the room — for the route. A street hawker's kebabs, followed all the way to the city's main shopping street. That's Nairobi's story in a single menu.

When you've eaten, walk to the corner of Kimathi Street and Mama Ngina Street and find the statue of Dedan Kimathi — the Mau Mau leader this road is named after, rifle in hand. The man who fought from the forests, standing in the middle of the city that came after.
