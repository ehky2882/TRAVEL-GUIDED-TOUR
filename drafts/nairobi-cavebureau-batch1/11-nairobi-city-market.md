## Script (clean)

Nairobi City Market

Step inside and look up. The roof is the reason you're here.

Muindi Mbingu Street, in the city centre — a street that was once called Stewart Street. The City Market is a low, cream-painted building with a taller hall rising from the middle. Go in through the main entrance, past the stalls, and into the hall. You'll know you're in the right spot when the ceiling suddenly soars away above you in a run of tall, pointed concrete arches.

Those arches are the story. They're parabolic — shaped a bit like the inside of an egg — and they spring straight up from square piers to carry the whole roof. The market opened in nineteen thirty-two, replacing an older market that stood nearby. It's an Art Deco building, and it has since been gazetted as a national monument.

You'll often hear that this was once an aircraft hangar. The cave_bureau guide says it too, and you can see why — with that soaring vaulted roof, it really does look the part. The historical record, though, describes a purpose-built municipal market, designed after the town council called for a modern market hall. So the hangar is probably a story the building tells about itself. A rather good one.

Now notice how cool it is in here. That's not an accident. In the early nineteen thirties, Nairobi had barely any electricity to light or cool a building, so the design does it all by itself. Windows face north and south to dodge the sun. Deep fins shade the glass. Hot air rises and slips out through high louvres, drawing cooler air in below. And the stepped roof bounces soft daylight down onto the stalls.

[beat]

So before anybody used the phrase "green architecture", here it was — not as a moral stance, just as common sense. This building has been keeping its traders cool for over ninety years without switching anything on.

Before you go, wander through the flower sellers and the curio stalls, then stop at the far end of the hall and look back along the arches. It's the best view in the building — and it costs nothing at all.
