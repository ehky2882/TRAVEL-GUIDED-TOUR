# BOSTON · W2 — The Second Revolution
Introduction — State Street · walk · segment 1 of 6 · TTS-safe · 29 Sept 2026
---
This walk climbs once, at the end, and the climb is the point.

You're on State Street, a little below Devonshire, looking uphill at the small brick building with the white tower: the Old State House. The harbor is behind you. For most of the next hour the ground is easy. State Street rises gently, Court Street and treh mont run level, and Park Street lifts you a few steps to Beacon. Then it turns up Joy Street, climbs two short, steep blocks to the crest of Beacon Hill, and drops down the far side to a street most visitors never reach. Brick sidewalks, uneven in places.

The building in front of you was the seat of government for this colony and then this state, until the government moved out, to the new State House on the hill, in seventeen ninety eight. The move is the shape of this walk. It starts at the seat the government left and ends behind the seat it moved into, at a house on the back of the hill where a man who defied the law for a decade later sat in the room where it was made.

Boston's first revolution was announced from the balcony above you. Its second one, against slavery, was fought closer to the ground: in a courtroom a block from here, on the steps of a church, at a corner of the Common, and in a brick house on the north slope. Stay where you are. The Old State House comes first.
