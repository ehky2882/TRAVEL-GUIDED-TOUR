# BOSTON · W4 — The Other Bank

The ship in front of you was built on the other side of that river and has spent most of her life on this one.

You're at the fence on Pier One, the ship's side toward you, her bowsprit reaching over the water and the North End across the channel behind her. She was launched in seventeen ninety seven from a yard on the North End shore, a few hundred yards from where you're looking, one of six frigates the young republic ordered when it decided it needed a navy. The Charlestown yard opened three years after her, and she has been coming back to it for repairs ever since.

Her name for the public came out of the War of eighteen twelve, when she beat three British warships in single combat and cannon shot was seen to bounce off her oak sides. The sailors who watched gave her a nickname, and the nickname has done more for her than the Navy ever did. Twice in the eighteen hundreds the Navy proposed to break her up, and twice the public wouldn't have it.

What you're looking at is a working commission. She has a Navy crew, and a few times a year she is towed into the harbor, turned so that she weathers evenly, and brought back. Almost nothing you can see is original; wooden ships are rebuilt plank by plank as they age, and she has been rebuilt many times. The line of the hull, the plan, and the name are what the Navy has kept whole.

Boston made her and let her go. Charlestown, which had a yard and a dry dock and the patience, took her in. The whole of this side of the water follows that pattern, and it starts with a ship towed here to be looked after.

Leave the yard by the main gate and climb Chelsea Street and Monument Avenue to the square at the top. Stop across the street from the monument, on the side where the ground falls away toward Boston.
