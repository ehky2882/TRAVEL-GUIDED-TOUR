# BOSTON · W2 — The Second Revolution
Old State House · walk · segment 2 of 6 · TTS-safe · 29 Sept 2026
---
The people this building was built for moved out, and it has been trying on other jobs ever since.

You're where you were, on State Street, looking up at the east face of the Old State House: the balcony above the ground floor, the lion and the unicorn at the corners of the roofline, the tower behind. If fencing is up on the plaza side, everything you need is on this face.

It went up in seventeen thirteen to hold the colony's government and, downstairs, its merchants. After the Revolution the state government met here too, until seventeen ninety eight, when it moved up the hill. The building it left served as city hall for a decade, then filled with shops and offices, signs pasted across the brick, and by the eighteen eighties it was shabby enough that Chicago offered to buy it and haul it west. Boston kept it, stripped the signs, and opened it as a museum in eighteen eighty two.

Court Street runs on past the far side of this building, and a block along it, on the right, was Court Square and the county courthouse. In February eighteen fifty one a waiter named shad rack Minkins was taken there. He had escaped slavery in Virginia and was working in a coffee house nearby when federal marshals arrested him under the Fugitive Slave Act, passed the year before. When the hearing was adjourned, a crowd of Black Bostonians walked into the courtroom and walked out with him, and within days he was in Canada. Several men were charged with the rescue. One, a clothing dealer named Lewis Hayden, was tried and not convicted. His house is where this walk ends.

The first revolution was read out from that balcony. The second didn't get one. It got a courtroom a block away and a jury that wouldn't convict. Walk up State Street to Washington, continue straight along Court Street to treh mont, and turn left, with the Common ahead on your right. The Granary Burying Ground is the iron fenced ground on your left, just before the white steeple.
