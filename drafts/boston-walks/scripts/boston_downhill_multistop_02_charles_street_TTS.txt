# BOSTON · W1 — Downhill
02 · Charles Street and the dam · walk · segment 3 of 6 · TTS-safe · 29 September 2026

The street running away from you is a dam.

You're at the corner of Beacon and Charles, at the bottom of the slope, on the Common side. Charles Street crosses in front of you, and Beacon Street carries on beyond it, straight as a rule, along the edge of the Public Garden and into the Back Bay. Until the eighteen twenties Beacon Street ended right here. This corner was the shore. The tide reached about where you're standing, and Charles Street was laid on earth cut from a knoll of the hill behind you and carried a few hundred feet down to the water. That's the second measure on this walk: a few hundred feet.

Then, in eighteen twenty one, the street kept going, on top of a wall. A company built a dam from this corner straight west across the mouth of the bay, a mile and a half of stone with a toll road on top, and that road is the Beacon Street you're looking down. The idea was power. Close off the bay, let the tide in and out through gates, and run mills on the flow. So the open water west of here became an enclosed basin, a cove the city made on purpose.

It didn't work the way the drawings promised. The mills came slowly, the tide could no longer flush the basin, the town's drains ran into it, and the penned up bay began to stink. That smell is the reason the Back Bay is a neighborhood. Once you've closed off water and spoiled it, the next idea is to be rid of it altogether, and the ground for the rest of this walk was the answer.

Cross Charles Street here at Beacon and go into the Public Garden through the gate at the corner. Follow the path along the Beacon Street side, then bear left toward the tall statue of a man on horseback. That's George Washington, and the next stop.
