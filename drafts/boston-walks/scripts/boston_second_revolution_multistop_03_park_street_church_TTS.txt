# BOSTON · W2 — The Second Revolution
Park Street Church · walk · segment 4 of 6 · TTS-safe · 29 Sept 2026
---
For sixty years the bell in this steeple was a recording.

You're at the corner of Park and treh mont, looking up at Park Street Church, brick below and a white steeple in stages above, with the Common across treh mont at your back. Finished in eighteen oh nine on the granary's lot, it's still a working congregation.

The bell went into the steeple in eighteen nineteen, cast in London at the Whitechapel foundry, the same shop that cast the Liberty Bell in Philadelphia. It rang daily until nineteen sixty, when the frame that swung it wore out and the church put a loudspeaker in its place. In twenty nineteen the bell came down by crane for the first time since it went up, was repaired in Ohio, and was rehung in twenty twenty one with five new bells beside it. If you hear ringing from up there, it's bronze again, not a speaker.

The steeple has a part in this walk too. By the church's own count it stands ten feet higher than the dome of the State House. In eighteen twenty nine, on the steps below it, a young newspaperman named William Lloyd Garrison gave his first major public speech against slavery, under a steeple that looked down on the seat of the state's government.

Walk up Park Street beside the church, a short rise along the Common, to the corner of Beacon. The bronze relief set into stone at the Common's edge is the Shaw Memorial. Face it, with the State House across Beacon Street behind you.
