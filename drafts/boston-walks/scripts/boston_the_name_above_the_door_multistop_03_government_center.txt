# BOSTON · W5 — The Name Above the Door
Across Government Center · walk · segment 4 of 6 · clean · 29 September 2026
---
The place you're about to cross is named for a job, not a person.

You're at the edge of City Hall Plaza, the plaza sloping down past City Hall to Congress Street. Government Center is the name on the T station and the maps, and it names a function: city offices, state offices, federal ones across the way. Boston has places named for merchants, preachers, governors and saints, and this one is named for a fact.

The square on this ground before the 1960s carried a family's name, Scollay, and was a district of theaters, bars and cheap hotels the city had come to regard as a problem. The buildings went, and the name went with them. The new one was meant to sound like nobody in particular, and it does. On this walk, notice it. A city that argues over whether a name should stay has also, right here, taken one off the map with hardly any argument.

*[beat]*

Cross the plaza toward Congress Street, keeping City Hall on your left. Parts of it are fenced off at times, so follow whatever path is open. Below you across Congress Street is a small brick hall with a white cupola, and behind it the long granite roof of the market.

Cross Congress Street at the light into Dock Square, walk past the front of the brick hall and along its side, and stop when you come out between it and the columned end of the granite building, which is Quincy Market.
