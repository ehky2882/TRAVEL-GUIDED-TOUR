# BOSTON · W2 — The Second Revolution
Granary Burying Ground · walk · segment 3 of 6 · clean · 29 Sept 2026
---
This ground stopped taking anyone in 1880.

You're on Tremont Street at the stone gateway of the Granary Burying Ground, looking in, with the white steeple of Park Street Church just to your left. If the gate is shut, all of this reads from the sidewalk.

The burying ground has been closed to burials since 1880. Before that it took in the people who ran the first revolution. John Hancock and Samuel Adams are both here, along with a third man who signed the Declaration, and near the fence a single stone stands over the victims of the Boston Massacre. Set aside in 1660, it was so full by 1880 that the city's doctors said it should stop, and it did.

The timing matters to this walk. The second revolution's people are not here. William Lloyd Garrison, whose speech you'll hear about at the next corner, was buried across town at Forest Hills the year before the gate closed to burials. Lewis Hayden lies in Everett. The people who finished what the Declaration started had to be buried somewhere else, because the ground that holds its signers had run out of room.

*[beat]*

The name, before you move. The granary it's named for was the town's grain store, and it stood on the lot beside this one, where the steeple now stands. It was carted off in 1809 to make room for the church. Walk the few steps to the corner of Park Street and turn to face the steeple.
