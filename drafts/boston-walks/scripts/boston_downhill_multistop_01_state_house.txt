# BOSTON · W1 — Downhill
01 · The State House, from the top · walk · segment 2 of 6 · clean · 29 September 2026
---
This is as high as the walk gets, and the ground under your feet is about the only ground on it that nobody put here.

The path you're on runs along the top of the Common's slope. Look down and to your left, across the grass toward the far corner of the park, and you can see the fall of the land, a long green tilt toward Charles Street. That slope is Beacon Hill itself, its southern face, grazed for two centuries and never leveled. Everything you'll walk on after the bottom of it was carried in.

Across Beacon Street is the State House, Charles Bulfinch's brick building of 1798 under its gilded dome. It sits on a ledge the hill still has. Behind it, the ledge used to keep climbing: the summit stood a short way north of the dome, and in the early 1800s it was cut away and carted downhill into a pond on the far side of the neighborhood. The State House can tell you that story on its own. For this walk, keep one piece of it. The earth moved a few hundred yards. It went north, downhill, and stopped. That is about the shortest trip any ground in Boston took, and it's the first measure on this route.

*[beat]*

Watch the dome as you go. It stays visible over the trees for most of Beacon Street, and it climbs higher above you as you walk, because you're going down and it isn't.

From here, walk west along Beacon Street with the Common on your left. It's about a third of a mile, downhill the whole way, to Charles Street. Stop at the corner on the Common side and look straight ahead along Beacon Street, past the crossing.
