# BOSTON · W3 — Under the Artery
City Hall Plaza · walk · segment 2 of 6 · clean · 29 September 2026
---
The walkway that slopes across this plaza follows a street that no sign shows.

You're on Congress Street at the plaza's edge. If construction fencing is up along this side, the sidewalk is enough. Ahead and to your right, a long paved ramp climbs gently from Congress Street up toward Cambridge Street on the far side. The city opened it in 2022 as part of a redesign of the plaza by the landscape firm Sasaki, and it gave the ramp a name: the Hanover Promenade.

The name is a trace. Hanover Street ran through here. Its downtown end came off Court Street, where the plaza now meets Cambridge Street, and it dropped down through Scollay Square toward the water, a neighborhood of small streets, theaters and cheap hotels that the city had decided by the nineteen fifties to remove rather than repair. In the early nineteen sixties it came down, Hanover Street's downtown blocks with it, and the ground was laid out again as Government Center, with City Hall finished in 1968. In the same years the city cleared the West End, a short walk north, for new housing.

People still argue about the building beside you, and whether it reads as generous or as hostile has depended for half a century on who is looking. This walk leaves that argument where it found it.

*[beat]*

What the walk cares about is the street. Boston took Hanover Street's downtown end away and never put it back. Sixty years on, it laid a walkway across the open ground on roughly the old line and gave it the old name. The street is gone. Its direction survived as a ramp.

A few minutes from here the same street was cut a second time, by a different project, and that cut came out differently. Walk to the north end of the plaza, cross Congress Street onto Hanover Street, the street itself, and turn right into Blackstone Street, the market block.
