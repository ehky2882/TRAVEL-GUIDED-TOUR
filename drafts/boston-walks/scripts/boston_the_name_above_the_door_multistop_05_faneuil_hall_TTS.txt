# BOSTON · W5 — The Name Above the Door
Faneuil Hall · walk · segment 6 of 6 · TTS-safe · 29 September 2026
---
The name on this building is the one thing about it Boston hasn't finished deciding.

You're at the east front of fan yul Hall, facing the central doors, the market at your back. The hall was a gift to the town in seventeen forty two. Peter fan yul, who paid for it, was a merchant whose business included the buying and selling of enslaved people, and the town put his name on his gift. Upstairs is the room that earned the building its other name: the meetings against British taxes were held there before the Revolution, and the hall has been called the Cradle of Liberty ever since. Both names have been on the building a long time. One is now in question.

Whether fan yul's name should stay is a question Boston has been asking in the open since twenty eighteen. In twenty twenty three the City Council asked for the hall to be given a new name. The commission with the power to change it has not taken that step, and City Hall has not pressed for one. There are people in this city who want the name gone and people who want it kept, and the name is still on the building.

Go back to the beginning of the walk. In eighteen oh five, in the room above these doors, twenty one Black Bostonians met and decided to build a hall of their own, and you stood under their door an hour ago. Frederick dug lus, who had escaped slavery, spoke upstairs here in eighteen forty nine and up the hill at the meeting house, and stood under both names.

This is what the walk was for. On Smith Court a congregation chose a name and cut it into the stone, and it has stayed two centuries because the people who put it there meant it. Here a town chose a name, and it has stayed longer, and the town is still deciding what it meant. Between the two doors Boston has been putting names on buildings and taking them off for three hundred years, and it hasn't stopped. Both stand.
