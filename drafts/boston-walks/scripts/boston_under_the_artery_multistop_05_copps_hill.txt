# BOSTON · W3 — Under the Artery
Copp's Hill Burying Ground · walk · segment 6 of 6 · clean · 29 September 2026
---
From up here the whole walk is below you.

You're inside Copp's Hill Burying Ground, at the far corner above Snowhill and Hull Streets, with slate headstones behind you and the water opening ahead toward Charlestown. The burying ground was opened in 1659 and holds the neighborhood that lived around it, among them Black Bostonians, free and enslaved, most of them without markers. Behind you, over the rooftops, is the steeple of Old North; the one you see was put up long after 1775.

Look across the water to the left. A pair of white towers carries a wide bridge over the mouth of the Charles, its cables fanning out like rigging. It opened in 2003, and it is the one piece of the highway project you have been walking above that was built up instead of down. The traffic that runs under the Greenway comes out of its tunnel just north of here, crosses that bridge in the open, and goes into Charlestown. The road you've been following the ghost of is visible from here, once, for the length of a river crossing.

*[beat]*

The water in front of you was the harbor's inner edge when the Massachusett fished it, and the Charlestown shore across from you is where this walk's story continues on another day.

Turn and look back over the neighborhood. From here the North End reads as what it is: a hill of brick with one street running down its spine, and a green edge where the wall of steel used to be. The plaza is out of sight beyond it. The market is under the trees at the bottom. Boston built a highway along this neighborhood and a plaza against it, and then, in its own time, took the highway down and gave the street its name back.

The walk ends here. Hull Street runs back down to Salem Street and Old North, and Hanover is a block beyond it; it will take you all the way to the Greenway without a break.
