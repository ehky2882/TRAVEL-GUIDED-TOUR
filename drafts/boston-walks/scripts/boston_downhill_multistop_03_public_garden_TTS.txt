# BOSTON · W1 — Downhill
03 · The Public Garden, where the grade ends · walk · segment 4 of 6 · TTS-safe · 29 September 2026

From here on, nothing goes up.

You're in the Public Garden near the equestrian statue of George Washington, facing west toward the Arlington Street gate. Turn once and check. The Common climbed behind you, Beacon Street sloped, and this garden is flat as a floor. That isn't landscaping. It's the level the ground was brought to.

The Garden is the seam. Charles Street behind you was the old shore, and the state's great project of burying the bay began in eighteen fifty eight at Arlington Street in front of you. This stretch in between was done earlier and more slowly, in pieces, and for years it was a ragged, half finished place the city argued over. There were plans for houses on it. The city voted instead to keep it open, and through the eighteen sixties the paths and the lagoon and the flowerbeds went in. Which is how the level ground nearest the hill became a park, and the streets and houses began one block farther on.

Washington faces the gate for a reason. When the statue was set here, the avenue beyond Arlington Street was being built, and he was placed to look straight down the middle of it, at a street that didn't yet reach very far.

Go out through the Arlington Street gate and cross Arlington. The wide strip of grass and elms running down the middle of the avenue ahead is the Commonwealth Avenue Mall. Walk onto it and stop at the first statue you reach, a stone figure in a heavy cloak. That's the next stop.
