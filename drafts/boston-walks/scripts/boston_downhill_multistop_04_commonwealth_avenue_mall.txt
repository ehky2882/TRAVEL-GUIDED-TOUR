# BOSTON · W1 — Downhill
04 · Commonwealth Avenue Mall, read as a clock · walk · segment 5 of 6 · clean · 29 September 2026
---
You can read this street like a clock, and it runs forward as you walk.

You're on the Commonwealth Avenue Mall, the strip of grass and elms down the middle of the avenue, beside a granite statue of Alexander Hamilton in a cloak. Look west along the Mall. It runs straight for well over a mile, and the houses along both sides look of a piece: brick and brownstone, bay windows, iron rails. They aren't the same age. The ones behind you, nearest Arlington Street, went up around 1860. Each block west of here is a few years younger than the last, and by Massachusetts Avenue you'd be into the 1880s and beyond.

That's because the ground arrived that way. The bay behind the dam was buried a street at a time, from this end toward the west, and the houses followed the new edge as it moved. For thirty years and more there was a line somewhere along this avenue where paved street stopped and raw fill began, and every year it was a little farther off. People bought lots on paper and waited for the earth to reach them.

*[beat]*

Hamilton marks one year on the clock. He was put up in 1865, a gift from a Boston merchant, carved from a single block of granite, and he faces east, toward the Garden and the old town. In 1865 that was the only direction with anything finished in it. The avenue ran a few blocks behind his back and then gave out into open ground and water. Everything behind him arrived afterward, and he has never turned around to look at it.

Keep walking west along the Mall, past two cross streets, to Dartmouth Street. Turn left onto Dartmouth and walk two blocks. The square that opens on your left, with a brown stone church on its far side and a long granite building beside you, is Copley Square, the end of the walk.
