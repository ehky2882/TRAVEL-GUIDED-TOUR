# BOSTON · W4 — The Other Bank
The Charlestown Bridge · walk · segment 5 of 5 · clean · 29 September 2026
---
You've reached the spot the lanterns were meant for.

You're at the middle of the Charlestown Bridge, on the harbor side, with the North End across the water in front of you and the white towers and cables of the Zakim Bridge rising just upstream on your right. Ahead, above the brick of the North End, is a single white steeple. It belongs to Old North Church. The steeple you see was rebuilt in 1954 after a hurricane; the one that held the lanterns in April 1775 is long gone, and so is the one that replaced it.

This walk began with Boston's stories as they were aimed from the far bank. Here is the plainest case. On the night of April eighteenth, two lanterns were hung in that tower for a few minutes, and the people who needed to see them were on this side, in Charlestown, waiting to carry the news inland. You're roughly where the message was addressed. Every version of the night that starts in the steeple has the story backward. It started here, with people watching for a light.

*[beat]*

The Zakim beside you belongs to the same family. Its two towers were shaped to echo the obelisk on the hill behind you, so the highway leaving its tunnel arrives under a pair of monuments. The water under all of it is the mouth of the Charles, where the Massachusett fished and crossed long before either town, and it has been the space between the two ever since: a river one town looked across, and the other looked back.

The walk ends here. The bridge carries you the rest of the way into the North End, and Hanover Street is a few minutes past the far end.
