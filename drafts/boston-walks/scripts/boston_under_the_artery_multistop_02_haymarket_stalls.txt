# BOSTON · W3 — Under the Artery
The Haymarket Stalls · walk · segment 3 of 6 · clean · 29 September 2026
---
The market you're walking into is older than both of the things that were built on either side of it.

You're on Blackstone Street, a short curving block off Hanover. The plaza is behind you; the Greenway is a block over, beyond the stalls. On Fridays and Saturdays the block fills with produce stalls under blue tents, and on the other five days it's an ordinary street with a few crates left over. This is Haymarket. Selling has gone on in the open around these streets since the eighteen thirties, and the association of vendors that runs it dates itself to 1820.

Look at where it sits. One side of you was cleared in the nineteen sixties for Government Center. The other side was cleared in the nineteen fifties for a highway. The market was in between, and it was never in the plan for either. In 1953 an elevated expressway went up beyond the far end of this block, close enough to shade the stalls, and it was there until 2004. A state law in 1952 and a city ordinance in 1978 reserved Blackstone Street for peddlers, and the vendors held it through the highway's whole life.

*[beat]*

The two clearances you're walking between were each meant to fix downtown. Neither touched the market. On a Friday you're looking at the one piece of this walk's ground that has done the same job, in the same place, the entire time.

Walk back out to Hanover Street, turn right, and follow it to the Greenway.
