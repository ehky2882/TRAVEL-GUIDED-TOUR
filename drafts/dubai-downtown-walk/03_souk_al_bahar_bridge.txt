ATLAS — DUBAI
Walk W3: The Downtown Loop
Segment 03 — The Souk Al Bahar Bridge (finale)
(clean version)

---

Walk up onto the bridge and stop wherever the rail is free.

The advantage this spot has over the promenade you just left is roughly the height of a short staircase. That's all it is. And it's enough to rearrange everything: you're above the crowd rather than inside it, the lake reads wider from up here, the tower runs uninterrupted from spire to waterline, and the entire geometry of the district lines itself up in front of you at once. Look along the water to your left and you can pick out the green edge of Burj Park, where this walk started. The whole loop, in one frame.

The low buildings at the far end are Souk Al Bahar — market of the sailor, in Arabic — an arcade of stone corridors, arches and lanterns that opened in 2008, on an island, in an artificial lake, nowhere near the sea.

Now the part worth noticing about where you're standing. This is the best free position in Downtown Dubai, and nobody designed it as a position at all. It's a crossing. Its job is to move people from a mall to a souk without sending them the long way round the water. Every deliberate viewpoint in this district — the terraces, the decks, the reserved tables, the ticketed floors — was planned, priced and sold. The best of them turned out to be a piece of pedestrian infrastructure that came free with the plumbing.

[beat]

Which is a fitting place to end a walk through a room built around one object. The tower will run its light show again before long, the water will assemble another audience below you, and there'll be a steady drift of people crossing behind your shoulders on their way to dinner. You don't have to move for any of it. This bridge was built to be walked over. Standing still on it is the small, free, entirely permitted luxury at the end of the loop.
