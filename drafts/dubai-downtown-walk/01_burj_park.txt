ATLAS — DUBAI
Walk W3: The Downtown Loop
Segment 01 — Burj Park
(clean version)

---

Find a spot near the middle of the grass and turn around to face the tower.

Distance is the whole point of this stop. At the base, the Burj Khalifa is unreadable — too close, too steep, a wall of glass leaving the ground faster than your eye can follow it. Out here, with a few hundred metres of lawn and water in between, the building finally holds still long enough to show you its shape.

And the shape rewards a proper look. Three wings meet at a central spine, and the tower steps inward as it climbs — wing by wing, setback by setback, spiralling upward until there's nothing left at the top but the spire. The plan came from a desert flower, the spider lily, petals arranged around a centre. Which sounds like something invented afterwards for a brochure, except that the geometry is doing work: each setback breaks up the wind before it can settle into a rhythm and start pushing the building around. The flower is also a wind strategy.

[beat]

Which is what a lawn is doing at the foot of the tallest building on earth. A park here isn't decoration; it's viewing distance, landscaped — an island of grass at roughly the remove a person needs in order to take in eight hundred and twenty-eight metres in a single glance, and then handed over for nothing. The good positions in this district usually cost something. This one is grass and a footbridge.

Stay as long as you like. It's the only stop on this walk where sitting down is an option, and the tower is better company from a seated position than most buildings are from any position at all.

When you're ready, cross back over the footbridge and follow the water's edge towards the mall, until the lake opens out wide in front of you. That's the Dubai Fountain.
