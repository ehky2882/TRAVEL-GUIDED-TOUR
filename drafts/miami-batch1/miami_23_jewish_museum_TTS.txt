# MIAMI 23 — Jewish Museum of Florida, the 1936 Beth Jacob building
Tier 2 · B3 · docket 18 · TTS-safe · 24 September 2026

---

The location of this synagogue is part of its history, not an accident of real estate. It stands on Washington Avenue a few blocks south of Fifth Street, in the corner of Miami Beach where the island's first Jewish residents were permitted to live.

You are outside three oh one Washington Avenue, the home of the Jewish Museum of Florida, run by Florida International University. There are two buildings here, joined together: a plain stucco synagogue from nineteen twenty nine, the first built on Miami Beach, and beside it the larger nineteen thirty six sanctuary with its copper dome, Art Deco details, and eighty stained glass windows.

The congregation, beth jay kub, was founded in nineteen twenty seven, and within a few years it had outgrown its first home. The new sanctuary was designed by one of its own members, Henry hoh how zer, in what was likely his first completed building, and he went on to design many of the hotels whose rounded corners you may already have seen along Ocean Drive and Collins Avenue.

By the nineteen thirties, exclusion was written into the Beach itself. Deeds across much of the island barred sale to Jews and to Black buyers, which is why the congregation built south of Fifth; hotels advertised restricted or gentile clientele in plain words, Black guests were shut out entirely, and the practice held into the late nineteen forties, when first the city and then the state outlawed it.

The building served its congregation until nineteen eighty six and then stood empty, slated at one point for demolition to make room for apartments, until a traveling exhibition on the history of Florida's Jews found it and made it a permanent home, opening as a museum in nineteen ninety five. So the synagogue built where Jews were allowed to be now holds the record of everywhere they went afterward, across the whole state and over more than two centuries.

Spend a moment with the facade and the dome. They were paid for, prayed in and saved by a community that was once told exactly where on this island it could stand, and they are still standing in that same place.
