Everything you're standing on arrived through a pipe. In eighteen ninety-six the shoreline of bis kayn Bay ran roughly where bis kayn Boulevard runs now, and the park under your feet was open water until the mid-nineteen twenties, when the city dredged the bay bottom, pumped it ashore and let it settle into thirty-two acres of ground. The park opened in March of nineteen twenty-five. It is a made thing.

You should be inside Bayfront Park on the main walkway, with bis kayn Boulevard behind you and the bay ahead. Off to your left as you face the water, up near the boulevard at the park's northwest corner, a bronze torch stands on a tall column; straight ahead, the walkway runs down to a big round fountain; beyond it, a steel spiral climbs about a hundred feet into the air.

Walk toward the water. The torch first: the Torch of Friendship, put up in nineteen sixty as a gesture toward Latin America and rededicated in nineteen sixty-four to john eff kennedy. The flame is gas; whether it's lit when you pass varies.

The fountain and the walkway under you are the work of ee sah moo noh goo chee, the Japanese American sculptor, who took on the redesign in nineteen eighty, when the park was worn out and half hidden behind a public library that had been built on it in the nineteen fifties. His first move was to talk the city into tearing the library down so the ground could see the water again. He worked on this park until his death in nineteen eighty-eight, and his longtime collaborator finished it in nineteen ninety-six.

The steel spiral is noh goo chee's too, and it is a memorial to the crew of the space shuttle Challenger, dedicated in nineteen eighty-eight on the second anniversary of the loss. Two ribbons of steel twist upward and never quite meet. Give it the quiet it asks for.

So the sequence runs: bay bottom, then ground, then a garden, then a library on the garden, then a sculptor arguing for the water back. This land has been argued over since the day it was made, and every one of those arguments began with a decision that there should be land here at all.

Before you leave, walk to the edge where the seawall meets the bay and look along it. That is the one line in this park that nature never drew. Somebody chose exactly where it goes.
