This is maw rees ay fair ay Park. Until the nineteen sixties it was the port.

You should be on the park's main lawn, east of bis kayn Boulevard, with the bay ahead of you. To your left as you face the water, a wide, low building sits up on a deck under a deep flat canopy, with columns of hanging plants dropping from its edge: that's the art museum. Beside it, a taller building with a big pale cone rising through the middle: the science museum. To your right, an inlet cuts in from the bay, and beyond it the arena.

The ships came in roughly where the art museum stands. From the early nineteen hundreds this was the working waterfront, freight and passenger docks on land that had been dredged up out of the bay for that purpose, and the ships stayed until the port moved in the nineteen sixties to dodge island, out in the bay to your right, also made ground. The old docks were cleared, and in nineteen seventy-six the site opened as Bicentennial Park. A Grand Prix ran through it for a few years; then it sat fenced and mostly closed; and it reopened in twenty fourteen, remade around the museums, and took its present name in twenty nineteen from a former mayor.

The art museum opened in twenty thirteen, by the Swiss firm hair tsog and duh muh rohn, and the building is mostly porch: the galleries are lifted onto a platform, the roof spreads far past the walls, and the plant columns hang in the gap so the whole thing reads as a shaded deck you can walk under without a ticket, and should. The science museum followed in twenty seventeen, and inside the cone is an aquarium you look down into from the top and up into from below.

So count the ground here. The bay was filled to make a port. The port moved to a second piece of filled bay. The first piece became a park, then a fenced lot, then a park again, and the museums that landed on it were designed to sit up off it. Every square foot east of the road is a decision somebody made, then re-made, then built a museum on.

Walk to the seawall and look across at the island the port went to. Two pieces of manufactured land, facing each other across the water they were both taken from. The view is the history.
