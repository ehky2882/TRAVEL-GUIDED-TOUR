# MIAMI 24 — Fontainebleau, Collins Avenue exterior
Tier 2 · B3 · pays S3 · legend slot 2 · TTS-safe · 24 September 2026

---

The story Morris lap ih dus liked to tell about this hotel begins on a staircase. Guests dressed for dinner, he said, would ride up one floor just so they could sweep back down a curving stair into the lobby in full view of everyone waiting below, and the stair came to be called the staircase to nowhere. It is a wonderful story and mostly his own; the stair did lead somewhere, to a mezzanine with a coat check and a powder room, and whether anyone climbed it only to descend is the part nobody can check.

You are on Collins Avenue at the fon tin bloh, which opened in nineteen fifty four on the site of the old Firestone estate, a long slab bent into a gentle curve. Everything for this stop is visible from the public sidewalk.

Before the developer Ben noh vak handed him this commission, lap ih dus had spent most of his career designing stores and hotel interiors, and he treated the job as a stage: curves instead of corners, a lobby like a ballroom, and an openly theatrical idea of what a vacation should feel like. The public loved it, and the architecture critics treated it as an embarrassment, a vulgar confection by a man who had never learned restraint; he answered them decades later by titling his memoir Too Much Is Never Enough.

Nothing about it is small. When lap ih dus designed the ee den rok next door for noh vak's estranged former partner, the fon tin bloh responded at the end of the nineteen fifties with a tall, nearly blank tower on its northern edge that threw the neighbor's pool into afternoon shade, and Florida's courts ruled that no property owner has a right to the sunlight falling across a boundary.

For decades this style had no respectable name. Preservationists eventually called it Miami Modern, or my moh, and in two thousand eight the hotel critics once despised was listed on the National Register of Historic Places, which makes it the second generation of buildings this city was told were not worth keeping and then decided to save. The small stucco hotels on Ocean Drive were dismissed as too cheap; this one was dismissed as too much, and the same stubborn argument rescued both.

Enjoy the curve without apology on your way out, since that is exactly what its architect always intended.
