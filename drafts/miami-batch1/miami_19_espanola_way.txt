# MIAMI 19 — Española Way
Tier 2 · B3 · clean · 24 September 2026

---

This street is pretending, and it has been pretending since 1925: the arcades, the barrel-tile roofs, the iron balconies and the stucco washed in the colors of a Mediterranean afternoon were all built new, at the height of the Florida land boom, to make a short strip of Miami Beach feel like a village that had been standing for centuries.

You are on Española Way, the pedestrian lane that runs west from Washington Avenue, with its striped awnings, its café tables, and strings of lights crossing overhead between the palms. It is barely two blocks long, and nothing on it rises more than three stories.

A first colony of Spanish-style buildings went up here in 1922, and in 1925 the developer N.B.T. Roney bought in with a larger idea. He wanted an artists' quarter, a place like Greenwich Village or the Left Bank where painters and writers would gather, and his architect, Robert A. Taylor, drew on real towns for the look: San Sebastián and Fuenterrabía on the Basque coast of Spain, Biarritz just over the French border, and Cannes on the Riviera. The result borrows from two countries and belongs to neither, and the artists never arrived in the numbers Roney imagined, so the shops that opened sold antiques, books and tea instead.

None of it was ever Spanish, strictly speaking, and after a hundred years that turns out not to matter. The buildings have now stood long enough to become history in their own right, and in 1986 the city designated the street a historic district, protecting a 1920s imitation of old Europe as a genuine piece of old Miami Beach.

*[beat]*

What happened here is a slow conversion. A developer built a stage set to sell an atmosphere, the atmosphere took, and the set aged into the real thing; the plaster cracked and was patched, the tiles weathered, generations of residents and visitors laid down their own memories, and the fake-old became simply old. Most cities eventually lose their make-believe, but this one kept it long enough for it to turn authentic.

Sit for a few minutes at one of the tables if you can, especially toward evening when the lights come on, and enjoy the street for what it has become: an honest place built on a charming pretense.
