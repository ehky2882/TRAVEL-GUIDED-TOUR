# MIAMI 10 — Coconut Grove, The Barnacle and Peacock Park, from Main Highway
T1 · B5 · plants S4 · clean · 24 Sept 2026

---

Stand under the trees along Main Highway for a moment and the temperature drops before anything else changes: the canopy over this stretch closes almost completely, and the air beneath it is cooler and stiller than the open road behind you. You're on the edge of what remains of a tropical hardwood hammock, with the Barnacle's gate on the bay side and Peacock Park opening toward the water just north.

The Barnacle is a pioneer house, finished in 1891 for Ralph Munroe, a yacht designer from Staten Island, and the state park that keeps it describes it as the oldest house in the county still standing where it was built. Neighbors raised it for him out of timbers salvaged from shipwrecks and set it up off the ground for the breeze, and in 1908, when the family outgrew it, they jacked the whole house into the air and built a new ground floor underneath.

The inn's guest book tells half the story. In 1882 an English couple, Charles and Isabella Peacock, opened a hotel on the ground that is now Peacock Park, remembered as the first on this shore of the bay, and its guests, winter visitors from the North, left the letters and photographs the Grove's early history was written from. The people who cooked, gardened and cleaned for them came, most of them, from the Bahamas by way of Key West, bringing a working knowledge of tropical plants and building the newcomers lacked. Among the first was a woman from Eleuthera named Mariah Brown, whose house of 1890 still stands a few blocks west, on a street the settlers cleared for themselves and called Evangelist Street for its churches; it is Charles Avenue now, and the historical marker there calls it the first Black community on the South Florida mainland.

*[beat]*

Miami likes to date itself from 1896, a railroad and a vote. On this ground a working community was already close to a decade old by then, and it was Black and Bahamian, and it built a good part of what the pioneers' photographs show. That community is still here, west of Main Highway, underneath the Grove most visitors come to see.

Come back in the last hour of daylight, when the light comes flat off the bay and the hammock goes dark behind you, and the two halves of the Grove are easy to tell apart.
