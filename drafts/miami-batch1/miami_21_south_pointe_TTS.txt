# MIAMI 21 — South Pointe Park, Government Cut
Tier 2 · B3 · pays S2 · TTS-safe · 24 September 2026

---

If a cruise ship is sliding past the end of this park, it is closer than your eyes want to accept: close enough to count the balconies and moving through a channel that was dry ground when the twentieth century began.

You are at the southern tip of Miami Beach, in South Pointe Park, and if you face the water, the Atlantic opens to your left while downtown Miami stands across the bay to your right. The strip of water in front of you is Government Cut, and the name is a plain description, since this is the channel the federal government sliced through the island so that ships could come straight in from the ocean.

Until nineteen oh five there was no gap here at all. The barrier island kept running south as one long ribbon of beach and mangrove, and any vessel bound for the small port at the mouth of the Miami River had to swing all the way around Cape Florida, at the bottom of key bis kayn, before feeling its way north through the shallows. Congress authorized a straight path in nineteen oh two; the dredges opened it three years later and went on deepening it for years afterward.

The low green island across the channel, crowded with red tile roofs, was the southern end of the ground under your feet until the dredges came through. The cut severed it, and it has lived ever since as a separate place with its own name, Fisher Island, reached only by boat.

Most afternoons, somewhere between four and six, the departures begin, and every one of them comes this way. The ships are leaving one of the busiest cruise ports in the world, tied up on an island in the bay to your right, and all of them share exactly one road to the open sea: the line the government drew here in nineteen oh five.

People tend to describe Miami Beach by its ocean edge, the long east-facing sand that runs north from the pier. Its southern edge belongs to a different kind of history, because no tide or current put it here; an act of Congress did, and the place where the Beach stops and the shipping lane begins is, quite literally, a line on a federal survey. If you have the time, wait for one departure, and watch something the size of a city block pass through the gap that line became.
