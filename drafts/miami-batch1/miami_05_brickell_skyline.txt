MIAMI — 05 — Brickell Avenue, the skyline from the river bridge
Tier 1 · Batch B2 · pays S6 (1980s and 2008 facets) · docket 12 · Clean version
---

This is Brickell: a residential neighborhood dressed as a financial district, or a financial district that people happen to sleep in, and after fifty years nobody has settled which. The skyline in front of you is the argument, in glass.

You should be on the sidewalk of the Brickell Avenue bridge, on the downtown side of the river, facing south toward Brickell. The tall column with the bronze figures is at the south end of the bridge; beyond it, towers rise on both sides of the avenue. The river runs under you, and if a boat is coming, the bridge will lift and you'll wait.

Read it in layers. The oldest layer is invisible: in 1871 a couple from Ohio named Brickell ran a trading post on the south bank right about where the column stands, and for the first decades of the city this avenue was a row of big houses along the bay. Almost none of that is left.

The first layer you can actually see is from the 1970s and 1980s, when banks arrived. Money from across Latin America came looking for a safe place to sit, and not all of what arrived in those years was clean; the city has said so plainly and moved on. Look for the tower with a square hole punched through its middle and a palm tree growing in the hole, put there in 1982 by a young local firm. It ended up in a television show's opening credits, and for a while it was the shape people meant when they said Miami.

The second layer is the 2000s, when nearly every remaining lot got a tower, and the third is what happened next. In 2008 the bust left thousands of those units dark, and three or four years later they were full again, bought in cash from abroad. By 2016 a shopping city had opened in the middle of it, and in the 2020s the finance firms came in earnest, so the neighborhood grew into the costume it had been wearing since the eighties.

This skyline isn't a record of growth. It's a record of digestion: each layer built in a boom, emptied in a bust, and filled by whoever arrived next with money and a reason to leave somewhere else.

*[beat]*

The avenue has burned through four economies without ever once looking worried. Watch the bridge lift for a boat, and count how many of these towers had a bad decade you would never guess from here.
