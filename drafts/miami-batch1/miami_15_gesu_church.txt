MIAMI — 15 — Gesu Church
Tier 2 · Batch B1 · exterior only · Clean version
---

A salmon-pink church on a corner where nearly everything else is glass and parking, its two towers holding a big arched window between them. You could pass it as a period piece; it isn't one. Mass is still said here, on the same ground the parish has held since before there was a city to hold it in.

You should be across Northeast Second Street from the church's front doors, with the towers rising ahead of you and Northeast First Avenue running along the building's side. Stay on the sidewalk; the exterior is the stop.

Find the letters. In the ironwork of the gates and grilles, three letters repeat: I, H, S. They are the emblem of the Jesuits, the Society of Jesus, who have run this parish from the beginning, and the name says the same thing twice over. The word Gesu is Italian for Jesus, the short form of the parish's full name, the Holy Name of Jesus, and it is borrowed from the Jesuits' mother church in Rome.

The parish is older than the city. It was organized in the spring of 1896, some months before the incorporation vote, on a lot given by Henry Flagler, who was not Catholic. A Presbyterian handing a Catholic congregation a downtown lot is a fair picture of how a boomtown thought about churches: you needed them, all of them, quickly. The first church was wood and cost a few thousand dollars. By the early 1920s the congregation had outgrown it, and this building rose on the same ground: cornerstone in December 1920, dedication in 1925, eight hundred seats, marble altars from Italy, and windows of colored leaded glass made in Germany.

Here is what makes it more than a survivor. Almost every building of its generation on these blocks has changed jobs. The courthouse emptied; the newspaper tower became a museum; a bank became a hall full of jewelers; hotels became apartments. This one has never done anything but what it was built to do, at the same address, for one hundred and thirty years, and it did it through every boom and every bust without once pausing, which no bank on Flagler Street can say.

*[beat]*

You don't need to go in, and if a service is underway you shouldn't. Stand here and let it be the one fixed thing in the frame while everything around it keeps changing hands.
