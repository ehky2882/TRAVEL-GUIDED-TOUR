Don't look for it. You won't find it, and that is the whole point of this stop: the most important thing here is under the grass, on purpose, and will stay there.

You should be in the small park at the tip of brik ul Point, on the south bank where the Miami River meets bis kayn Bay. The river is on one side of you and the bay opens ahead; a low grassy rise sits near the middle of the park, with metal interpretive markers beside it; upriver, the brik ul Avenue bridge crosses to downtown, and on its far side a tall stone column carries bronze figures of a family. Dog walkers use it, which is fine and a little strange.

Underneath, then. In nineteen ninety-eight a pair of nineteen fifties apartment buildings had just come down for two new towers, and a routine salvage dig went in first. What it found, cut into the limestone bedrock about a foot down, was a circle thirty-eight feet across: twenty-four basins and hundreds of smaller holes, the footprint of a building whose posts had been set into the rock. The radiocarbon dates put it around two thousand years old. The people were the tuh kes tuh, or their ancestors, and the village that stood on both banks of this river mouth was still here when the Spanish arrived in the fifteen sixties.

The developer wanted to build; the public argued otherwise, loudly, and the state bought the land. In two thousand nine the site was named a National Historic Landmark, and in twenty eleven it opened as this park, with the circle reburied beneath the rise for its own protection and the markers set beside it. What you are looking at is the ground over it, and nothing more.

Then it happened again. Across the river, on the lot beside the bridge, demolition for another set of towers began in twenty twenty-one, and the excavation underneath turned up more than a million objects from the same village. The city designated part of that site in twenty twenty-three, the towers are going up on the rest, and the argument about what the finished buildings owe the ground is still running.

Every deep hole dug at this river mouth in the last thirty years has hit the same town. The oldest address in the city is among its most expensive, and the city keeps finding out, one foundation at a time, exactly what it built on.
