# MIAMI 12 — Wynwood Walls, the streetscape from NW 2nd Avenue
T1 · B6b · contested-change block · docket 15 · clean · 24 Sept 2026

---

Listen to the sidewalk before you look at the walls: the languages passing you are from everywhere, and the one you'll hear least is the Spanish this neighborhood spoke when people here called it El Barrio.

You're on Northwest Second Avenue in Wynwood, a district of low, windowless warehouses that spent decades as a garment and industrial quarter with a Puerto Rican neighborhood beside it. In 2009 a developer named Tony Goldman, who had worked on SoHo and South Beach, bought six of the warehouses on this block and had their blank exterior walls painted by invited street artists, with the curator Jeffrey Deitch assembling the first group; he called the result Wynwood Walls and meant it, in his own words, as a town center for a neighborhood that had never had one.

None of what you're looking at is meant to last. The walls are repainted on a cycle, most heavily each December when the art fairs come to town, and a mural that draws crowds one winter is primed over the next, so the district is less a collection than a schedule, and the only permanent thing on the block is the habit of change.

Two accounts of what happened here are held with equal conviction. The company that built the Walls describes them as the engine of a revival: art brought people to a district nobody visited, and restaurants, galleries and apartments followed. Longtime residents and preservation advocates describe the same sequence from the other end, with the art as the advance guard of rents that the neighborhood's families and small businesses could no longer pay, a place made famous and made unaffordable in one motion.

*[beat]*

Both accounts are true to the people who hold them, and the walls will not settle the argument, since a painted surface can be read as a gift or as a signal depending on who is reading and where they live.

The Walls are a ticketed compound now, where they once were free, and the gate will tell you what it costs to walk in, but the streets around it are open, and the murals spill down the side streets for blocks in every direction.

Come back in a year and stand on this same corner, because the wall in front of you will be a different wall, and how you feel about that is the whole subject of this stop.
