# MIAMI 30 — Virginia Key Beach Park, the 1945 beach

---

Turn your back to the parking lot and look along the sand, and you won't find a single tower; downtown is a few miles off, yet from here the city might not exist, and the distance was part of the arrangement.

This is Historic Virginia Key Beach Park, on a barrier island between the mainland and key bis kayn, and from nineteen forty five until desegregation it was the county's designated beach for Black residents. It had a bathhouse, a dance pavilion, a carousel and a miniature train, and churches held services on the sand.

The idea of a Black beach here is older than this one. In nineteen eighteen dana ay dorsey, by most accounts the city's first Black millionaire, bought the small island just north of here for a Black resort; the next year he sold it to the developers of Miami Beach, and the island now carries its buyer's name.

Nobody handed this beach over. On May ninth, nineteen forty five, the day after the war in Europe ended, a group of Black Miamians led by the attorney lawson ee thomas walked into the surf at Baker's Haulover, a whites only county beach to the north, carrying bail money in case they were arrested. No one was taken in; a county commissioner agreed to meet Thomas instead, and within weeks the county announced this beach, which opened that August, reachable only by boat until the causeway of nineteen forty seven.

A concession meant to settle the matter became something else, as the people it was built for turned a segregated beach into a place they loved, while the demand behind it kept going, to a second wade in at Crandon Park in nineteen fifty nine and the gradual end of beach segregation in the county.

The city of Miami closed the park in nineteen eighty two, citing the cost of upkeep, and left it fenced for twenty six years. When private development was floated at the end of the nineteen nineties, residents marched against it, and a task force chaired by the former city commissioner em ath uh lee Range fought for its return; the park reached the National Register in two thousand two, reopened in two thousand eight, and broke ground for a museum on August twenty second, twenty twenty six.

Take your shoes off, walk down to where the sand turns wet, and stand there, because the right to do exactly that on this shore was won by people prepared to go to jail for it.
