MIAMI — 14 — Alfred I. duPont Building
Tier 2 · Batch B1 · carries the grid garnish · Clean version
---

Put your hand on the black granite that wraps the base of this building; on a hot afternoon it is the coolest thing on the block. Above it the wall turns to pale limestone and goes straight up for seventeen stories, windows in neat pairs, the panels between them tinted green, the top two floors stepping back like a stair.

You should be at the corner of East Flagler Street and Northeast Second Avenue, on the corner diagonally opposite the building, so the whole shaft is in view at once. The main doors face Flagler.

Follow it up. This is the Alfred I. duPont Building, begun in 1937 and finished in 1939, and the years matter more than the name. Downtown Miami built nothing tall between the courthouse in 1928 and this: the boom had died, the Depression had settled in, and a whole decade of skyline simply didn't happen. Then a Delaware fortune put a bank headquarters on the site of the old Halcyon Hotel, and the city read it as permission to begin again. The man himself had died in 1935; the money kept building without him.

The gift is inside, if the lobby is open when you pass: the elevator doors are brass, worked in relief with palm trees, flamingos and other Florida creatures, and the ceilings above the old banking floor are painted. Where the buildings around it reached for Spain and Italy, this one decorated itself with the place it was actually in, herons and Seminole patterns instead of Andalusian towers. It's a small shift in confidence and a real one; by 1939 Miami had decided it was a subject and not only a stage set.

While you're on this corner, a practical gift. In 1921 the city renumbered itself, and Flagler Street became the line dividing north from south, while Miami Avenue, two blocks west of where you're standing, became the line dividing east from west. Every address in the city, and across most of the county around it, counts outward from that crossing into one of four quadrants: northeast, northwest, southeast, southwest. Once you know that, you can't really get lost here.

*[beat]*

Look at the top once more, at the way it steps back. That crown was the first thing Miami built after it stopped being afraid.
