# Mumbai 17 · Colaba Causeway

*Vantage: on Colaba Causeway, outside Leopold Cafe, at the corner of Nowroji Furdonji Road* · 332 words · about 133 s

The street you're standing on used to be under water at high tide. That's not a figure of speech.

You're on Colaba Causeway, officially Shahid Bhagat Singh Road, outside Leopold Cafe — the one with the open front and the busy ceiling fans. The pavements on both sides are crowded with stalls: sunglasses, scarves, silver jewellery, books, and a great deal of bargaining.

Here's the story of the street. Colaba was once two separate islands at the bottom of Bombay, reachable on foot only at low tide; at high tide you went by boat, and overcrowded boats sometimes capsized. In eighteen thirty-eight the East India Company finished a causeway to join them to the rest of the city. It was widened twice in the eighteen sixties, and the road you're standing on is that causeway, long since swallowed by buildings on either side.

Have a look at Leopold. It was founded in eighteen seventy-one by Iranis — Zoroastrian immigrants from Persia — and named after the King of the Belgians. In its early years it was a shop of one kind or another — accounts differ as to whether it sold cooking oil or medicines — before becoming the cafe and bar it is now. In November two thousand and eight it was one of the first places attacked in the assault on the city, and the bullet holes in the walls were deliberately left in place. Look for them inside.

And here's the thing. The guide that sent you here notes there are no high-end shops on the Causeway. That's not quite a criticism. This is a street where everything is for sale, cheaply, to everybody, as it's been for a century and a half.

If you've time, walk south along the Causeway into Colaba proper. The stalls thin out, and in fifteen minutes or so you'll reach Sassoon Dock, the fish market where the city's catch has come ashore since eighteen seventy-five. Early morning is when it's at its loudest.
