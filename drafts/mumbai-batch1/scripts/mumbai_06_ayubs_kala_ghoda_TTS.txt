MUMBAI, 06, EYE YOOBS
---
A kebab roll here cost two rupees fifty when it started. That's not the interesting part.

You're on Forbes Street, which signs call vee bee gaan dee marg, in kaa laa go daa, a few doors from where Rhythm House, the city's most famous record shop, used to stand. Look for the eye yoobs sign and a grill counter working flat out. eye yoobs opens in the late afternoon and keeps going until well past one in the morning.

The family story is a short one. eye yoobs began in nineteen eighty five as a food cart, started by a man who had come to Bombay from Delhi, and run now by his son, aa jaaz eye yoob shake. In the early days it was meat only, mutton seek kebabs, mostly. A vegetarian kitchen was added in nineteen eighty nine, which, in this city, is how you double your customers overnight.

Have a look at the menu board. The roll is the thing: a puh raa taa wrapped round chicken tikka, resh mee kebab or seek kebab, with sliced onion, lime and two chutneys. Eaten standing up, or sitting in a taxi, or on the steps of an office building. Paneer and mushroom tikka on the vegetarian side, and, for the brave, the bay daa roti, a flatbread folded round egg and mince.

And here's the thing. The Fort is the city's grand district by day: high courts, banks, the old university. But at night it empties, and then it belongs to the people who feed it, office cleaners, students, party crowds heading home, taxi drivers. eye yoobs has fed all of them for forty years, and the prices have gone up roughly a hundredfold in that time without the queue noticing.

If you've time, walk round the corner to the kaa laa go daa junction and the black horse. After dark, with the galleries shut and the stone glowing, it's the quietest the district ever gets.
