# Mumbai 01 · ELCO Pani Puri, Hill Road

*Vantage: Hill Road, Bandra West, on the pavement outside the ELCO shopfront* · 346 words · about 138 s

The most famous name on this road belongs to a market most people have forgotten.

Hill Road, Bandra West. Look for the sign reading ELCO above a busy shopfront, with a counter at street level and a queue that never quite clears. Upstairs, a family restaurant. At the counter, a man filling small, hollow puris faster than you can count them.

Here's how it began. The Bhagnani family were Sindhi merchants, uprooted by Partition in nineteen forty-seven, who rebuilt their lives in Bombay. In nineteen seventy-five their son, Mohandas, set up a single hand cart outside the ELCO market, then one of the smartest places to shop in Bandra. His pani puri was good — properly good — and his customers started calling him the ELCO pani puri wala. The man at the cart outside the market. The name stuck so firmly that the business outlived the reputation of the market it was named after.

Now have a look at what they're actually serving. Pani puri is a thin, crisp shell, punctured with a thumb, filled with potato or chickpea, then dunked in tamarind water and a sharp, green, spiced water. You eat it in one go, or not at all. The family's own account puts the secret down to two unglamorous things: good Sindhi puris, and bottled water, at a time when nobody at a street cart bothered. One cart became two. Fifteen or twenty years on, they bought a shop on this spot, and later the floors above it.

And that, if you ask me, is the real story. Mumbai street food is usually talked about as something wild and anonymous. This is the opposite — one family, fifty years, one corner, and a reputation built almost entirely on not making people ill.

[beat]

If you're eating, stand at the counter and let them hand you each puri as it's made — that's how it's meant to go. And if you fancy a walk afterwards, Hill Road is one long market in both directions — clothes, shoes, fruit and phone cases, all the way along.
