MUMBAI, 32, BMC HEAD OFFICE
---
The city put its own town hall directly across the road from its greatest railway station, and gave both jobs to the same architect.

Stand on the station side of the junction, where daa daa bye now roe jee Road meets maa haa paa lee kaa marg, and look across at the building on the corner. Grey gold stone, rows of pointed arches, and a tall tower rising from the angle of the two streets, with a dome behind it. This is the headquarters of the bree hun mum bye Municipal Corporation, the body that runs the whole of Mumbai. Turn round, and the station behind you is chuttra puttee shiva jee terminus. Both are the work of Frederick William Stevens.

Here's how it came about. When the city wanted a home for its corporation, two designs were put forward: a Gothic one by Stevens, and an Indo sarra sennick one by Robert Fellowes chizzum, which borrowed from moo gul and Indian architecture. Stevens won, though if you look carefully you'll see he hedged his bets, the domes and some of the detailing are a nod to the other side. The Viceroy, Lord rippon, laid the foundation stone in December eighteen eighty four, and the building was finished in eighteen ninety three. The tower is about seventy seven metres high.

Now look up at the gable above the main entrance. There's a winged female figure there, standing over the city's coat of arms. She represents urbs preema in indees, the first city in India. Bombay wasn't shy about it, and this was the building where it said so in stone. At street level, by the entrance, there's a bronze of Sir feh roze shaa may taa, the lawyer often called the father of the city's municipal government.

What's worth knowing, standing here, is that this isn't a museum. A great many people work in it, and the water you drink in this city, the roads, the drains, are all argued over inside. For more than a century you could only admire it from out here.

That changed in twenty twenty one, when the corporation and the state tourism board started running heritage walks inside, usually at weekends, starting from Gate Two. If you can book one, do. Otherwise, walk down the side on maa haa paa lee kaa marg and look at the carving on the arches, among the foliage you'll find animals, including a monkey, if you look hard enough.
