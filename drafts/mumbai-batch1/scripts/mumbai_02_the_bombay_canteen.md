# Mumbai 02 · The Bombay Canteen

*Vantage: inside the Kamala Mills compound, Lower Parel, at the entrance to Process House* · 344 words · about 138 s

You're standing in an old cotton mill, about to walk into a bungalow that was built to look like it had always been here.

Kamala Mills, Lower Parel. The compound around you was once a working textile mill; now it's glass offices, bars and restaurants. Find Process House and the entrance marked The Bombay Canteen — patterned tiles, low stone walls, and through the door, high ceilings and stained glass.

Have a look at the room first. It's a recreation of an old Bombay bungalow — the sort of house with a deep verandah and a dining room nobody ever quite finished furnishing. When it was refreshed after its first seven years, the architect Shonan Purie Trehan kept the idea and sharpened it: traditional tiles, warm wood, terrazzo tabletops, and Art Deco lamps of the kind you'd find in a grand flat on Marine Drive.

Then the people. The Bombay Canteen opened in twenty fifteen, founded by the New York chef Floyd Cardoz with Sameer Seth, Yash Bhanage and the chef Thomas Zacharias. Cardoz, Bombay-born, had made his name in Manhattan cooking Indian flavours for American diners. Here the idea was turned round: regional Indian food, cooked with seasonal ingredients, for a city that thought it already knew its own cuisine. Cardoz died in twenty twenty, early in the pandemic. The restaurant carried on.

And here's the thing. A place like this could easily have been a pastiche of colonial nostalgia, all ceiling fans and wistfulness. Instead it treats Indian food without borders as the point — a Goan dish beside a Bengali one beside a street snack, taken as seriously as anything in the city. The bar, too: it has appeared on the list of Asia's fifty best.

If you're staying, ask what's on the seasonal menu and order from that rather than the regulars. And on your way out, walk the length of the compound. The street names and the scale of the plots are what's left of the mills — a reminder of what Lower Parel made before it made dinner.
