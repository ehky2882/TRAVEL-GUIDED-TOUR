# Mumbai 25 · Pali Village, Bandra

*Vantage: in the lanes of Pali Village, Bandra West, a short walk from Pali Naka* · 303 words · about 121 s

Pali Hill has film stars. Pali Naka has traffic. Both are named after the small village you're standing in.

You're in Pali Village, a small cluster of lanes in Bandra West, a few minutes from Linking Road and about six hundred and fifty metres from the sea. Around you: low cottages with tiled roofs and painted fronts, small shrines and crosses, narrow lanes, and, slotted in among them, cafes, bakeries and restaurants.

Bandra was once a scatter of villages — Catholic, fishing, farming — set among paddy fields, coconut groves and salt pans. Pali was one of them, established around the seventeen hundreds, and it's what's called a gaothan: a traditional village settlement, with its own rules and its own sense of who belongs. Pali Hill, now one of the most expensive addresses in the country, and the busy junction at Pali Naka both took their names from it.

Have a look at the houses as you walk. Some are old cottages with timber balconies and terracotta roofs. Others have been rebuilt, sometimes in bright colours. The residents are mainly Catholic and Muslim, and the village still has its own civic forum to speak for it, which tells you how seriously the people here take keeping it a village.

And here's the thing. Pali has become fashionable — spas, healing centres, smart little restaurants — partly because it looks like the Bandra people remember. That's a double-edged sword. The cafes bring life and money; they also bring rents that some of the families who made the village can't match.

[beat]

So be a considerate visitor: these are homes first. Then, if you fancy it, walk west to Carter Road and the sea wall, or north-west to Chimbai, the fishing village on the shore, which is one of the other old Bandra villages still standing.
