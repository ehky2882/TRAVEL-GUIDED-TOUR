MUMBAI, 26, CHUTTRA PUTTEE SHIVA JEE MAHA RAJ VAASTOO SUNGRA HAA LIE
---
The Prince of Wales laid the first stone of this museum. By the time it opened, it was being used as a military hospital.

You're inside the garden gate on muh haat maa gaan dee Road, in kaa laa go daa, looking along the path to a large domed building in grey and buff stone, the chuttra puttee shiva jee maha raj vaastoo sungra haa lie, which the guide that sent you here, and many older bom bay ites, still call the Prince of Wales Museum.

The foundation stone was laid in November nineteen oh five by the Prince of Wales, later King George the Fifth. The design came from an open competition, and was won by George wittet, the same Scottish architect who would go on to design the Gateway of India down the road. The building was finished in nineteen fifteen, but the First World War got there first: it served as a children's welfare centre and a military hospital before the museum finally opened in January nineteen twenty two.

Have a look at the stone. The grey is local basalt quarried at koor laa; the warm buff is trachyte from maa lud. Then the white dome, which wittet modelled on the domes of the deck un sultanates. Under it, a mix of moo gul, muh raa taa and Jain detail, the style called Indo sarra sennick, which tried to give imperial buildings an Indian face.

And here's the thing. In nineteen ninety eight the museum was renamed after shiva jee, the seventeenth century founder of the muh raa taa kingdom. A building begun to honour an English prince now carries the name of an Indian king. It hasn't changed a stone of it, which is perhaps the most Bombay thing about it.

Go in if you have an hour. The museum holds around fifty thousand objects, Indus Valley finds, sculpture from the elly fanta caves, miniature paintings, and a suit of armour said to have belonged to the emperor Akbar. And if you haven't yet, the black horse of kaa laa go daa is just outside the gate.
