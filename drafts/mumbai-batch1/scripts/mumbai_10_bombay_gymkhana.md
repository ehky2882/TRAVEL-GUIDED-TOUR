# Mumbai 10 · The Bombay Gymkhana

*Vantage: on Mahatma Gandhi Road at the southern end of Azad Maidan, facing the Gymkhana clubhouse* · 341 words · about 136 s

India's first home Test match was played on the grass in front of you. The bar behind it is members only.

You're on Mahatma Gandhi Road, at the triangular southern end of Azad Maidan, the great open ground of south Bombay, a short walk from the Victoria Terminus. The low, timbered clubhouse in front of you is the Bombay Gymkhana, and the cricket ground you're looking across is its own.

The club was founded in eighteen seventy-five, as a gentlemen's club for the British — and only the British. The clubhouse was designed by the English architect Claude Batley, with a broad verandah facing the ground, from which members could watch the game with a drink in hand. Have a look at the timbering and the deep roof: it's an English idea of a sports pavilion, adapted for a climate with a monsoon.

Now the gift detail. On the fifteenth of December, nineteen thirty-three, India played its first ever home Test match here, against England, under the captain C K Nayudu. A young batsman called Lala Amarnath scored a century in it — India's first Test hundred — and people who saw it talked about it for the rest of their lives. Within a few years the big matches had moved to the Brabourne Stadium, and this ground went back to club cricket.

Here's the thing about the bar. The guide that sent you here says it's usually full, and you need a member to take you in. That's quite true. It's still a private club, with private rules. But the club that kept Indians out at its founding is now entirely Indian, and the ground where the empire played its game became the place where India first played it back against England on home soil.

If you can't get in, walk out onto the maidan itself. On almost any day there are a dozen informal matches going at once, and you can stand at a boundary and watch the game the club made famous being played for nothing at all.
