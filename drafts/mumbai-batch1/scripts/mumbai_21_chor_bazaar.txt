MUMBAI — 21 — CHOR BAZAAR
Vantage: on Mutton Street, in the middle of the antique and furniture shops
---
Chor Bazaar means Thieves' Market. It almost certainly started out as something else.

You're on Mutton Street, in the old Muslim quarter near Bhendi Bazaar, in south Mumbai. Around you, shopfronts overflowing onto the road: brass lamps, gramophones, ships' clocks, carved doors, old film posters, chandeliers, chairs stacked six high. The lanes either side carry on in the same vein for blocks.

Here's the trouble with the name. The story most people here will tell you is that this was once Shor Bazaar — the noisy market — and that the British couldn't, or wouldn't, pronounce it, and turned shor into chor, the word for thief. Nobody has ever quite proved it. But it's the sort of explanation that sticks because it fits: the place is noisy, and over the years enough stolen goods passed through it that the new name earned itself.

Then there's the other story — that Queen Victoria lost a violin, or some other possessions, while visiting India, and that they turned up for sale here. It's a lovely tale. It also has a small flaw: Queen Victoria never visited India.

Have a look at what's actually on sale. A great deal of the furniture is genuinely old, rescued from bungalows and mansions as the city tore them down. A great deal of the rest is skilfully made to look old, in workshops round the corner. The Bollywood posters are a speciality — hand-painted ones from the old cinemas are the real prize. The skill is telling one from the other, and the shopkeepers are, it must be said, better at it than you are.

[beat]

The market has been here for something like a hundred and fifty years, through every redevelopment scheme the city has thrown at it. If you've time, walk east into Bhendi Bazaar for kebabs and sweets on the main road — and haggle here, politely and at length. Nobody in this street expects the first price to be the last.
