MUMBAI, 13, THE TAJ MUH HUL PALACE
---
The hotel in front of you had electricity before most of the city did. It ran its own power station to manage it.

You're on the waterfront at Apollo bunder, with the Gateway of India at your shoulder and the harbour behind you. Facing you, the old wing of the Taj muh hul Palace: grey stone with pale trim, rows of arched balconies, small domes at the corners, and a great red dome in the middle. To its right, your right, the taller tower block added in the nineteen seventies.

The Taj was built by jum shed jee Tata, the industrialist, and it opened on the sixteenth of December, nineteen oh three, before the Gateway existed. There's a popular story that Tata built it after being turned away from a whites only hotel; historians have their doubts, and the truth is probably more ordinary and more ambitious. He wanted Bombay to have a hotel as good as any in Europe, and he went to the exhibitions of Europe and America to equip it. He came back with lifts and American electric fans, and installed a power plant in the building to run them. The hotel likes to say it was the first in India with electricity, and it's at least true that very few buildings here had it.

Have a look at the architecture. The architects were two Indians, seeta raam kaan day rao vied ya and dee en meer zaa, and the result is a mixture, moorish, Florentine, raaj poot, that only really makes sense in Bombay. Inside, the Harbour Bar claims to be the city's first licensed bar.

On the twenty sixth of November, two thousand and eight, gunmen attacked the hotel. The siege lasted three days; at least thirty one people died here, guests and staff among them, and the great dome caught fire on live television. Parts of the hotel reopened within a month. The staff who stayed at their posts to protect guests are remembered inside, and that is the story people in this city tell about the building now.

If you'd like to see it properly, walk in. The lobby and the arcades of the old wing are open to anyone, and the sea lounge on the first floor looks straight out at the Gateway, the view Tata built the whole thing for.
