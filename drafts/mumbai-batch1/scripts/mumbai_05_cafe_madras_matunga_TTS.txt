MUMBAI, 05, CAFAY MADRAS
---
Breakfast here has been served by the same family since nineteen forty. The queue is part of the menu.

You're in mah toong gaa, near King's Circle, outside a small, plain vegetarian restaurant with cafay Madras painted above the door. Expect a crowd on the pavement, a waiter calling names, and a smell of ghee and coffee drifting out on the air.

The cafay was opened in nineteen forty by the kaa mutt family, and three generations on it's still theirs. It started as a hole in the wall serving breakfast, four items on the menu, by one account. It belongs to a very particular Bombay tradition: the oo doo pee restaurant, named after the temple town on the kar naa tuh kaa coast. Owners from the coast would send word home that there was work in the city, and young men would come up to wait tables, cook and make deliveries, with a bed and meals thrown in.

Have a look at what's on the tables. doe saaz, thin and crisp. id leez, soft rice cakes. oop maa, raa vaa dosa, and filter coffee served in a steel tumbler set in a little steel bowl, poured from one to the other to cool it. And watch for the small pots of white butter, made in house. Regulars spoon it onto everything. The family says simply that their recipes are their own: nothing is textbook.

Here's what I find rather lovely. mah toong gaa became the South Indian heart of Bombay as families from the south settled here in the twentieth century, and within a short walk of this door there are a dozen other places doing much the same thing. cafay Madras has survived eighty odd years of that competition largely by word of mouth.

Before you go, walk round King's Circle itself, with its garden in the middle. The streets around it are lined with other South Indian cafays and with shops selling coffee powder and spice mixes, so you can take a little of breakfast home.
