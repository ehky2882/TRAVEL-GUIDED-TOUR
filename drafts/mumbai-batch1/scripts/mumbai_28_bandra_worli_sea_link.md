# Mumbai 28 · Bandra–Worli Sea Link and the Coastal Road

*Vantage: on the ramparts of Bandra Fort, Land's End, Bandra, looking south across the bay at the Sea Link* · 343 words · about 137 s

From a Portuguese watchtower, you're looking at the newest way into the city. Both were built to control traffic.

You're at Land's End in Bandra, on the old stone ramparts of Bandra Fort, looking south across Mahim Bay. That great curve of bridge across the water, with its tall pylons and fans of cables, is the Bandra–Worli Sea Link. On the far shore, the towers of Worli.

The fort first. The Portuguese built it in sixteen forty, as a watchtower over the bay and the sea. Its real name is Castella de Aguada — the fort of the watering place — because there was a freshwater spring here where passing ships could fill their casks.

Now the bridge. It's five point six kilometres long, eight lanes wide, and opened in two thousand and nine. Have a look at the central sections: those are cable-stayed spans, hung from pylons around a hundred and twenty-six metres tall, with hundreds of steel stays fanning down to the deck. The rest is concrete viaduct on piers in the sea. Before it opened, the drive from Bandra to Worli could take the better part of an hour. Now it can take about ten minutes, for a toll.

At its southern end, the bridge now meets the Coastal Road, the first phase of which opened in twenty twenty-four. It runs along the shore to Marine Drive, partly on reclaimed land and partly through twin tunnels, about two kilometres long, under Malabar Hill and under the sea off Chowpatty Beach.

And here's the thing. Mumbai has always been a city of islands that refused to stay islands — the causeways, the reclamations, the railways. This is just the latest version. Whether it's progress or just more traffic is a fierce local argument, and you'll hear both sides.

[beat]

If you'd like to cross it, take a taxi over the Sea Link at dusk, when the cables are lit. Or stay here a while: the bay at sunset, from the Portuguese walls, is one of the best free views in the city.
