MUMBAI, 14, THE OBEROI, NAA REE MUN POINT
---
Every grand old hotel in this city faces the harbour. This one chose the open sea.

You're at naa ree mun Point, at the southern tip of Marine Drive, by the sea wall. With the water behind you, look at the tall, pale hotel with The Oberoi on it. Next to it stands its older sister, the trident, the two are joined, and for most of their lives they've been thought of as one place.

The neighbour came first. It opened in nineteen seventy three as the Oberoi Sheraton, later the Oberoi Towers, on land that had been reclaimed from the sea only a few years earlier. naa ree mun Point itself is a piece of engineering: the bay was filled in during the nineteen sixties and seventies to create the office district you're standing in. The Oberoi beside it, the smaller and smarter of the pair, followed, with two hundred and thirty seven rooms that look out over the Arabian Sea or across Back Bay to Malabar Hill.

Have a look at the view the rooms are built for. Turn round, if you like. The long curve of Marine Drive runs away to your right, the Art Deco blocks along it, and at the far end, the green bulk of Malabar Hill. At night that curve becomes the Queen's Necklace.

What's worth knowing, standing here, is what happened in this building in November two thousand and eight. Gunmen attacked both The Oberoi and the trident, and guests and staff died inside. The hotel closed for more than a year and was rebuilt, reopening in twenty ten. It's a strange thing, a luxury hotel as a memorial, but the people who work here have never treated it as anything else.

Before you go, head up to the oh bar terrace if it's evening, or simply walk north along the sea wall. Most of Marine Drive's Art Deco is within a twenty minute stroll, and the light on the buildings at sunset is the best way to see it.
