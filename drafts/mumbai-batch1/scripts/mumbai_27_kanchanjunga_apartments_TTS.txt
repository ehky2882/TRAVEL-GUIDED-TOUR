Here's a problem. In Bombay, the side of a building with the best view and the best breeze also gets the worst sun and the worst rain. This tower is the answer.

Look up at the white tower with large square holes cut out of its corners, great double height openings, with glimpses of plants and walls painted in bold colours inside. That's kun chun jung gaa Apartments, by Charles kor ree uh, one of India's great modern architects.

kor ree uh worked on it from nineteen seventy, and it was completed in nineteen eighty three. It holds just thirty two apartments, of three to six bedrooms. The tower is about twenty one metres square and eighty four metres high, exactly four times as tall as it is wide.

Now the gift detail. To catch sea breezes and the views of the Arabian Sea on one side and the harbour on the other, a building here needs to face east and west. But those are exactly the directions of the hot afternoon sun and the monsoon rain. The old Bombay bungalow solved this with a verandah wrapped round the main rooms. kor ree uh took that idea and stacked it: every flat has a double height garden terrace at the corner, shaded by the floor above, so you can sit outside in the monsoon and stay dry. The apartments interlock in plan and section, which is why the cut outs alternate up the facade. The solid end walls carry it all.

And here's the thing. kor ree uh was arguing that a tower in India didn't have to be a glass box copied from somewhere else. It could be built from the logic of the old houses of the city. Half a century later, most of the towers going up around it ignore that argument completely. Look around you, and you'll see what he was up against.
