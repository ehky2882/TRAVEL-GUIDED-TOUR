MUMBAI, 25, PAA LEE VILLAGE, BAAN DRAA
---
paa lee Hill has film stars. paa lee naa kaa has traffic. Both are named after the small village you're standing in.

You're in paa lee Village, a small cluster of lanes in baan draa West, a few minutes from Linking Road and about six hundred and fifty metres from the sea. Around you: low cottages with tiled roofs and painted fronts, small shrines and crosses, narrow lanes, and, slotted in among them, cafes, bakeries and restaurants.

baan draa was once a scatter of villages, Catholic, fishing, farming, set among paddy fields, coconut groves and salt pans. paa lee was one of them, established around the seventeen hundreds, and it's what's called a gao tun: a traditional village settlement, with its own rules and its own sense of who belongs. paa lee Hill, now one of the most expensive addresses in the country, and the busy junction at paa lee naa kaa both took their names from it.

Have a look at the houses as you walk. Some are old cottages with timber balconies and terracotta roofs. Others have been rebuilt, sometimes in bright colours. The residents are mainly Catholic and Muslim, and the village still has its own civic forum to speak for it, which tells you how seriously the people here take keeping it a village.

And here's the thing. paa lee has become fashionable, spas, healing centres, smart little restaurants, partly because it looks like the baan draa people remember. That's a double edged sword. The cafes bring life and money; they also bring rents that some of the families who made the village can't match.

So be a considerate visitor: these are homes first. Then, if you fancy it, walk west to Carter Road and the sea wall, or north west to chim bye, the fishing village on the shore, which is one of the other old baan draa villages still standing.
