MUMBAI, 29, DOCTOR BOW DAA JEE LAAD MUSEUM
---
The oldest museum in the city is also where its old statues came to retire.

You're in the gardens at bye kulla, called jee jaa maa taa ood yaan, standing in front of a pale classical building with a columned portico: the doctor bow daa jee laad Museum. Near you, on the lawn, a great stone elephant.

The museum began in eighteen fifty five as a collection of the decorative and industrial arts of the region. The cornerstone of this building was laid in eighteen sixty two, and it opened on the second of May, eighteen seventy two, as the Victoria and Albert Museum, Bombay. Its benefactors were the merchant princes of the city, David Sassoon, jum shed jee jee jee boy, jugger naat shunker sate. In nineteen seventy five it was renamed after doctor bow daa jee laad, a physician, scholar and philanthropist who did much to found it.

Have a look at the elephant first. It's a basalt sculpture from elly fanta Island in the harbour; the Portuguese named the island after it. It came apart when it was moved, and was pieced back together here early in the twentieth century. Then look for another familiar face: the original kaa laa go daa statue, the black horse and its royal rider, removed from the city centre in nineteen sixty five, now stands in these grounds.

Inside, if you go in, the hall is a Victorian jewel box, gilded ceilings, painted columns and patterned tiles. By the late twentieth century it was crumbling. A restoration that began in two thousand and three, carried out by the heritage trust in tack with private and municipal partners, brought it back, reopening in two thousand and eight. It won a you nesco award for conservation.

And that's the point of this place. It's not just a museum of Bombay's history. It's where the city put the bits of its past it didn't know what to do with, and then, rather to its credit, decided to look after them.

Before you leave, walk to the garden's entrance to see the David Sassoon clock tower, and then into the gardens behind the museum, which also hold the city's zoo, one of the largest green spaces in south Mumbai.
