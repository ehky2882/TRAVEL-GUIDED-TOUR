# Mumbai 09 · Slink & Bardot

*Vantage: in Worli Koliwada, outside Thadani House, opposite the Coast Guard station* · 333 words · about 133 s

To find one of the smartest bars in Mumbai, you have to walk into a fishing village. That's rather the point.

You're in Worli Koliwada — the Koli fishing village at the northern tip of Worli, on the road that runs opposite the Indian Coast Guard station. In front of you, an old colonial bungalow called Thadani House. Inside it is Slink & Bardot. Behind you, the lanes of the village run down to the boats.

Have a look at the bungalow first. It's been refurbished rather than replaced — which, in Worli, is the exception rather than the rule. Inside, it's a French bar and kitchen, with a speakeasy feel: low light, booths, lounge seating, a series of connected rooms, and music that starts unobtrusive and gets louder as the night goes on, with DJs at the weekend. The drinks are French classics and aperitifs — the French seventy-five is the one regulars point you to — and the food is small French plates.

Now look the other way, at the village. The Kolis are among the oldest inhabitants of the seven islands that became Bombay; the city's name, and the goddess Mumbadevi, belong to their world before they belonged to anyone else's. This koliwada still fishes. In the morning you'll see nets drying and boats coming in, a few hundred metres from tower blocks and the Sea Link.

And here's the thing. There are two Mumbais within a hundred yards of where you're standing — a bar where a cocktail costs more than a fisherman might take home in a day, and a village that was here first. Neither is going anywhere soon. Being honest about that, standing here, is part of seeing the city properly.

[beat]

Walk on into the village while it's light. At the end of the lanes, on the point, is the little Worli Fort, put up by the British in the late seventeenth century, with one of the best views of the Sea Link in the city.
