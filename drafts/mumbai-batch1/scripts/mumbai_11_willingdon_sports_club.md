# Mumbai 11 · Willingdon Sports Club

*Vantage: on the road outside the main gate of the Willingdon Sports Club, Tardeo* · 346 words · about 138 s

This club exists because another club refused to let some Indians in.

You're in Tardeo, outside the gates of the Willingdon Sports Club — behind the wall, thirty-odd acres of lawns, a golf course and a clubhouse. You'll see the club's purple on the gate and the signs. Purple, as it happens, was Lady Willingdon's favourite colour; it went into the crest, and for decades into the waiters' uniforms.

Here's the story. Lord Willingdon became Governor of Bombay in nineteen thirteen, sent out, it was said, with the King's instruction to smash through the fences that divided the city's communities. He tried to bring Indian friends into the Royal Bombay Yacht Club, near the Gateway of India. They were refused. Willingdon resigned his membership, and in nineteen seventeen he founded a club of his own — this one — with no colour bar. Indians and Europeans could join on equal terms. It opened to members that December.

Now, inside those gates, the clubhouse was designed by F G B Hawkins, the architect of the city's Improvement Trust, and laid out with tennis courts, croquet lawns, a cricket ground and one of the first full-size polo fields in India. Indian princes came to play polo here; the Prince of Wales became an honorary member in nineteen twenty-one. And one small claim to fame: the club is said to be where eggs Kejriwal — fried eggs on cheese toast, with green chilli — was invented, by a member who couldn't eat eggs at home.

And here's the thing. It would be easy to tell this as a story of a liberal governor and a happy ending. It's subtler than that. This was still a club for the rich, and today membership is mostly closed except to members' children. But in its moment, a door opened here that had been shut elsewhere, and the city noticed.

If you fancy completing the story, the Royal Bombay Yacht Club is on the waterfront at Apollo Bunder, beside the Gateway. Stand outside both and you've seen both sides of the same argument.
