Mimì alla Ferrovia

It's the autumn of 1943, the war has only just moved on from Naples, and a couple called Emilio and Ida Giugliano are opening a trattoria near the railway.

A few minutes' walk from Napoli Centrale, Mimì alla Ferrovia occupies numbers 19 to 21 Via Alfonso d'Aragona, with its name over the door, and the ferrovia in question is that main railway station.

Have a look at the walls once you're inside, because they're the first thing people remember. They're covered with photographs of the guests who have eaten here over the decades, among them Totò, Eduardo De Filippo, Marcello Mastroianni, Federico Fellini, Robert De Niro and Diego Maradona. Mimì was the name Emilio went by, and his restaurant has stayed in the family ever since, passing through his son and nephew to a third generation, with Salvatore Giugliano, born in 1991, now running the kitchen.

The cooking is the Neapolitan repertoire done properly, so look out for candele alla genovese, long tubes of pasta in a sauce of onions and beef cooked down for hours, ziti with tomato, pancetta and pecorino, and a babà to finish. Salvatore is known for buying daily at the old city markets of Porta Nolana and Sant'Antonio Abate, so the menu follows what's good that morning.

In 2023 the restaurant turned eighty, received the Medal of the City of Naples, and became the first restaurant entered in Italy's register of historic brands of national interest.

A station restaurant is meant to be somewhere you pass through on your way to somewhere else, and this one has spent eighty years persuading people to stay. That, more than any of the famous faces on the walls, is the real achievement of the place.

If you're here in the morning, walk ten minutes south to the Porta Nolana fish market, where the stalls spill out beneath an old city gate and you can see where tonight's dinner begins.
