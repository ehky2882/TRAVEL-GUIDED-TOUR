Super Ohteeum

The Romans had a word, ohteeum, for time that isn't spent working but isn't quite wasted either, and this small hotel has made it its name.

The long red building beside you is the National Archaeological Museum, and Super Ohteeum occupies a nineteenth century apartment in the building that overlooks its exhibition halls, a private address with only a modest sign at street level.

Super Ohteeum was founded by Neekohlah Chanchoh, a writer and curator, and Vinchentsoh Falchohneh, who works in ecology and environmental projects, and they describe it as an art hotel and a residency for artists and cultural professionals visiting Naples. The name joins two Latin words, super and ohteeum, to suggest leisure that goes beyond the ordinary, the kind of free time in which ideas actually happen.

The apartment covers about three hundred and seventy square metres, with five bedrooms, a suite and a large shared space built around a central kitchen, where breakfast is served at a common table. The interiors were designed by the studio Hyper Eden, and the fabrics on the canopies and screens carry patterns taken from the Pompayan mosaics in the museum next door, while the bedrooms borrow their colours from different sections of its galleries.

Much of the art arrives with the guests, too, since many of the works on the walls were left by artists who came here on residencies.

The Romans set ohteeum against negohteeum, the business of earning a living, and Naples is usually sold to visitors through its food and its views. Here, a stone's throw from one of the great collections of ancient art, someone has built a place that asks you to slow down and make something instead.


Next door, the museum's Farnayzeh collection is the obvious first stop, and the Farnayzeh Hercules, resting on his club after his labours, is about as fine a portrait of well earned ohteeum as you'll find anywhere.
