Doctor Vintage

This little street in Kyaya is named after the man who taught the world to see the people of Pompay.

Among the caffays and boutiques of one of the smartest neighbourhoods in Naples, on the side street of Veeah Joozeppeh Fyorelli, Doctor Vintage is the small shopfront at number twelve, where you should be able to make out rails of clothing through the glass.

The name on the street sign belongs to Joozeppeh Fyorelli, who directed the excavations at Pompay in the eighteen sixtys, and it was his idea to pour plaster into the hollows that the bodies of the dead had left in the volcanic ash, which is why we can still see their shapes today. It's a rather moving namesake for a shopping street, since his career was about recovering what others had left behind.

Doctor Vintage is a small independent business, run by its owner, Rohzahryoh Rehkahnoh, and it deals in second hand and vintage clothing. That scale matters, because there's no head office deciding what arrives on these rails; the stock is whatever has been found, bought and chosen, so this week's find is unlikely to be there next time.

Kyaya is where Neapolitans have shopped for tailoring and labels for generations, with Veeah day Meeleh and Veeah Feelanjehree a few minutes away. A vintage shop in the middle of all that makes a gentle counterpoint, with clothes that have already been worn, enjoyed and let go, and the pleasure lies in finding the one that suits its next owner.

Seen from this pavement, the archaeologist and the shop are doing a version of the same job, giving things that had been put aside a second life in plain view.


From here, Pyatsah day Marteeree is a short walk east, with its tall column and four stone lions, and the tailoring houses of Kyaya all around it if you'd like to compare new with well worn.
