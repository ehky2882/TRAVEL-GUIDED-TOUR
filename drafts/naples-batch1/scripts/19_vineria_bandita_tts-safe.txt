Veenereeah Bandeetah

Up on the Vohmeroh, one of the most respectable neighbourhoods in Naples, there's a wine bar that has named itself after an outlaw.

On Veeah Meekehleh Kerbaker, one of the busy shopping streets of the hill, Veenereeah Bandeetah sits at number fifty seven, a small bar with shelves of bottles behind the counter and a handful of little round tables.

The name on the sign, bandeetah, means a woman outlaw, and the bar's logo, by the illustrator Jahnlookah Kanneetsoh, shows exactly that figure. The owners are Joolyahnoh Granahtah and Federeekah Paloomboh, partners in life and in business, who opened in twenty nineteen and modelled the room on the small wine bars of France, where tables sit close together and conversation drifts between them.

The wine is entirely natural, made by small producers with as little added as possible. There are more than a hundred and fifty labels in rotation, mostly Italian and especially from Kampahnyah, with about a third from elsewhere in Europe, France above all, and glasses that won't break the bank.

The food follows the same thinking, with up to fifteen small dishes and no set order to eat them in: boorahtah, anchovies from Chetarah with buckwheat bread, or a Neapolitan pantsahnellah, all built on seasonal ingredients from local producers and cooked as little as they need.

The owners have said they don't much care what the wine is called, natural or otherwise, because to them it's simply wine, and that's the gentle joke inside the name. What once looked like an outlaw on the wine list has settled very comfortably into the Vohmeroh, and nobody here seems to be on the run.


If you arrive early, walk up through the Vohmeroh to Kastel Sant Elmoh, about fifteen minutes away, and catch the late light over the bay from its ramparts before you come back down for a glass.
