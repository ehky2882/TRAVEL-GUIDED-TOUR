Ex Chiesa di Santa Maria della Misericordia ai Vergini

For years after the Second World War this church was a dumping ground, and the art that rescued it is still made inside.

At the corner of Via Misericordiella, close to the old Porta San Gennaro, the church in front of you shows a tall, flat facade in two storeys, with pilasters below, a large window above and a triangular pediment on top that holds a round window at its centre. The doorway is the giveaway, framed in dark grey stone carved into curling eighteenth century shapes.

That stone is piperno, a volcanic rock quarried around Naples, and it's carved here in a playful rococo style that seems almost too light for such a heavy material.

The church itself is far older than its portal, and a tombstone dated 1358 turned up during sixteenth century renovations. In 1533 the building passed to the Theatine fathers, whose founder, Saint Gaetano da Thiene, stayed next door. By the early 1700s it had been filled with paintings by leading Neapolitan artists, among them a work by Giovanni Balducci on the corporal works of mercy, which means feeding the hungry, sheltering strangers and caring for the sick. Beneath the nave lies a burial chamber of the same size, where the confraternity laid its dead.

War damage closed it for decades, and that's when the rubbish arrived. Since 2015 it has been looked after by SMMAVE, a centre for contemporary art founded by the sculptor Christian Leperino, whose volunteers cleared and restored the building and now run exhibitions, concerts, workshops and studio places for young sculptors.

What I find rather lovely is that the old dedication still fits, since a church built around works of mercy, rescued by volunteers, now spends its days teaching.
