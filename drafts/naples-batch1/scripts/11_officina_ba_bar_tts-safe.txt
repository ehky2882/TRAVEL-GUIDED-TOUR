Offeecheenah Bah Bar

The sea is only a minute's walk from here, but for most of the history of Santa Loocheeah it came right up to the houses.

Look for a black fronted bar with tables set out on the pavement and, after dark, low lights glowing inside, because that's Offeecheenah Bah Bar, at number one hundred and sixty nine Veeah Santa Loocheeah.

This was Santa Loocheeah, a quarter of fishermen and boatmen that gave its name to one of the best known Neapolitan songs, published in eighteen forty nine as a boatman's invitation to come out on the water. Much of the ground between here and today's seafront was reclaimed in the late nineteenth century, when the city pushed its shoreline outwards and laid the new promenade of Veeah Partenohpeh along the edge.

As for the bar, offeecheenah means workshop, and this one opened in twenty sixteen as the seaside offshoot of Bah Bar, a bar in the Kyaya street of Veeah Beezeenyahnoh. Inside it's minimalist and dimly lit, with jazz in the background, and it runs from midday until late. The drinks list moves from an Ahperol spritz to a whisky smash with basil, the wine list is longer than you'd expect, and plates of cured meats and cheeses come out to keep everyone steady.

What's worth knowing, standing here, is that Santa Loocheeah has always been about evenings by the water. The boatmen in the old song were selling exactly that, a cool hour by the sea once the heat of the day had gone, and the tables outside a bar like this one are the modern version of the same offer, even if the water itself has moved a little further away.


Just a few steps away, where this street meets the seafront, stands the Fontahnah del Jeegahnteh, a stone fountain of three arches, and from there the promenade runs west past the Kastel dell Ohvoh, with Vesuvius behind you across the bay.
