Pizzeria Concettina ai Tre Santi

In 1951 the pizzas made here were sold on credit, out of a woman's home, to neighbours who would pay when they could.

The pizzeria in front of you, on Via Arena della Sanità, is Concettina ai Tre Santi, named after a street shrine close by, and at most mealtimes the small crowd waiting outside is a helpful clue that you've found it.

The woman in the name was Concetta Oliva, known as Concettina, who began making pizzas at home while her husband Antonio worked as a shoemaker, and the three saints are the figures in the neighbourhood shrine, an edicola where the Madonna was venerated together with Saint Anne and Saint Vincent.

Her great grandson, Ciro Oliva, born in 1993, took charge while still in his early twenties, renovated the place, added a counter for fried food and turned a local pizzeria into one of the most talked about in Naples. Look for the montanarina, a little fried pizza topped with whipped ricotta and lemon, or the panino named after his father Antonio, with carrot, smoked provola and green olives.

The detail that tells you most, though, is the pizza sospesa. Customers can pay for an extra pizza that's kept for someone who can't afford one, in the same spirit as the Neapolitan habit of leaving a coffee paid for at the bar.

So the story comes full circle, from a grandmother's pizzas on credit to her great grandson's pizzas paid forward. Both rest on the same idea, that in a neighbourhood like the Sanità people look after one another, and that a pizza is a perfectly good way to do it.

When you step back outside, look up towards the Ponte della Sanità, the bridge built across the valley in the early nineteenth century, which carried traffic over the top of this neighbourhood on its way to Capodimonte.
