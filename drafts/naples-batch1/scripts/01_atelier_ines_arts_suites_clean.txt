Atelier Inès Arts & Suites

The money that built this house was earned in Brooklyn, by a carpenter who emigrated to America and came home to Naples with enough to buy a plot of land.

Via dei Cristallini climbs gently here towards the hill of Capodimonte, and the building in front of you is Atelier Inès Arts and Suites, part guesthouse, part gallery and part working jewellery studio. Look for the tall wooden gates at street level, because they're the surest sign that you've found the right door.

Those gates, which are the place to begin, are made of American pitch pine, a wood so rich in resin that it barely rots, and they belong to the house's early life after 1947, when the carpenter's family ran a timber business from the ground floor. Until about 1930, this patch of the Sanità was an open air cinema and theatre, which feels rather fitting for a house whose owners keep no televisions in the rooms on the grounds that Naples is show enough.

The family today are Vincenzo Oste and Inès Sellami, both jewellery designers, who opened the suites in 2017. Downstairs, the workshop is still a workshop, turning out jewellery and small decorative pieces by hand, and one napkin ring made here is said to have reached Giorgio Armani's house on Panarea. The sculpture and painting throughout the building are by Vincenzo's father, Annibale Oste, whose archive the couple are still putting together.

What strikes me about this house is how little it has had to pretend. The street takes its name from the glassmakers who once worked along it, the neighbourhood was full of glovers and silk weavers, and the trade in this building has simply changed its material, from timber to metal and enamel and on to art.
