Lanteekwahryoh

Behind a discreet door on a narrow lane near the seafront, the bartenders still work in immaculate white jackets.

Vahnella Guyetahnee is the little lane in front of you, tucked between the shops of Kyaya and the water, and Lanteekwahryoh is at number two, a speakeasy style bar that gives very little away from the street, which is rather the intention.

Once you're through the door, red velvet, floral wallpaper and dark wood set the tone, somewhere between a gentlemen's club and a nineteen fifties jazz lounge, and on weeknights there's usually live music to go with it. The bar was opened in the middle of the twenty tens by Alex Fretsah, a Neapolitan bartender with a considerable local following, and this year it was placed twenty eighth in the list of Europe's fifty best bars.

The menu is split into three parts: classic cocktails, modern drinks from the early twentieth century onwards, and the bar's own contemporary creations. The one that most belongs to this coastline is Oon Grandeh Leemohneh, built on gin, citron and leemonchelloh, and if you'd rather keep things traditional, there's a trio of negrohneez that arrives on a custom silver tower.

And here's the thing about that name: an anteekwahryoh is a dealer in antiques, someone who keeps old things in good condition and passes them on, and that's very much how this bar treats its drinks. The classic recipes are handled like heirlooms, polished and served with a little ceremony, while the new ones are kept few enough that they never crowd the old ones out.


When you leave, it's a couple of minutes' walk down to Veeah Partenohpeh and the seafront, where the Kastel dell Ohvoh sits out on its rock, and the lights along the water make a fine end to the evening.
