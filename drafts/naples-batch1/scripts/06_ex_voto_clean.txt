Ex Voto

In Naples an ex voto is a thank you, usually a small silver heart, hand or leg left in a church once a prayer has been answered.

Number 18A Via Carlo Poerio, one street in from the Riviera di Chiaia, is the address of Ex Voto, a boutique whose windows give the first hint of what lies inside: clothes cut by some of fashion's most uncompromising designers.

The street's namesake, Carlo Poerio, was a nineteenth century Neapolitan liberal who spent the best part of a decade in Bourbon prisons for his politics, and his name now sits on one of the most elegant shopping streets in the city, which says something about how Naples chose to remember him.

Inside the shop, the list of labels reads like a short history of fashion's awkward squad: Yohji Yamamoto, Comme des Garçons and Issey Miyake from Japan, Dries Van Noten from Belgium, Uma Wang from China and Sacai, alongside dozens of others. There's clothing for women, men and children, plus pieces for the home, and visits can be booked by appointment as well as made on the spur of the moment.

That's where the name stops being merely clever, because the shop describes its stock as timeless design that honours the people who make it and the places where it's made, and an ex voto does much the same thing, as a made object offered as thanks, with the skill of the silversmith built into the gratitude. Seen that way, a rail of carefully constructed clothes in a city of votive silver feels less like a joke and more like a family resemblance.

To see the originals, head into the old centre to the church of the Gesù Nuovo, where the rooms dedicated to Saint Giuseppe Moscati are lined with silver ex votos left by people giving thanks.
