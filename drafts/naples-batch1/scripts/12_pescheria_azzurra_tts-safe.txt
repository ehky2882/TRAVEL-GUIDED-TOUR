Peskereeah Adzoorah

The fish lying on the ice at this counter may well be the fish someone at the next table is about to eat.

Where Veeah Portamedeenah meets the Peenyahsekkah market, a few steps from Monteh Santoh station, Peskereeah Adzoorah occupies the shopfronts at numbers three to five, with its counters of fish and shellfish facing straight out onto the street.

The market around you, the Peenyahsekkah, is usually described as the oldest market in Naples, and its name means dry pine cone, after a local story that the pine trees which once grew here lost their cones. It's still very much a working market, with fish, vegetables, cheese and housewares sold alongside some of the best fried street food in the city.

The shop itself is run by the Galyottah brothers as a fishmonger and a trattoria at the same time, so you can buy fish to take home or sit down and have it cooked for you. The menu takes in raw seafood, linguine with lobster, spaghetti with clams and crumbled tahrahlloh, a pesto made with mussels, and the kwoppoh, a paper cone of mixed fried fish that's eaten standing up as often as sitting down. Prices are modest, the room is noisy, and nobody is in any hurry to make it otherwise.

Most restaurants keep the kitchen at a polite distance from the source of the food, and this one does the opposite, which makes it the shortest journey from sea to plate you're likely to find in the city. The noise and the queue are part of the deal, because a place this close to the market was never going to feel like a dining room.


When you've eaten, the Monteh Santoh funicular is just round the corner, and it will lift you up the hill to the Vohmeroh, a short walk from Kastel Sant Elmoh and its views right across the bay.
