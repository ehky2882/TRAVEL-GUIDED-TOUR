Casa D'Anna ai Cristallini

There's a curious thing about the guest rooms in this old palazzo: every one of them is named after an island in the Bay of Naples.

Halfway along Via dei Cristallini, behind one of the street's old palazzo doorways, sits Casa D'Anna ai Cristallini, a guesthouse of just five rooms. From the pavement you'll see little more than the entrance and shuttered windows, which suits a house that saves its best side for its guests.

The house itself occupies part of Palazzo Giannattasio, a building with centuries of history behind it, and the main rooms rise to ceilings about six metres high, crossed with chestnut beams. The guesthouse was created in 2013 by Davide D'Anna, an architect and artist, together with two friends, one Scottish and one French, and they filled it with what they had gathered on their travels: art books, paintings and sculpture, old opera glasses and ballet programmes.

The rooms carry the islands' names, Capri, Ischia, Procida, Palmarola and Nisida, and up on the top floor there's a terrace hung with bougainvillea and jasmine, where breakfast is served whenever the weather allows.

Since 2020 the house has belonged to Alessandra Calise Martuscelli, and that's where the story takes a turn. Her husband's family owns the Ipogeo dei Cristallini, the painted Greek tombs about twenty metres from here, which came to them through an uncle who was a baron.

That, if you ask me, is the quiet charm of this corner of the Sanità, because the family looking after the living upstairs is also looking after the dead below. One house is full of other people's souvenirs, the other holds burial chambers carved and painted more than two thousand years ago, and both are being kept, carefully, by the same hands.
