Meemee alla Ferrohveeah

It's the autumn of nineteen forty three, the war has only just moved on from Naples, and a couple called Emeelyoh and Eedah Joolyahnoh are opening a trattoria near the railway.

A few minutes' walk from Nahpohlee Chentrahleh, Meemee alla Ferrohveeah occupies numbers nineteen to twenty one Veeah Alfonsoh Darahgohnah, with its name over the door, and the ferrohveeah in question is that main railway station.

Have a look at the walls once you're inside, because they're the first thing people remember. They're covered with photographs of the guests who have eaten here over the decades, among them Tohtoh, Edwardoh Deh Feeleeppoh, Marchelloh Mastroyahnee, Federeekoh Felleenee, Robert De Neeroh and Dyaygoh Mahrahdohnah. Meemee was the name Emeelyoh went by, and his restaurant has stayed in the family ever since, passing through his son and nephew to a third generation, with Salvahtohreh Joolyahnoh, born in nineteen ninety one, now running the kitchen.

The cooking is the Neapolitan repertoire done properly, so look out for kandehleh alla jenohvayzeh, long tubes of pasta in a sauce of onions and beef cooked down for hours, tseetee with tomato, panchettah and peckoreenoh, and a babbah to finish. Salvahtohreh is known for buying daily at the old city markets of Portah Nohlahnah and Sant Antohnyoh Abahteh, so the menu follows what's good that morning.

In twenty twenty three the restaurant turned eighty, received the Medal of the City of Naples, and became the first restaurant entered in Italy's register of historic brands of national interest.

A station restaurant is meant to be somewhere you pass through on your way to somewhere else, and this one has spent eighty years persuading people to stay. That, more than any of the famous faces on the walls, is the real achievement of the place.


If you're here in the morning, walk ten minutes south to the Portah Nohlahnah fish market, where the stalls spill out beneath an old city gate and you can see where tonight's dinner begins.
