Officina Ba-Bar

The sea is only a minute's walk from here, but for most of the history of Santa Lucia it came right up to the houses.

Look for a black fronted bar with tables set out on the pavement and, after dark, low lights glowing inside, because that's Officina Ba-Bar, at number 169 Via Santa Lucia.

This was Santa Lucia, a quarter of fishermen and boatmen that gave its name to one of the best known Neapolitan songs, published in 1849 as a boatman's invitation to come out on the water. Much of the ground between here and today's seafront was reclaimed in the late nineteenth century, when the city pushed its shoreline outwards and laid the new promenade of Via Partenope along the edge.

As for the bar, officina means workshop, and this one opened in 2016 as the seaside offshoot of Ba-Bar, a bar in the Chiaia street of Via Bisignano. Inside it's minimalist and dimly lit, with jazz in the background, and it runs from midday until late. The drinks list moves from an Aperol spritz to a whisky smash with basil, the wine list is longer than you'd expect, and plates of cured meats and cheeses come out to keep everyone steady.

What's worth knowing, standing here, is that Santa Lucia has always been about evenings by the water. The boatmen in the old song were selling exactly that, a cool hour by the sea once the heat of the day had gone, and the tables outside a bar like this one are the modern version of the same offer, even if the water itself has moved a little further away.

Just a few steps away, where this street meets the seafront, stands the Fontana del Gigante, a stone fountain of three arches, and from there the promenade runs west past the Castel dell'Ovo, with Vesuvius behind you across the bay.
