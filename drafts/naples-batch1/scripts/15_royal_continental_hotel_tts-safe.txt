Royal Continental Hotel

On the roof of this hotel there's a swimming pool filled with water pumped up from the Gulf of Naples.

In front of you, at numbers thirty eight to forty four Veeah Partenohpeh, the long modern block facing the Kastel dell Ohvoh across the road is the Royal Continental.

This was once the site of the Grand Hotel Royal day Zaytronjay, a nineteenth century hotel destroyed by German forces during the Second World War, and its replacement, the Hotel Royal, opened in nineteen fifty five, designed by the Neapolitan architect Ferdeenandoh Kyahrahmonteh with the engineer Robairtoh Fernandez.

Then came the famous name: Joh Pontee, the Milanese architect who did more than almost anyone to define Italian design after the war, was brought in to design the furniture, the swimming pool and even the hotel's logo. The pool was moved up from a lower floor to the roof so that swimmers could look straight out over the bay, and in nineteen fifty four it appeared on the cover of Dohmus, the magazine Ponti had founded.

If you're staying or able to visit, look for the Ponti floor, where twenty four rooms on the first floor keep their original furniture: three drawer chests, round mirrors, wall units with fold down writing desks and Kasseenah's Lejjairah chairs in pale ash. In two thousand and three the hotel merged with its neighbour to become the Royal Continental, which now has nearly four hundred rooms.

The timing is the remarkable part, since barely ten years after the old hotel was blown up, Naples answered with a building full of lightness, and Lejjairah, the name of that slender chair, means exactly that, which makes it postwar optimism you can actually sit on.


From the hotel's front door, cross the road and walk out along the causeway to the Kastel dell Ohvoh, where the small harbour of Borgoh Mahreenahree gives you the best view back towards this whole stretch of the seafront.
