ScottoJonno

In 1883, under the new glass roof of this arcade, a café opened where Neapolitans could hear the latest songs while they drank.

The Galleria Principe di Napoli is the arcade of iron, glass and pale stucco around you, across the road from the National Archaeological Museum, and ScottoJonno occupies rooms inside it, with a cocktail bar and café at ground level and more spaces above.

The man in the name, Vincenzo Scotto Jonno, was an entrepreneur from the island of Procida who modelled his café chantant on the café concerts of Paris, with singers performing between the tables. His first star, Ersilia Sampieri, was billed as the Sarah Bernhardt of the café concert, and for a while this was one of the places where Neapolitan song set its trends.

A long silence followed, because after the First World War the rooms were turned into treasury offices for the Banco di Napoli, and they stayed that way until 2021, while the galleria itself, built between 1873 and 1883, fell out of use for much of the twentieth century.

The revival came from the entrepreneur Luca Iannuzzi, who worked with the artist Eugenio Tibaldi to bring the space back, and it reopened in 2023. Look closely at the walls, which were deliberately left weathered, with the traces of every earlier use still showing through. There's a library of around eighteen hundred books that anyone can sit and read, a stage for music and readings, and a dragonfly as the house emblem, chosen as a sign of rebirth.

Plenty of restorations scrub a building back to a single moment, and this one refuses to, letting the café, the bank and the years of neglect all stay in the room together.
