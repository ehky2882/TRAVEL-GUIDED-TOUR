Peetsehreeah Konchetteenah eye Tray Santee

In nineteen fifty one the pizzas made here were sold on credit, out of a woman's home, to neighbours who would pay when they could.

The pizzeria in front of you, on Veeah Arehnah della Sahneetah, is Konchetteenah eye Tray Santee, named after a street shrine close by, and at most mealtimes the small crowd waiting outside is a helpful clue that you've found it.

The woman in the name was Konchettah Oleevah, known as Konchetteenah, who began making pizzas at home while her husband Antonio worked as a shoemaker, and the three saints are the figures in the neighbourhood shrine, an edeekohlah where the Madonna was venerated together with Saint Anne and Saint Vincent.

Her great grandson, Cheeroh Oleevah, born in nineteen ninety three, took charge while still in his early twenties, renovated the place, added a counter for fried food and turned a local pizzeria into one of the most talked about in Naples. Look for the montahnahreenah, a little fried pizza topped with whipped ricotta and lemon, or the panino named after his father Antonio, with carrot, smoked prohvohlah and green olives.

The detail that tells you most, though, is the peetsah sohspayzah. Customers can pay for an extra pizza that's kept for someone who can't afford one, in the same spirit as the Neapolitan habit of leaving a coffee paid for at the bar.

So the story comes full circle, from a grandmother's pizzas on credit to her great grandson's pizzas paid forward. Both rest on the same idea, that in a neighbourhood like the Sahneetah people look after one another, and that a pizza is a perfectly good way to do it.


When you step back outside, look up towards the Ponteh della Sahneetah, the bridge built across the valley in the early nineteenth century, which carried traffic over the top of this neighbourhood on its way to Kahpohdeemonteh.
