The best view in monna stee rah kee is behind an antique shop, at the top of an old lift.

Find nor mah noo Street, a narrow side alley off er moo, right in the middle of monna stee rah kee. Number three looks like nothing much from the street: an arcade, and an entrance through what appears to be a shop full of old furniture. Go in anyway. That's the point.

Past the furniture, you'll find a slightly old school lift. Take it up. The doors open onto a rooftop bar spread over the upper floors of the building, and straight ahead, filling the sky, is the Acropolis, with the Parthenon on top.

That's the whole gift, really. You've walked in off one of the busiest shopping streets in Athens, through a junk shop, up in a creaking lift, and come out at eye level with the most famous building in the western world. Athens does this better than almost anywhere.

As for the drinks: cocktails, wine, and plates to share. The crowd is mostly young and mostly local, and on a Friday or Saturday evening it's worth booking ahead for sunset.

And here's what I like about it. Look across the rooftops from up here and you'll see dozens of other flat roofs, with laundry, plants, water tanks and chairs. The roof terrace is how ordinary Athenians have always escaped the street. koo lur loh kahl just put a bar on one and turned the lights on.

Before you go, take a walk up the rest of nor mah noo. The famous Poet Sandal Maker used to have his workshop on this very alley, before moving near the Acropolis Museum. And the flea market of monna stee rah kee is only a couple of minutes away, if you fancy rummaging.
