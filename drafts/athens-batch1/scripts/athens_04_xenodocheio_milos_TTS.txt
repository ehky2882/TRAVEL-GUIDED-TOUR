If you know restaurants, you may know the name. ess tee ah toh ree oh mee loss is the seafood restaurant group started by the Greek chef koss tas spee lee ah dees, with branches in seven cities around the world. Their style is famously plain: very good fish, very little interference. This is the group's first hotel, and the restaurant on the ground floor is, in a sense, the founder coming home.

The forty or so rooms have the high ceilings and marble bathrooms you'd hope for in a building like this, and some of the terraces look straight up at Lycabettus.

What I like is the honesty of the name. kseh noh doh hee oh: a house for strangers. And the idea they've hung it on is fee loh kseh nee ah, the very old Greek obligation to treat a stranger as a friend. You can argue about whether a five star hotel quite manages that. But it's a good word to have over the door.

Before you go, turn round and cross to the Old Parliament. In front of it stands the bronze of theh oh doh ross kollo ko troh nees on horseback, the hero of the War of Independence this very street is named after. Worth a minute of your time.
