Watch the older men in any Athens café and sooner or later one of them will start clicking a string of beads. This is where those beads come from.

You should be on ah meh ree kees Street, the short street running between stah dee oo and pah neh pee stee mee oo, a few minutes from Syntagma Square, outside number nine. The shop window is full of strings of beads of every size and colour. The sign says kom boh loh gah dee koh, which means, roughly, the kom boh loy shop.

A kom boh loy is a string of worry beads. It's descended from the knotted prayer strings of Orthodox monks, but in Greece it lost its religious job a long time ago. Nobody prays with one. You flick it, swing it, click the beads through your fingers, and pass the time. It's a fidget toy with a very long history.

The family behind this shop, the ee eh roh poo loss family, started in naf plee oh, in the Peloponnese, and then brought the business to Athens, with this shop as its headquarters. Inside, the range runs from simple, inexpensive strings to serious collectors' pieces, gathered from all over the world.

Here's a little gift detail to take home. A Greek kom boh loy almost always has an odd number of beads, and one popular account says twenty three became the usual count. Count them when you pick one up.

And that's why I like this shop. It's a whole business built around a habit, not a need. Nobody needs a kom boh loy. Half of Greece has one anyway.
