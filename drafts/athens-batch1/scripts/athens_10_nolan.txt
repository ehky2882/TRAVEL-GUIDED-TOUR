ATLAS · ATHENS · 10 · Nolan
---

In a city that takes its grandmothers' recipes very seriously, Nolan was a small act of mischief.

You should be on Voulis Street, a few minutes south of Syntagma Square, outside numbers thirty one to thirty three. Nolan is the restaurant here: small, and easy to walk past if you weren't looking for it.

Nolan was opened by the Greek Japanese chef Sotiris Kontizas, and it did something Athens wasn't used to. It took Greek ingredients and cooked them with the flavours of Japan, Korea, Vietnam and China, all at once, without making a fuss about it. The most famous dish became the fried chicken: very crisp, with a tangy, slightly hot sauce. People came back for it again and again. The Michelin guide gave it a Bib Gourmand, its award for very good cooking at a fair price.

Restaurants change, and this one has. The Michelin guide now describes a Nolan two point oh, with the chef Michalis Nourloglou in the kitchen and a short, simple menu: sea bream tartare, say, or breaded beef cheeks with truffle. So go expecting the spirit of the place rather than a particular dish. The menu may well have moved on since I last looked.

[beat]

And that, really, is the point. Athens can be a city that eats as if it were still nineteen sixty. Nolan made a modest argument that a restaurant could be completely Athenian without being traditionally Greek, and the city, rather to its own surprise, agreed.

From here, walk up Voulis to the corner of Kolokotroni. The handsome pair of neoclassical buildings on that corner is the Xenodocheio Milos hotel, with the Old Parliament just across the road.
