Benaki Museum, pee reh oss one hundred and thirty eight

The Benaki is the grand old private museum of Athens. This is the part of it that moved into a factory.

You should be on pee reh oss Street, the long, straight road that once carried the industry of Athens down to the port of Piraeus, outside number one hundred and thirty eight. The building in front of you is plain and modern, with nothing of the mansion about it. That's the point.

The main Benaki Museum is a neoclassical house in kollo nah kee, full of jewellery, costume and centuries of Greek history. In the early two thousands, the museum needed space for large exhibitions, and it found it out here, in a former industrial building of the nineteen sixties. The conversion kept the original plan of the building and added a floor on top, and the new Benaki opened here in two thousand and four.

Go inside and you'll notice the main idea straight away. The building turns its back on the busy street and opens inwards, around a big central courtyard. All the light and life of the place faces in, which, on a road as loud as pee reh oss, is rather sensible.

The exhibitions change, so check what's on before you come. These are the big temporary shows, the kind that would never fit in a nineteenth century house.

What's worth noticing is the street itself. pee reh oss was the industrial spine of the city: factories, warehouses, the gasworks at gah zee. In the last few decades it's become a line of cultural buildings instead. The Benaki was one of the first to make that move.

From here, walk up pee reh oss towards the centre and you'll come to gah zee, where the brick chimneys of the old gasworks still stand. It's now a cultural centre called tek noh poh lees, and on a summer evening the whole neighbourhood comes out.
