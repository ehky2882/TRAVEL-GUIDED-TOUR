When this gallery opened in 2012, it described itself with unusual precision as seventy-two per cent art and twenty-eight per cent coffee.

The ground-floor space at number nine Annankatu, here in Punavuori at the heart of Helsinki's Design District, is Lokal, and from the pavement you can see into its two rooms, one of them set aside for a changing exhibition and the other for the work it sells.

Its founder, Katja Hagelstam, spent around twenty-five years photographing art, design and interiors for magazines and books before opening Lokal in April 2012, the year Helsinki was World Design Capital, and she has said she wanted a home-like room where art could be handled, used and passed down through the generations.

The coffee has since stepped aside, and what fills the space now is the work of more than two dozen Finnish makers, among them Matias Liimatainen and Nathalie Lautenbacher, with ceramics, wood, glass, textiles, jewellery and paintings, many of them made in very small numbers.

Recognition followed fairly quickly, with the Finnish Design Deed award in 2015 and the Finnish State Prize for Design in 2017, and in 2024 a selection from Lokal crossed to Paris for a show at the Finnish Institute in the Latin Quarter, where eleven of its makers were shown side by side.

The point Lokal makes is a gentle one, that a bowl or a stool can carry as much thought as a painting, and that when you buy it to use every day rather than to hang on a wall, the art simply becomes part of how you live.

Ask what's on in the exhibition room, and then spend a little time in the surrounding streets of Punavuori, where small design shops, studios and galleries fill the ground floors of the old apartment houses on almost every block.
