Telling a genuine piece of mid-century Finnish art glass from a convincing copy is a rare skill, and the man behind this small gallery has been sharpening it for more than forty years.

Yrjönkatu's quieter stretch of stone apartment blocks is where number eight stands, and since Gallery Lemmetti opens by appointment only, its window may well be your view of it today, with glass, ceramics and lamps set out with the care of a private collection.

That man is Juhani Lemmetti, who began collecting Finnish design in the early 1980s and has since assembled private collections and supplied museums abroad. In 1995 he acquired the rights to put Ilmari Tapiovaara's furniture back into production, and at this same address he once ran a design shop called Aero, which Artek later took over.

The gallery itself opened in 2016, and its heart is glass and ceramics from Finland's great post-war decades, with art glass by Kaj Franck, Timo Sarpaneva and Oiva Toikka, ceramics by Toini Muona, and the brass lamps of Paavo Tynell. Among the pieces it has handled are an Alvar Aalto Savoy vase from 1936 and a rare 1960s Bambu vase by Toikka, which was tracked down in Australia and brought home.

Here's the thought worth taking away from a place like this: Finland's designers sent their glass all over the world, and some of the finest pieces have spent decades in homes abroad, so a gallery of this kind is partly a shop and partly a slow homecoming, carried out one vase at a time.
