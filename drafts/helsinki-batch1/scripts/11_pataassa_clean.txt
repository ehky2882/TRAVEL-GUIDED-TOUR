On a dignified street of banks and old parliament buildings, the loudest room for several blocks is very probably this one, at almost any hour between eight in the evening and six in the morning.

A few doors from the old House of the Estates and the solid stone headquarters of the Bank of Finland, at number thirteen Snellmaninkatu, you'll find Pataässä, whose name means the Ace of Spades, behind a doorway that gives very little away in daylight.

Pataässä opened in 1994 on Mariankatu, a few streets away, as Helsinki's first karaoke restaurant, and it moved to this address in 2011, so these respectable surroundings have had a karaoke bar for a neighbour for well over a decade. It celebrated thirty years in 2024, and its regulars know it simply as Patis.

Inside there are two sides, a karaoke room and a tiny disco beside it, but the detail I love is the pair of karaoke kiosks, small booths for no more than two people, where anyone feeling nervous can practise a song before facing the room.

The hours are generous to the point of heroism, because on most nights the doors stay open until six in the morning, and at weekends the singing can begin as early as nine o'clock in the morning.

Finland has a reputation for reserve, and karaoke is one of the places where that reputation gives way, so a bar like this works as a kind of public living room, where a stranger's ballad earns the same applause as a friend's and nobody worries much about the high notes.

If you're passing in daylight, walk the few steps to the House of the Estates, where the Finnish Diet met in the nineteenth century, and look up at the sculpted relief above its entrance, before coming back after dark to hear who's braving the microphone.
