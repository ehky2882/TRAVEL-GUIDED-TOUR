The odd name over this door comes from two nicknames, Baskeri and Basso, belonging to the two founders who have built a small restaurant family around this street corner.

Tehtaankatu, Factory Street, meets Telakkakatu, Dockyard Street, right here, and BasBas Kulma occupies the corner itself, kulma being the Finnish word for corner, with a long bar counter inside and the founders' first restaurant, the Baskeri and Basso bistro, just upstairs at the same address.

Those founders are Nicolas Thieulon, known as Baskeri, and Kalle Kiukainen, known as Basso, who opened the bistro in 2015 with a menu inspired by Parisian bistros, casual San Francisco joints and small Italian osterias, and it soon became a fixture on lists of Finland's best restaurants.

Kulma followed in 2020 as the more casual sibling, a busy wine bar of small plates and mostly natural wines, serving from late afternoon until around midnight, and the dishes regulars recommend run from charcuterie and cheese through salmon crudo and beef tartare to fried whitefish and grilled pork belly.

The wine is the real draw, chosen from growers the team knows and loves, and the BasBas list has collected Star Wine List awards, while the family has kept on growing, with a studio for pop-ups in 2023 and, in September 2026, a new place called Sirkus across the Long Bridge in Siltasaari, named after the travelling circuses that once stopped in the little park there.

The corner tells a neat story, since its two street names recall the factories and shipyard that once gave this end of town its living, and today its trade is sociability itself, a long counter where cooks, sommeliers and neighbours end up shoulder to shoulder after their own shifts are over.

Get here early if you want a seat at the bar, since walk-ins are welcome but the room fills quickly, and afterwards stroll down Telakkakatu towards Hietalahti, where the shipyard still builds icebreakers on the edge of the city centre.
