The little black cabins on these rocks have no water pipes, no power cables and no sewer, and yet the city centre is only around half an hour away by bus.

Out on Vuorilahdenniemi, a rocky headland on the island of Laajasalo, the cabins sit in a loose row along the shore, small gabled huts painted black and linked by a narrow wooden deck that runs across the rocks, each with a wide window turned towards the sea.

This is Majamaja, an off-grid retreat designed by the Paris-based architect Pekka Littow, which opened here in the autumn of 2020 after a prototype had been shown in 2019 in the courtyard of Station F, a start-up campus in Paris. Each cabin covers about twenty-three square metres and was built in a factory, then assembled on site without heavy machinery.

Look up at the roofs, where solar panels supply the electricity, and then think about the water, which comes from rain and from the sea itself, cleaned by a purification system the company has patented, while water from the shower and sink is treated and reused and the toilets are dry. Inside, the furniture folds down from the walls and the kitchen hides behind birch plywood doors.

Even the bedding is local, since the cabins are dressed in textiles from Lapuan Kankurit, the family weavers whose mill is in the town of Lapua.

The company's chief executive, Tuomas Autio, has said he hopes to do for housing what Nokia did for telephones, freeing it from fixed networks, which is a big ambition for a cabin you can cross in a few strides, but that's precisely the idea, with the smallest possible building carrying its whole infrastructure on its back.

[beat]

The cabins are open to guests from spring to late autumn, and even if you're only visiting, it's worth finding a spot on the warm rock along the shore and looking out across the islands, which is exactly the view each cabin's big window was placed to frame.
