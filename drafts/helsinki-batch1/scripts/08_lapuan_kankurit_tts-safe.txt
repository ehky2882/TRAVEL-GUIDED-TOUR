This linen company started life making felt boots, and more than a century later the same family is still running it, now into its fifth generation.

Katareenankatoo is the short street climbing between the Market Square and Senate Square, in the old block known as the Tori Quarter, and the Lapuan Kankoorit store at number two is easy to recognise by its calm window of folded linen, woollen blankets and towels in natural, earthy colours.

The story begins in nineteen seventeen in Lapua, a small town in western Finland, where Yooho Annala opened a felt boot factory; his family bought their first weaving machines in the nineteen thirties, and in nineteen seventy three his grandson Yooha Yelt and Yooha's wife Leesa founded the weaving mill of Lapuan Kankoorit, which means the Weavers of Lapua, with just three employees.

They began with traditional wall hangings, and the company has since become known for linen and wool woven on jacquard looms at the mill in Lapua, which is still very much at work. Yooha's son Esko and his wife Yahna took over in nineteen ninety nine, the fifth generation joined in twenty twenty five, and these days there's a Lapuan Kankoorit shop in Tokyo as well.

Step inside and notice how the shop has been designed, because when it was renewed in twenty nineteen, the Japanese studio Eema design, run by Mana and Takashi Kobayashi, set Finnish materials such as birch shingles against a pared back Japanese calm, and there's even a faint scent of tar, the traditional Finnish preservative for wooden boats and buildings.

It feels entirely natural that a Japanese studio was asked to frame these textiles, since the two cultures share a respect for natural materials and quiet rooms, and the shop makes that kinship feel obvious rather than exotic.

Run your hand over the linen towels, which feel stiff at first and soften with every wash, and then walk the few steps up to Senate Square, or round the corner to the Helsinki City Museum, which tells the story of the city from its earliest days.
