This linen company started life making felt boots, and more than a century later the same family is still running it, now into its fifth generation.

Katariinankatu is the short street climbing between the Market Square and Senate Square, in the old block known as the Tori Quarter, and the Lapuan Kankurit store at number two is easy to recognise by its calm window of folded linen, woollen blankets and towels in natural, earthy colours.

The story begins in 1917 in Lapua, a small town in western Finland, where Juho Annala opened a felt-boot factory; his family bought their first weaving machines in the 1930s, and in 1973 his grandson Juha Hjelt and Juha's wife Liisa founded the weaving mill of Lapuan Kankurit, which means the Weavers of Lapua, with just three employees.

They began with traditional wall hangings, and the company has since become known for linen and wool woven on jacquard looms at the mill in Lapua, which is still very much at work. Juha's son Esko and his wife Jaana took over in 1999, the fifth generation joined in 2025, and these days there's a Lapuan Kankurit shop in Tokyo as well.

Step inside and notice how the shop has been designed, because when it was renewed in 2019, the Japanese studio IMA design, run by Mana and Takashi Kobayashi, set Finnish materials such as birch shingles against a pared-back Japanese calm, and there's even a faint scent of tar, the traditional Finnish preservative for wooden boats and buildings.

It feels entirely natural that a Japanese studio was asked to frame these textiles, since the two cultures share a respect for natural materials and quiet rooms, and the shop makes that kinship feel obvious rather than exotic.
