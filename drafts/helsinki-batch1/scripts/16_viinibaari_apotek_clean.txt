The shelves in this wine bar are protected by law, which is not something many bars can say about their furniture.

Where Lapinlahdenkatu meets Eerikinkatu, look through the corner windows of Wine Bar Apotek, and you should see dark wooden shelves running round the walls, lined with old brown glass pharmacy bottles lit from above.

This was the Swan Pharmacy, which served the neighbourhood from the early 1910s until 2018, when it finally closed because it no longer paid its way, and its fixed fittings, in the Art Nouveau style the Finns call Jugend, had been protected since 1999, so nothing could simply be ripped out.

The restaurateurs Tio Tikka and Asta Nurmilaukas took on the space, and after a fifteen-month renovation and a long chain of permits, Apotek opened in June 2020, keeping the warm wooden shelving, the bottles and even an old mechanical cash register, along with two small side rooms that once belonged to the pharmacy.

The wine leans towards small, little-known family producers from Europe, with grower Champagne, German Riesling and Burgundy among them, and since the bar shares its ownership with a wine importer and the restaurant Carelia, the list is built from bottles the team knows well. To eat, there are cheeses, charcuterie and seasonal small plates.

The law that protected those shelves turned out to be the making of the place, because the bar had to fit itself around the pharmacy rather than the other way round, and that's why the room feels so unhurried, with something of a dispensary's hush still hanging in the air.

[beat]

Apotek opens on Tuesday, Thursday, Friday and Saturday evenings, so check before you come, and if you're early, walk a few minutes south-east to the park around the Old Church, built in the 1820s and the oldest church in central Helsinki.
