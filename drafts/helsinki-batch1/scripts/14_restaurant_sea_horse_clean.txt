Seahorses have been watching diners from the back wall of this restaurant for generations, and nobody seems entirely sure who painted them.

Kapteeninkatu, Captain Street, cuts through the quiet Art Nouveau blocks of Ullanlinna, and at number eleven you'll find the Sea Horse, which has been serving Finnish home cooking at this address since December 1933.

Inside, head for the main dining room and look at the back wall, where the famous seahorse mural swims above the tables. One story says art students broke in during the 1940s and painted it; another says a penniless art student painted it to settle a bill, and the restaurant itself simply credits art students, which is probably the wisest position to take.

The menu is a roll-call of Finnish classics, from fried Baltic herring and herring platters to cabbage rolls, salmon soup and Wiener schnitzel, but the dish with an international reputation is the onion steak, which the journalist Tyler Brûlé wrote about warmly in the 1990s, sending a fresh wave of visitors to the door.

Regulars have long called the place Sikala, the Pigsty, with obvious affection, and over the decades it has been a haunt of local writers, artists and musicians, which gives it something of the air of a neighbourhood club that happens to let strangers in.

I find it rather touching how little the Sea Horse has tried to keep up, because restaurants around it have come and gone through ninety years of fashions, while this one has gone on frying the herring and keeping the seahorses on the wall, and that steadiness has become its whole appeal.

After your meal, walk five minutes east into Kaivopuisto, the old seaside park, and follow the shore path, where on a clear day you can watch the ferries heading out between the islands.
