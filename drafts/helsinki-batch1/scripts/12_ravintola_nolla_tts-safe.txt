Ask to see the bin in this restaurant's kitchen and you'll be disappointed, because there simply isn't one.

Find number twenty two on Fredrikinkatoo, a shopping street running through Poonavwori, and you've found Nolla, a neighbourhood restaurant whose name is the Finnish word for zero.

It was founded by three chefs from three countries, Karlosh Enrikesh from Portugal, Albert Franch Soonyer from Spain and Looka Balach from Serbia, who ran it first as a pop up in twenty eighteen and opened here permanently in July twenty nineteen, with a promise to produce as close to no waste as a working kitchen can.

Suppliers are asked not to deliver anything in single use packaging, the staff aprons are cut from discarded bed linen, used cooking oil is turned into soap, and in the dining room you can see the composting machine, which turns up to twenty eight kilos of scraps into soil overnight, soil that then goes back to the farms that grew the food.

The ingredients follow the same logic, since Balach has explained that almost everything comes from within three hundred kilometres, with exceptions for salt, coffee, tea, wine, seaweed and rapeseed oil, and the Michelin Guide recommends Nolla for good value, singling out bold, hearty cooking such as roast deer with a glossy reduction, with a chef's table for anyone who'd like to watch it being made.

Nollas quiet radicalism lies in refusing to make you eat less well in order to waste less, and the founders treat zero waste as a standard any restaurant could reach, so the pleasure of the meal becomes the argument they use to make that case.

Nolla opens for dinner from Tuesday to Saturday, and the Chef's Choice menu is the easiest way in; beforehand, walk ten minutes west to the red brick Hyeta lahti Market Hall, where food stalls and small restaurants fill the old trading hall.
