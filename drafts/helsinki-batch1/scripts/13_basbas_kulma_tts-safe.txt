The odd name over this door comes from two nicknames, Baskeri and Basso, belonging to the two founders who have built a small restaurant family around this street corner.

Tehtahnkatoo, Factory Street, meets Telakkakatoo, Dockyard Street, right here, and Bas Bas Koolma occupies the corner itself, koolma being the Finnish word for corner, with a long bar counter inside and the founders' first restaurant, the Baskeri and Basso bistro, just upstairs at the same address.

Those founders are Neekolah Tyoolon, known as Baskeri, and Kalleh Kyookainen, known as Basso, who opened the bistro in twenty fifteen with a menu inspired by Parisian bistros, casual San Francisco joints and small Italian osterias, and it soon became a fixture on lists of Finland's best restaurants.

Koolma followed in twenty twenty as the more casual sibling, a busy wine bar of small plates and mostly natural wines, serving from late afternoon until around midnight, and the dishes regulars recommend run from charcuterie and cheese through salmon crudo and beef tartare to fried whitefish and grilled pork belly.

The wine is the real draw, chosen from growers the team knows and loves, and the Bas Bas list has collected Star Wine List awards, while the family has kept on growing, with a studio for pop ups in twenty twenty three and, in September twenty twenty six, a new place called Sirkus across the Long Bridge in Silta sahri, named after the travelling circuses that once stopped in the little park there.

The corner tells a neat story, since its two street names recall the factories and shipyard that once gave this end of town its living, and today its trade is sociability itself, a long counter where cooks, sommeliers and neighbours end up shoulder to shoulder after their own shifts are over.

Get here early if you want a seat at the bar, since walk ins are welcome but the room fills quickly, and afterwards stroll down Telakkakatoo towards Hyeta lahti, where the shipyard still builds icebreakers on the edge of the city centre.
