When this gallery opened in twenty twelve, it described itself with unusual precision as seventy two per cent art and twenty eight per cent coffee.

The ground floor space at number nine Annankatoo, here in Poonavwori at the heart of Helsinki's Design District, is Lokal, and from the pavement you can see into its two rooms, one of them set aside for a changing exhibition and the other for the work it sells.

Its founder, Katya Hahgelstam, spent around twenty five years photographing art, design and interiors for magazines and books before opening Lokal in April twenty twelve, the year Helsinki was World Design Capital, and she has said she wanted a home like room where art could be handled, used and passed down through the generations.

The coffee has since stepped aside, and what fills the space now is the work of more than two dozen Finnish makers, among them Matias Leematainen and Natalee Lowtenbaker, with ceramics, wood, glass, textiles, jewellery and paintings, many of them made in very small numbers.

Recognition followed fairly quickly, with the Finnish Design Deed award in twenty fifteen and the Finnish State Prize for Design in twenty seventeen, and in twenty twenty four a selection from Lokal crossed to Paris for a show at the Finnish Institute in the Latin Quarter, where eleven of its makers were shown side by side.

The point Lokal makes is a gentle one, that a bowl or a stool can carry as much thought as a painting, and that when you buy it to use every day rather than to hang on a wall, the art simply becomes part of how you live.

Ask what's on in the exhibition room, and then spend a little time in the surrounding streets of Poonavwori, where small design shops, studios and galleries fill the ground floors of the old apartment houses on almost every block.
