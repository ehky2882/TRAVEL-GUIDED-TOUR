Telling a genuine piece of mid century Finnish art glass from a convincing copy is a rare skill, and the man behind this small gallery has been sharpening it for more than forty years.

Yuryonkatoo's quieter stretch of stone apartment blocks is where number eight stands, and since Gallery Lemmetti opens by appointment only, its window may well be your view of it today, with glass, ceramics and lamps set out with the care of a private collection.

That man is Yoohani Lemmetti, who began collecting Finnish design in the early nineteen eighties and has since assembled private collections and supplied museums abroad. In nineteen ninety five he acquired the rights to put Ilmari Tapiovahras furniture back into production, and at this same address he once ran a design shop called Aero, which Artek later took over.

The gallery itself opened in twenty sixteen, and its heart is glass and ceramics from Finland's great post war decades, with art glass by Kai Frank, Teemo Sarpaneva and Oyva Toyka, ceramics by Toyni Moo ona, and the brass lamps of Pahvo Tunell. Among the pieces it has handled are an Alvar Aalto Savoy vase from nineteen thirty six and a rare nineteen sixties Bamboo vase by Toyka, which was tracked down in Australia and brought home.

Here's the thought worth taking away from a place like this: Finland's designers sent their glass all over the world, and some of the finest pieces have spent decades in homes abroad, so a gallery of this kind is partly a shop and partly a slow homecoming, carried out one vase at a time.

Call ahead to arrange a visit if you'd like to see inside, and in the meantime carry on along Yuryonkatoo to the swimming hall at number twenty one, whose nineteen twenties interior is a design treasure of an entirely different kind.
