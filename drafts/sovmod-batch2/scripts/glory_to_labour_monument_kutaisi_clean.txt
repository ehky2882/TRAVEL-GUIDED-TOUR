Glory to Labour Monument, Kutaisi
Central Kutaisi, Georgia · 42.270282, 42.703214

Most monuments ask you to stand back and look up, but this one, in the middle of Kutaisi, practically invites you to walk through it.

In the centre of the city, not far from the opera house and the city hall, you'll find a cluster of abstract stone blocks and figures with short flights of steps running in and around them, still carrying a Soviet star and a hammer and sickle, with inscriptions in Georgian and Russian.

It dates from 1980 and was made by the sculptors Elguja Amashukeli and Bastanashvili, and Amashukeli is a name worth remembering, because twenty-two years earlier he had made Kartlis Deda, the giant Mother of Georgia who stands over Tbilisi.

Look for the inscriptions honouring the workers and people of Kutaisi, with names and construction years cut into the stone. They were meant to tie the monument to the actual people who built the city's factories and housing in the Soviet decades, rather than to an abstract idea of the worker.

The monument has not been looked after, and pieces have crumbled away, one of the statues is missing, and graffiti has spread across the stone, so what was built to celebrate labour now quietly shows the wear of the years since that labour was organised by the state.

It was also built remarkably late, barely a decade before the Soviet Union came apart, which gives it a slightly poignant feel, an optimistic statement made just as the system behind it was running out of road.

That uncertainty is still live, because Georgia's laws against communist symbols raise real questions about whether pieces like this will stay. Climb the steps and walk through the middle of the composition while you can, and then cross the street, where a few good cafés give you a seat with a view of the whole thing.
