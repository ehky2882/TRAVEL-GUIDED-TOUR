Mother Armenia, Gyumri
Hill beside the Black Fortress, Gyumri, Armenia · 40.780500, 43.824800

Gyumri's Mother Armenia is younger than her sister in Yerevan and holds something quite different in her hands.

At the top of a long, two-sided staircase on a hill at the western edge of the city, right beside the round stone walls of the Black Fortress, the statue faces you, and from the bottom of the steps you can see stepped pools running up between the flights of stairs towards a steep, dark tuff pedestal with the copper figure on top.

She was unveiled on the ninth of May 1975, for the thirtieth anniversary of victory in the Second World War, when this city was still called Leninakan, and the figure alone is about twenty metres tall, standing on a pedestal of roughly the same height again.

Look at what she carries, because in one hand she holds a model of the spire of Zvartnots, the seventh-century cathedral near Yerevan, meant as a symbol of peace and of Armenian building, and in the other a palm branch, rather than the sword her Yerevan counterpart is famous for.

Halfway up the slope, to the left of the stairs, stands a plain column shaped like a cross, marked with the dates 1941 to 1945, and at the foot of the monument an eternal flame burns beside a raised clenched fist.

The quiet twist is on her back, where, according to the published descriptions, the sculptors carved a figure of Nemesis, the winged goddess of retribution, on the reverse, facing west towards the border, so the statue offers peace on one side and a warning on the other.

[beat]

Climb the stairs and walk round behind the pedestal to see that second figure for yourself. Afterwards, follow the path along the outside of the Black Fortress, whose circular walls date from the nineteenth century, and from its edge you can look back over the city and the plain stretching west.
