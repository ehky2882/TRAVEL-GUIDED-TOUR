Pripyat Amusement Park and Ferris Wheel
Central Pripyat, Chernobyl Exclusion Zone, Ukraine · 51.408133, 30.057125

The yellow Ferris wheel in Pripyat was due to start turning on 1 May 1986, five days after the accident at the Chernobyl power station a few kilometres away.

Behind the town's central square, past the Energetik Palace of Culture and the old Polissia hotel, the park opens out ahead, with the wheel and its yellow gondolas standing above the young trees and the bumper-car pavilion off to one side.

The park was built as a typical Soviet park of culture and rest, with attractions made by a firm based in Yeysk, in southern Russia, and it had five rides in all: the twenty-six-metre wheel, bumper cars, a spinning ride nicknamed the Chamomile, swing boats and a shooting gallery.

It was meant to open for the May Day holiday, but the town was evacuated before that could happen. The wheel was tested while it was being assembled, but it never carried the families it was built for.

Please keep to the paved ground, because radiation here is uneven, and the concrete areas are far cleaner than the moss and soil around them, which in a few spots measures among the highest readings in the town.

That's why this wheel has become the emblem of the whole disaster, because there's nothing technical about it at all, just a fairground ride waiting for a holiday that never came, and everyone who sees it understands that loss without needing it explained. Take a moment by the bumper cars, where the little cars still sit on their track, and then walk back through the square to the Palace of Culture, whose empty halls show how much of ordinary town life was simply left in place.
