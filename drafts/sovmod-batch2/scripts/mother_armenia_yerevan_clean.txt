Mother Armenia, Yerevan
Victory Park, Yerevan, Armenia · 40.195250, 44.524817

The pedestal under this statue was built for Stalin, and the architect, by his own later account, designed it knowing the dictator wouldn't stay up there for long.

Climb to the terrace at the edge of Victory Park, high above the city, and the copper figure is right in front of you, holding a sword horizontally across her body, on top of a massive stepped pedestal of dark stone, with old Soviet military hardware parked around its base.

Start with that pedestal, which the architect Rafael Israelyan completed in 1950 under a statue of Stalin by Sergey Merkurov, shaping the stone block, inside and out, like a simple three-aisled Armenian church, reasoning that the glory of dictators is temporary while a church endures.

He turned out to be right, because the Stalin figure came down in 1962, and five years later Ara Harutyunyan's Mother Armenia went up in its place, twenty-two metres of hammered copper, bringing the whole monument to more than fifty metres. Her model was a seventeen-year-old called Genya Muradyan, whom the sculptor met in a shop and persuaded to pose.

Look at the shield at her feet, because a persistent local story claims it hides Stalin's boots, left behind from the old statue, which isn't true, but it tells you how long the previous occupant has lingered in the city's imagination.

What has really changed is the meaning gathered around her, because inside the pedestal the military museum opened in 1970 to commemorate the Second World War, and today much of its space covers the Karabakh war of the early nineteen nineties, so the same stone now carries two different wars.

Before you go, walk to the edge of the terrace and look straight down the slope, because the statue sits on the main axis of Mashtots Avenue, directly above the Matenadaran manuscript library, at the top of one of the most deliberate lines in the city plan.
