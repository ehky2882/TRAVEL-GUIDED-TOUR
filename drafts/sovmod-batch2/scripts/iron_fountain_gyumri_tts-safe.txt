The Iron Fountain, Gyoomree

The earthquake that flattened much of this city in nineteen eighty eight knocked down nearly everything on this campus, and the strangest structure on it was one of the few things left standing.

On open ground at the edge of gyoomree, where the old Polytechnic campus used to be, you should find it rising from a dry basin in front of you, a cluster of rusted iron sheets opens outwards in great curved panels, like a pair of butterfly wings caught halfway through unfolding.

It was completed in nineteen eighty two and designed by artoor tarkhanyan, the same architect behind the genocide memorial at tsitsernakaberd and the circular terminal at zvartnots airport, and its official name was the Friendship Fountain. Locals, perhaps predictably, ignored that and nicknamed it the UFO, which, standing in front of it, seems the more honest description.

Have a look at the joints between the panels, where the weathering is heaviest. The fountain has been dry and neglected for more than thirty years, and the iron has turned the colour of autumn leaves, but the structure itself held together through the nineteen eighty eight earthquake while the campus around it came down.

That survival is why the fountain has quietly become a local symbol of resilience, something nobody planned when it was conceived as a decorative centrepiece for a university courtyard. Recent proposals from architects, championed by tarkhanyan's daughter anaheet, have suggested bringing it back to life, so its future is still being argued over.

Walk all the way round before you leave, because from the far side the wings line up and the whole thing looks almost ready for take off. Then head back towards the centre and the Black Fortress, where the Mother Armenia statue keeps watch from the top of a long flight of steps.
