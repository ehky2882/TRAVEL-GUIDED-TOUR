Monument of Armenian Gratitude to the Arab People

This is one of the few monuments anywhere built to say thank you to another people, rather than to celebrate a victory over them.

On the edge of Yerevan Lake, just off Admiral eesakov Avenue, the monument stands on a small plot of its own, a composition in pale orange tuff made of three separate pieces, with a stone eagle at one side, a tall upward reaching block beside it, and a third, flatter form whose outline traces the map of Syria.

The idea came from varoozhan salatyan, a member of the Armenian community in Damascus, as a gesture of gratitude to Syria and the Arab world for sheltering survivors of the genocide, many of whom ended up in aleppo and the desert towns around it. A design competition was held in nineteen ninety eight, building finished in two thousand and nine, and the official opening only came in the autumn of twenty twelve.

The three pieces are meant to be read together. The eagle stands for the strength of the Armenian people, the rising block for the nation's rebirth after nineteen fifteen, and the map of Syria for the friendship between the two peoples, all carved from tuff brought from armaveer.

It was made by a family, the mkurtchyan brothers, sculptors and an architect who had spent years working in Syria, carving decoration for Armenian churches in aleppo, so the monument was shaped by people who knew both sides of the story.

And here's what makes it worth a detour, more than two hundred donors in Armenia, Syria, France, Russia and the United States paid for it, so the gratitude it expresses comes from the diaspora as much as from the state.

When you've seen it, walk down to the shore of the lake and follow the path along the water, where the view back across the city towards the hills above Yerevan is one of the calmer ones in the capital.
