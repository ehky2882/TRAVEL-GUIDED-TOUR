Kadeenyacha Memorial Complex

On a mountain pass above oozheetseh, a single battalion of factory workers made the last stand of a short lived free territory, and this memorial is laid out along the ground they held.

At the pass between the villages of voloyats and zaglavak, standing at the start of the memorial path, you'll see the complex stretched out ahead of you, with its large white sculptural forms on the slope, a path leading uphill between them, and the forested hills of western Serbia on every side.

In the autumn of nineteen forty one the partisans held a liberated area around oozheetseh, known as the oozheetseh Republic, and on the twenty ninth of November that year the Workers' Battalion under andreeya joorovich fought a far larger force here, in a stand that cost almost all of them their lives.

The first memorial, a pyramid above an ossuary holding their remains, went up in nineteen fifty two. The complex you see now was designed by the sculptor miodrag zheevkovich and the architect aleksandar jokich, and teeto opened it on the twenty third of September nineteen seventy nine.

It's arranged in three parts, which you meet in order, starting with an amphitheatre dedicated to the oozheetseh Republic, then an avenue named after the Workers' Battalion, and finally a high plateau of freedom, so the walk itself becomes the story, ending in open sky.

That sequence is the quiet genius of the place, because it lets you climb the hill before you reach the meaning, much as the battalion would have done. Lines by the oozheetseh poet slavko vookosavlyevich are carved on the memorial, and the Memorial Home holds more than three hundred objects, from documents to photographs. Look out too for the park of eighty eight trees planted in nineteen eighty four, one for each year of teeto's life, opposite the monument.
