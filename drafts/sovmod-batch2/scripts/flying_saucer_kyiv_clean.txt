The Flying Saucer, Kyiv
Antonovycha Street 180, Lybidska Square, Kyiv, Ukraine · 50.411278, 30.525056

The architect of this building hid its supports on purpose, so that the saucer on the corner would look as if it were hovering just above the pavement.

On Lybidska Square, at the end of Antonovycha Street, look for the round, disc-shaped hall resting at the foot of a tall slab block, and you're looking at the building Kyiv simply calls the Flying Saucer.

It was finished in 1971, designed by the architects Florian Yuriev and Lev Novikov for the Institute of Scientific and Technical Information, and the complex combines three parts, a long two-storey building, a sixteen-storey tower, and the saucer itself, which holds a lecture and cinema hall.

The design grew out of the 1960s, when space flight and science fiction made the future feel close enough to touch, and Yuriev had hoped the saucer would house a light and music theatre. The hall has remarkable acoustics, able to carry the whole range of the human voice, though it ended up as the library's lecture hall rather than a concert venue.

By the 1990s the building was decaying, and in 2015 plans for a vast shopping centre next door threatened to swallow it, until a campaign to save Kyiv's modernist buildings took up its cause, with Yuriev himself writing an open letter.

[beat]

The happy part is that the campaign worked, and in October 2020 the ministry of culture added the Flying Saucer to the national register of architectural monuments, so a building designed to look like it had landed from the future has been formally given a future of its own. Walk round the disc to find the angle where its supports disappear, and then follow Antonovycha Street north towards the city centre, where more of Kyiv's post-war modernism lines the route.
