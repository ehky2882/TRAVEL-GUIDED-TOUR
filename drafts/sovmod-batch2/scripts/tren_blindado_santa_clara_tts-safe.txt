Tren Blindahdo, Santa Clara

In the last days of December nineteen fifty eight, a bulldozer did more to decide the Cuban Revolution than any single gun.

Beside the railway on aveneeda leeberasyon, just past Santa Clara's station depot and near a level crossing, the memorial park spreads out in front of you, with several old railway wagons tipped at angles beside the track, a bulldozer raised on a plinth, and a group of concrete obelisks set at odd slants.

The government of foolhensyo bateesta had sent an armoured train from Havana loaded with soldiers, weapons and supplies, hoping to turn the battle for Santa Clara. It stopped at the foot of the loma del kapeero hill, and on the twenty ninth of December a small force of guerrillas under Che Guevara used a bulldozer to rip up about thirty metres of track, so that when the train tried to move, it ran off the rails.

That bulldozer is the one in front of you, and the wagons are the derailed cars themselves, now used as the rooms of a small museum. The soldiers on board surrendered that evening, and a remarkable detail of the day is that many of them were soon fraternising with the men they'd been fighting only hours earlier.

The memorial was designed by the sculptor hosay delarra, who also worked on the great Che Guevara memorial across town. His slanted obelisks are meant to evoke the moment of the explosion and derailment, frozen in concrete.

It's worth noticing how domestic the whole thing feels. Instead of a triumphal column, the decisive moment of the revolution is told through a farm machine and a row of damaged wagons, which makes the story both more ordinary and more vivid.

Step into the wagons to see the photographs and weapons inside, and then look for the loma del kapeero, the hill nearby where the train halted before the attack, which is worth the short climb if you have the energy for a view over the city.
