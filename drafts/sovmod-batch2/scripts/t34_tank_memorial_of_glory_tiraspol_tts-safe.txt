T thirty four Tank, Memorial of Glory, tiraspol

The tank on this plinth didn't arrive as a museum piece, because it fought its way here, rolling home from Hungary at the end of the Second World War.

At the Memorial of Glory on Twenty Fifth of October Street, look for the green tee thirty four, eighty five raised on its stone plinth near the eternal flame, with the memorial walls, the small chapel and the rows of graves behind it.

The tank came back from Hungary in nineteen forty five under the command of Lieutenant boris sergayev, and it was later set up here as a monument, so it stands as a real veteran rather than a replica.

The memorial itself was opened in nineteen seventy two, designed by the architects fishbine and fyfermakher, to honour the war dead of nineteen forty one to nineteen forty five, and later generations kept adding to it, with a memorial to soldiers killed in Afghanistan in nineteen ninety five, a Wall of Memory in two thousand and nine and twenty ten, and a chapel dedicated to Saint George in twenty eleven.

Some of the graves here belong to people who died in the conflict of nineteen ninety two, when the region on this bank of the neester broke away from Moldova, and that war remains a painful and contested memory on both sides of the river.

That layering is what makes the place worth your time, because a single memorial ground now holds three separate wars, each generation adding its own, and the tank at the front ties them back to the oldest of the three. The fact that Transnistria is recognised internationally as part of Moldova makes the memorial a sensitive place, so please move quietly and respectfully. Walk through to the chapel at the back, and then cross to the riverside park along the neester, just a short stroll to the south.
