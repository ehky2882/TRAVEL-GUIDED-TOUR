Kadinjača Memorial Complex
Kadinjača pass, near Užice, Serbia · 43.912030, 19.742250

On a mountain pass above Užice, a single battalion of factory workers made the last stand of a short-lived free territory, and this memorial is laid out along the ground they held.

At the pass between the villages of Volujac and Zaglavak, standing at the start of the memorial path, you'll see the complex stretched out ahead of you, with its large white sculptural forms on the slope, a path leading uphill between them, and the forested hills of western Serbia on every side.

In the autumn of 1941 the partisans held a liberated area around Užice, known as the Užice Republic, and on 29 November that year the Workers' Battalion under Andrija Đurović fought a far larger force here, in a stand that cost almost all of them their lives.

The first memorial, a pyramid above an ossuary holding their remains, went up in 1952. The complex you see now was designed by the sculptor Miodrag Živković and the architect Aleksandar Đokić, and Tito opened it on 23 September 1979.

It's arranged in three parts, which you meet in order, starting with an amphitheatre dedicated to the Užice Republic, then an avenue named after the Workers' Battalion, and finally a high plateau of freedom, so the walk itself becomes the story, ending in open sky.

[beat]

That sequence is the quiet genius of the place, because it lets you climb the hill before you reach the meaning, much as the battalion would have done. Lines by the Užice poet Slavko Vukosavljević are carved on the memorial, and the Memorial Home holds more than three hundred objects, from documents to photographs. Look out too for the park of eighty-eight trees planted in 1984, one for each year of Tito's life, opposite the monument.
