kartlis deda, Mother of Georgia, Tbilisi

The woman standing over Tbilisi holds a bowl of wine in one hand and a sword in the other, and which one you get depends on why you've come.

Up on the sololakee ridge, along the path from the nareekala fortress, kartlis deda towers above you, a twenty metre silver figure of a woman in Georgian dress, gazing out over the old town and the river far below, with the bowl held out in her left hand and the sword in her right.

She went up in nineteen fifty eight, when the city celebrated fifteen hundred years since its founding, the work of the sculptor elgooja amashookelee. The name means Mother of the Kartvelians, the Georgians, and amashookelee's own title for the work was simply Capital, which says a lot about how closely the statue and the city were meant to be identified.

Look at what she's carrying, because the bowl of wine is for those who arrive as friends, and the sword is for those who arrive as enemies, a summary of Tbilisi's long history of welcoming travellers and fighting off invaders that nearly everyone in the city can recite.

The first statue was a temporary one, made of wood, intended only for the anniversary celebrations. It proved so popular that it stayed, and in nineteen sixty three the wood was sheathed in aluminium to protect it from the weather.

By the late nineteen nineties that version was replaced with a new figure, and the change is subtle but telling, because her dress was given shorter, more modern sleeves and her headdress was simplified, so the mother of the nation was quietly updated for independent Georgia.

Walk along the ridge past her towards the botanical garden side, and you'll get a profile view of the bowl and sword against the sky. If you came up by the cable car from reekeh Park, consider walking down through the old streets of sololakee instead, which brings you out right in the heart of the old town.
