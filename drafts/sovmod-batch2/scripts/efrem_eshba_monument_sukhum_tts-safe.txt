Monument to efrem eshba, Sookhoom

The marble figure in front of you was carved by the subject's own daughter, for what would have been his eightieth birthday, thirty four years after he was executed.

Near the seafront in the centre of sookhoom, close to the old city council building, you'll find the monument, a pale marble figure on its pedestal, framed by trees and the low buildings of the town, a few minutes' walk from the water.

efrem eshba was an Abkhaz revolutionary, born in eighteen ninety three, and one of the key figures in establishing Soviet power in Abkhazia after the First World War. In the purges of the nineteen thirties he was accused of Trotskyism, arrested in nineteen thirty six and executed in nineteen thirty nine, one of countless loyal party members who disappeared in those years.

In nineteen seventy three his daughter, the sculptor marina eshba, made this monument in marble for the eightieth anniversary of his birth. So the statue is a public memorial and a private act of remembrance at the same time, a daughter restoring her father to the city.

The monument had a hard second life, too, since it was badly damaged during the war of nineteen ninety two and nineteen ninety three, and it was reconstructed in the middle of that decade, which means part of what you see today is a repair of a memorial to a man the state itself had once tried to erase.

What strikes you, standing here, is how many layers of loss a single figure can carry, from the purges, through the rehabilitation, to the war and the rebuilding afterwards, without any of it being written on the plinth. When you're ready, walk down to the seafront promenade nearby, where the long line of palms and the old pier give you a sense of how grand this resort city once intended to be.
