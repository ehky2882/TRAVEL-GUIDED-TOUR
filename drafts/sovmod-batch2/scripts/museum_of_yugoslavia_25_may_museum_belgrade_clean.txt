Museum of Yugoslavia and the 25 May Museum, Belgrade
Dedinje, Belgrade, Serbia · 44.786667, 20.451667

For his seventieth birthday, the city of Belgrade gave Josip Broz Tito a museum, and this is the building it built to hold his presents.

Up the hill in the leafy district of Dedinje, at the entrance to the museum park, the 25 May Museum is the light, modernist pavilion ahead of you, and the paths beyond it lead through the trees to the House of Flowers, where Tito is buried.

The museum opened on 25 May 1962, the date celebrated as Tito's official birthday, and its architect, Mihailo Janković, designed it to display the gifts the president had received from across Yugoslavia and around the world.

The real treasure here is the batons, which came from the Relay of Youth, a run held every year across the whole country, in which young people carried a carved baton from hand to hand before presenting it to Tito on his birthday. Thousands of them survive, made of wood, metal and stone, and each one is a small piece of folk art.

The House of Flowers was added in 1975 as a winter garden, and after Tito's death on 4 May 1980 it became his resting place, where his wife Jovanka was later buried too.

What the site has become depends rather on who's visiting, because for some it is a shrine to the better years they remember, especially every 25 May, while for others it is simply the best place to understand a country that no longer exists. The museum itself, now one of the most visited in Serbia, leaves that judgement to you. Do spend a few minutes in the House of Flowers, and then look in the Old Museum next door, where the collection widens out from Tito himself to the everyday culture of Yugoslavia.
