Morača Sports Centre, Podgorica
Right bank of the Morača River, Podgorica, Montenegro · 42.437778, 19.254167

On a match night, six thousand people pack into the main hall here, and almost all of them have come to cheer on a single club, Budućnost Podgorica.

Facing the complex from the street, on the right bank of the Morača River in the newer part of the city, the big main hall is the building straight in front of you, with the smaller halls and the open-air pools spread out around it across several hectares of ground.

Construction began in 1978 to a design by the architect Miško Dimitrijević, and it was built by two firms from Belgrade, back when Podgorica was still called Titograd and was the capital of a Yugoslav republic rather than of an independent country.

The opening game set the tone for what was to come, a friendly between the Yugoslav national basketball team and an American college side, Wichita State. Since then the hall has hosted six group games at EuroBasket 2005, rounds of the European women's handball championship, and a long list of concerts by the biggest names from across the former Yugoslavia.

The pools tell a story of their own, because water polo grew so popular in Montenegro that two new open-air pools were added in 2009, while an old indoor pool, unused for over a decade, was later turned into a second hall with room for 2,200 spectators.

What I like about the place is that it never became a monument to an era, since it simply kept being used, renovated in 2005 and again in 2018 for top-level European basketball, and renamed in 2024 as the M Tel Morača Arena after its sponsor. Check the fixture list before you go, because a Budućnost home game is the best way to see the building doing what it was made for, and afterwards the riverside path along the Morača makes a pleasant walk back towards the centre.
