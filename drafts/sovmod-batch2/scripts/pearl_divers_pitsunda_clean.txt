The Pearl Divers, Pitsunda
Seafront, Pitsunda, Abkhazia · 43.155819, 40.349400

Of all the things the Soviet Union built on its Black Sea coast, one of the most cheerful is a pair of naked swimmers caught in the air.

On the seafront at Pitsunda, where the relict pine grove runs almost to the beach, the sculpture stands in front of you, two bronze figures, a young man and a young woman, frozen mid-dive and stretched out above their base, about three metres tall, with the open sea right behind them.

They were made in 1969 by the Georgian sculptor Irakli Ochiauri, as part of the vast resort built here in the sixties on the orders of Nikita Khrushchev, designed by the architects Mikhail Posokhin and Ashot Mndoyants, and put up by more than three thousand workers. The sculpture was meant to celebrate the start of the swimming season, the moment each summer when the beaches filled.

Look at how little is holding the figures up. They seem to lean off the plinth into empty air, which is exactly the point, because the whole composition is about that half second before you hit the water.

The nudity is the other thing people notice, and it's said the piece never ran into trouble with the censors, which tells you something about how seaside leisure was imagined at the time, as healthy, athletic and entirely wholesome.

Strip away the title and the divers were never really about pearls at all, because they advertised a promise made to Soviet workers, that a holiday by a warm sea was part of the deal, and they became the symbol of this resort within a few years.

[beat]

The divers are still the place where everyone in Pitsunda arranges to meet. Walk along the promenade beneath the pines towards the high-rise resort buildings, the boarding houses of the same Soviet resort project, and you'll see how the sculpture and the architecture were planned as one holiday landscape.
