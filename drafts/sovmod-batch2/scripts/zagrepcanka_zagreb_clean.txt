Zagrepčanka, Zagreb
Savska 41, at Vukovar Avenue, Zagreb, Croatia · 45.798333, 15.962222

Less than ten years after this tower opened, slabs of marble started falling off it, and for a while the staff had to walk in through a tunnel of planks.

At the corner of Savska Street and Vukovar Avenue, Zagrepčanka towers over the junction, an office building in three parts, a central slab flanked by two slightly lower wings that curve gently away from it, all faced in white stone and bands of green reflective glass, with a radio mast on the roof.

The architects were Slavko Jelinek and Berislav Vinković, who took their inspiration from the Thyssen-Haus in Düsseldorf, and it opened on the twenty-third of December 1976. At almost ninety-five metres, or a hundred and nine with the mast, it was for decades the tallest office building in Croatia.

Look up at the top of the central slab, where a crown of pointed projections runs around the uppermost floors, and then at the wings, which are curved along their height, so the whole tower looks slightly different from every angle. Inside, the decoration included mosaics by the painter Edo Murtić.

The marble is the twist, because the architects used Carrara marble for the cladding, a stone that turned out to cope badly with Zagreb's winters, and heavy tiles began coming loose, especially on the sunny south face, which is why employees had to enter through a protective tunnel of wood and steel. By 2014, about five thousand square metres of facade had been replaced on the east and west sides.

There's a cheerful postscript, too, because since 2012 the building has hosted an annual race up its staircase, and people now queue to run to the top of a tower that once had to protect them from its own walls. From here, it's a short walk south-east to Richter's three Rockets, completed eight years earlier, which make an interesting contrast in how differently two generations of Zagreb architects imagined height.
