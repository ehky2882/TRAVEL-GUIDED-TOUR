T-34 Tank, Memorial of Glory, Tiraspol
Memorial of Glory, 25 October Street, Tiraspol, Transnistria (Moldova) · 46.835507, 29.608870

The tank on this plinth didn't arrive as a museum piece, because it fought its way here, rolling home from Hungary at the end of the Second World War.

At the Memorial of Glory on 25 October Street, look for the green T-34-85 raised on its stone plinth near the eternal flame, with the memorial walls, the small chapel and the rows of graves behind it.

The tank came back from Hungary in 1945 under the command of Lieutenant Boris Sergeev, and it was later set up here as a monument, so it stands as a real veteran rather than a replica.

The memorial itself was opened in 1972, designed by the architects Fishbein and Faifermacher, to honour the war dead of 1941 to 1945, and later generations kept adding to it, with a memorial to soldiers killed in Afghanistan in 1995, a Wall of Memory in 2009 and 2010, and a chapel dedicated to Saint George in 2011.

Some of the graves here belong to people who died in the conflict of 1992, when the region on this bank of the Dniester broke away from Moldova, and that war remains a painful and contested memory on both sides of the river.

That layering is what makes the place worth your time, because a single memorial ground now holds three separate wars, each generation adding its own, and the tank at the front ties them back to the oldest of the three. The fact that Transnistria is recognised internationally as part of Moldova makes the memorial a sensitive place, so please move quietly and respectfully. Walk through to the chapel at the back, and then cross to the riverside park along the Dniester, just a short stroll to the south.
