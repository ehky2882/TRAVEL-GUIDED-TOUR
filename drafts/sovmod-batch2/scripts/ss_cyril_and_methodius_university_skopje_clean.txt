Ss. Cyril and Methodius University Campus, Skopje
University campus, central Skopje, North Macedonia · 42.000400, 21.443400

Architects writing about this campus often reach for an underwater image, comparing it to a coral reef cast in concrete.

Standing in the campus grounds between the faculty buildings, look around and you'll see clusters of concrete cylinders and rounded towers growing out of one another, pierced with circular windows, all linked by walkways and lower blocks that run between them.

The university itself has deep roots, with a first faculty of philosophy opening in December 1946, but the earthquake of 26 July 1963 destroyed much of Skopje and left the university without its buildings and its laboratories. Help came from abroad, through UNESCO, and a new campus was planned for the rebuilt city.

The design you're standing in was produced by the Slovenian architect Marko Mušič and completed in 1974, and its cylinders were meant to read as a cluster of related forms rather than one single building, a little like organisms growing side by side.

Those round windows are the gift here, because once you notice them, you'll find them everywhere, peering out like portholes from walls that curve away from you in every direction.

[beat]

What the campus offers is an argument about what a university should be, with faculties that grow together rather than standing apart, and that idea was made all the more pointed by a city that had just watched its buildings fall. Today it is the oldest and largest university in the country, and the concrete reef is still full of students. Before you leave, follow the walkways from end to end and then look back at the campus from across the street, where the cylinders finally gather into a single, slightly unruly skyline.
