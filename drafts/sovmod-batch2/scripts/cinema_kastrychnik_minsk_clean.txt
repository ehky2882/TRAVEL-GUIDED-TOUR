Cinema Kastrychnik, Minsk
Independence Avenue 73, Minsk, Belarus · 53.922389, 27.596350

There's a cinema on Independence Avenue that was designed to look like a piece of a giant broken column, and it has kept live parrots in its lobby.

Set back from the line of the avenue, Kastrychnik sits behind its own forecourt, and as you face it you should see a rounded upper drum, ribbed vertically like a fluted column that has been sliced off at the top, resting on a low glass ground floor fronted by a row of white marble pillars, with staircases running along a gallery on three sides.

Its name means October, and it opened on the thirtieth of January 1975, on the site of an older cinema called the Zorka, with the premiere of a war film called Blokada. The architect was Valentin Malyshev, and inside the drum is the largest auditorium in the city, arranged as a steep amphitheatre facing a single seamless screen twenty-two metres wide.

In 1976 alone, more than two million tickets were sold here, which tells you how central an evening at the pictures was to life in Soviet Minsk.

Two smaller details reward the visit, starting in the lobby, where among the exotic plants, a large cage holds live parrots, while outside stands a bronze figure of a postman, added in 2005, assembled from a hundred separate pieces.

And here's a nice footnote: Leonid Bortkevich, the singer of Pesniary, the most famous Belarusian band of the Soviet era, later said he helped design this cinema as his diploma project at architecture school, which is the kind of story Minsk loves to tell about itself.

[beat]

Climb the side stairs to the gallery, where you can look along the ribs of the drum up close. Then walk west along Independence Avenue towards the centre, which is lined almost the whole way with the grand post-war ensemble the avenue is famous for.
