Chay Gevara Memorial, Santa Clara

chay gevara's statue in Santa Clara is said to face south towards Bolivia, where he died, rather than towards the city he helped to capture.

On the wide parade square at the edge of the city, the memorial rises ahead of you, a seven metre bronze figure of gevara on a tall granite pedestal, in a battered jacket with a rifle held low, and beneath him the words asta la victoria see em pray, until victory, always. On either side, large stone blocks are carved with reliefs and long inscriptions.

The complex was begun in nineteen eighty two and opened in December nineteen eighty eight, for the thirtieth anniversary of the battle of Santa Clara, the last great fight of the Cuban Revolution, the work of several architects alongside delarra, the sculptor of the armoured train memorial across town.

Look at the reliefs, where one shows gevara with feedel Castro and kameelo syenfwaygos in the syerra mai estra mountains, another shows him at work as a minister of industry, and one of the long blocks carries the full text of his farewell letter to Castro.

Underneath the statue is a museum of his life, with personal objects including his asthma inhaler, and beside it the mausoleum. His remains, found in Bolivia, were brought here in October nineteen ninety seven with those of fellow fighters killed there in nineteen sixty seven, and an eternal flame burns inside.

It's worth holding two things at once here, since in Cuba this is a place of real devotion, and many visitors come as pilgrims, while elsewhere gevara's legacy is judged in very different ways, and the memorial, unusually for a monument, has to carry both kinds of visitor through the same quiet rooms.

The mausoleum is a quiet space, so go in slowly. Afterwards, head to the opposite side of the square and look back, since from that distance the statue and the carved blocks line up as intended, with the whole city stretching away to one side.
