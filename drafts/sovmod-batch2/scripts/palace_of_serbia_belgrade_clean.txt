Palace of Serbia, Belgrade
Bulevar Mihajla Pupina, New Belgrade, Serbia · 44.820278, 20.427778

This building has carried four different names, and it has never been officially renamed once, which tells you a fair amount about the country that built it.

Between Bulevar Mihajla Pupina and Bulevar Nikole Tesle, with the wide lawn in front of you, the palace stretches out as a long, pale, symmetrical building, its central block flanked by two wings that give the whole plan the shape of a letter H.

The design came out of a competition held just after the war and won by a team of Zagreb architects led by Vladimir Potočnjak, and work began among the very first buildings of New Belgrade, but it stopped in 1949, leaving only the concrete skeletons of the wings standing. When construction resumed in 1955, the architect Mihailo Janković took over, and he finished the building and its interiors in 1959.

The pause matters, because the original scheme leaned towards the monumental style of Soviet architecture, while by the time Janković picked it up, Yugoslavia had broken with Moscow and the design moved closer to modernism, so you're looking at a building that changed its mind halfway through.

It opened properly in 1961 for the first summit of the Non-Aligned Movement, and Tito kept an office here.

[beat]

So the names on this building have followed the borders around it. On the official register it is still the Palace of the Federal Executive Council, in the 1990s it was the Palace of the Federation, and since 2006 most people have called it the Palace of Serbia, while ministries carry on working inside. Walk round to the side wings to see the length of the H for yourself, and then head down to the Danube, where the riverside path leads past the site of the old Hotel Jugoslavija.
