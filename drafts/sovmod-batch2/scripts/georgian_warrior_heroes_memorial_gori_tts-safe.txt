Memorial of Georgian Warrior Heroes, Goree

These eight bronze warriors spent a quarter of a century guarding a grave in Tbilisi before they were moved to sit at the foot of a fortress.

Below the walls of goree Fortress, on the slope beneath the old citadel, the memorial lies in front of you, eight seated bronze figures arranged in a circle on blocks of stone, with the fortress walls rising behind them on the hill.

They were made between nineteen eighty one and nineteen eighty five by the sculptor georgee ochiaoori, known as Gogi, and the work was called Requiem. For years they sat in Tbilisi's vahkeh Park, four on each side, around the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier, keeping watch over a Second World War memorial in the capital.

In two thousand and nine the whole group was brought here to goree, a year after the war of August two thousand and eight brought fighting to this town, so a set of figures created to mourn one war now sits in a place marked by another.

Walk slowly around the circle and look at each warrior in turn. They aren't heroic in the striding, flag waving sense, they sit still, heavy and inward looking, more like men at a wake than soldiers on parade, which is presumably why the sculptor called them a requiem.

Nearby, a single stone with small niches holds candles, which visitors light as they pass, and that tiny ritual does more than any inscription to show that the memorial is still in use.

What changed with the move is the backdrop, because in vahkeh Park the warriors faced a flame, and here they face a medieval fortress and the town that grew up beneath it, which makes their mourning feel older and wider than any single war. Climb the path up to the fortress walls when you're done, and from the top you can look down on the circle of warriors and out across the Shida Kartli plain.
