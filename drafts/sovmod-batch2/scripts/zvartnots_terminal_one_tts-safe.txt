zvartnots Airport, Old Terminal one

Few airports have a building that people in the capital will sign petitions for, and this round terminal, empty since twenty eleven, is one of them.

From the public forecourt of zvartnots airport, look past the newer glass terminal towards the older building beside it, and you should see a broad, low ring of concrete with a single tall tower rising from its centre, a bit like a flying saucer that has landed and put up its aerial.

It was designed after an open competition in nineteen seventy, won by a team that included artoor tarkhanyan, the architect behind the genocide memorial on tsitsernakaberd and the Iron Fountain in gyoomree, working with the structural engineer Sergei bagdasaryan. Passengers checked in around the ring, and planes pulled up to gates spread around its edge, so the whole airport worked like a wheel turning around its control tower.

The terminal carried Armenia's air traffic through the last Soviet years and the first decades of independence, until the new building next door took over, and since then it has been closed and largely unmaintained, with cracks appearing in the concrete of the tower.

In August twenty twenty five it was granted protection as a monument of national significance, which campaigners had been asking for over many years. More recently, however, the government began proceedings to remove that status, so that expansion plans for the airport could include partial or complete demolition.

So you may be looking at a building whose future is being decided right now, which makes it worth a proper look while the ring is still whole. Before you head inside to check in, walk to the far end of the forecourt, where the curve of the old terminal is easiest to follow, and you can see how much of the original idea still survives behind the newer additions.
