Che Guevara Memorial, Santa Clara
Plaza Ernesto Che Guevara, Santa Clara, Cuba · 22.402636, -79.979383

Che Guevara's statue in Santa Clara is said to face south towards Bolivia, where he died, rather than towards the city he helped to capture.

On the wide parade square at the edge of the city, the memorial rises ahead of you, a seven-metre bronze figure of Guevara on a tall granite pedestal, in a battered jacket with a rifle held low, and beneath him the words Hasta la victoria siempre, until victory, always. On either side, large stone blocks are carved with reliefs and long inscriptions.

The complex was begun in 1982 and opened in December 1988, for the thirtieth anniversary of the battle of Santa Clara, the last great fight of the Cuban Revolution, the work of several architects alongside Delarra, the sculptor of the armoured train memorial across town.

Look at the reliefs, where one shows Guevara with Fidel Castro and Camilo Cienfuegos in the Sierra Maestra mountains, another shows him at work as a minister of industry, and one of the long blocks carries the full text of his farewell letter to Castro.

Underneath the statue is a museum of his life, with personal objects including his asthma inhaler, and beside it the mausoleum. His remains, found in Bolivia, were brought here in October 1997 with those of fellow fighters killed there in 1967, and an eternal flame burns inside.

It's worth holding two things at once here, since in Cuba this is a place of real devotion, and many visitors come as pilgrims, while elsewhere Guevara's legacy is judged in very different ways, and the memorial, unusually for a monument, has to carry both kinds of visitor through the same quiet rooms.

The mausoleum is a quiet space, so go in slowly. Afterwards, head to the opposite side of the square and look back, since from that distance the statue and the carved blocks line up as intended, with the whole city stretching away to one side.
