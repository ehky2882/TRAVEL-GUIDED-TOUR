blok Twenty Three, New Belgrade

In New Belgrade the neighbourhoods don't have names so much as numbers, and blok twenty three is one of the best places to see what that system was trying to achieve.

From milenteeya popovicha Street on the eastern edge of the block, look in and you'll see two long, twelve storey slabs, known locally as peteetsa and sheshteetsa, the Five and the Six, with a group of slender tower blocks rising beyond them and low buildings filling the gaps in between.

Each block was planned as a small town in its own right, so inside this one you'll find a primary school named after the poet laza kostich with more than nine hundred pupils, two nurseries called Little Bee and Butterfly, tennis courts, bakeries and a couple of supermarkets.

The long slabs run around the edge of the block, and the kitchens and bathrooms of many flats face inward on to internal courtyards, so the noise of the boulevards stays outside while the life of the household turns towards the centre.

Have a look at the scale of the green space between the buildings, because that open ground was not leftover land at all, and it was the whole point, giving every family light, air and room for children to play.

It's easy to read these blocks as anonymous, and yet the people who live here gave the two big slabs nicknames of their own, which is a fair sign that the place became home rather than simply housing. As a residential area, it's best enjoyed from the public paths, so walk through to the south side and look back at the towers, then carry on towards the larger blocks further south, where you can see how the same idea was repeated across the whole of the plain.
