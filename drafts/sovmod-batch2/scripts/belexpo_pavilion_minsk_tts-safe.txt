Bel Expo Exhibition Pavilion, Minsk

The roof of this pavilion was supposed to be green, so the whole building would look like a flower that had just opened, and it ended up black because the right materials couldn't be imported.

Facing the pavilion from the corner of Victors Avenue and arlowskaya Street, beside the State Flag Square, you should see a low exhibition hall under a roof made of great curved, pointed segments, each one rising and dipping like a petal, with no columns holding them up from inside.

The decision to build a new home for international exhibitions in Minsk came in nineteen seventy five, and the hall went up between nineteen eighty three and nineteen eighty eight, designed by a team of architects including Leonard moskalevich, with the engineers Leonid Volchetsky and Sofia Moiseeva working out the structure.

Start with the roof, because the ministry originally asked for a conventional hall propped up on columns, the architects refused, and they persuaded the client to let them build curved shells, hyperbolic parabolloids in engineering terms, with steel cables stretched between them and concrete poured over the cables. It was the first building in the country made with that kind of cable structure, which is why getting permission for it was such a struggle.

There used to be a tall clock stele out front, topped with a statue of Mercury, and the composer eegor loochenok wrote music for the clock to play, music that never actually sounded. The stele was taken down in twenty thirteen, when the flag square was built in its place.

Notice, too, that the boldest part of this building came from architects saying no to a simpler brief. Walk along the side of the pavilion to see the petals rise and fall against the sky, then turn towards the giant flagpole on the square, standing where the clock once kept its silent time.
