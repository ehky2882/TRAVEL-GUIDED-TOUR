M R T Centre, Skopyeh

For three decades the tallest building in this country belonged not to a bank or a hotel, but to the national broadcaster.

Look for the tall, chunky concrete tower that steps outwards as it climbs, with blocks of floors jutting out from its sides at different levels, and once you've found it, you're facing the headquarters of Macedonian Radio and Television.

It stands about seventy metres high, with twenty five floors, and the architect kiril atsevskee saw it through from the early nineteen seventies until it was finished in nineteen eighty four. From that year until twenty fifteen, when a pair of new skyscrapers overtook it, no building in the country stood taller.

Have a look at the way the upper sections cantilever out over the street, with heavy volumes seeming to hang off the central core, because that's the building's signature move and the reason it reads so differently from the flat towers around it.

The tower was built as a working studio complex, with radio and television operating from the same address, so for a generation it was quite literally the place from which the national voice was broadcast.

The idea behind the tower is that broadcasting deserved a landmark as visible as the signal it sent out, and in a city rebuilding itself after the earthquake, the sight of this concrete block on the skyline was a public promise of a confident, modern future. It still does its old job today, which is more than many of its contemporaries can say. The skopyeh railway station, with its long concrete viaduct, lies within easy walking distance and makes a natural next stop if you want to see how the post earthquake city was put together.
