Khatyn Memorial
Khatyn, Lahojsk District, Minsk Region, Belarus · 54.335187, 27.944105

Every thirty seconds a bell sounds over this field, and it keeps doing so all day, every day.

The memorial lies in open ground about sixty kilometres north of Minsk, and from the entrance path you should see a tall bronze figure of a man holding a child in his arms, and beyond him a wide, quiet space where low concrete outlines mark the footprints of houses, each one with a grey chimney rising from it and a small bell at the top.

This was the village of Khatyn, and on the twenty-second of March 1943 it was destroyed by German occupation forces in reprisal for a partisan attack nearby. One hundred and forty-nine people were killed, seventy-five of them children, and the village was never rebuilt, so the memorial opened here in July 1969 stands on the ground where it was.

The bronze figure is Iosif Kaminsky, the village blacksmith, one of the very few survivors, holding his son Adam, and the sculptor Sergei Selikhanov made him six metres tall, while Kaminsky himself spoke at the opening ceremony in 1969.

Walk out among the chimneys, where each house carries the names and ages of the people who lived in it, and then find the cemetery of villages further on, where rows of identical graves each represent a whole village that was burned and never came back, a hundred and eighty-five of them in total.

That is the shift this place makes, from one village to all of them. Khatyn stands for more than six hundred Belarusian villages destroyed with their inhabitants during the war, and the design keeps widening the frame until the scale becomes very hard to hold in your head.

This is a place for quiet, so take your time. Near the eternal flame, look for the three birch trees growing beside it, with the flame itself standing in for a fourth, a reminder that around one in four people in Belarus did not survive the war.
