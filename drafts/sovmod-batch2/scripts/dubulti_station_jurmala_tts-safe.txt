Doobooltee Station, Yoormala

At a small seaside station on the line out to the Baltic coast, the Soviet railways built what was claimed to be the most modern station building in Latvia.

On the platform at doobooltee, or on the forecourt beside it, look up at the station building in front of you, a low station hall crowned by a sculptural shell of concrete that rises in a curve, like a wave caught at the moment it breaks.

A station has stood here since September eighteen seventy seven, when the line from Riga to the coast first opened, and doobooltee was one of its original stops. The present building dates from nineteen seventy seven, a century later, the work of eegor yaveyn, who intended the whole structure to conjure up the waves of the sea a few minutes' walk away.

Look at how the concrete is handled, because instead of a flat roof over a waiting room, the shell rises and curves, so that a building made of the heaviest material imaginable looks as though it could roll away down the track.

At the time it opened, it was celebrated as the most modern station in Soviet Latvia, a showpiece for the thousands of holidaymakers who poured out of Riga every summer to the beaches of yoormala.

What's rather lovely is how the building has found a second life, because the hall now doubles as Art Station doobooltee, a contemporary art space, so travellers waiting for the Riga train may find themselves standing in an exhibition. Step inside if it's open, and then walk north from the station through the pines to the beach, about ten minutes away, where the real waves will show you exactly what the architect had in mind.
