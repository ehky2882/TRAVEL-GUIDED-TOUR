Sljeme TV Tower, Medvednica
Sljeme summit, Medvednica, above Zagreb, Croatia · 45.899444, 15.948056

In the autumn of 1991, the Yugoslav air force attacked this tower, and Zagreb's television carried on broadcasting anyway.

Near the summit of Sljeme, the highest point of Medvednica mountain above Zagreb, the tower stands in front of you, a slim shaft of reinforced concrete with a cluster of platforms partway up and a long steel antenna on top, a hundred and sixty-nine metres in all, standing among the forest at just over a thousand metres above sea level.

Work began in 1973, and the tower went into service in 1976, timed to mark fifty years of radio and twenty years of television in Zagreb, carrying two television channels and three radio stations from the start. Long before that, this summit had a twelve-metre iron observation tower, built in 1889, the same year as the Eiffel Tower, which was later moved to the peak of Japetić in the Samobor hills.

The attack in 1991 came during Croatia's war of independence. Bombs heavily damaged several floors and knocked the tower out of action, but the two staff on duty sheltered at its base and were unhurt, and because an attack had been expected, low-power backup transmitters were already in place around the city.

It took three months to repair the damage, and the tower became a small symbol of the city refusing to go quiet.

There's a café and an observation deck at around eighty metres, and on a clear day the view runs across the whole of Zagreb and the plain of the Sava far beyond.

What makes it more than a transmitter is that the city has come to recognise itself by it, because the tower is often the first thing you see of Zagreb as you approach from any direction. If the deck is open, go up, and if not, walk the forest paths along the ridge, where the tower keeps appearing between the trees.
