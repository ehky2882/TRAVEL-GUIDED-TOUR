Eternity Memorial Complex, Chișinău
Pantelimon Halippa Street, Chișinău, Moldova · 47.009167, 28.833056

Five stone shapes the height of an eight-storey building lean together over an eternal flame here, and they're meant to be rifles, one for each year of the war.

Along Pantelimon Halippa Street, the memorial rises ahead, five tall pointed blades of stone, about twenty-five metres each, gathered into a pyramid, with a five-pointed star at their base and a flame burning in its centre.

It was inaugurated on Victory Day in 1975, three decades after the war ended, in memory of the Soviet soldiers who died in the Iași–Chișinău offensive of August 1944. The architect was Minaev, and the reliefs were carved by the sculptors Maiko and Poniatovsky. In Soviet times it was simply the Victory Memorial, and it was renamed Eternity after independence.

Walk down the avenue leading to the flame, where six large reliefs show the main stages of the war, from invasion to victory, and look for the soldiers of the Moldovan army standing guard, who usually change every hour.

The complex has gathered new meanings since 1991, and close by stands a memorial to those who died in 1992 defending Moldova's independence and territorial integrity, so the site now honours two very different wars, fought under two different flags.

It has had its share of neglect too, restored in 2006 before ownership passed in 2024 to the city of Chișinău, whose mayor described it as rundown and promised renovation.

This is a Soviet victory monument that a small independent country has decided to keep, guard and extend rather than remove, which is not the choice every neighbour has made. Take the path around the back of the pyramid, and then walk the length of the relief avenue again in the other direction, because the story reads differently when you follow it from victory back to invasion.
