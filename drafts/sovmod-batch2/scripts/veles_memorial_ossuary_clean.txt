Memorial Ossuary, Veles
Above the Vardar River, Veles, North Macedonia · 41.723333, 21.789278

A long flight of concrete steps climbs the hillside here, and at the top waits a building that seems to have been cracked open from the inside.

Starting at the foot of the stairs on the slope above the Vardar River, look up and you'll see the memorial at the top of the climb, a heavy shell of reinforced concrete split into four separate curved pieces, with the river valley and the town of Veles stretched out below and behind you.

It was built in 1979 and 1980 by three men, the architect Savo Subotin, the sculptor Ljubomir Denović and the painter Petar Mazev, and between them they turned a burial place into something close to a complete museum, with a crypt, a hall of remembrance with sculpture, and an exhibition of documents from the war years.

The usual reading of the shape is that it represents a German army helmet, broken into four, which turns the whole structure into an image of the occupying force shattered. Once you've seen it that way, it's quite hard to see anything else.

Inside and around the walls, Mazev's mosaics show scenes from the partisan struggle, and they bring a warmth of colour to a building that is otherwise almost entirely grey.

[beat]

The trick of the design is that it never shows you a hero, a soldier or a flag, and lets an enemy's helmet, split apart, carry the whole message about who won. The remains of fighters and civilian victims from Veles and the surrounding area rest within, so it remains a grave as well as a sculpture. Take your time on the way back down the steps, and pause halfway to look along the Vardar, where the old town climbs both banks of the gorge.
