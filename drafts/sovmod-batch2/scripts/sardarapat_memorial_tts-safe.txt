sardarapat Memorial

On a hill where the land falls away towards the Turkish border, the Armenian Soviet government built a memorial that, quite openly, celebrated a battle won by a republic the Soviets had replaced.

From the car park, climb the steps towards the entrance, where two enormous winged bulls carved from red toofa stone face each other on either side of the path, and beyond them, rising above the square, you should see a tall open bell tower built like a lattice, with twelve bells hanging inside its frame.

The battle fought here in late May nineteen eighteen was the decisive stand of that spring, when Armenian troops and local volunteers stopped the Ottoman army short of Yerevan, and the memorial opened in May nineteen sixty eight for its fiftieth anniversary. It was designed by the architect rafa el izrayelyan, with sculpture by ahra harootyoonyan, arsham shaheenyan and samvel manassyan.

The bell tower is about twenty six metres tall, and the twelve bells are rung every year on the anniversary of the victory. Further along, a pair of winged lions guard the long processional route, deliberately ancient in style, as though the memorial were older than the state that built it.

It's worth sitting with how unusual that was, because a Soviet republic in the late sixties was given permission to commemorate a national army, a national victory and, by extension, a national survival, which tells you how much the fiftieth anniversaries of nineteen fifteen and nineteen eighteen mattered to Armenians at home and abroad.

Next to the monument is a museum of ethnography and the liberation movement, and if you have time, go inside, and then come back out to the top of the steps, because on a clear day the whole ararat valley lies in front of you, which is exactly the land that battle was fought to keep.
