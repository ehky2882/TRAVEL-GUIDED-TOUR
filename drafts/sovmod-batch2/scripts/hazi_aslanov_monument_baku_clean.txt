Monument to Hazi Aslanov, Baku
Upland Park, Baku, Azerbaijan · 40.357258, 49.829393

This monument is also a grave, which is easy to miss if you arrive expecting only a statue.

Upland Park climbs the hill above the old city and the bay, and you should find the monument on one of its paths, set among trees, with the curve of Baku Bay and the city spread out below.

It marks the burial place of Hazi Aslanov, a tank commander from Lankaran in the far south of Azerbaijan, who rose from a brick-factory job to the rank of major-general in the Soviet armoured forces and was killed in January 1945, a few months before the end of the war. The monument was put up over his grave in 1949, by the sculptor Fuad Abdurahmanov, and it was thoroughly restored in 2012.

Aslanov was made a Hero of the Soviet Union in his lifetime, and the second time he received the title came much later, in 1991, awarded by Mikhail Gorbachev after years of lobbying by Heydar Aliyev. That makes him the only Azerbaijani to hold the title twice, which is the detail most people in Baku will tell you about him.

That late second award changes how the monument reads. It was raised in the high Stalin years as a Soviet war grave, and in the very last months of the Soviet Union it was quietly turned into a point of Azerbaijani national pride, without a single stone being moved.

Spend a moment at the grave before you walk on, and then continue uphill through the park towards the ridge, where Baku's main memorial alley and the viewing terraces look out over the bay, because the whole hill has long been the place where the city chooses to remember its dead.
