Hotel Zlateebor, Oozheetseh

The people of oozheetseh have a nickname for this hotel, seevonya, which roughly means the grey one, and once you've seen it you'll understand why.

On Partisans' Square in the centre of town, look up at the tall concrete tower in front of you, which rises fifteen storeys above the square in a heavy, sculpted mass of grey, and you're facing the Hotel zlateebor.

There had been a Hotel zlateebor in oozheetseh before the war, named after the nearby mountain that's popular for winter sports, but after the old building was pulled down the town noticed that fewer visitors were coming. The answer was a new hotel, begun in nineteen seventy five and opened on the twenty fourth of September nineteen eighty one, to a design by the Montenegrin architect svetlana kana radevich, who had already made her name with the Hotel Podgorica.

Take a moment with the facade and watch how the grey concrete changes as you move, because the tower was clearly designed to be walked round and seen from every corner of the square, not simply admired from the front.

When it opened it had one hundred and forty eight rooms and was considered remarkably luxurious, and the new railway line from Belgrade to Bar, with a station close by, kept it busy.

The nickname sounds unkind, and yet it's the kind of teasing people reserve for a building they've decided is theirs, since the hotel became the setting for the town's dances, weddings and gatherings and a symbol of what oozheetseh could offer. Walk round the full square to see the tower from each side, and if you have the afternoon, the road south west climbs to zlateebor mountain itself, the place that gave the hotel its name.
