Macedonian Academy of Sciences and Arts, skopyeh

After the earthquake of nineteen sixty three left much of skopyeh in ruins, a new republic needed a home for its scholars, and this low, careful building beside the river is what it got.

On the embankment beside the vardar, with the river on one side, the academy sits ahead of you, a long, horizontal building of concrete and glass, its upper storey overhanging the level below, and between its wings you can glimpse a courtyard set with sculpture.

The academy itself was founded on the twenty third of February nineteen sixty seven, with the poet and linguist blazheh koneskee among its leading figures, and it was meant to be the country's highest institution for the sciences and the arts. Its building, designed by the architect boris cheepan, was completed in nineteen seventy six, at a time when much of the centre was still being rebuilt.

Notice how restrained it is compared with the bolder concrete experiments elsewhere in the city, because instead of reaching upwards it stays low and spreads out, keeping its back to the traffic and opening towards the courtyard and the river.

That courtyard is where the building's sense of hospitality shows, since its sculptures make the space feel more like a small garden for thinking than a ceremonial forecourt.

What I find rather touching is that the academy chose modesty in a city that had every excuse to build big, and that the young institution put its trust in a quiet courtyard rather than in a monumental staircase. In a centre now crowded with newer statues and colonnades, that understatement stands out more than it did in nineteen seventy six. When you're ready, follow the embankment upstream along the vardar to the old Stone Bridge, which marks the way into the Old Bazaar, the one quarter of the centre that came through the earthquake largely recognisable.
