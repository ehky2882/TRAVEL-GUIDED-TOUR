Museum of Yugoslavia and the Twenty Fifth of May Museum, Belgrade

For his seventieth birthday, the city of Belgrade gave yosip broz teeto a museum, and this is the building it built to hold his presents.

Up the hill in the leafy district of dedeenyeh, at the entrance to the museum park, the Twenty Fifth of May Museum is the light, modernist pavilion ahead of you, and the paths beyond it lead through the trees to the House of Flowers, where teeto is buried.

The museum opened on the twenty fifth of May nineteen sixty two, the date celebrated as teeto's official birthday, and its architect, mihailo yankovich, designed it to display the gifts the president had received from across Yugoslavia and around the world.

The real treasure here is the batons, which came from the Relay of Youth, a run held every year across the whole country, in which young people carried a carved baton from hand to hand before presenting it to teeto on his birthday. Thousands of them survive, made of wood, metal and stone, and each one is a small piece of folk art.

The House of Flowers was added in nineteen seventy five as a winter garden, and after teeto's death on the fourth of May nineteen eighty it became his resting place, where his wife yovanka was later buried too.

What the site has become depends rather on who's visiting, because for some it is a shrine to the better years they remember, especially every the twenty fifth of May, while for others it is simply the best place to understand a country that no longer exists. The museum itself, now one of the most visited in Serbia, leaves that judgement to you. Do spend a few minutes in the House of Flowers, and then look in the Old Museum next door, where the collection widens out from teeto himself to the everyday culture of Yugoslavia.
