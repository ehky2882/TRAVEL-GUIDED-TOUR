Rikhter's Skyscrapers, the Rockets, Zagreb

Zagreb calls these three towers the Rockets, and the shape that earned them that name was partly the result of an earthquake in another city.

In the vurbik neighbourhood, close to slavonska Avenue, the three residential towers stand in a row in front of you, tall concrete slabs whose silhouettes, seen from below, look rather like booster rockets waiting on a launch pad.

They were designed in the nineteen sixties by the Centar fifty one studio, a team of four architects, berislav sherbetich, lyooboh iveta, vyentseslav rikhter and olga korenik, and they were finished in nineteen sixty eight. rikhter, the best known of the four, was also a sculptor and theorist, which is probably why the towers carry his name.

The earthquake was in skopyeh, in nineteen sixty three, when much of the Macedonian capital was destroyed, and afterwards the design was reworked to make sure the towers could survive a similar shock, which is what gave them their distinctive shape.

There's a second, more practical story in the heights. All three were meant to be identical, but it was found that the outer two would block the path of signals from the television and radio transmitters, so they were built lower, and only the central one reaches about seventy metres.

What you're looking at, then, is an icon shaped as much by engineers and broadcast signals as by any architect's sketch, which is rather typical of the best Yugoslav modernism, rigorous and practical but still with a sense of drama. Walk round to the open ground beside the towers to see them lined up side by side, and then look north along the avenue, where several other landmark towers of the same era punctuate the Zagreb skyline.
