Cinema Moskva, Minsk
Victors Avenue 13, Minsk, Belarus · 53.909803, 27.547589

The giant block hanging over the entrance of this cinema is the auditorium itself, and it has been held up with extra steel cables for years.

Across Victors Avenue from the Palace of Sports, the cinema faces you behind a small square with a fountain at each end, and above the long glass wall of the foyer, a solid, windowless box juts out over the pavement, faced with metal panels and dotted with lamps of coloured glass.

It opened on the nineteenth of September 1980 as the first wide-screen cinema in Belarus, and at the time the largest in the republic, designed by the architects Viktor Kramarenko, Vladimir Shcherbina and Mikhail Vinogradov. The box over your head contains the main hall, with more than a thousand seats, cantilevered out over the foyer, so you walk in underneath the audience.

Inside, on the far side of the building, the smaller hall's foyer holds a large stained-glass work titled Moscow, Capital of Our Motherland, by the artists of the Vashchenko family, which is protected today as a monument of art.

The twist is structural, because according to the city's own designers, the supports holding up that overhang were reinforced incorrectly during construction, cracks appeared, and steel cables were added to hold it, and by 2019 a plan was approved in principle to wrap the whole thing in a new glass and metal facade.

What makes the story unusual is who argued about it, since one of the original architects presented the reconstruction, while his two co-authors opposed it and more than twelve hundred people signed a petition to keep the building as it was, so the cinema's future was debated by the very people who designed it.

If it's open, buy a ticket or just step into the foyer to look for the stained glass. Then cross back towards the Palace of Sports and look at the two buildings together, because they were designed to face each other along the avenue.
