Sava Centar, Belgrade
Milentija Popovića 9, Blok 19, New Belgrade, Serbia · 44.808500, 20.430300

The architect of this building was given just one month to come up with the whole concept, and he spent much of it on aeroplanes.

On Milentija Popovića Street, in New Belgrade's Blok 19, the long, low congress building in front of you is Sava Centar, spread out horizontally beneath its broad roof, with the hotel tower of the Crowne Plaza rising just to the north.

In 1975, after the Helsinki conference on security and co-operation in Europe, Tito agreed that Belgrade would host the next meeting, but the city had nowhere big enough. In March 1976 the task went straight from Tito's office to Stojan Maksimović, chief designer of the city's construction office, who toured congress halls in Paris, The Hague, Copenhagen and Helsinki before producing his design.

Building took eleven months, and Tito opened the first stage on 14 May 1977, just in time for the conference that began that June. The great hall followed, built for the congress of the League of Communists, and it remains the largest auditorium in the former Yugoslavia, seating around four thousand people.

Here's the detail that's easy to miss: the plans called for a revolving stage, but it was never installed, and a large fixed stage went in instead.

[beat]

Sava Centar was built for diplomacy and stayed on as the place where Belgrade goes to concerts, festivals and conferences, and in 2021 the state recognised that by declaring it a cultural monument, before a major reconstruction that reopened it in 2024. If the foyer is open, step inside and take in how generous the approaches to the hall are, and then walk north past the Crowne Plaza, which began life as the InterContinental, built alongside the centre for a World Bank meeting in 1979.
