In nineteen seventeen, the city renamed a narrow street called Pine.

It became North Michigan Avenue three years before there was any way to get onto it from Michigan Avenue. The bridge wasn't built. The street it had just been named after stopped dead at the river, a block and a half short.

The name came first. The street was made to fit the name afterward, widened, cleared, bridged, and then renamed a second time by a real estate man who thought it needed better branding. Everything you're about to look at was labeled before it was finished.

Almost everything.

This walk goes half a mile north, from the river to an old stone water tower, in four stops. It's flat, it's entirely on sidewalk, and there are no stairs and no admission of any kind. It is also one of the busiest half miles in the city, so if you're doing this between about noon and six you'll be sharing it. Nothing about the walk depends on the crowd being thin. The things worth looking at are all above eye level or across the street.

The avenue keeps going north after we stop, another several blocks to Oak Street. We're going to end early, and deliberately, at the one thing on this street that nobody thought up first.

Start at the south end of the bridge, with the water in front of you.
