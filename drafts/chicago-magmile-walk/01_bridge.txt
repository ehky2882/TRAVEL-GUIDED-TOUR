ATLAS — CHICAGO
WALK 4: THE MAGNIFICENT MILE
Segment 01 — The Bridge
Vantage: south end of the DuSable Bridge, Michigan Avenue at Wacker Drive

---

Michigan Avenue used to end here. Not narrow, not bend — end.

South of you it's a boulevard. North of you, before 1920, there was no version of this street at all: Pine Street was a modest thing of workshops and rooming houses, and if you wanted to reach it you walked a block west to Rush Street and took your turn on a swing bridge with everybody else, which in practice meant standing there a while.

Point yourself north, across the water.

The thing that changed it was a book. Daniel Burnham and Edward Bennett's Plan of Chicago, published in 1909, proposed connecting Michigan Avenue to Pine Street with a bridge, then widening Pine to match. The concept was approved around 1913, the street was renamed in 1917, and construction started the following spring. It opened in May of 1920.

Bennett designed the bridge himself, which is unusual — the man who co-wrote the proposal got to build the one piece of it everything else depended on. He modeled it on the Pont Alexandre the Third, over the Seine. You can see the argument he was making: two decks, stone-faced towers at all four corners, a river crossing dressed as a civic monument rather than a piece of traffic equipment.

Widening the street on the far side cost something. Buildings that stood in the way had their fronts sliced off to the new curb line; others were simply removed.

*[beat]*

Most streets accumulate. They start as a track to somewhere and thicken over a century. This one was issued — proposed on paper, approved by committee, and then constructed underneath its own name.

The first thing built on it is directly across the river, and it's white. Cross the bridge and stop in the plaza at the far end.
