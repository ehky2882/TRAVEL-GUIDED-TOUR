Bay ah Mosque

This mosque is easy to miss. It is also one of the oldest standing places of worship anywhere near Makkah.

The Bay ah Mosque lies in Meena, below the slopes near the Jamaraht Bridge, at the foot of the Akaba hill. You should be looking at a small, walled mosque with an open courtyard inside, standing quite apart from the modern structures around it.

Start with its age. It was built at the request of the Abbaasid caliph Aboo Jaafar al Mansoor, in seven hundred and sixty one, which makes it more than twelve and a half centuries old.

Then what it marks. This is the place where, before the migration to Madinah, the Prophet Muhammad met a group of people from Madinah, the Ansaar, and they swore him their allegiance, the pledge of Akaba. Bay ah means pledge, and the mosque takes its name from that promise.

And here's what I find remarkable. A short walk away, the Jamaraht Bridge moves hundreds of thousands of people an hour, and the tent city of Meena stretches across the valley. This modest courtyard, open to the sky, has outlasted almost everything that ever stood around it, simply by being small and staying put.

Walk round the outside before you go, and notice how little there is of it. Then follow the road down towards the Jamaraht, and notice how quickly the twentieth century takes over again.
