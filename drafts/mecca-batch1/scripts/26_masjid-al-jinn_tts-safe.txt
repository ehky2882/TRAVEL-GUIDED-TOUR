Masjid al Jinn

The name means the Mosque of the Jinn, and it records one of the stranger audiences in the story of Islam.

Masjid al Jinn is in al Hajoon, about nine hundred metres north of the Grand Mosque, just east of the Moo alla cemetery. You should be looking at a neighbourhood sized mosque on Masjid al Harahm Road, modest beside the towers to the south.

Start with the story. According to tradition, it was here that the Prophet Muhammad recited the Koran to a gathering of jinn, the unseen beings of Islamic belief, and they accepted Islam. The opening verse of the seventy second chapter of the Koran, Soorat al Jinn, describes a company of jinn who listened and said they had heard a wondrous recitation. The tradition adds that the Prophet drew a circle around his companion Abdullah ibn Masood, and told him to stay inside it until the gathering was over.

Then the other name. It is also called Masjid al Harras, the Mosque of the Guards, after the night watchmen of old Makkah.

The building itself has been rebuilt more than once. A mosque has stood here since at least the third century of the Islamic calendar; it was renovated in nineteen seventy nine and rebuilt again in two thousand and one, under King Fahd, in a modern Islamic style.

And that is rather the pattern of this whole quarter. The stories are very old, the walls are very new, and the sense of place is carried almost entirely by the name.

From the mosque, cross over to the walls of the Moo alla cemetery next door. It is the resting place of some of the Prophet's closest family, and it is a good deal quieter than the road outside.
