Conrad Jabbal Omar Makkah

Until two thousand and six, this was a hillside of old houses. What you are looking at is the hill's replacement.

The Conrad stands in Jabbal Omar, the district rising just south west of the mosque plaza, along Jabbal Omar Street. Look for a pair of hotel towers set into a dense cluster of new buildings that crowds right up to the edge of the plaza.

Start with the name. Jabbal Omar was a real hill on the old southern edge of the city, covered in housing that had grown up piecemeal over generations. In late two thousand and six a clearance began, and a development company was set up with an initial budget of one point six billion dollars and plans for more than ninety buildings, with Foster and Partners and Buro Happold among the designers.

The Conrad was the fourth hotel to open here, after the Hilton, the Hyatt Regency and the Marriott, and it was the first Conrad anywhere in Saudi Arabia. It has four hundred and thirty eight rooms across its two towers, sixty two of them suites, and most look towards the Harahm.

Inside, the designers reached back to the city that was cleared. There are mashrabeeya screens, the carved wooden latticework that once shaded Hijaazi windows, an inner courtyard in the manner of a traditional sahn, chandeliers patterned with leaves, and Koranic verses worked into the walls and ceilings.

And that is the tension of the place. The old houses are gone, and their vocabulary has come back indoors as decoration. The grand plan has not been finished either: as of two thousand and twenty four, the original vision was still unrealised.

Walk down to the south west corner of the mosque plaza if you fancy it. That is where the fifteen towers of the Jabbal Omar complex meet the sanctuary, and you can see the whole idea of the project in a single glance.
