Jabbal Thowr

Heera is the mountain of the beginning. Thowr is the mountain of the escape.

Jabbal Thowr rises in the south of Makkah, beyond the district of Misfala, about eight kilometres from the Grand Mosque. From its base, look up: a high, steep, rocky peak, around seven hundred and fifty metres above sea level, with the path to the cave winding up its face.

Start with the story. In six hundred and twenty two, as the Prophet Muhammad left Makkah for Madinah, he and his companion Aboo Bakr hid in a cave near the top of this mountain while the Koraish searched for them. The cave is Ghaar Thowr, and the journey that followed, the Hijra, is the event from which the Islamic calendar is counted.

Then the climb. It is long and steep, so carry water and set off early in the day, before the heat.

And that's what's worth sitting with. Heera marks where the revelation began; Thowr marks the moment it nearly ended, hidden in a crack in the rock with pursuers outside. Two caves, a few kilometres apart, holding the first and the most frightening moments of the story.

From the top, look north. Somewhere in the haze is the Clock Tower above the Harahm, standing over the city the two travellers left behind.
