Les Étoiles de Renaudie

Every flat in this building has a garden. In social housing built around nineteen seventy, that was almost unheard of.

The centre of Ivry-sur-Seine, a short walk from the Mairie d'Ivry metro. In front of you, a heap of raw concrete that seems to have grown rather than been built: pointed balconies jutting out at sharp angles, terraces stepping back floor by floor, and plants spilling over almost every edge. At street level there are shops. That's the Centre Jeanne-Hachette.

Some background first. In the nineteen sixties the town decided to pull down its run-down centre and start again. The architect Renée Gailhoustet led the project, and she brought in Jean Renaudie to work alongside her. Building began in nineteen sixty-nine and ran for about six years, and the star shapes you're looking at made Renaudie's name.

Have a look at the terraces. Because the plan is a star rather than a box, every floor throws out points and corners, and each of those becomes somebody's outdoor space. That's why the whole thing looks so green. The residents did the planting, and over fifty years they've rather outdone the architects.

Then look at how it's stacked. Shops on the ground and first floors, offices above them, and homes on top, all folded into the same structure. Because the floors are staggered, the flats inside come in a huge variety of layouts. You could live here for years and never visit two neighbours with the same front room.

And here's the point of it. The standard estate of the period stacked one identical flat on top of another. Gailhoustet and Renaudie argued that ordinary people deserved what the wealthy took for granted: light, a view, and a piece of ground of their own, even on the fifth floor.

[beat]

It hasn't all aged gracefully. The shopping centre slid into decline for the best part of thirty years, and in two thousand and twenty-one the town began working on bringing it back. But stand here in late spring, with everything in leaf, and it's hard not to think they were onto something.

If you've got a few minutes, walk to the Danielle Casanova block, a few streets away. It's another of Renaudie's stars, and seeing the second one is the best way to understand the first.
