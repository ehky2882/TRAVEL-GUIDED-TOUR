Leebrereeah Leebreeda Bottayga

Across the river from the old centre, just past one of Turin's grandest churches, there's a bookshop where the books hang from the ceiling.

On Corso Kasahleh, where it meets Veeah Feleecheh Romahnee in Borgo Poh, Leebreeda Bottayga has a small shopfront a few steps from the great domed church of the Gran Mahdreh dee Deeoh. Behind you, the bridge leads back across the Poh to Piazza Veettoreeo Venneto, and above you rises the green slope of the hill.

Look in through the window and you'll see books and lights suspended from the ceiling on threads, so that the room seems to float, which gives it a gentle, lantern lit feel once you're inside.

The shelves explain the name, which means the hybrid workshop, because the shop mixes books, mostly from independent publishers rather than the bestseller lists, with handmade things: bags, backpacks and jewellery by local makers sit beside the novels and picture books.

This is also very much a neighbourhood place, with book crossing shelves where you can leave one book and take another, a vote for the books that changed customers' lives, and readings, small theatre shows and games for children.

What a shop like this does, really, is give a neighbourhood a living room. The grand church a few steps away is built for ceremony, while this little bookshop is built for conversation, and Borgo Poh is the richer for having both on the same corner.

Before heading back over the bridge, climb the steps of the Gran Mahdreh and turn round, because the view across the river to Piazza Veettoreeo and the Mohleh is one of the best in the city. If you've more energy, the road up the hill behind leads to the Monteh day Kappoocheenee.
