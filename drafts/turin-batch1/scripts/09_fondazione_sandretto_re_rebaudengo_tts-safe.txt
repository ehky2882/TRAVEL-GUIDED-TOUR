Fondatsyohneh Sandretto Reh Rebowdengo

Some of the most adventurous contemporary art in Italy is shown in a quiet residential street on the western side of Turin, a long way from the baroque squares most visitors stick to.

Out here in Borgo San Powlo, an old working neighbourhood, Veeah Modahneh is lined with apartment blocks, and at number sixteen the Fondatsyohneh Sandretto Reh Rebowdengo stands apart from them as a long, low, deliberately plain building with very little decoration at all. The entrance is at street level, and there's a cafe and bookshop inside.

The building deserves a proper look before you go in. The architect Clowdyo Silvestreen designed it, and it opened in two thousand and two in a district that grew up around Turin's factories; the plainness is the point, because the rooms are meant to step back and let whatever is being shown take over.

Behind the collection is one person's eye: Patreetsya Sandretto Reh Rebowdengo began collecting in nineteen ninety two and set up the foundation in nineteen ninety five, and she has tended to buy the work of artists early, often while they were still young and largely unknown, working directly with them rather than through the safer routes of the market.

The foundation also runs a strong education programme, with mediators in the galleries whose job is to talk with visitors about the work, and that's free during exhibitions.

What makes this place matter is its choice of address. It could easily have taken a palatso in the centre, and instead it planted itself in a neighbourhood shaped by industry, which says something clear about who contemporary art is meant to be for.

Exhibitions change through the year and the opening days are limited, so check what's on before making the trip. Once you're out here, walk the surrounding streets of Borgo San Powlo, where traces of the old workshops and factory buildings still give the district its character.
