Hotel Victoria Torino

It's 1961, and on a quiet side street a few minutes from Turin's grand squares, a small hotel opens with an unusual ambition: to feel like a Victorian English house that has wandered somewhere sunnier.

Cross to the far pavement of Via Nino Costa, a short and peaceful street, and face number four, where Hotel Victoria has a frontage that looks more like a private residence than a hotel. Through the door you'll glimpse the lobby, all warm colours and soft lamplight.

The lobby and the sitting rooms off it set the tone. The owners, Mia Rolla and Riccardo Vallinotto, have filled them with what they love — Victorian comfort, French and Italian art, and touches borrowed from colonial interiors — so there are deep sofas, floral fabrics, oriental pieces and books left out for guests, and each room has its own decorative theme.

Picture next the breakfast room, which is designed as an indoor winter garden. Its showpiece is a historic panel of Zuber wallpaper, one of the French scenic papers printed from carved wooden blocks that were once the height of fashion in grand houses.

Downstairs is the third surprise, the Iside Spa, named after the Egyptian goddess Isis as a nod to the Egyptian Museum nearby, with a heated pool long enough to swim proper lengths in, which is a rarity in a city-centre hotel.

The idea that ties all this together is that a hotel can be a collection rather than a product. Nothing here comes from a corporate design manual; it's the accumulated taste of particular people, and that has become rather unusual in the world of hotels.

From the door, it's a short walk to Via Lagrange, one of the city's elegant pedestrian shopping streets, and from there the Egyptian Museum that inspired the spa is only a few minutes further north.
