Madamadorè

Behind this shopfront is a quietly old-fashioned trade — dressing a room in fabric — carried on in a city that has always taken its interiors seriously.

Madamadorè's windows, at number forty-three Via Giolitti in the Borgo Nuovo, are full of fabric swatches, rolls of wallpaper and the odd upholstered chair. Stand where you can see in, and you'll notice books of samples piled on the tables inside.

The walls and the sample books are the heart of it. Madamadorè specialises in textiles and wallpapers for the home, drawn from high-end makers, and it's one of the Turin stockists of Pierre Frey, the French family firm known for richly patterned fabrics and papers; some of the designs on display are the sort you'd normally see in a chateau or a grand hotel.

Just as important is the work that happens around the samples. The showroom collaborates with seamstresses, upholsterers and decorators, so this is a place where you choose a fabric and someone then turns it into curtains, cushions or a recovered sofa.

That's worth pausing on, because Turin was a royal capital for centuries, and its palaces were lined with silk, damask and painted paper. The skills that kept those rooms looking their best — measuring, cutting, stitching and stretching fabric over a frame — haven't disappeared; they've moved into shops like this one, serving apartments rather than palaces.

So the real story of Madamadorè isn't fashion at all, but continuity: the same appetite for beautiful rooms that built the royal residences, scaled down to the size of a living room on a Turin side street.

[beat]

The showroom is closed on Sundays and Mondays. From the door, wander a few minutes north to Piazza Cavour, a small square with a garden laid out on a gentle mound, and then on towards Via Po, where the long arcades lead down to the river.
