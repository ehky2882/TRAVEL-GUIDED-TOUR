Pastry Goostahvo Fahtish

Underneath this pastry shop, down a staircase from the back room, there's a small museum of old chocolate making machines.

Number forty two Veeah Sahkkee, a short walk south of Porta Nwova station, is an elegant Art Nouveau building designed by Peeyetro Fennolyo, the architect who brought that flowing, decorative style to Turin, and Fahtish has its name above the windows on the ground floor. From the opposite pavement you'll see the building's curves and ironwork, and the cakes lined up in the window.

The shop's history starts in nineteen fifteen, when the master pastry chef Goostahvo Fahtish opened here, and the business went on to become a supplier to the royal household, a title given to a select few Turin shops that still carries some weight in this city.

In the window and on the counter, look for the Festeevo, the best known of the cakes, created in the nineteen sixties with layers of cocoa meringue and chocolate cream. The savoury pastries are serious too, and in twenty twenty three the guide Gambairo Rosso named them the best in Italy.

Down in the basement, the chocolate museum holds machines from the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, a reminder that Turin was one of Europe's great chocolate cities long before it became famous for cars.

Recent years haven't been easy for Fahtish, since in twenty twenty the family that ran it went into voluntary bankruptcy and the premises went to auction, and the city held its breath a little, because places like this are woven into Turin's identity; happily, the shop carried on under new owners, and it's still making the Festeevo.

Ask inside whether the chocolate museum is open, because it's worth the short climb down. Then walk north along Veeah Sahkkee towards Porta Nwova, and within a few minutes you'll reach Piazza Karlo Feleecheh and its gardens.
