Floris House Torino

There's no big shop sign on the outside of this place, and that's quite deliberate, because the surprise is meant to happen once you're through the door.

Floris House is set into a late nineteenth-century palazzo in the historic centre, and from the street you'll see a handsome stone frontage, tall windows and a doorway that gives very little away; only when you step inside does the colour start.

The shop floor comes first, and it's part perfumery and part home store, with creams and make-up, scented candles, silk flowers and fresh ones, and objects for the house, gathered from big beauty names like Laura Mercier as well as small artisan producers such as Officina delle Spezie. Everything is arranged in bright clusters, so the room feels less like a shop and more like a conservatory that someone has filled with presents.

Further in, you'll find the café, which lives in a room lined with mirrors. It serves breakfast, tea and light meals through the day, and the mirrors do what mirrors in Turin's cafés have always done, which is to double the light and make a modest room feel generous.

The clever thing about Floris House is that it's built for lingering. It mixes scent, flowers, food and furniture so that buying something becomes almost incidental, and in a city famous for its historic cafés it offers a modern version of the same old pleasure: a beautiful room where you can sit for rather longer than you meant to.

[beat]

Floris House closes on Mondays, so plan around that. When you come out, wander towards Piazza Carignano, a few minutes away, where Turin's grandest brick palace and its oldest gelato shop face each other across the cobbles, and where a Pinguino makes a fine follow-up to afternoon tea.
