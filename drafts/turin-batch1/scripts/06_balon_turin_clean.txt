Dealers Association Balôn - Balôn Turin

People have been buying and selling second-hand things in these streets, on and off, since 1857, which makes the Balôn one of the oldest flea markets in Italy.

Via Borgo Dora runs downhill here from the Porta Palazzo end towards the river, and on a Saturday it's lined with stalls, awnings and shopfronts piled with furniture, lamps, frames and boxes of old postcards. Off to one side is the Cortile del Maglio, a large covered courtyard where more dealers set up under a high roof.

Glance first at the shops behind the stalls, because there are around fifty permanent ones in these few streets — Via Borgo Dora, Via Lanino, Via Mameli and Via Canale Molassi among them — and they're the reason the neighbourhood smells faintly of beeswax and old paper even on a weekday.

On a Saturday the stalls themselves take over, with something like two hundred and fifty to three hundred sellers: serious antique dealers, vintage specialists, house-clearance traders and craftspeople. The joy of it is that a silver teapot and a box of mismatched forks can end up on the same table.

The second Sunday of every month brings the Gran Balôn, a bigger gathering of more than two hundred and fifty antique dealers and artisans, and the dealers will tell you that the serious collectors arrive early.

Over the years the market has grown so famous that its name has almost swallowed the neighbourhood, and plenty of people in Turin say they're going to the Balôn rather than to Borgo Dora. That tells you something about how a weekly habit, kept up for long enough, can quietly redraw a map.
