San Karlo dahl nineteen seventy three, La Mohda a Toreeno

Some of Turin's most interesting fashion is sold in a converted stable, hidden in a courtyard off the city's grandest square.

Head for number one hundred and sixty one under the arcades of Piazza San Karlo — the square with the bronze horseman in the middle and twin baroque churches at its southern end — and go through into the courtyard of Palatso Villa, where the boutique San Karlo dahl nineteen seventy three has its home.

The courtyard is where the surprise lies, because the shop moved here in twenty nineteen and the architect Zhanno Cherootee turned the space into around five hundred square metres on several levels, linking an old stable building to the rest with a structure covered in climbing plants, so the greenery is the first thing you notice.

Jorjeena Seevyairo founded San Karlo in nineteen seventy three, and the shop built its reputation on a carefully chosen mix of clothing, accessories and objects, the kind of edit that makes a shop feel like the taste of a particular person rather than a list of brands.

It does a good deal more than sell clothes, hosting book launches, talks on cinema, art, music and food, and the occasional concert, so the courtyard sometimes feels more like a salon than a store.

And that's where the shop is cleverest, because Turin's grand squares were built to impress from the outside, while this one turns the trick inside out, using the hidden space behind a famous facade to create something intimate, which is perhaps the most Turinese move of all.

Back in Piazza San Karlo, take a minute with the statue of Duke Emmanwelleh Feeleebairto in the middle, and then wander south to the twin churches of San Karlo and Santa Cristina. Beyond them, Veeah Roma carries on towards Porta Nwova under its long arcades.
