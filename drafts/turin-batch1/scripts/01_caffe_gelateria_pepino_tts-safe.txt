Kaffeh, Jelahtereeah Peppeeno

The world's first chocolate covered gelato on a stick was patented in nineteen thirty nine, and the shop that patented it is still trading on the square in front of you.

The brick palace curving along one side of the square is Palatso Kareenyahno, and Peppeeno sits directly opposite it at number eight, with its tables set out on the cobbles and its windows full of cones. The Tayahtro Kareenyahno is right next door, which is why the after show crowd has been wandering in here for generations.

Begin at the freezer, where small foil wrapped bars are stacked, because that's the Peengweeno — the penguin — a stick of gelato dipped in a hard shell of chocolate. The patent behind it, number five eight zero three three, is still part of the shop's own story, it comes in five flavours, and it has quietly become as much a Turin emblem as the beecherreen.

Step inside next and have a look at the room itself, with its wood panelling, its mirrors and its velvet seats, all very much of the early twentieth century. The business goes back to eighteen eighty four, when Domenneeko Peppeeno brought what he called true Neapolitan gelato north to Turin; in nineteen sixteen he sold the name and the recipes, the Kavanyeeno family has looked after them ever since, and the shop has stood on this square since nineteen twenty nine.

There's a pleasing contrast in all of this, because Palatso Kareenyahno is where the Kingdom of Italy was proclaimed in eighteen sixty one, so the square was built for very large ideas — and yet what most people carry away from it is a penguin on a stick. That's a rather Italian arrangement: grand history on one side, serious gelato on the other, and nobody asked to choose.

If you take your Peengweeno for a walk, follow the curve of that brick facade to the end of the palace and round onto Piazza Karlo Albairto, where the same building wears a completely different, far more formal face.
