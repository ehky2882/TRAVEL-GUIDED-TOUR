Fondazione Sandretto Re Rebaudengo

Some of the most adventurous contemporary art in Italy is shown in a quiet residential street on the western side of Turin, a long way from the baroque squares most visitors stick to.

Out here in Borgo San Paolo, an old working neighbourhood, Via Modane is lined with apartment blocks, and at number sixteen the Fondazione Sandretto Re Rebaudengo stands apart from them as a long, low, deliberately plain building with very little decoration at all. The entrance is at street level, and there's a café and bookshop inside.

The building deserves a proper look before you go in. The architect Claudio Silvestrin designed it, and it opened in 2002 in a district that grew up around Turin's factories; the plainness is the point, because the rooms are meant to step back and let whatever is being shown take over.

Behind the collection is one person's eye: Patrizia Sandretto Re Rebaudengo began collecting in 1992 and set up the foundation in 1995, and she has tended to buy the work of artists early, often while they were still young and largely unknown, working directly with them rather than through the safer routes of the market.

The foundation also runs a strong education programme, with mediators in the galleries whose job is to talk with visitors about the work, and that's free during exhibitions.

What makes this place matter is its choice of address. It could easily have taken a palazzo in the centre, and instead it planted itself in a neighbourhood shaped by industry, which says something clear about who contemporary art is meant to be for.

Exhibitions change through the year and the opening days are limited, so check what's on before making the trip. Once you're out here, walk the surrounding streets of Borgo San Paolo, where traces of the old workshops and factory buildings still give the district its character.
