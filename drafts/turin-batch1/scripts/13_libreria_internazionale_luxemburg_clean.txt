Libreria Internazionale Luxemburg

This bookshop has been selling books in Turin since 1872, and in January 2025 it moved house, about fifty metres down the street.

At the corner of Via Accademia delle Scienze and Via Cesare Battisti, the Libreria Internazionale Luxemburg has two entrances: one on Via Battisti, and another opening straight into the Galleria Subalpina, the arcade beside it. From the corner you'll see the windows stacked with books in several languages.

The history is the place to begin, because the shop started life as the Libreria Beuf, a Turin branch of a Genoa bookseller, and later became the Luxemburg, an international bookshop where you could find newspapers and novels from across Europe. Antonio Pittarelli and Gigi Ràiola have run it since 2002.

Step inside to see the new rooms, where three spaces were joined together, more than two hundred square metres in all, and where the designers Barbara Brondi and Marco Rainò kept the old wooden shelving and set it against exposed brick vaults and dark walls, so the old shop came along and simply got more room.

Look for the counter at the centre, too, because there's a small café now, created with the ceramics firm Mutina and the historic Turin coffee house Baratti and Milano, with a ceramic counter by the designers Ronan and Erwan Bouroullec.

Seen from the street, this is simply a bookshop that moved fifty metres, but seen from inside it's a small piece of the city's civic memory. It helped launch Turin's international book fair, and in the nineteen seventies it was connected with FUORI, one of Italy's first gay rights movements, founded by Angelo Pezzana; art, design and LGBTQ literature remain among its specialities today.

When you've finished browsing, take the door into the Galleria Subalpina and walk through towards Piazza Castello. On the way you'll pass an antiquarian bookshop whose walls are hung with old maps, which makes a rather nice pair with this one.
