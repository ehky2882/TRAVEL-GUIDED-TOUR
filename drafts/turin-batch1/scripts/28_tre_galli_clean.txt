Tre Galli

This is a restaurant at lunchtime and a wine and cocktail bar by night, and on a summer evening its tables spill out onto one of the liveliest corners of the old Roman quarter.

Here in the Quadrilatero Romano, the grid of narrow streets in the oldest part of Turin, Tre Galli has its tables at number twenty-five Via Sant'Agostino, set out under the street lamps in front of a long, warmly lit room. From across the street you'll see shelves of bottles inside.

The streets around you are worth a glance first, because the Quadrilatero follows the grid of the Roman city, Augusta Taurinorum, and the narrow, straight lanes you can see in each direction are the descendants of streets laid out two thousand years ago.

Tre Galli itself serves traditional regional cooking at lunch and dinner, with both meat and fish, and in the evening it's known for its wine, especially Nebbiolo, Piedmont's great red grape, alongside Champagne, cocktails and a serious list of grappas and liqueurs.

The atmosphere is often described as more Parisian than Alpine, with people standing outside with a glass at aperitivo time, and the place stays open until two in the morning, from Monday to Saturday.

It's a small sign of how the neighbourhood has changed. The Quadrilatero was once one of the shabbier parts of central Turin, and places like this helped turn it into the quarter where the city goes out at night, without losing the old streets that give it its character.

Wander along Via Sant'Agostino to the church of the same name, before carrying on to Via Bellezia, where another restaurant with a very similar name has been serving Piedmontese food for a long time; just don't mix them up when you book.
