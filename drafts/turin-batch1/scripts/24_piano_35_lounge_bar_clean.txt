Piano 35 lounge bar

You're about a hundred and fifty metres above Turin, in a garden made of glass, and the bar around you is the highest space in the building.

Piano 35 occupies the top of the Intesa Sanpaolo skyscraper, inside the bioclimatic greenhouse that crowns the tower, and the lounge bar is on the greenhouse's uppermost level. Look around and you'll see two views at once: on one side the plants and trees of the winter garden below you, and on the other, through the glass, the whole city.

That double view is the heart of the place. The greenhouse rises over three floors, and from here you're looking down into its planting as well as out over the rooftops, which gives the odd, pleasant feeling of being in a conservatory and on a mountain at the same time.

There's also a walkway suspended along three sides of the tower, the highest of the building's hanging walkways, so stroll along it and watch the city change as you turn each corner, from the old centre and the Mole on one side to the Alps on the other.

The bar itself has a drinks list built around the idea of art and emotion, and in the evenings there's a food station with street-food dishes and some of the restaurant's best-known plates; the restaurant is run by the Michelin-starred chef Marco Sacco with the resident chef Christian Balzo.

The skyscraper below you was designed by Renzo Piano to stop just short of the Mole's height, and up here you can see why that mattered. You're looking across at the city's old symbol from not far beneath the tip of its spire, and it still has the last word on the skyline.

Booking is recommended, especially for sunset, which is the moment to come. On a clear evening, look west for the outline of the Alps turning pink, and then east again for the Mole lit up against the dark.
