Moozayo Ejeetsyo

The oldest museum in the world devoted entirely to ancient Egypt isn't in Cairo, London or Paris, but here, behind this baroque brick front in the middle of Turin.

The long brick building at number six Veeah Akkadaymya dellay Shentseh, beside Piazza Kareenyahno, is the Moozayo Ejeetsyo, with its main entrance on the street. It shares the building with the Academy of Sciences, so the whole block rising above you belongs to learning of one kind or another.

The building has a history of its own, having been built in sixteen seventy nine as the Kollayjo day Nobeelee, a school for the sons of the aristocracy, and the museum came into being after King Karlo Feleecheh bought the great collection of Bairnardeeno Drovettee in eighteen twenty four, the founding moment of the Moozayo Ejeetsyo.

One man did more than anyone to fill it further. Airnesto Schiaparelli, director in the early twentieth century, led excavations in Egypt that brought around thirty thousand objects back to Turin between nineteen oh three and nineteen thirty seven, and today the collection runs to roughly forty thousand pieces across more than twelve thousand square metres.

Inside, the gallery of statues is the moment most visitors remember. In two thousand and six the film designer Dante Ferretti, who worked with Fellini and Scorsayzee, rearranged the great figures of kings and gods in dim light against mirrors, so that they seem to loom out of the dark.

The French scholar Shampolyon, who deciphered hieroglyphs, is said to have remarked that the road to Memphis and Thebes passes through Turin, and that still holds. A city in the far north west of Italy became one of the world's great places for ancient Egypt, almost by accident of royal shopping.

Tickets are best booked in advance, because the queues can be long. Afterwards, walk round the corner into Piazza Kareenyahno for a gelato at Peppeeno, which has been on that square since nineteen twenty nine.
