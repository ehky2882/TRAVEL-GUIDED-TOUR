Dealers Association Baloon, Baloon Turin

People have been buying and selling second hand things in these streets, on and off, since eighteen fifty seven, which makes the Baloon one of the oldest flea markets in Italy.

Veeah Borgo Dorah runs downhill here from the Porta Palatso end towards the river, and on a Saturday it's lined with stalls, awnings and shopfronts piled with furniture, lamps, frames and boxes of old postcards. Off to one side is the Korteeleh del Malyo, a large covered courtyard where more dealers set up under a high roof.

Glance first at the shops behind the stalls, because there are around fifty permanent ones in these few streets — Veeah Borgo Dorah, Veeah Laneeno, Veeah Mahmaylee and Veeah Kanahleh Molahssee among them — and they're the reason the neighbourhood smells faintly of beeswax and old paper even on a weekday.

On a Saturday the stalls themselves take over, with something like two hundred and fifty to three hundred sellers: serious antique dealers, vintage specialists, house clearance traders and craftspeople. The joy of it is that a silver teapot and a box of mismatched forks can end up on the same table.

The second Sunday of every month brings the Gran Baloon, a bigger gathering of more than two hundred and fifty antique dealers and artisans, and the dealers will tell you that the serious collectors arrive early.

Over the years the market has grown so famous that its name has almost swallowed the neighbourhood, and plenty of people in Turin say they're going to the Baloon rather than to Borgo Dorah. That tells you something about how a weekly habit, kept up for long enough, can quietly redraw a map.

Follow Veeah Borgo Dorah down to the Korteeleh del Malyo and wander through it, then climb back uphill to Porta Palatso, the enormous food market at the top of the street, for a coffee among the fruit stalls.
