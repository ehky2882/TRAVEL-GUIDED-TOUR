San Carlo Dal 1973 | La Moda a Torino

Some of Turin's most interesting fashion is sold in a converted stable, hidden in a courtyard off the city's grandest square.

Head for number one hundred and sixty-one under the arcades of Piazza San Carlo — the square with the bronze horseman in the middle and twin baroque churches at its southern end — and go through into the courtyard of Palazzo Villa, where the boutique San Carlo dal 1973 has its home.

The courtyard is where the surprise lies, because the shop moved here in 2019 and the architect Jeannot Cerutti turned the space into around five hundred square metres on several levels, linking an old stable building to the rest with a structure covered in climbing plants, so the greenery is the first thing you notice.

Giorgina Siviero founded San Carlo in 1973, and the shop built its reputation on a carefully chosen mix of clothing, accessories and objects, the kind of edit that makes a shop feel like the taste of a particular person rather than a list of brands.

It does a good deal more than sell clothes, hosting book launches, talks on cinema, art, music and food, and the occasional concert, so the courtyard sometimes feels more like a salon than a store.

And that's where the shop is cleverest, because Turin's grand squares were built to impress from the outside, while this one turns the trick inside out, using the hidden space behind a famous facade to create something intimate, which is perhaps the most Turinese move of all.

Back in Piazza San Carlo, take a minute with the statue of Duke Emanuele Filiberto in the middle, and then wander south to the twin churches of San Carlo and Santa Cristina. Beyond them, Via Roma carries on towards Porta Nuova under its long arcades.
