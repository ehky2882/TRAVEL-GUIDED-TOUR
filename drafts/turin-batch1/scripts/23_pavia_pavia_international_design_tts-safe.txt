Pahveea and Pahveea International Design

A good deal of the furniture in this showroom was made by hand in Italian workshops, which puts it in a rather different league from most of what's sold as design today.

Gardens fill the middle of Piazza Bodohnee, the Joozeppeh Vairdee Conservatory lines one side, and at number one you'll find Pahveea and Pahveea International Design, its windows showing sofas, cabinets and tables in polished wood and lacquer, arranged as though in an elegant apartment.

The materials reward a closer look, and they're the core of the business: Pahveea and Pahveea was founded in nineteen eighty seven, and it designs, produces and distributes its own collections, all of them made in Italy by skilled artisans using precious woods, lacquer and fine fabrics; look for the deep shine on the lacquered surfaces, which takes layer upon layer to achieve.

The mix of styles is the other thing to notice, because the collections blend contemporary lines with a more classical tradition, so a sofa that seems quite modern might sit beside a cabinet that wouldn't look out of place in one of the city's old palatsee.

The firm also works as an interior designer, handling everything from floor plans and materials to lighting and project management, for clients across Europe.

In a sense, a showroom like this keeps alive the old idea that furniture is made rather than manufactured. Turin has a long tradition of cabinet makers who fitted out royal residences and grand apartments, and the craft behind those polished surfaces is the same kind of patient work, carried into the present.

While you're here, stay a moment in Piazza Bodohnee and listen, because students practising at the conservatory can often be heard through the open windows. Then head north up Veeah Karlo Albairto towards the centre, a pleasant pedestrian street lined with shops.
