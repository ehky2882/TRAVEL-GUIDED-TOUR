Marco Polo

To find this place you have to know it's there, because it's tucked into the inner courtyard of a Turin palatso, well away from the shopfronts on the street.

Go through the carriage arch of the building and into the courtyard beyond, and you'll find the entrance to Marco Polo, a gallery and shop whose windows show a jumble of furniture, art, clothes and lamps. Stand in the courtyard where you can see through the glass, with the walls of the palatso rising around you.

The furniture and objects make the first impression. The owner, Monica Bruno, opened Marco Polo in January twenty sixteen after years running a consignment business and working the city's markets, and she likes to mix opposites: Chinese vases beside plush sofas, Buddha heads beside high heels, Eames chairs and rattan armchairs on the same floor, and lacquered papyay mashay shelving painted with Chinese motifs.

The clothes are just as eclectic, with high end vintage fashion — Chanel, Eev San Loran, Issay Meeyahkay and Loobootan have all passed through — alongside dresses and suits from Turin's own tailors of the nineteen fifties and sixties, a local tradition the gallery has celebrated with exhibitions.

The name, Monica has explained, is about travel: a journey through beauty and through time, with more than a hint of the East in the things she chooses. She describes the result as having the warmth of a home, the energy of a souk and the refinement of a boudoir, and that's a fair summary.

The key to Marco Polo is that nothing here is arranged by category, because everything is chosen by one eye. That's the difference between a shop and a gallery; you come to browse a point of view as much as to buy a chair.

Marco Polo opens Tuesday to Saturday, with a break at lunchtime, so time your visit. Once you're back on the street, glance into the other courtyards along the way, because Turin hides a surprising amount behind its carriage arches.
