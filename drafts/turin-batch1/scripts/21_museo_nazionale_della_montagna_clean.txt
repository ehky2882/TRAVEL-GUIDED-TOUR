Museo Nazionale della Montagna "Duca degli Abruzzi"

On a clear day, from the terrace on this little hill, you can see something like four hundred kilometres of the Alps laid out in a single sweep.

Make your way to the open terrace in front of the old Capuchin church at the top of Monte dei Cappuccini, the wooded hill just across the Po from the city centre. The Museo Nazionale della Montagna is in the buildings beside the church, the city is spread out below you with the river nearest, and the mountains stretch along the far horizon.

Pick out the Mole's spire among the rooftops first, then lift your eyes to the white wall of peaks beyond it, which runs most of the way round the western horizon when the air is clean.

The reason the museum is up here goes back to 1874, when the Italian Alpine Club set up an observation post on this hill with a telescope, so that people in Turin could study the mountains they were planning to climb. It was inaugurated on the ninth of August that year, and the first museum rooms followed in 1877.

The museum takes its name from Prince Luigi of Savoy, Duke of the Abruzzi, an explorer and mountaineer who gave it the equipment from his expeditions in 1901. Today it covers climbing, skiing, tourism and the lives of the people who live in the mountains, and it also holds the Alpine Club's national library.

This city has always had the Alps in its line of sight, and the museum turns that view into a subject, treating the mountains not as scenery but as neighbours that have shaped how Turin thinks about itself.

The museum's own viewing terrace, at the top of the building, is higher still and has the best of the panorama. Coming down, take the winding footpath back towards the river and the bridge to Piazza Vittorio, catching glimpses of the city through the trees.
