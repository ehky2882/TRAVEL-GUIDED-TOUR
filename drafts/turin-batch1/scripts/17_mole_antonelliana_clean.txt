Mole Antonelliana

The building above you was meant to be a synagogue, and you'll find it on the back of every Italian two-cent coin.

Back up against the buildings on the far side of Via Montebello until the whole height fits into view: a square base, a tall, steep, ribbed dome above it, and then a slender spire climbing to a point a hundred and sixty-seven metres above the street.

Begin at the base, where the story starts. In 1863 Turin's Jewish community commissioned the architect Alessandro Antonelli to build their new synagogue, and he kept changing the design, raising it from forty-seven metres towards the height you see now; the costs rose with it, and in 1877 the community handed the unfinished building to the city.

Trace the dome upwards to the spire, which has had a rough time. A winged statue, the Genius, was placed at the very top in 1900, until a storm on the eleventh of August 1904 knocked it down and it was replaced by a star, and in 1953 another violent storm brought down more than half the spire, which took until 1961 to rebuild.

That same year a panoramic lift was installed, and today it rises up through the middle of the great hall to a viewing terrace. Since 2000 the building has been home to the National Cinema Museum, with the old temple hall turned into a vast space for watching films.

The Mole is what happens when one architect refuses to stop and a city decides to adopt the consequences. It began as a place of worship, became a civic monument, and ended up as a cathedral to the cinema, which is quite a career for one building.

Take the lift to the terrace if you've time, for the view over the rooftops to the Alps. Otherwise, walk down Via Montebello to Via Po and look back from the arcades, where the spire appears suddenly between the buildings.
