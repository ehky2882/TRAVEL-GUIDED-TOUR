Gallereeah Jeeleebert

Walk through this door and you leave the twenty first century more or less behind, because the walls inside are covered almost to the ceiling with old maps and prints.

Inside the Gallereeah Soobalpeena, the covered arcade linking Piazza Kastello with Piazza Karlo Albairto, look for number seventeen, where a shopfront of dark wood and old glass belongs to Gallereeah Jeeleebert, an antiquarian bookshop. Position yourself so you can see in through the window, with the arcade's iron and glass roof above you.

Your eye will go straight to the walls, where prints and maps hang in close rows: framed views of cities and mountains, and the occasional botanical plate. The bookcases below are dark wood and properly old, which gives the whole room the feel of a library in a Victorian novel.

The shelves reveal a shop with particular passions. Alfraydo Jeeleebert, a lifelong collector, opened it in nineteen ninety two with his son Massimo, who runs it today, and alongside European literature they specialise in local history, art, gastronomy and mountaineering — an unusually Turinese mix, in a city that can see the Alps from the end of its streets.

There's also an upstairs room where they hold book launches, talks and small exhibitions, so the shop works as a gathering place as well as a store.

Jeeleebert's real charm lies in how personal it feels. Much of what's here began as one family's collecting habit, and the shop has the slightly obsessive quality of a private library opened to the public, the kind of place where the person behind the desk can tell you exactly why a particular map is worth your attention.

On your way out, walk the length of the arcade and look up at that glass roof. At the far end, close to Piazza Karlo Albairto, a much bigger international bookshop has a door opening onto the arcade, if one bookshop simply isn't enough.
