Caffè - Gelateria Pepino

The world's first chocolate-covered gelato on a stick was patented in 1939, and the shop that patented it is still trading on the square in front of you.

The brick palace curving along one side of the square is Palazzo Carignano, and Pepino sits directly opposite it at number eight, with its tables set out on the cobbles and its windows full of cones. The Teatro Carignano is right next door, which is why the after-show crowd has been wandering in here for generations.

Begin at the freezer, where small foil-wrapped bars are stacked, because that's the Pinguino — the penguin — a stick of gelato dipped in a hard shell of chocolate. The patent behind it, number 58033, is still part of the shop's own story, it comes in five flavours, and it has quietly become as much a Turin emblem as the bicerin.

Step inside next and have a look at the room itself, with its wood panelling, its mirrors and its velvet seats, all very much of the early twentieth century. The business goes back to 1884, when Domenico Pepino brought what he called true Neapolitan gelato north to Turin; in 1916 he sold the name and the recipes, the Cavagnino family has looked after them ever since, and the shop has stood on this square since 1929.

There's a pleasing contrast in all of this, because Palazzo Carignano is where the Kingdom of Italy was proclaimed in 1861, so the square was built for very large ideas — and yet what most people carry away from it is a penguin on a stick. That's a rather Italian arrangement: grand history on one side, serious gelato on the other, and nobody asked to choose.

If you take your Pinguino for a walk, follow the curve of that brick facade to the end of the palace and round onto Piazza Carlo Alberto, where the same building wears a completely different, far more formal face.
