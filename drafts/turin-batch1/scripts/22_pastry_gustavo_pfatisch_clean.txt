Pastry Gustavo Pfatisch

Underneath this pastry shop, down a staircase from the back room, there's a small museum of old chocolate-making machines.

Number forty-two Via Sacchi, a short walk south of Porta Nuova station, is an elegant Art Nouveau building designed by Pietro Fenoglio, the architect who brought that flowing, decorative style to Turin, and Pfatisch has its name above the windows on the ground floor. From the opposite pavement you'll see the building's curves and ironwork, and the cakes lined up in the window.

The shop's history starts in 1915, when the master pastry chef Gustavo Pfatisch opened here, and the business went on to become a supplier to the royal household, a title given to a select few Turin shops that still carries some weight in this city.

In the window and on the counter, look for the Festivo, the best known of the cakes, created in the nineteen sixties with layers of cocoa meringue and chocolate cream. The savoury pastries are serious too, and in 2023 the guide Gambero Rosso named them the best in Italy.

Down in the basement, the chocolate museum holds machines from the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, a reminder that Turin was one of Europe's great chocolate cities long before it became famous for cars.

Recent years haven't been easy for Pfatisch, since in 2020 the family that ran it went into voluntary bankruptcy and the premises went to auction, and the city held its breath a little, because places like this are woven into Turin's identity; happily, the shop carried on under new owners, and it's still making the Festivo.
