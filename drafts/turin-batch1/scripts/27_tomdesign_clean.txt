Tomdesign - Arredamenti e Lampade

In a quiet stretch of the Vanchiglia neighbourhood, an architect's studio and a design shop share the same front door.

Lamps fill the windows at number seventy-three B Corso San Maurizio — some hanging, some standing, some sitting on desks — among chairs, tables and sofas, and that's Tomdesign. Stand in front of the window and let your eye travel upwards to the lights.

Those lamps include pieces from Bocci, the Canadian studio known for glass pendants that look like blown bubbles, alongside furniture from Scandinavian and European brands such as HAY, Gubi and Fredericia, with classics and new names mixed together.

The people behind it are architects, since Tomdesign is also an architecture practice, led by the architect Laura Maria Marchesi, which handles renovations, interior design, lighting plans and custom furniture, often in historic Turin apartments, so the shop isn't just a showroom but a working library of the things they use in real homes.

That combination says something about how Turin lives. Many of its apartments are in old buildings with high ceilings and tall windows, and the challenge is to bring in modern furniture and light without fighting the architecture; a shop run by architects is designed to solve exactly that problem.

It says something about Vanchiglia as well, which has changed from a working-class district into one of the city's more creative neighbourhoods, with studios and small shops filling the ground floors.

A short way along Corso San Maurizio, at the corner with Via Giulia di Barolo, stands the Fetta di Polenta, a nine-storey house only a little over half a metre wide at its narrow end, and it's one of the strangest buildings in the city, well worth the detour if you want to see a corner plot pushed to its absolute limit.
