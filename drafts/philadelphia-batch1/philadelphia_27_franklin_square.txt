PHILADELPHIA — 27 — FRANKLIN SQUARE
Tier 2 · single stop · batch B4b · register AMERICAN
Vantage: the fountain at the center of the square, facing north toward Vine Street and the bridge approach
Spines: S1 (names the square only; no payoff) · docket 10 (the expressway's edge, one clause) · Franklin not named
---
Every sign in this square tells you what it is. The fountain is from 1838; the square is one of the five laid out in 1682; the carousel and the miniature golf course are what they look like. None of the signs carries the one sentence that matters here, which is that for most of the twentieth century this square was on its way to not being here.

The north edge is Vine Street, lanes of it feeding the bridge. The east edge is the bridge itself, which came down onto Sixth Street between 1922 and 1926 and took the blocks of houses that had faced the square from that side. Two edges gone to traffic. In the 1950s and 1960s the land south of Race was cleared for the Mall, and a square that had been a neighborhood's lawn became the far end of a vista nobody walked the length of. In 1991 the expressway went into its trench along Vine — the same trench that cut the north edge off Chinatown, four blocks west of here — and the square sat between a bridge approach and a highway with its fountain dry.

*[beat]*

What it had been first was a pasture, and a livestock market, and from 1741 to 1835 the burial ground of the German Reformed congregation, under the lawn you are standing on. The square was leveled, planted and fenced in 1815, and the fountain was set at its center in 1838: forty jets in a stone ring with an iron rail around it. That fountain is the one piece of the square that stood through everything that followed. It stopped running by the late 1970s and stayed dry for a generation, which is a long time for the center of anything.

The repair came between 2003 and 2006, and the square reopened at the end of July 2006 with the fountain running on its original rail and center stone, new brick underfoot, a carousel on one side and the golf course on the other. Those two are stated once here and they are what they look like; the square around them is the thing worth the time.

It was fenced in 1815 and cut off in 1926, cleared around in the 1950s and trenched in 1991. Through all of it the fountain stood dry at the center, and in 2006 the water came back on.
