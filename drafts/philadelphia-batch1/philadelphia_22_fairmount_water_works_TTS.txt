This is the wrong river. Everything the city is famous for happened on the other one, a mile and a half east: the wharves, the ships, the room where the papers were signed. The del a wair was the front door, and it worked for a living. This river was never asked to. The skoo kull is the one the city kept for looking at, and it took a waterworks to make that decision.

The decision had a first draft in the city's center. In eighteen oh one the city set a steam pump in the center square to lift river water to a tank and let it run downhill through wooden pipes, and the pump was underpowered from the start. Frederick graf, who had drafted the plans for his employer on that job, was running the system by eighteen oh five, and by eighteen eleven he was recommending a bigger one here, at the foot of a rocky hill the first settlers had called Faire Mount. The new works opened in eighteen fifteen, still on steam, in a stuccoed house built to look like a temple.

Which is where the dam comes in. Steam burned coal, so between eighteen nineteen and eighteen twenty one the city built a dam across the whole width of the river, and water went over it for the first time in July eighteen twenty one. Behind the dam, in the long mill house below you, water wheels turned pumps, and the pumps lifted the river up the hill to a reservoir where the museum stands; from there gravity did the rest. Turbines replaced them in the decades after, and the works ran until nineteen oh nine, when newer plants upriver took the duty and this one was handed over to an aquarium, then a swimming pool, then quiet.

What the terrace is for is the view, and that was intended, since graf dressed every machine in a classical shell and set a walk along the top so people could watch water being lifted by water and call it beautiful, which they did. The city is flat; it sits between two rivers on ground that is more or less what it was, and no neighborhood here was built on fill. The del a wair got the work. This one got the picture, and the picture has a dam in it.

The pumps stopped in nineteen oh nine. In eighteen twenty one graf had set the river's level, and the city never found a reason to change it.
