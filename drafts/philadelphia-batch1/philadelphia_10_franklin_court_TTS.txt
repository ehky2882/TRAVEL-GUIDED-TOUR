PHILADELPHIA — 10 — FRANKLIN COURT
Tier 1 · Single stop · Batch B2 · Register: American
Vantage: through the arched passage from Market Street into the court, under the taller steel frame
Spine: PLANTS S2 (the founded city — Franklin's Philadelphia as a set of founded things) · docket 16 (by deed only) · S8 CONTAINMENT (added B2)
---
He left this city a hospital, a lending library, a fire company, an insurance company, a college that became a university, a learned society and a post office that still delivers, and of the one thing he built for himself in all of Philadelphia, he left no drawing at all.

Through the arched passage from Market Street, between Third and Fourth, you come out into a brick court with two frames of white painted steel standing in it. The taller one, about fifty four feet, draws the shape of the house Benjamin Franklin had built in seventeen sixty three and spent his last five years in; the smaller one draws the print shop beside it. His heirs pulled both down in eighteen twelve to put up rentals that paid better. When the Park Service came to rebuild the court for nineteen seventy six, nobody could say what the house had looked like from outside, no sketch survived, only letters about chimneys and the width of rooms, so the architects, Robert ven toor ee and Denise Scott Brown with John Rauch, declined to guess. They drew the shape in the air and stopped. The concrete hoods at your feet look down onto the foundations it actually stood on.

The row of brick houses on Market Street you just came through, Franklin built as well, in the seventeen eighties as rentals, and the one with the presses in it is a working eighteenth century print shop where the Park Service sets type by hand. There's a museum below the court; if it's open and you want it, it's there.

Carry this across the city. Franklin's monuments aren't monuments. The hospital he raised money for in seventeen fifty one is still a hospital, a few blocks south; the library he and his friends subscribed to in seventeen thirty one is still lending; the insurance company he helped start in seventeen fifty two still writes policies; the Academy he argued for in seventeen forty nine is the University of Pennsylvania; the philosophical society he called together in seventeen forty three still meets. You'll pass his institutions all over Philadelphia without a sign to tell you so, because they look like a hospital, a library, a university. A founded thing outlives a built one. That is the whole argument of this court, and the architects made it the honest way, by refusing to invent the building and letting the institutions stand for the man.

Stand a minute inside the taller frame. Then go find the hospital.
