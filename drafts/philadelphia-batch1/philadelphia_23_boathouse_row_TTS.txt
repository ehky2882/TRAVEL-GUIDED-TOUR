PHILADELPHIA — 23 — BOATHOUSE ROW
Tier 2 · single stop · batch B5 · register AMERICAN
Vantage: the upstream end of the Water Works terrace, the row along the east bank ahead of you; the west bank across the water is where the picture was composed from
Spines: S3 payoff (the Schuylkill as picture — the row is lit to be looked at from the other side)
---
The row was built to be rowed from and lit to be looked at, and the two audiences have never stood in the same place. The clubs use the river side: the ramps, the docks, the water at dawn. Everyone else uses the far bank, or the expressway above it, where after dark the houses show up as outlines drawn in light with the river doubling them underneath. That second version has existed since nineteen seventy nine.

The first version is older. Rowing clubs put up boathouses along this bank from the eighteen fifties, and in eighteen fifty eight several of them joined into the skoo kull Navy, a governing body that is still the one they answer to. The houses that stand along Kelly Drive mostly date from the decades after, each club building its own, in stone and brick and shingle, with nothing shared but the waterline; the row is a committee that never met. The dam downstream, the one the waterworks needed, gave them what rowers need, which is a long stretch of flat water that does not go anywhere in a hurry, and the regattas have run on it since. The Dad vale is the college race, run here every May.

The lights went up in nineteen seventy nine, strings of bulbs tracing the eaves and gables, and the effect was immediate: a row of buildings that nobody on the far bank could see at night became the thing the far bank came for. The bulbs were swapped for diodes in two thousand five and again in two thousand sixteen, and in March two thousand twenty three the whole system came down for a year while a new one was mounted house by house. It was switched back on in March two thousand twenty four, and it can change color, which it does for occasions.

The row is two things at once, then, and the river is what joins them. From the dock side it is a working waterfront of the smallest kind: boats in, boats out, oars under arms. From the far side it is a drawing of itself, and the water holds the copy. The city chose this river for looking at a long time ago; the people who row it just happen to be in the frame.

The clubs built the houses to get onto the water. The city wired them so everyone else could watch from across it.
