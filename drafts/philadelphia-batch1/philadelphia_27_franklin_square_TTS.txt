Tier 2 · single stop · batch B4b · register AMERICAN
Vantage: the fountain at the center of the square, facing north toward Vine Street and the bridge approach

Every sign in this square tells you what it is. The fountain is from eighteen thirty eight; the square is one of the five laid out in sixteen eighty two; the carousel and the miniature golf course are what they look like. None of the signs carries the one sentence that matters here, which is that for most of the twentieth century this square was on its way to not being here.

The north edge is Vine Street, lanes of it feeding the bridge. The east edge is the bridge itself, which came down onto Sixth Street between nineteen twenty two and nineteen twenty six and took the blocks of houses that had faced the square from that side. Two edges gone to traffic. In the nineteen fifties and nineteen sixties the land south of Race was cleared for the Mall, and a square that had been a neighborhood's lawn became the far end of a vista nobody walked the length of. In nineteen ninety one the expressway went into its trench along Vine, the same trench that cut the north edge off Chinatown, four blocks west of here, and the square sat between a bridge approach and a highway with its fountain dry.

What it had been first was a pasture, and a livestock market, and from seventeen forty one to eighteen thirty five the burial ground of the German ree formd congregation, under the lawn you are standing on. The square was leveled, planted and fenced in eighteen fifteen, and the fountain was set at its center in eighteen thirty eight: forty jets in a stone ring with an iron rail around it. That fountain is the one piece of the square that stood through everything that followed. It stopped running by the late nineteen seventies and stayed dry for a generation, which is a long time for the center of anything.

The repair came between two thousand three and two thousand six, and the square reopened at the end of July two thousand six with the fountain running on its original rail and center stone, new brick underfoot, a carousel on one side and the golf course on the other. Those two are stated once here and they are what they look like; the square around them is the thing worth the time.

It was fenced in eighteen fifteen and cut off in nineteen twenty six, cleared around in the nineteen fifties and trenched in nineteen ninety one. Through all of it the fountain stood dry at the center, and in two thousand six the water came back on.
