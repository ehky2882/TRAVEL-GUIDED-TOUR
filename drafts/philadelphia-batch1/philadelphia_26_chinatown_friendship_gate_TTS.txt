Philadelphia weather is hard on red paint. The winters are too cold for it and the summers too hot, and by two thousand seven the gate above you had faded to the color of a brick left out in the rain. The city that wanted it red again could not do the job with local crews, because the lacquer, the pigments and the method belonged to artisans in tee en jin; so tee en jin sent them, as it had the first time, and in two thousand eight the gate was red again.

The first time was the winter of nineteen eighty three. Twelve artisans arrived from tee en jin in October, worked through the cold, and the gate was dedicated at the end of January nineteen eighty four: forty feet high, built in the style of the ching court to a design by Sabrina soong, its tiles fired in tee en jin, which had been Philadelphia's sister city since nineteen seventy nine. The gate was Chinatown's own idea, carried to the city by its development corporation and moved to the top of the list by a commerce director who liked it better than the housing on the same page. The characters across the beam say "Philadelphia Chinatown." They say the neighborhood's name and nothing else.

The neighborhood has been here since the eighteen seventies, and the gate went up in a decade when its edges were being decided elsewhere. Seven years after the dedication, in nineteen ninety one, the Vine Street Expressway opened in a trench along the north side, through blocks that had been Chinatown's. Two years after that, the Convention Center took the blocks to the south. In two thousand twenty two a basketball arena was proposed for the ground between Arch Street and Market; it was cancelled in January two thousand twenty five, and the blocks south toward Market Street are waiting on a plan.

Market Street is two blocks that way, and this city has spent three centuries arguing with itself about where its middle is; each time the argument was settled, on a highway, on a convention hall, very nearly on an arena, the settlement was built at this neighborhood's edge. The gate stands at the one edge nothing was built across.

It was made in tee en jin, shipped in pieces, raised in a Philadelphia winter and painted twice. Everything around it has been argued about; the gate has only ever needed repainting.
