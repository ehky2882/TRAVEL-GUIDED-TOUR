ATLAS · PHILADELPHIA · 13 · Carpenters' Hall
---
This is a guild hall. It still is.

You should be in the brick paved court off the south side of chess nut Street, between Third and Fourth, with a small two story building in front of you, red brick with white trim and a cupola. It sits back from the street at the end of its own short walk, which makes it easy to walk past. The Carpenters' Company of Philadelphia built it in the early seventeen seventies as their meeting house, and they own it still, a trade organization founded in seventeen twenty four that has never sold its hall.

Begin with the building's size, because the size is the story. It's modest. Four rooms, more or less. And in September of seventeen seventy four, when delegates from twelve colonies arrived in Philadelphia to decide what to do about Boston, the Pennsylvania Assembly declined to lend them the State House. So they met here instead, in a carpenters' meeting room, for seven weeks. That was the First Continental Congress, and it sat in a borrowed hall because the official one was closed to it.

Now look up at the second floor. In seventeen seventy four that floor was already rented out, to the Library Company of Philadelphia, the subscription library Benjamin Franklin had founded with a group of friends in seventeen thirty one. So the delegates downstairs had a library above their heads, and the librarian opened the shelves to them. A lending library started by tradesmen and clerks pooling their money for books, lending to a Congress.

That's the Philadelphia habit on display. Somebody founds a thing, a library, a fire company, a hospital, a trade guild, and the thing outlives its founders and ends up hosting history more or less by accident. The Carpenters' Company just needed a room for their meetings and a place to keep their rule book. They got a Congress.

The building has had other tenants since: the first Bank of the United States rented it in the seventeen nineties before its own marble house went up around the corner, and a customs office, and an auction room.

The guild still meets here. That is the quiet fact to leave with. The country was first argued over in a room that belonged to builders, and the builders never gave the room up.
