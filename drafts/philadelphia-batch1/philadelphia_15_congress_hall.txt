ATLAS · PHILADELPHIA · 15 · Congress Hall
---
The room that matters is upstairs.

You should be at the corner of Sixth and Chestnut, looking at the plain brick building immediately west of Independence Hall — two stories, a modest doorway, nothing to suggest a national legislature. It was built in the late 1780s as a courthouse for Philadelphia County, and the county got to use it for about a year.

Start with how it was borrowed. When the federal government arrived in 1790 for its ten temporary years in the city, Congress needed a building, and the new courthouse was standing empty. So the House of Representatives took the ground floor and the Senate took the upper. The building was extended at the back to fit them. For a decade the national government met in a county courtroom, and when it left for Washington in 1800, the county moved back in.

Now the ground floor, the House chamber. On the fourth of March 1797, George Washington walked into that room as president and walked out as a private citizen, and John Adams took the oath in his place. It was the first time the office had passed from one man to another, and it passed quietly, in a courthouse, with the departing president standing in the room as a witness. A head of state simply left, with no guard and no ceremony beyond the oath.

Upstairs is the Senate chamber, where Washington had taken his own second oath four years earlier, and where the Senate ratified the Jay Treaty and admitted three new states to the union. It's a smaller room than you'd think, with a gallery around it.

Here is the point to carry. Independence was declared next door, and this country is understandably proud of that. But a declaration is a beginning. What happened in this plainer building was the thing the declaration had promised and hadn't yet proved — that power could be handed over, on a stated day, to a man the other side had voted for, and that the city would carry on as usual the next morning. It did.

*[beat]*

The county got its rooms back in 1800, and the building spent the next century as a court and a records office before it was restored. It looks like a courthouse because it was one. The republic just passed through.
