The photograph everyone takes here is cropped wrong. The sculpture fills the frame, City Hall fills the sky behind it, and the thing this plaza was built for, a mile of boulevard running away behind the photographer toward a museum on a hill, is out of the picture entirely. Stand with the sculpture at your back and the Parkway opens in front of you, cut on the diagonal through the grid in the 1920s, with fountains set along it at Logan Square and at the museum end.

This plaza was made to be that line's starting point. It was built in 1965 over a parking garage, planned by Edmund Bacon, the city's planning director, and designed by Vincent Kling, and in 1967 it was dedicated to a president. Its own fountain came in 1969, a single jet meant to answer the two farther down the line. For eleven years that was the plaza: granite steps, a jet of water, a view.

*[beat]*

In 1976 Robert Indiana's sculpture was lent to the city and set down here: four aluminum letters, two over two, the O tipped on its side, a word Indiana had first made for a greeting card in 1964. The loan ran two years. In 1978 the gallery took it back to New York for a buyer, and the chairman of the city's Art Commission, Fitz Eugene Dixon, bought it himself and gave it to the city, which put it back. The plaza has answered to the sculpture's name since.

The park around it was rebuilt between 2016 and 2018, the garage roof replaced and the sculpture moved to the center, where it stands over a fountain with jets that can be programmed and lights that can be. Across Fifteenth Street at your back is Dilworth Park, City Hall's west apron, rebuilt in 2014 over the transit concourse as a sheet of water in summer and a rink in winter. Above it the tower closes the axis with the hat on top; this plaza is the near end of the line the agreement held, and the one building that broke it is a block to the south.

The sculpture came as a loan in 1976, left in 1978 for a sale, and was back the same year because one man paid for it. The line to the museum had been there the whole time.
