Philadelphia weather is hard on red paint. The winters are too cold for it and the summers too hot, and by 2007 the gate above you had faded to the color of a brick left out in the rain. The city that wanted it red again could not do the job with local crews, because the lacquer, the pigments and the method belonged to artisans in Tianjin; so Tianjin sent them, as it had the first time, and in 2008 the gate was red again.

The first time was the winter of 1983. Twelve artisans arrived from Tianjin in October, worked through the cold, and the gate was dedicated at the end of January 1984: forty feet high, built in the style of the Qing court to a design by Sabrina Soong, its tiles fired in Tianjin, which had been Philadelphia's sister city since 1979. The gate was Chinatown's own idea, carried to the city by its development corporation and moved to the top of the list by a commerce director who liked it better than the housing on the same page. The characters across the beam say "Philadelphia Chinatown." They say the neighborhood's name and nothing else.

*[beat]*

The neighborhood has been here since the 1870s, and the gate went up in a decade when its edges were being decided elsewhere. Seven years after the dedication, in 1991, the Vine Street Expressway opened in a trench along the north side, through blocks that had been Chinatown's. Two years after that, the Convention Center took the blocks to the south. In 2022 a basketball arena was proposed for the ground between Arch Street and Market; it was cancelled in January 2025, and the blocks south toward Market Street are waiting on a plan.

Market Street is two blocks that way, and this city has spent three centuries arguing with itself about where its middle is; each time the argument was settled — on a highway, on a convention hall, very nearly on an arena — the settlement was built at this neighborhood's edge. The gate stands at the one edge nothing was built across.

It was made in Tianjin, shipped in pieces, raised in a Philadelphia winter and painted twice. Everything around it has been argued about; the gate has only ever needed repainting.
