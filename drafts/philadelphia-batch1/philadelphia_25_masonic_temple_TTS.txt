There are seven rooms inside this building, and no two of them agree on what century it is. One is ee jip shun, with hieroglyphs copied from inscriptions along the Nile and checked, since, by people who read them. One is Gothic; one is Norman, one Renaissance, one eye on ik, one kor in thee un, and the seventh the builders called Oriental, which was a nineteenth century way of saying Moorish. Seven halls, seven architectures, stacked on two floors behind one granite front: that is what is in there, and the front is the one part of it the street is given.

The front earns the look on its own. The porch is Norman, deep and round arched, and the whole facade is New England granite, cut, squared and numbered at the quarry and fitted here like a kit, which is how a society that calls its members builders would want it done.

The Grand Lodge of Pennsylvania bought this corner in eighteen sixty seven, held a competition, and gave the work to James win drim, who was twenty seven and a member. The cornerstone went in during eighteen sixty eight; the building opened in eighteen seventy three; and the decorating of the seven halls ran on through the eighteen eighties under George her zog, who was painting ceilings across the street at the same time.

The date is the thing to carry out of here. When the Temple opened, the square it faced was a construction site, because City Hall's foundations had been going in since eighteen seventy one and would be going in, one way and another, for twenty eight more years. The Masons raised a two hundred and fifty foot tower to face a hole in the ground, and for two decades it had nothing to answer to across Broad Street. Then the hat arrived in eighteen ninety four, more than twice as high, and the Temple became what it has been since: the building across from the building.

None of that is a loss. A tower built to be seen and then quietly overtaken is a specific kind of object, and it keeps its porch pointed the same way regardless. The halls behind it are ticketed and the tours run on the Lodge's own calendar, so this stop stays on the sidewalk and takes the seven rooms on report. What the sidewalk gets is the granite, the arch and the tower, and in eighteen seventy three those were finished while the thing across the street was still a wall going up.
