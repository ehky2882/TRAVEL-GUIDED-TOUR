The water at the center of this circle is pretending to be three rivers. The jet goes up the height of a small building when it is running, and around its base three bronze figures recline, one for each of the rivers the city drinks from: the del a wair, the skoo kull, and the wiss a hick un, the creek that feeds the second. None of them is within a mile of here; the fountain stands in for all three.

Wilson Cary swon ran a society that put drinking fountains on the streets in the decades when the water came from the works on the skoo kull, and after he died the society built him this, finished in nineteen twenty four to a design by the architect Wilson air and the sculptor Alexander stur ling kawl der, the son of the sculptor whose figure stands on City Hall's tower. The frogs and turtles around the rim spit; the swans are a pun on the name; the whole thing is switched on for the warm months and drained for the cold.

The square itself is one of the five from sixteen eighty two, the northwest one, and for its first century and a half it was a burial ground and a place of public punishment. The circle inside the square came in nineteen seventeen, when the Parkway's planners cut the diagonal boulevard through the grid and needed a hinge halfway along it; Jacques gray bare laid out the circle, and the fountain was set at its center seven years later as the midpoint between the tower at one end and the museum on its hill at the other.

The buildings that face the circle were placed to face it: the library and the family court as a matched pair on the north side, the natural sciences academy on the south, and across the Parkway the cathedral, built between eighteen forty six and eighteen sixty four, whose exterior is this stop's and whose interior is its own. The whole arrangement is a Paris idea executed in Philadelphia brick and limestone, and the fountain is the one part of it that moves.

Three rivers in bronze, a creek among them, and a jet of water going up out of a traffic circle. The man it was built for spent his life getting that water to the curb.
