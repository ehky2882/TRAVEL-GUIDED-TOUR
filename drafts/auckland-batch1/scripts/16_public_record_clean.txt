Public Record

This shopfront on Ponsonby Road went up in the 1890s, and these days it's dedicated to things made slowly, by hand, to last.

Eighty-two Ponsonby Road, at the quieter, lower end of the street near Western Park, is Public Record: a shop downstairs and a gallery up above. From the pavement you'll notice the ground floor first, because its walls are painted a deep charcoal black that makes everything on display look as though it's floating.

That black was chosen to give the room a calm, almost otherworldly feel, and against it sit ceramics, glass, brass incense holders, lampshades, weavings and garments, made mostly by artisans in New Zealand and Japan. Upstairs, the walls switch to pale grey for the gallery, which changes its exhibition roughly once a month, and the largest of those rooms looks out over the trees of Western Park.

The owner, Yuka O'Shannessy, was born in Tokyo, studied fashion in Auckland, and still runs a clothing label with her husband, Tristan, who designed the space with her. Public Record opened in 2020 a few doors away and moved into this larger building in 2023, and the couple have said they tried to respect its history while bringing in their own sense of craft.

The idea that ties it together is about visible effort. Yuka is drawn to objects with clean outlines but with enough texture that you can see the maker's hand, and the shop sets itself against anything disposable or temporary, which, on a street where shops come and go fairly quickly, is a pointed thing to build a business around.

When you've finished browsing, cross into Western Park and follow one of the paths that wind down through the trees; it's a lovely green fold right below Ponsonby, and much quieter than the road above.
