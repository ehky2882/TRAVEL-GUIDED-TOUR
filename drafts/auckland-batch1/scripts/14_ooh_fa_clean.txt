OOH-FA

Twenty-two seats and one round wood-fired oven: that's more or less the whole of OOH-FA, and it's been enough to fill the room most nights since 2022.

Dominion Road is long, busy and better known for dumplings than for pizza, and at number three-fifty-seven, wedged between a dairy and a hairdresser, is a small shopfront with OOH-FA written above it. Look inside and you'll see a counter facing the kitchen, a few low tables, and at the back the oven itself, a great dome that dominates the room.

The oven is where the attention belongs, and what goes into it is sourdough, built on a starter that's been fed every day for years, and it's fermented over about three days before it's baked, which gives the crust that light, puffy, slightly charred edge and a gentle sourness underneath.

The menu is short and changes with the season, mixing familiar pizzas with stranger ones; the one regulars mention is lardo, mozzarella, parmesan and honey, and the vegetables get serious treatment too, as with wood-roasted carrots under whipped ricotta and pistachio. There's house-made gelato to finish and a wine list that leans towards natural and organic bottles.

It's run by Jono Thevenard, Kazuya Suzuki and Gemma Hareb, and it's the sister restaurant to Pici on Karangahape Road. What's appealing is how little it tries to be: in a city where new restaurants often arrive with big rooms and bigger budgets, three people put a serious oven in a former shop between a dairy and a hairdresser, and let the food bring the neighbourhood in.

[beat]

Book ahead if you can, since seatings run to about ninety minutes, and if you have time beforehand, walk south along Dominion Road towards Balmoral, where the dumpling houses and noodle bars give you a very different sort of dinner to compare.
