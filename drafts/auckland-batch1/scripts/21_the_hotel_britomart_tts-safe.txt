The Hotel Britomart

When Kon day Nast Traveler published its Hot List of new hotels in twenty twenty one, only one in New Zealand made the cut, and it's the brick tower rising over this corner.

Gore Street and Galway Street meet at the foot of the building, so find the corner and look up. The Hotel Britomart rises ten storeys in handmade clay brick, with its windows scattered across the fassahd in an irregular pattern, a little like a constellation, rather than lined up in neat rows.

That brick is the first thing to notice, because it was chosen to talk to the older brick warehouses around it. Cheshire Architects wanted a new building that felt as though it belonged to the street rather than standing apart from it, and the handmade surface catches the light much more softly than glass or concrete would.

The second thing is invisible from here, because when it opened in October twenty twenty, this became New Zealand's first hotel with a five star Green Star rating, built to produce about half the carbon emissions of a building made to the minimum code, with most of the construction waste recycled. Inside there are ninety nine rooms, lined in timber like cabins, plus five suites at the top called the Landing Suites, three of them with gardens in the sky.

The third is the lane beside you, Too ah fee tee Lane, which separates the new tower from two restored heritage buildings, the Masonic and the Buckland. Here's where the building makes its case, because sustainability usually announces itself with solar panels and green walls, and this hotel says it with old fashioned brick, careful reuse of its neighbours, and a lane anyone can walk through.

Stand at the entrance to Too ah fee tee Lane and look up between the old fassahds and the new brick, which frames one of Britomart's best narrow views; then follow the lane to its end and see where it leads.
