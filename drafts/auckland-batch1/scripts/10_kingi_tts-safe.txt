King ee

The name is simply short for kingfish, which tells you most of what you need to know about where this kitchen's attention lies.

Step into the laneway beside The Hotel Britomart and look for the restored Victorian building on the other side of it, once the home of the Freemasons and now the setting for king ee. Through the windows you should see light tones of brick, stone and marble, a fireplace, and a long counter curving through the room.

That counter is a single, sweeping piece of kowry timber, and above it hangs a chandelier of hand blown glass, made in West Auckland, with its shapes inspired by the scales of a kingfish. Look along the walls as well, where the lights are shaped rather like jellyfish, and the whole room carries on that theme without ever turning into a novelty.

The restaurant is run by Tom Hish on and Josh Helm, who made their names at Orphans Kitchen in Ponsonby, and the menu is built around seafood bought from independent fishers who work with long lines and hooks rather than nets. The dish people talk about is buttermilk blue cod wings, from a third generation fishing family on the Chatham Islands, nearly eight hundred kilometres east of the mainland, alongside crayfish rolls, oysters from the raw bar, and seaweed worked into all sorts of things.

Tom has described the menu as a celebration of independent fishermen, and that's the point to hold on to, because the provenance is doing the work that luxury usually does in a hotel restaurant. The glamour here isn't in the marble; it's in knowing who caught the fish and how.

king ee serves from breakfast onwards, so if dinner isn't on the cards, a morning coffee at the counter is a good way to see the room; and while you're in the lane, look back at the new hotel's brickwork against the old Masonic fassahd, which is one of Britomart's best side by side views.
