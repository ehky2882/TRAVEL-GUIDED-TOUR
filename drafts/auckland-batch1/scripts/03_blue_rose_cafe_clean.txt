Blue Rose Cafe & Catering

Somewhere between three and four thousand pies a week leave one small café on Sandringham Road, and the most famous of them is filled with palusami.

Sandringham Road's main run of shops is around you, and at number four-fourteen is a modest frontage with the name Blue Rose above the door; look through the window, or step inside, and the thing that catches your eye is the wallpaper: a pattern of blue roses, which is where the café got its name.

That wallpaper came from the kitchen of co-owner Lenny's grandmother, and so did plenty of the recipes, including the koko Samoa cupcakes. Lenny and his business partner, Robbie Kainuku, began by catering from home, moved into a commercial kitchen when word of mouth outgrew it, and opened here in 2014 with only a handful of tables.

The pies are the reason for the queue, and the logic behind them is simple: palusami is a Samoan dish of taro leaves baked in coconut cream, often with corned beef, and Robbie's idea was to fold it into pastry, while the hāngi and boil-up pies do the same, taking food usually cooked for a crowd at home and turning it into something you can eat on the pavement. The counter usually holds ika mata too, raw fish cured in citrus and coconut, alongside mussel fritters.

It's tempting to call all this fusion, but the owners describe it more simply as Pacific and Māori soul food, made by taste rather than by recipe card. None of these flavours is new to Auckland, which has one of the largest Pacific populations of any city in the world; what Blue Rose did was put them in a café cabinet, at café prices, for anyone walking past.

Take a palusami pie with you and wander along Sandringham Road, where the Indian sweet shops, grocers and small eateries make this one of the most rewarding food streets in the central suburbs.
