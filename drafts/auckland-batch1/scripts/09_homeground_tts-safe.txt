Home Ground

For centuries, buildings for people in hardship were designed to keep them out of sight, and this one was designed on exactly the opposite principle.

The tall building in front of you on Hobson Street, near the corner of Federal Street, is HomeGround, home of the Auckland City Mission, Teh tah poo ee ah tah fie. You'll recognise it by the steel lattice laid across its face in a pattern of diamonds, with balconies tucked in behind, and at street level by a public laneway that runs right through the building.

The lattice is more than decoration, since it braces the structure against sideways forces, and it also echoes a net, which links to a Mowree proverb the project drew on: the old net is set aside, and the new net goes fishing. Above the lower floors, much of the building is made of engineered timber panels, used at a scale that was new for New Zealand when it went up.

Inside are eighty small apartments, most for one person, each with its own kitchen, bathroom and balcony, along with a medical centre, detox facilities, a pharmacy, a cafe, creative spaces, and a rooftop garden. Next to the new building, the old Prince of Wales Hotel has been restored and folded into the site rather than demolished.

HomeGround opened in twenty twenty two, designed by Stevens Lawson Architects, and won that year's Auckland Architecture Award for public architecture, with the judges calling it a project built on ah roh hah, on care. The argument made by the laneway is the most important one, because it lets anyone walk through, so that the people who live here and the people passing by share the same path.

This is people's home, so please keep a respectful distance from windows and doorways; but the laneway is open to the public, and walking through it is the best way to understand the building, before continuing to Saint Matthew in the City, the old stone church close by.
