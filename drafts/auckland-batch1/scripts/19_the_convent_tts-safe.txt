The Convent Boutique Hotel

It's nineteen twenty two, and on a bend of Great North Road in Grey Lynn, a new convent is going up beside the parish church, built in a Spanish mission style more often seen in California.

Find the spire of Saint Joseph's Church, and beside it a long, pale building with arched openings and dark framed windows. That building is The Convent, which housed Saint Joseph's Convent until nineteen ninety three, fell into disrepair, and reopened as a small hotel in October twenty twenty.

The windows facing the church are worth a moment, because from some of the rooms inside, guests look straight out at the spire and at a statue of Mary in the grounds next door, so the old relationship between convent and church has survived the change of use, at least as a view.

The restoration was overseen by the developer Andy Davies, who is also behind Ponsonby Central, and his approach was to keep and reuse what was already there: exposed brick, high ceilings, carved timber, and period furniture, with stone basins and copper pipework added alongside energy saving lighting. There are twenty two rooms, and the names lean into the building's past, with one called Mother Superior and another called The Sakristy.

Auckland has knocked down a great many buildings like this one, so it's worth pausing on why this one survived. A convent is an awkward building to reuse, full of small rooms built for a very particular way of life, and yet those small rooms turned out to suit a boutique hotel almost perfectly; sometimes the most practical future for an old building is the one that sits closest to its original shape.

Walk around to the front of Saint Joseph's to see the church that the convent was built to serve, and then carry on along Great North Road towards the cafes and bars of Grey Lynn and Ponsonby.
