Jemmay zeh Street

In the eighteen nineties, a family left the mountain town of Buh sharry, in northern Lebanon, and settled in Dun eedin, and more than a century later one of their descendants opened a restaurant in an Auckland arcade and named it after a street in Bay root.

Saint Kevin's Arcade opens off Karrang ah hah peh Road, and Jemmay zeh St is inside it, so walk in from the street along the arcade's corridor of small shops and look for a doorway glowing with candles and low glass pendants. That's shops fifteen and sixteen, and the room beyond runs back towards the arcade's rear windows.

The chef is Sameer Allen, who grew up in Dun eedin, cooked for five years at The Grove under Ben Bailey, and spent four months in Lebanon in twenty fourteen reconnecting with his mother's side of the family. He opened Jemmay zeh St in late twenty fifteen, years earlier than planned, with his mother, Lee ann, running the floor.

Have a look at the bar and kitchen wall, which is panelled in hammered copper that Sameer beat by hand himself. Around it hang family portraits and brass trays, while the glass pendants overhead are the work of the glass artist Luke Jay komb, and the whole effect sits somewhere between a family dining room and a very good night out.

The cooking is Lebanese made with New Zealand produce: hummus topped with fried chickpeas, kibbeh nyeh, and a slow braised lamb shoulder for the table. Sameer has said he wanted to challenge the idea that Lebanese food is something you only eat late at night after the pubs close, and the room makes that argument better than any menu could, because nobody sits under hand beaten copper and family photographs expecting a kebab in a paper bag.

If you're eating here, ask for one of the back tables, where old leadlight windows look down over the trees of Myers Park; Saint Kevin's Arcade itself dates from the nineteen twenties, and it's worth walking its full length before you leave.
