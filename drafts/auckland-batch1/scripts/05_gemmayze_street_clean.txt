Gemmayze St

In the 1890s, a family left the mountain town of Bsharri, in northern Lebanon, and settled in Dunedin, and more than a century later one of their descendants opened a restaurant in an Auckland arcade and named it after a street in Beirut.

St Kevin's Arcade opens off Karangahape Road, and Gemmayze St is inside it, so walk in from the street along the arcade's corridor of small shops and look for a doorway glowing with candles and low glass pendants. That's shops fifteen and sixteen, and the room beyond runs back towards the arcade's rear windows.

The chef is Samir Allen, who grew up in Dunedin, cooked for five years at The Grove under Ben Bayly, and spent four months in Lebanon in 2014 reconnecting with his mother's side of the family. He opened Gemmayze St in late 2015, years earlier than planned, with his mother, Liane, running the floor.

Have a look at the bar and kitchen wall, which is panelled in hammered copper that Samir beat by hand himself. Around it hang family portraits and brass trays, while the glass pendants overhead are the work of the glass artist Luke Jacomb, and the whole effect sits somewhere between a family dining room and a very good night out.

The cooking is Lebanese made with New Zealand produce: hummus topped with fried chickpeas, kibbeh nayeh, and a slow-braised lamb shoulder for the table. Samir has said he wanted to challenge the idea that Lebanese food is something you only eat late at night after the pubs close, and the room makes that argument better than any menu could, because nobody sits under hand-beaten copper and family photographs expecting a kebab in a paper bag.

If you're eating here, ask for one of the back tables, where old leadlight windows look down over the trees of Myers Park; St Kevin's Arcade itself dates from the 1920s, and it's worth walking its full length before you leave.
