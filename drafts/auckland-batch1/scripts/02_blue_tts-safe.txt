Blue

Every December, Franklin Road becomes one of the busiest streets in Auckland after dark, when the villas running down the hill switch on the Christmas lights they've been putting up for more than thirty years. The rest of the year the top of the road belongs to the neighbours, and since twenty twenty five, to Blue.

The corner building at the head of Franklin Road, just off Ponsonby Road, is the one to look for: pale timber floors, bare brick walls, shelves of pottery and wine bottles, and, if you can see through to the back, a sunny courtyard that wouldn't look out of place in Tuscany. The space used to be Agnes Curran, and the brief for its designer, Sam Boh ah nass, was to strip it back and uncover what the old building had once been.

Blue is really two places sharing one address: a cafe in the morning and a wine bar at night, run by four owners, Joe Dale Dickson, Katie Riley, and Ophelia and Karl Bailey, who came up through Auckland's restaurant scene. Breakfast is egg buns on bree osh baked in house, along with Scandinavian style plates, while the evening brings Kiwi onion dip, teh reen with rye, and wines by the glass that change weekly.

The detail to take away is on the supplier list, where alongside the creameries and growers sits produce from the garden at Mah noo reh wah High School, in South Auckland. That one line makes a quiet case for the whole venture, because it suggests a fashionable Ponsonby corner can still behave like a neighbourhood cafe, buying from a school garden and pouring the same glass for a regular as for a passer by.

From the doorway, look down Franklin Road at the line of plane trees and old villas falling away down the hill; the walk to the bottom takes about ten minutes, and if you're here in December, it's worth coming back after dark to see the whole street lit up.
