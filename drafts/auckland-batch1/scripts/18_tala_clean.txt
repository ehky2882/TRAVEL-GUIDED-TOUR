Tala

Tala is the Samoan word for a story or a tale, and in this dining room the chef tells you one with every course.

Behind a discreet, understated frontage at two-thirty-five Parnell Road, along a stretch lined with older shopfronts, sits Tala. Inside, it's an intimate room arranged around an open kitchen, and at its heart is an umu, the Samoan way of cooking with fire and hot stones.

Watch the umu if you can, because it's used to cook a spatchcock chicken in front of the guests, a modern take on food that in Samoa would be cooked outdoors, on stones heated in a fire, for a family gathering. Before the meal begins there's also the apa fafano, a traditional hand-washing ritual, which sets a gentle, ceremonial tone from the start.

The chef, Henry Onesemo, grew up in Samoa and cooked in Hawaii, Florida and Thailand before working at Meredith's in Auckland, and he opened Tala with his wife, Debby, in 2023. A year later the magazine Cuisine named it New Restaurant of the Year, praising a room that felt like an intimate conversation in someone's home.

One dish captures the spirit better than any other. It's called raw ramen, and it's built on a childhood snack many Samoan children will recognise, dry instant noodles eaten straight from the packet, reworked with crunchy noodles and sweet, chewy dried vegetables. Turning a schoolyard snack into a fine-dining course takes a certain confidence, and it's that sense of humour, alongside the care, that makes Tala feel like a story rather than a menu.
