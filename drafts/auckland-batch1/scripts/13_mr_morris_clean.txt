Mr Morris

Mr Morris is named after someone who never got to eat here, and the story behind that name is a gentle one.

On the corner of Galway and Commerce Streets, the old heritage building in front of you is the Excelsior Building, and its ground floor belongs to Mr Morris. Through the windows you should be able to see pale timber, rough brick and plaster walls, and an open kitchen set up like a stage at the centre of the room.

Ray Morris was the uncle of Claire Baudinet, chef Michael Meredith's business partner, and a keen supporter of his cooking, and he died unexpectedly while the restaurant was still being planned. Naming it after him was a way of keeping him at the table, which explains why the private dining room, hidden behind an unmarked door, is called Mrs Morris.

This room has a past too, because until 2020 it was Cafe Hanoi, which moved across the road, and Nat Cheshire's redesign turned it into something lighter, with a forest-green ceiling and door handles cast in brass in the shape of Samoan fans. At the heart of the kitchen is an asado grill, the South American fire-and-wood set-up, believed at opening to be the first of its kind in New Zealand.

Meredith has described the food as having worldwide influences, just like New Zealand, and that's a fair summary of a menu that can run from steamed pāua and masala-spiced quail to pani popo, the soft Samoan coconut buns, here served with passionfruit ice cream. Put simply, it's fine dining built out of a country made of many places, cooked over fire and served without fuss.

From the corner, follow Commerce Street a few minutes north towards the harbour and the old Ferry Building on Quay Street, which is a good place to watch the boats come and go once you've eaten.
