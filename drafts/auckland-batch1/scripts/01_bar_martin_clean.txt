Bar Martin

On a quiet residential street in Mount Albert, there's a bar that behaves rather more like a front room than a business.

Find number forty-three on Martin Avenue, the narrow shopfront with Bar Martin painted above it, and through the glass you should make out a long wooden bar down one side, a handful of small tables near the window, and, if the back door is open, a courtyard beyond with communal tables and a pair of olive trees.

Start with the bar itself, which is built from reclaimed kauri — the great native timber colonial Auckland felled and built itself from, and which now turns up mostly as salvage. The walls around it are painted half in deep nautical navy and half in cream, a split that came from Hannah Maurice, an architect, who designed the room with her husband Ruben, the owner.

The menu is small, partly by choice and partly because the kitchen is tiny, and its best-known dish has an unlikely origin: coppa and crisps with fennel oil, which Hannah suggested after coming across the recipe in the New Yorker. Around it sit anchovies, burrata and good bread, chosen to keep a glass of wine company, and there's an off-licence too, so the bottle you liked can go home with you.

The brief behind all this is what makes the place worth a stop, because Ruben set out to make somewhere anyone from twenty to ninety would feel at ease, and it welcomes children and dogs to match. It opened in November 2020, after months of Covid and council delays, which is a long wait for a room this modest.

If the evening is warm, head through to the courtyard and find a seat under the olives; Ōwairaka, the old volcanic cone of Mount Albert, is about twenty minutes away on foot if you fancy watching the light go from the summit afterwards.
