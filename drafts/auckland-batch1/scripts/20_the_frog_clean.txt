The Frog

The colour came first and the name came afterwards, which is why The Frog is so very green.

Towards the western end of Karangahape Road, the giveaway is a small bar painted green, with outdoor seats on the pavement and art covering the walls inside. If you spot lamps that seem to be made from T-shirts glowing through the window, you're standing in front of it.

Those lamps are the work of Robbie Fraser, a contemporary artist and one of the two owners, and they make the room feel as much a gallery as a bar. His partner in the venture is Tāné Williams, who used to run a café in St Kevin's Arcade, and the team from Rose's, the restaurant that was here before, stayed on as partners and advisers when The Frog opened in November 2025.

The food is kept deliberately simple: chips with a house-made onion dip, roast rosemary potatoes, olives with orange, and spiced nuts. On the drinks side, look for The Bush, an alcohol-free cocktail full of herbs, alongside a lager from Gisborne on tap, a yuzu martini, and a coffee drink called Daddy's Treat, and there's an off-licence if you'd rather take a bottle home.

The owners have said they wanted somewhere to socialise and chat rather than a place to rage, and on K Road, which has been Auckland's late-night street for generations, that's a considered choice. It puts The Frog in the street's other tradition, the one of artists, small galleries and neighbourhood bars, where the point of going out is the conversation.

[beat]

Head east along K Road under the shop verandas to St Kevin's Arcade, five minutes' walk away, and follow its steps down at the far end into the green hollow of Myers Park.
