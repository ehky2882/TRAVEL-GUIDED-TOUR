ATLAS · SHANGHAI · 10 · The sook oh tie Shanghai
---
A Thai hotel, in Shanghai, designed as a garden by two Shanghai architects. It sounds like a muddle, but it works rather well.

You're on way hi Road, at number three eighty, at the hotel's entrance, in the middle of jing ahn. The old lanes of jahng yooen are a short walk away. Go in through the doors, and the lobby opens up in front of you.

The sook oh tie is a hotel name from Bangkok. When it came to Shanghai, it opened in twenty eighteen, and the brand asked the local studio nairy and hoo to design it. Their brief to themselves was an urban oasis. Every part of the building was planned as some kind of garden. The ground floor was treated as an open square. The upstairs corridors became garden paths. And each guest room was designed as a small house of its own.

Have a look at the staircase in the lobby. It's made of thick slabs of grey tuh rat so. Each one is shifted slightly from the one below, so the stair seems to float as it climbs. It has a thin bronze handrail that looks barely attached.

Then look up. Above the stair hangs a huge wooden lantern, a woven lattice of timber. Inside it are dozens of bronze and glass globes. As you climb the stairs, you walk up through the lights.

Last, find the all day restaurant and garden bar. The floors and walls are green tuh rat so and tiles with a pattern of leaves, so you're in a garden even when it's raining.

What I find interesting is that nothing here is directly Thai. There are no golden spires and no elephants. The architects took the spirit of the brand, which is calm and a closeness to nature, and translated it into Shanghai materials. It's a bit like a good translation of a poem: the same feeling, in another language.

When you leave, walk a few steps along way hi Road to number four twenty, the Cha House. It's a European style mansion from the nineteen twenties that was moved and carefully restored. Then turn the corner into jahng yooen.
