ATLAS · SHANGHAI · 12 · Xin Rong Ji
---
This restaurant group started in a basement in a small coastal town. Its Shanghai flagship is in a mansion that belonged to the family of one of the world's most famous architects.

You're on Nanyang Road, at number one-seventy, a quiet street a few minutes' walk from Jing'an Temple. Behind the wall is a heritage villa with a garden. This is Xin Rong Ji's Shanghai flagship, and lately it's also been listed under the name Rong Fu Yan. There's no sign outside saying "come in". This is somewhere you book in advance.

Start with the house. It once belonged to the Pei family, an old Suzhou banking family. The architect I. M. Pei, who designed the glass pyramid at the Louvre, came from that family. Inside, the restaurant hangs some of Pei's own drawings. Look out too for decorative details in the style of Suzhou's gardens and houses.

Then there's the food. Xin Rong Ji was started in October nineteen ninety-five, in a basement in Linhai, in the Taizhou area of Zhejiang province, south of here on the coast, by a local man called Zhang Yong. It began as a seafood restaurant with a live tank. It still cooks Taizhou food: very fresh fish from the East China Sea, cooked simply so that the fish does the work.

The dish everyone talks about is yellow croaker, a delicate sea fish that has become rare and expensive in the wild. Here it's usually steamed or braised, and it's served with some ceremony.

The success has been remarkable. This restaurant won two Michelin stars in twenty eighteen. The next year, the group's Beijing restaurant became the first in mainland China to be awarded three stars.

[beat]

So a seafood restaurant from a provincial basement ended up in a banker's mansion, in the most expensive part of Shanghai. It got there by doing one simple thing, very well, for a very long time.

From here, walk east along Nanyang Road to Shaanxi North Road. Within a few minutes of that corner are two more great family houses: Rong Zhai at number one-eight-six, the flour king's villa, and the Hotung residence at number four-five-seven.
