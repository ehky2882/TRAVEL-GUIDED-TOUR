ATLAS · SHANGHAI · 03 · zhahn zhorzh and mer kahto
---
The best views of Shanghai's new skyline are from inside one of its oldest office blocks.

You're on the Bund, at Number Three, where gwahng dong Road meets the river road. In front of you is a heavy stone building in the classical style. That's Three on the Bund. Behind you, across the road and the riverside promenade, the towers of poo dong are on the far bank.

This was built in nineteen sixteen for the Union Assurance Company, in the grand classical style that every bank and insurer on this stretch was competing in. In two thousand and four, the American architect Michael Graves turned the inside into restaurants, shops and a gallery, and the French chef zhahn zhorzh vonger ikten opened his restaurant on the fourth floor. It's still there.

Here's the curious bit. One of the architects who worked on that renovation for Graves was a young man called lindon nairy. He went on to found the Shanghai practice nairy and hoo with his partner, rozzahna hoo. In two thousand and sixteen, they were asked to redesign zhahn zhorzh. So the same architect came back to the same rooms, more than ten years later.

What they did was mostly take things away. Out went the heavy curtains and the dark wood. During the demolition, the builders found classical arched columns that had been hidden under later finishes. The architects left them bare, worn and all. They restored the old plaster mouldings, and they added antique mirrors etched with lines from French philosophers. If you go up, look for those.

Two floors higher, on the sixth, is mer kahto, the chef's more relaxed Italian place, also by nairy and hoo. mer kahto means market. It's sharing plates, wood and brick, and a noisier room.

What I like about this building is that you're never allowed to forget where you are. Every new room in it has been arranged around the windows, and every window looks at the other side of the river.

When you come out, cross over to the riverside promenade and turn round. From there you can see the whole curve of the Bund, and pick out Number Three among its neighbours.
