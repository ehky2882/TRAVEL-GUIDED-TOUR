# Jesse Restaurant

*Atlas · Shanghai · 04 · ~353 words, about 140 seconds*

---

This is one of the most famous restaurants in Shanghai, and it's about the size of a large kitchen.

You're on Tianping Road, at number forty-one, in the old French Concession, just off Huaihai Road. The restaurant is in a narrow, ordinary lane house. There's a small shopfront and the name Jesse above the door. In Chinese it's Old Jesse, and if there's a queue on the pavement, you've definitely found it.

Inside, it's two cramped floors of tables pressed close together. Most guides agree it's been cooking here since two thousand and four, and this is the original. There are other branches round the city now, but most people will tell you this is the one.

What they cook is benbang, which is the local Shanghai home cooking. The phrase people use for it means, roughly, "thick oil and red sauce". It's sweet, glossy and dark with soy, and it's a long way from the light Cantonese food many visitors expect.

Three dishes come up again and again. First, the red-braised pork belly, cooked slowly in soy and sugar until it shines. Then the scallion fish head: a whole fish head buried under a heap of spring onions fried until they're sweet and almost black. It's the one people photograph. And third, rice cooked in a hot pot with crab meat and roe.

None of this is fancy, and that's the point. Shanghai is usually sold as a city of cocktail bars and towers. But what Shanghai people actually miss when they're away is food like this, the food their grandmothers made, served in a room where you can hear the next table's conversation.

[beat]

Don't be put off by the wait. Even with a booking, people often stand outside for a while. It's part of eating here.

Afterwards, walk north to the top of Tianping Road, where it meets Huaihai Road. Five roads come together there, and on the corner is the Wukang Mansion. It's a long, wedge-shaped block of flats from the nineteen twenties, shaped rather like the bow of a ship. It's at its best at dusk.
