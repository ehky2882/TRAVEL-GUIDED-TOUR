# The Sukhothai Shanghai

*Atlas · Shanghai · 10 · ~353 words, about 140 seconds*

---

A Thai hotel, in Shanghai, designed as a garden by two Shanghai architects. It sounds like a muddle, but it works rather well.

You're on Weihai Road, at number three-eighty, at the hotel's entrance, in the middle of Jing'an. The old lanes of Zhang Yuan are a short walk away. Go in through the doors, and the lobby opens up in front of you.

The Sukhothai is a hotel name from Bangkok. When it came to Shanghai, it opened in twenty eighteen, and the brand asked the local studio Neri and Hu to design it. Their brief to themselves was an urban oasis. Every part of the building was planned as some kind of garden. The ground floor was treated as an open square. The upstairs corridors became garden paths. And each guest room was designed as a small house of its own.

Have a look at the staircase in the lobby. It's made of thick slabs of grey terrazzo. Each one is shifted slightly from the one below, so the stair seems to float as it climbs. It has a thin bronze handrail that looks barely attached.

Then look up. Above the stair hangs a huge wooden lantern, a woven lattice of timber. Inside it are dozens of bronze-and-glass globes. As you climb the stairs, you walk up through the lights.

Last, find the all-day restaurant and garden bar. The floors and walls are green terrazzo and tiles with a pattern of leaves, so you're in a garden even when it's raining.

[beat]

What I find interesting is that nothing here is directly Thai. There are no golden spires and no elephants. The architects took the spirit of the brand, which is calm and a closeness to nature, and translated it into Shanghai materials. It's a bit like a good translation of a poem: the same feeling, in another language.

When you leave, walk a few steps along Weihai Road to number four-twenty, the Cha House. It's a European-style mansion from the nineteen twenties that was moved and carefully restored. Then turn the corner into Zhang Yuan.
