ATLAS · SHANGHAI · 12 · shin rong jee
---
This restaurant group started in a basement in a small coastal town. Its Shanghai flagship is in a mansion that belonged to the family of one of the world's most famous architects.

You're on nahn yahng Road, at number one seventy, a quiet street a few minutes' walk from jing ahn Temple. Behind the wall is a heritage villa with a garden. This is shin rong jee's Shanghai flagship, and lately it's also been listed under the name rong foo yen. There's no sign outside saying "come in". This is somewhere you book in advance.

Start with the house. It once belonged to the pay family, an old soo joe banking family. The architect eye em pay, who designed the glass pyramid at the Louvre, came from that family. Inside, the restaurant hangs some of pay's own drawings. Look out too for decorative details in the style of soo joe's gardens and houses.

Then there's the food. shin rong jee was started in October nineteen ninety five, in a basement in lin hi, in the tie joe area of juh jyahng province, south of here on the coast, by a local man called jahng yong. It began as a seafood restaurant with a live tank. It still cooks tie joe food: very fresh fish from the East China Sea, cooked simply so that the fish does the work.

The dish everyone talks about is yellow croaker, a delicate sea fish that has become rare and expensive in the wild. Here it's usually steamed or braised, and it's served with some ceremony.

The success has been remarkable. This restaurant won two Michelin stars in twenty eighteen. The next year, the group's Beijing restaurant became the first in mainland China to be awarded three stars.

So a seafood restaurant from a provincial basement ended up in a banker's mansion, in the most expensive part of Shanghai. It got there by doing one simple thing, very well, for a very long time.

From here, walk east along nahn yahng Road to shahn shee North Road. Within a few minutes of that corner are two more great family houses: rong jie at number one eight six, the flour king's villa, and the ho tung residence at number four five seven.
