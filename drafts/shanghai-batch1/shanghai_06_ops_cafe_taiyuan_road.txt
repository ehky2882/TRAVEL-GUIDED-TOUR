ATLAS · SHANGHAI · 06 · Ops Cafe
---
There's no espresso on this menu. No latte, no Americano. And you'll drink whatever you order standing up.

You're on Taiyuan Road, near the corner of Yongjia Road, in the leafy western part of the old French Concession. Look for a ground-floor shop in a residential building at Lane one-seven-seven. There's no big sign. It's about ten square metres. The easiest way to spot it is the queue.

Ops opened in August twenty seventeen. It was started by two baristas, Ahguang and Siwei, who are both serious figures in Chinese competitive coffee. Ahguang represented China at the World Barista Championship. The idea came from an iced coffee he drank in Thailand that tasted more like a cocktail than a coffee. So that's what they make: original drinks, built the way a bartender builds a drink, out of coffee plus fruit, tea, herbs, salt, or the odd splash of whisky.

The menu is tiny. There are four or five drinks at a time, and they change every season, so whatever you have today probably won't be here in a few months. One summer there was a drink made with coconut water and topped with a foam of salted macadamia and a little seaweed.

Have a look at how the queue works. Staff walk down the line taking your order and payment before you reach the door. At the counter, a barista mixes your drink in front of you and tells you how to drink it. Foam first, usually, and they'll suggest finishing it in about ten minutes, before it changes.

The name stands for three words: open-minded, possibilities, and space. That last one matters. There are no chairs, just a narrow ledge, so everyone stands shoulder to shoulder. The founders say that's intended. The crowding is meant to get you talking.

[beat]

So you queue for half an hour, drink something in ten minutes, and leave having chatted with a stranger. It's not very efficient, but that's what it's for.

Afterwards, take a stroll along Yongjia Road. It's one of the quietest streets in this part of the city, with old garden villas behind the plane trees and walls.
