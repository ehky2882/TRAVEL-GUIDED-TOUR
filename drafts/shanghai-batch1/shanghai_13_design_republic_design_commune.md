# 设计共和 Design Republic

*Atlas · Shanghai · 13 · ~368 words, about 150 seconds*

---

This building started life as a police station. Now people come here to buy chairs.

You're on Jiangning Road, at number five-eleven, in Jing'an. In front of you is a red-brick building with a band of plain glass along the street, where you'd expect a row of shopfronts. This is the Design Republic Design Commune. In Chinese, it's Sheji Gonghe.

The building went up in the nineteen-tens as a police headquarters, built by the British when this was part of the International Settlement. By the time the Shanghai architects Neri and Hu took it on, in twenty ten, it was almost derelict. The wood was rotten and the plaster was falling off.

The outside was protected, so most of it had to stay as it was. Look at the brick. The architects cleaned it, repaired it, and left it bare. Then look at the glass at street level. That's where a row of small shops once stood. They were removed and replaced with one long sheet of clear glazing. You can see straight through it to the old brick and the rough concrete inside.

Once inside, look for the cuts. Neri and Hu removed bits of floor, wall and ceiling in small, precise openings. Through them you can see old wooden laths and older brickwork, so you can read the building's history in layers, like rock in a cliff face.

Then the shop itself. Design Republic is a furniture shop that Lyndon Neri and Rossana Hu started themselves. When the building reopened in twenty twelve, it became their flagship, with a gallery, a café and a restaurant around it. It even has a small flat upstairs.

[beat]

So the architects took a building that had been about control, a police station, and opened it up. You can see in through the glass and through the walls. And the architects were also the shopkeepers, which is a nice way of making sure the building works.

If you've enjoyed their work, Neri and Hu's buildings are all over this city. The Blue Bottle café in Zhang Yuan and the lobby of the Sukhothai hotel on Weihai Road are both about a fifteen-minute walk south-east of here.
