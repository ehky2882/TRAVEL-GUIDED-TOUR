# Meet the Bund

*Atlas · Shanghai · 05 · ~368 words, about 150 seconds*

---

Most of Shanghai's restaurants come from somewhere else in China. This one came from the south coast, from Xiamen, and it brought the whole province with it.

You're at the Bund Finance Center, on Zhongshan East Second Road, the newer, low-rise district at the southern end of the Bund, between the river and the old city. Meet the Bund is on the third floor of the South Block. Its Chinese name, Yu Waitan, means much the same thing.

It opened in twenty nineteen, set up by a Xiamen restaurant group called Yanyu, and it was the first serious Fujian restaurant in Shanghai. Two years later it won a Michelin star. A sister restaurant has since opened on the fifty-sixth floor of Raffles City, across Suzhou Creek, and the name now sits high on Asia's Fifty Best Restaurants list.

Fujian is the province facing Taiwan across the strait, and its cooking is very different from what you'll find here in Shanghai. It's lighter, built round seafood and slow, clear broths, and it uses a lot of red rice wine. The menu here is arranged by the dialects of Fujian, so you eat your way round the province one region at a time.

One dish worth looking for is Hetian chicken from Longyan, in the hills of western Fujian. It's steamed in a brine with rice wine, and it's simple and very good. The other is Fujian's most famous dish, Buddha Jumps Over the Wall. It's a rich soup of dried seafood, simmered for hours. The story goes that it smelled so good that a monk climbed over the monastery wall to get some.

[beat]

Some cuisines arrive in a city through immigrants and cheap noodle shops. Fujian's came to Shanghai the other way round, at the top, in a finance district, with a chef trained in Xiamen. That tells you something about who's eating in Shanghai now.

Before you leave, look for the Fosun Foundation building here at the Bund Finance Center. Its front is covered by a curtain of bronze-coloured tubes that slowly moves to and fro. It was designed by Thomas Heatherwick and Foster and Partners, and it's well worth waiting to see it move.
