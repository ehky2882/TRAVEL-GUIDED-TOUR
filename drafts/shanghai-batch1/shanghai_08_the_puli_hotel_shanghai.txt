ATLAS · SHANGHAI · 08 · The PuLi Hotel and Spa 璞丽酒店
---
The first thing this hotel asks you to do is slow down, which in Jing'an is quite a request.

You're on Changde Road, at the entrance to the PuLi, a tall, plain tower just off Yan'an Road. Behind it are the trees of Jing'an Park, and the hotel has been arranged to face them.

It opened in twenty oh nine as the first hotel from a small company that called its idea an "urban resort": something meant to feel like a retreat, in the middle of one of the busiest districts in Shanghai. The tower is twenty-six storeys tall, designed by the Japanese firm Kume Sekkei. The inside was done by the Indonesian studio Jaya and Associates. The brief said the park had to be part of the design, and you'll see that it is.

Go into the lobby and look for three things. First, the water: a long, shallow reflecting pool running beside the main public room, with rows of bamboo along the glass. Second, what everything is made of. The materials were chosen to be local: dark Chinese timber, cast bronze, air-dried clay tiles, and the grey brick you see in old Shanghai lane houses. Third, and this is the one people remember, in the library, above the fireplace, the panels are covered in stingray leather.

And the name. The pu in PuLi is the Chinese character for uncut jade, stone before anyone has polished it. It's a fair description of the building, too: nothing shiny, nothing gold.

[beat]

When this hotel opened, luxury in Shanghai meant chandeliers and marble. The PuLi went the other way. It went for quiet, grey brick, water and a view of trees. That approach has been copied all over the city since.

When you leave, cross into Jing'an Park itself. It's an old cemetery turned into a park, with some fine old plane trees. Keep going through it to Nanjing West Road and you'll come out opposite the golden roofs of Jing'an Temple.
