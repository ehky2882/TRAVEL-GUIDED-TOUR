Patricia Ready Gallery of Art

This is one of Santiago's larger private galleries, and its architects spent most of their effort making the building disappear.

Concrete, glass and a courtyard are what greet you at number three thousand one hundred and twenty-five on Espoz, a quiet Vitacura street close to Alonso de Córdova: a long, low building in bare concrete, with tall windows opening onto an inner court.

The gallery was founded by Patricia Ready, an art restorer by training who has been championing Chilean artists since 1981; it moved into this building in 2008 after several years in La Dehesa, and she also publishes La Panera, a magazine about art and culture.

The building is by the architects Izquierdo Lehmann, working with Elton Léniz, and their stated aim was to remove from view anything that might compete with the art, so the materials are deliberately few: exposed concrete, travertine floors, and a ceiling of translucent glass and steel that hides its lighting and needs no columns.

Now find the freestanding partition that hangs a little over three metres back from the tall courtyard windows, because it's the clever bit, filtering the glare and giving the main room its edge so that daylight reaches the work without ever flooding it.

Notice what that does to the classic white cube. Galleries often seal themselves off from the city, whereas this one opens its main room onto a sculpture courtyard, so contemplation and street life sit side by side, and with a café, a bookshop and a ninety-nine-seat auditorium downstairs, it behaves more like a small public institution than a shop.

[beat]

It's a building that does its best work by staying out of the way.

Do go in and give the main room a slow lap, then finish in the courtyard, where the sculpture changes with each exhibition and the light changes with the hour.
