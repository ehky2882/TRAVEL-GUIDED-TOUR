Fukasawa

This restaurant is named after a teacher, which is not something you can say about many places.

Head for the CasaCostanera complex on Avenida Nueva Costanera and take the lift right to the top, because Fukasawa occupies the fourth-floor rooftop, a space the building simply calls Roof, with a terrace that looks east towards the Andes.

The chef is Marcos Baeza, a veteran of Santiago's Japanese kitchens, who opened Fukasawa in December 2022 and cooks here alongside his twin sons, Marcus and Lucas. The name honours Naoki Fukasawa, the Japanese chef who mentored Baeza when they worked together years ago, so every booking is, in a small way, a thank-you note.

The menu brings Japanese technique to the Chilean coast, with touches of Nikkei, the cooking of Peru's Japanese community, and a very local larder: Patagonian king crab, lobster, truffles and caviar from Chilean sturgeon farms, alongside gyoza filled with lamb and foie gras.

If you'd rather hand the decisions over entirely, there's an omakase, the Japanese custom of letting the chef choose for you, which seems especially fitting in a kitchen built on trust between master and pupil.

It's worth noticing how naturally the pairing works, because Chile's long Pacific coastline produces some remarkable seafood, and Japanese cooking is, at heart, about respecting exactly that kind of raw material, so the marriage here feels less like fusion than an old friendship made official.

[beat]

A teacher's name over the door, and a father and his sons at the counter, make for a restaurant that wears its lineage rather proudly.

If you're up on the roof around sunset, wait until the last light has gone off the mountains before you leave, then stroll a block over to Alonso de Córdova, where the galleries and design shops make for a pleasant evening walk.
