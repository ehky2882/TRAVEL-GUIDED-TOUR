Cenco Costanera Mall

Three hundred metres of glass and steel rise out of this shopping centre, and the tower in front of you is the tallest building in South America.

Tip your head right back from the pavement on Avenida Andrés Bello, with the Mapocho river and its park behind you, and the Gran Torre Costanera fills the sky ahead, a tapering glass shaft rising out of the mall's long, low base. The mall itself, now branded Cenco Costanera, is the six-storey block you'd walk into at street level.

The tower, designed by the Argentine-born architect César Pelli, has sixty-two floors, and the last two are given over to Sky Costanera, an observation deck that opened in 2015 with the full sweep of the city and the Andes on a clear day.

What's easy to forget is that construction began in 2006, but in January 2009 the global financial crisis brought it to a halt, leaving a half-built skeleton on the skyline until work resumed at the end of that year, and the tower finally reached its full height in February 2012.

The mall below opened that June and is often described as the largest in South America, with six floors of shops and restaurants and a vast Jumbo hypermarket.

And here's the thing about it: the building that redrew Santiago's skyline wasn't commissioned by a government or a bank, but by Cencosud, the retail group behind those Jumbo supermarkets, which says a good deal about how modern Santiago grew.

Love it or not, and people here have argued about it from the start, it's now the landmark most of the city uses to find its bearings.

If the sky is clear, it's worth a ticket for the viewing deck late in the afternoon, when the low sun turns the Andes pink; otherwise, cross over into the riverside park and look back, because the tower makes far more sense from a distance than from directly underneath.
