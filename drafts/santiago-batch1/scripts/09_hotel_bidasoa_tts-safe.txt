Hotel Beedasoh ah

The Beedasoh ah is a river on the far side of the world, the short stretch of water that marks the border between Spain's Basque Country and France, and this hotel carries its name for a reason.

Aveneeda Veetakoora, number four thousand eight hundred and seventy three, is the spot: a family run hotel set among gardens, with a newer eight storey tower alongside the original house.

Its founder, Mowreeseeo Sahnz, was born near that river in nineteen thirty two and left Spain as a small child during the Civil War, eventually settling in Santiago. His family's name was already known here through Pinpeeleen Powsha, a Basque restaurant that opened downtown in nineteen forty and became a meeting place for business people, artists and intellectuals for decades; the name, rather sweetly, means butterfly in Basque.

In nineteen eighty nine, at the age of fifty six, Sahnz opened this hotel with thirty five rooms, wanting, as his son has put it, to make it a home for visitors from abroad and to bring a little of his Spain to Chile.

From twenty fourteen the family bought up neighbouring plots, added the tower and multiplied the floor space roughly sixfold, to more than a hundred rooms, while the original house became Casa Sahnz, a plant based restaurant with music and cultural events; the gardens, meanwhile, are looked after by the family matriarch, Mareea Elayna Rahb.

Seen that way, this isn't really a boutique hotel at all, but the latest chapter in a family story about exile and hospitality, about a boy who crossed an ocean and spent his life making strangers feel at home.

Three of his seven children now run the hotel, and Pinpeeleen Powsha lives on too, in a newer home in the El Golf district.

If you have time, wander into the garden or call into Casa Sahnz for a coffee, then follow Aveneeda Veetakoora west for twenty minutes or so to Parkay Beesentenahreeo, where the lagoons and lawns make a fine end to the walk.
