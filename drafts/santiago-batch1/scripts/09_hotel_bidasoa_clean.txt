Hotel Bidasoa

The Bidasoa is a river on the far side of the world, the short stretch of water that marks the border between Spain's Basque Country and France, and this hotel carries its name for a reason.

Avenida Vitacura, number four thousand eight hundred and seventy-three, is the spot: a family-run hotel set among gardens, with a newer eight-storey tower alongside the original house.

Its founder, Mauricio Sanz, was born near that river in 1932 and left Spain as a small child during the Civil War, eventually settling in Santiago. His family's name was already known here through Pinpilinpausha, a Basque restaurant that opened downtown in 1940 and became a meeting place for business people, artists and intellectuals for decades; the name, rather sweetly, means butterfly in Basque.

In 1989, at the age of fifty-six, Sanz opened this hotel with thirty-five rooms, wanting, as his son has put it, to make it a home for visitors from abroad and to bring a little of his Spain to Chile.

From 2014 the family bought up neighbouring plots, added the tower and multiplied the floor space roughly sixfold, to more than a hundred rooms, while the original house became Casa Sanz, a plant-based restaurant with music and cultural events; the gardens, meanwhile, are looked after by the family matriarch, María Elena Raab.

Seen that way, this isn't really a boutique hotel at all, but the latest chapter in a family story about exile and hospitality, about a boy who crossed an ocean and spent his life making strangers feel at home.

Three of his seven children now run the hotel, and Pinpilinpausha lives on too, in a newer home in the El Golf district.

If you have time, wander into the garden or call into Casa Sanz for a coffee, then follow Avenida Vitacura west for twenty minutes or so to Parque Bicentenario, where the lagoons and lawns make a fine end to the walk.
