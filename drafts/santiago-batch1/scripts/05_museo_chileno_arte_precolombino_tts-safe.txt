Chilean Museum of Pre Columbian Art

Chilean history, as most of us learn it, begins in fifteen forty one with a Spanish captain and a new city, yet the building in front of you tells a story thousands of years older.

The long, pale colonial building at the corner of Bandaira and Kompanyeea, one block west of the Plahsa deh Armahs and just behind the cathedral, is the old Royal Customs House, built between eighteen oh five and eighteen oh seven, and the museum's entrance is on Bandaira, at number three hundred and sixty one.

Sairheeo Larra een Garseea Morayno, the man behind it, was an architect who spent close to fifty years collecting pre Columbian objects, and in December nineteen eighty one he opened this museum to give his collection a permanent public home; it now holds more than three thousand pieces from around a hundred cultures, from Mesoamerica to the southern Andes.

Then head downstairs, into space that the architect Smeelyan Rahdeetch created beneath the old building in a renovation completed in twenty fourteen, which enlarged the museum by roughly seventy per cent. Down there is the permanent display called Chile Before Chile, and the title is the point.

Among its most moving pieces are those of the Cheenchorro people of the northern coast, who were preparing their dead with great care some seven thousand years ago, among the earliest such practices known anywhere and older than Egypt's; nearby stand chemamool, tall carved wooden figures from Mapoochay funerary tradition.

The curators' argument is gentle but firm, namely that the country's story didn't begin with a founding date on a plaque, and that the diversity of everything that came before is something to be proud of rather than tidied away.

It's a museum that quietly moves the start of the story back by several thousand years.

On your way out, look for the Inca keepoo, the knotted cords used to keep records without writing, then walk round to the Plahsa deh Armahs, where the official version of the founding sits in bronze, on horseback.
