Taller Roca Negra

High in the Cajón del Maipo, with the river below and mountains on every side, one of Chile's great sculptors chose to build a world of concrete.

The road up the Maipo valley, in the commune of San José de Maipo, brings you to Roca Negra, with the artist's house on one side and, across the road, the workshop and museum: a double-height studio behind a portico of red-pigmented concrete, and terraces of sculpture stepping up the slope. This is a private home and archive, so visits are by arrangement, and unless you've been invited in, please enjoy it from the roadside.

The sculptor is Federico Assler, born in 1929, a winner of Chile's National Prize for Visual Arts and three times of the Altazor award, and one of the generation of the 1950s who pushed Chilean sculpture towards abstraction.

His material is concrete, which he has worked since the 1970s using moulds cut from expanded polystyrene, which lets enormous weights take on soft, organic forms; he has said that his sculptures are made for people, to be touched and felt and even walked into.

With his partner, Francisca Délano, he settled here in 1993, turned a former inn into a house museum and built eleven terraces into the landscape, and the archive now runs to more than a thousand pieces, along with some two hundred numbered sketchbooks, each a record of how a work began.

The lovely part came in April 2026, when Chile declared the whole ensemble a national monument, and the decree protects not only the buildings and terraces but the relationship between them, the valley and the mountains, which means the law now recognises a view.

Few artists get to see their working life turned into a landscape worth protecting.

If you can't visit, look for Assler's concrete works back in Santiago, in the Parque de las Esculturas in Providencia beside the Mapocho, where you'll recognise the same instinct to make heavy things feel light.
