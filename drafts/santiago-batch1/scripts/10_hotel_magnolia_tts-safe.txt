Hotel Magnolia Santiago deh Chilay

Look up past the old stone facade and the building seems to repeat itself, upside down, in glass.

Outside number five hundred and thirty nine on Wairfanos, a block from the foot of Santa Looseea Hill, you should see a three storey mansion with Art Deco details in its stonework, and above it three newer floors clad in glass.

The house was built in nineteen twenty nine, designed by Edwardo Kostabal Sehgers for Ahna Sehgers Garseea Weedohbro, a prominent society figure and the daughter of the politician Hoolyo Sehgers.

Generations later, when it was turned into a hotel, the job went to the architect Kahsoo Sehgers, who discovered during the project that the original architect was one of her own forebears. Working with the interior designer Karoleena Delpeeahno, she restored the house, replicated its stonework where needed and added the three new floors, and the Hotel Magnolia opened in twenty sixteen.

Now look at that glass extension again, because the gift is printed on it: an upside down photograph of the floors below, so the new storeys announce themselves and disappear into the old house at the same time.

Here's what I love about this corner: rather than pretending the addition is old or shouting that it's new, the design simply lets the nineteen twenty nine house look at its own reflection, which is a graceful way of making peace with the past.

Inside, the old mansion now holds Daymo Magnolia, a restaurant with only fifteen seats that has made Latin America's Fifty Best list, and up top there's a roof terrace with views across the city to the Andes.

When you've had your look, walk a couple of minutes east and climb Santa Looseea Hill, and from the terraces see whether you can pick out the Magnolia's glass floors among the rooftops.
