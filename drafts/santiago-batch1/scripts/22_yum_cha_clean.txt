Yum Cha

Yum cha is Cantonese for drinking tea, and if you've come expecting trolleys of dumplings, this little restaurant has a surprise in store.

Tucked into a quiet stretch of La Herradura in Providencia, at number two thousand seven hundred and twenty-two, is the restaurant: an intimate room of only five or so tables, with a deliberately modest presence on the street.

The chef, Nicolás Tapia, fell for Chinese cooking while travelling through China, and he came home to build something unusual, a ten-course tasting menu that is entirely pescatarian, with fish and seafood but no red meat, and with every course paired not with a wine but with a different tea.

The cellar, if you can call it that, holds more than fifty teas, and Tapia changes at least one dish every week, so returning guests rarely eat quite the same meal twice; there is a wine pairing for the unconvinced, but the house gently steers you towards the tea.

The clever part is what the tea does, lifting a delicate piece of fish, resetting the palate between courses, or adding a smoky or floral note that changes how the next mouthful tastes, and Chinese technique applied to Chilean seafood turns out to be a natural fit for a country with this much coastline.

It has worked, too, since in 2025 Yum Cha placed twenty-eighth on Latin America's 50 Best Restaurants list and Tapia received a One Knife award from The Best Chef.

[beat]

A restaurant built around a cup of tea, in a country so devoted to wine, takes a certain nerve.

If tonight isn't a tea night, walk east for ten minutes or so to Tobalaba, where the garden terrace above the MUT market is a good place for a cup of something, if rather less ceremonially.
