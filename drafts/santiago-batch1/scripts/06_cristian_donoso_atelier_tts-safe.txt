Kreestyahn Donohso Atelier

There's a curious thing about this address on Alonso deh Kordova: it began as a furniture showroom and grew into something closer to a private world.

Alonso deh Kordova, Veetakoora's long street of galleries and design houses, runs right past you, and the atelier sits at number two thousand eight hundred and sixty, a short walk from the corner with Nwayva Kostanaira. What you're looking for is a showroom of handmade wooden furniture, the work of the cabinetmaker whose name is over the door.

Kreestyahn Donohso came to woodwork by a roundabout route, spending around fifteen years abroad, including time in India and in Ecuador, where he learned the craft, and his training is rooted in classical and colonial furniture making.

Look closely at the finishes, because that's where the craft shows. He works in sustainable timbers such as lengah, the southern beech of Patagonia, and walnut root, using traditional techniques like shellac alongside modern lacquers, and he mixes classical and modern lines because, as he has observed, most clients would rather combine styles than commit to one.

The gift detail is Lah Clay, a members' cafe and bistro that shares the premises with the workshop, where each member receives a small key shaped brooch designed by his wife, the jeweller Victoria Oddoh.

If you ask me, the pairing makes perfect sense, and Donohso has drawn the line himself: a cabinetmaker and a cook both work with their hands, choose their raw materials with care and fuss over presentation, so a beautiful table is only half the job without a good meal on it.

His stated aim for the place is a room so harmonious that only good things can happen in it, which is a fine brief for any piece of furniture.

If the showroom is open, spend a moment with the grain and the joints, then walk a few minutes north to Esposs, where the Patricia Ready gallery's concrete courtyard is well worth the detour.
