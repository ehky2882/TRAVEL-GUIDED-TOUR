Mercado Urbano Tobalaba

Beneath your feet is the busiest metro station in Chile, and somebody decided to build a market on top of it.

If you came up from Tobalaba station, where Lines One and Four meet, you're already inside it: the Mercado Urbano Tobalaba, usually just called MUT, wraps around the station, with its office towers rising overhead and a large clock in a nineteenth-century style marking the way through from the metro.

It opened in stages from July 2023, developed by Territoria, and the idea is right there in the name, an urban market spread over several levels around the station, with shops above ground, a food market two floors down and plenty of bicycle parking.

Head up to the third level for the part people love most, El Jardín, a planted garden walk that its creators compared to New York's High Line, with timber walkways threaded between the trees.

Behind the scenes, the green credentials are serious, from geothermal climate control and grey water for irrigation to some eighty-eight thousand square metres of green and common space across a site of almost three hectares.

Step back and the bigger idea comes into focus. Many of Santiago's big shopping centres were designed around the car, whereas this one was designed around a metro station, and in 2025 the Urban Land Institute named it among the ten best urban developments in the Americas, the only winner from outside the United States and Canada that year.

[beat]

A market whose front door is a train platform is a rather hopeful thing to find in a city of this size.

Ride the escalators down to the food market and pick up something to eat, then carry it back up to the garden, because a picnic among the trees above a metro station is very much the point of the place.
