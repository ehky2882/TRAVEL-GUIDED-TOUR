The Singular Santiago, Lastarria Hotel

Some new buildings shout, but this one arrived in Santiago's old bohemian quarter wearing a dark suit and keeping its voice down.

Easy to miss, the black façade with understated signage at number two hundred and ninety-four on Merced is The Singular, a few steps from the cafés and bookshops of Lastarria and a short walk from Santa Lucía Hill.

It opened in 2014 as the city sister of The Singular Patagonia, and it's the work of Felipe Sahli, whose family has been in Chilean hospitality since his grandparents arrived from Switzerland in 1913.

The architecture is new but deliberately neoclassical in spirit, a polite nod to the older buildings around it, and inside it's all rich wood, supple leather and soft lighting, with a hint of mid-century retro in the furniture, while the restaurant, under the chef Hernán Basso, applies classical French technique to Chilean produce.

Now think upwards, because the gift is on the roof: a bar built around a marble counter some eight metres long, with two terraces looking across to Santa Lucía, and a rooftop pool with views towards San Cristóbal Hill.

There's a quiet point in all this, because Lastarria is one of the city's best-loved neighbourhoods, and rather than competing with it, the hotel borrows its manners, so the newest building on the street manages to look as though it has always been there.

[beat]

Good manners in architecture are underrated, and this is a rather well-mannered building.

When you leave, turn onto José Victorino Lastarria, the street that gives the neighbourhood its name, and follow it north towards the Parque Forestal and the Museum of Fine Arts, a lovely ten-minute walk.
