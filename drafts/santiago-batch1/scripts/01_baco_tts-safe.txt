Bahko

Not long ago, ordering wine in Santiago meant choosing a bottle from a handful of big names, and this relaxed corner of Proveedensia is one of the places that quietly changed the habit.

Nwayva deh Lee ohn, a short stroll north of Aveneeda Proveedensia, is your street, and the address is number one hundred and thirteen: a low, airy frontage with tables spilling onto a broad outdoor patio, and next door a small wine shop belonging to the same house. The name, Bahko, is simply Spanish for Backus, the Roman god of wine, which seems fair enough.

Start with the list, which runs to more than two hundred bottles with around twenty five open by the glass at any one time. That glass matters, because Bahko is credited with helping to pioneer wine by the glass in Santiago, and the choice leans towards small and sometimes esoteric growers, cool coastal Sohveenyon Blonk, say, or old vine Kareenyan from the Mowlay Valley, rather than the labels you'd find in duty free.

The kitchen is French in spirit with a strong Basque and Spanish accent, so expect salmon tartare, baby squid and grilled hake, a proper board of French cheeses and, when they're in season, angoolas, the tiny baby eels that the Basque Country treats as a delicacy.

What I like most is the shop next door, where a bottle you enjoyed at the table can go home with you, and where Chilean winemakers have been known to drop by and open their own work, so the place feels less like a restaurant than a long running tasting club.

It's a modest room doing a fairly large job, helping a country famous for exporting wine to discover, one glass at a time, how much variety it has at home.

Before you go, ask in the shop what's newly arrived from the small producers of the south, then wander two blocks down to Aveneeda Proveedensia, where the old Drugstore arcade is waiting with its bookshops and cafes.
