Lah Kalma by Fraydess & Ohvahyay

The Pacific is the best part of two hours' drive from here, and yet nothing in this kitchen comes out of a freezer.

Behind the terrace canopies at number three thousand eight hundred and thirty two on Aveneeda Nwayva Kostanaira, close to the Kahsa Kostanaira complex, is Lah Kalma, a calm and sunlit dining room with a covered terrace for warmer days.

The chef is Eegnahseeo Ohvahyay, known to everyone as Nahcho, and the rules are simple and strict: the menu follows the day's catch, there's no frozen product at all, and the fish is bought through fair trade relationships with the people who land it rather than at whatever the wholesale price happens to be.

So the real gift is the menu itself, which shifts with whatever the boats bring in. Look out for Chilean oysters, sea bass slowly confited in olive oil, Patagonian king crab, linguine with crabmeat and grilled octopus, and if you're with company, the Lah Kalma platter, which puts a good share of the coastline on a single table.

There's a quiet confidence in the cooking that reviewers keep coming back to, the sense that the kitchen trusts its raw material and doesn't bury it, and that restraint has made Lah Kalma one of the hardest tables in Santiago to book and a regular fixture on the extended list of Latin America's Fifty Best.

Calm, it turns out, is a technique, because if you leave good fish alone it will do most of the work for you.

If you can't get a table, it's worth making the journey to the coast itself, to the fishing coves around Valpara eeso, where you'll see the same kind of catch come ashore; closer to hand, the restaurant's neighbours on Nwayva Kostanaira include the rooftop terrace of Fookasahwa, a short walk away.
