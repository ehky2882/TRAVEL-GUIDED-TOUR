Museu Nacional Honestino Guimarães

This museum opened on the fifteenth of December 2006. That was Oscar Niemeyer's ninety ninth birthday.

Stand on the open paving of the Cultural Complex of the Republic, at the western end of the Esplanada, between the bus station and the cathedral. Ahead of you is a perfect white dome, about twenty six metres high, sitting directly on the ground, with no base and no visible door. And peeling off its side, a long ramp that climbs up and into it.

The dome is one half of a pair. Opposite it, the long, flat block is the National Library, which opened on the same day. Lúcio Costa, who planned the city, conceived a cultural centre for this end of the Esplanada. It took almost fifty years to arrive, and Niemeyer, then in his late nineties, lived to see it finished.

Have a look at that outside ramp. It is fifty two metres long and leads straight into the dome at first floor level. Inside there is a second ramp, curving from the main hall up to the mezzanine and reaching out almost twenty metres without a single support underneath it. The engineer who made that work was José Carlos Sussekind.

And here's the thing. The museum carries the name of Honestino Guimarães, a geology student at the University of Brasília who became a leader of the student movement under the military dictatorship. He was arrested several times, and in October 1973 he disappeared. Naming a white dome on the Esplanada after him puts his memory a few hundred metres from the ministries of the government that took him.

The exhibitions change, so check what is on. Either way, walk up the outside ramp, then turn round at the top. From there you look straight down the Esplanada, past the cathedral, to the twin towers of the Congress.
