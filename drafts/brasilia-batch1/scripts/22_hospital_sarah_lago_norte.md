## Clean script

Hospital SARAH Lago Norte

This is a working hospital, so you are a guest here, and you will be looking from the outside. It is still one of the most important buildings in Brasília.

Stand by the entrance road, looking towards the lake. In front of you, the buildings are long and low, spread out among the trees and lawns that run right down to the water. Look at the roofs. They rise and fall in a series of curved waves, with openings along each crest.

This is the International Centre for Neurosciences and Rehabilitation of the Sarah network, which opened in December 2003. The architect was João Filgueiras Lima, known as Lelé, who designed hospital after hospital for the Sarah network across Brazil.

Those wavy roofs are the trick. Wind comes in off the lake, flows through the big rooms and escapes out of the vents along the curves. The openings, which Lelé called sheds, also let daylight fall from above. So the building is largely cooled and lit by the site it sits on, rather than by machines.

And here's the thing. Lelé believed that a hospital could help to heal, not just house the sick. Everything is horizontal, so patients in wheelchairs can move about freely, inside and out. The hospital's own description talks about teamwork and wide open spaces. And the lake is not just a view: its calm water is used for sport as part of rehabilitation.

[beat]

Please don't go inside unless you have business there. From the road, take a moment to look at how the buildings sit low under the trees, then head back towards the city. On the near shore of the lake is the University of Brasília, where Lelé began, on the long concrete building known as the Minhocão.
