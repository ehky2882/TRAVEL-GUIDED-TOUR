## Clean script

Espaço Lúcio Costa

Everything you have seen in Brasília started as a few pencil lines on a sheet of paper. Underneath the Square of the Three Powers, you can see them.

Stand on the Square, with the Congress behind you and the Supreme Court to one side, and look for a low entrance in the paving, leading down. It is easy to miss, because it was meant to be. Go down the steps.

The space opened on the twenty seventh of February 1992, for what would have been Lúcio Costa's ninetieth birthday. Oscar Niemeyer designed the building and set it underground so as not to compete with the landmarks above. Costa himself put together what is shown inside.

In the middle is a huge model of the whole Plano Piloto, built in 1988 over about eleven months. It has been updated several times since; look closely and you will see that parts of the city added after 1988 are shown in grey. Around the walls are copies of the sketches and the short written report that Costa submitted in 1957 to the competition for the plan of the new capital. There are also models of the two buildings he designed himself, the TV Tower and the bus station.

And here's the thing. Costa's entry was famously slight: a handful of freehand drawings and a few pages of text, against rivals who submitted volumes of detailed plans. It won anyway. Read the report and you will see why. He starts with the gesture of someone marking a place, two axes crossing at a right angle, the sign of the cross. That single gesture is the city above your head.

[beat]

It is open from Tuesday to Sunday. Come here first if you can, before the rest of the city, and everything else will make more sense. When you climb back up, walk to the edge of the Square and look west, down the Esplanada. You are looking along the line he drew.
