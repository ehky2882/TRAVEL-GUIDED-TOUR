Fayra da Torree dee Tay Vay

The market at your feet started in the nineteen seventies as a few stalls in the shade of a television mast.

Stand on the Monumental Axis at the foot of the tee vee Tower, with its three concrete legs and steel mast rising above you. Around it, spread across three or four pavilions, are the stalls of the Fayra da Torree, the tower market. Once they were tents on the grass. Since twenty ten they have had a permanent home.

Have a look at what is actually made here, rather than what is merely sold. There are miniature Congress buildings and key rings, of course. But walk on and you find ceramics, macramé, hammocks, leather and work in seeds and stone. The thing to look for is kapeeng dorahdoo, golden grass, a shiny wild grass from the Seh hahdoo, woven into bracelets, bags and boxes that glint like brass.

Some of the people behind these counters have been here for decades. In twenty twenty two, one craftsman had been working with stone, seeds and golden grass at this market for thirty five years. One potter, who taught herself, has sold ceramics here since nineteen eighty four.

What's worth knowing, standing here, is that the market is the tee vee Tower's other half. Loosio Costa, who planned the city, designed this tower himself, one of very few major buildings here not by Neemyer. He drew a monument. Nobody drew the market. It simply grew up around the tower's feet, and fifty years on it is as much a part of this place as the mast.

Come early, around nine, before the midday sun, as shade between the pavilions is scarce, and check the opening days before you set off, because even the stallholders give different answers. When you are done, take the lift up the tower. At seventy five metres, the viewing deck shows you the whole cross of the city's plan.
