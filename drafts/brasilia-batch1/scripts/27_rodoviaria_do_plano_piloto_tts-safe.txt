Hodoveeahreea doo Plahno Peeloto

If Brazeelya has a centre, a single point where everything meets, you are standing on it.

Stand on the bus platform of the Hodoveeahreea, the central bus station, with the buses pulling in beside you. It is a long, low concrete deck, open at the sides, crowded, noisy, full of snack stalls and people hurrying to work. Above your head runs one of the city's main avenues. And under your feet runs another.

This is the spot where Loosio Costa's two great lines cross: the Monumental Axis, running east to the Congress, and the Residential Axis, curving away to the north and south wings. Costa designed this station himself. It is one of very few major buildings in the city not by Neemyer. Zhooseleeno Koobitchek opened it on the twelfth of September nineteen sixty, his birthday, three months before he left office.

Have a look at how it is layered. There are three levels. On top, the road deck joining the entertainment sectors and their car parks. Down here, the platform, level with the Esplanahda of the Ministries. And below that, the underpass where the Ayshowng, the Residential Axis, runs through. Since two thousand and one there has been a metro station down there too.

And here's the thing. Most visitors to Brazeelya see the ministries and the palaces. The people who keep them running arrive here, by bus, from the satellite cities outside the plan, where most of the population of the Federal District actually lives. Costa saw that clearly. In nineteen eighty seven he said that he had always called this platform the link that joins the metropolis together.

So linger for a few minutes, at rush hour if you can. Then climb up to the top deck and look west. The tee vee Tower is straight ahead at the end of the axis, and turning round, the ministries march away towards the Congress. You can see both halves of the city from the one spot where they touch.
