Teahtro Nasseeonow Klowdeeo Santoro

The side of this building has a nickname. People call it Oo Sow fahss a festa, the sun throws a party.

Stand on the lawn beside the theatre in the Cultural Sector North, just across the axis from the bus station, facing one of its long sloping sides. It is a pyramid with the top sliced off, a shape that recalls the temples of pre Columbian America. And the wall in front of you is covered in raised concrete blocks, in five different shapes, set in a pattern that seems at first to be random.

That wall is by Ahtoss Boolsowng, from nineteen sixty six. It is about a hundred and twenty five metres long and twenty seven high, around seventeen hundred square metres of relief, and it is often described as the largest work of art built into a building anywhere in Brazil. The trick is the sun. As it moves, the blocks throw shadows of different lengths, so the wall looks different every hour of the day.

Oscar Neemyer designed the building. Work began in nineteen sixty and then stalled for years, and the full theatre only opened in nineteen eighty one. Its main hall, the Villa Lobos, has around fourteen hundred seats. Inside the main foyer is a garden by Boorlee Marks, and works by Seskyatti, Peretti and Ahtoss again. It took the name of Klowdeeo Santoro, the composer and conductor who founded its orchestra, after his death in nineteen eighty nine.

And here's the thing. In twenty fourteen the whole theatre was shut on safety grounds, and it stayed closed for about a decade. In December twenty twenty four part of it reopened, starting with the smaller Marteens Pena hall. The rest has been coming back slowly. A national theatre with its biggest stage dark for ten years is not the story anyone wanted. But the wall outside never closed.

Check the programme to see which halls are open. Then come back near sunset, and walk the full length of this side. The shadows on Ahtoss's blocks are at their longest, and you will see why it got its name.
