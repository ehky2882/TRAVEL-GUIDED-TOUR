Pontão do Lago Sul

Brasília is nearly a thousand kilometres from the sea. On a Sunday afternoon, this is where people go instead.

Stand on the promenade by the water, in the middle of the Pontão, with the restaurants behind you. Ahead is Lake Paranoá, wide and calm, often with sailing boats and paddle boarders out on it. Along the shore to either side runs a broad lakeside walk, with kiosks and tables.

The Pontão opened in March 2002, as part of the Projeto Orla, a scheme to open up the edge of the lake to the public. The land belongs to the public, but it is run by a private company, and it is packed with bars and restaurants, a children's play area and space to walk.

Have a look at the lake itself. It isn't natural. Lake Paranoá was made for the new capital, by damming a river, and it does a good deal to soften the dry climate of the central plateau. For most of its life the water was a view you looked at from a distance, rather than a place you could actually reach.

What's worth knowing, standing here, is that this is the Brasília the postcards leave out. Not ministers or monuments, but families, joggers and couples, out on a boardwalk on a hot evening. The planned city gave everyone a lake. It took more than forty years to give them a way to sit beside it.

[beat]

Come in the late afternoon and walk the promenade end to end. And looking east across the lake, the low lights of the Lago Sul shore lead your eye towards the arches of the JK Bridge.
