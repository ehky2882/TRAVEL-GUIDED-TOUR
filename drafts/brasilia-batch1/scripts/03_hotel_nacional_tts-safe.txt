Hotel Nasseeonow

In nineteen sixty eight Queen Elizabeth the Second came to Brazeelya, and the city had nowhere to put her. So she slept here.

Stand on the pavement of the Hotel Sector South, facing the tall slab with its name across the top. Ten floors of concrete and glass, a broad podium at its feet, and the revolving doors that once spun all day. The Monumental Axis, the city's great central avenue, runs just behind you.

It opened in nineteen sixty one, while the capital was still being finished around it. The architect, Nowroo Estevess, designed close to a hundred buildings in Brazeelya's first thirteen years, and this was the grandest. Three hundred and forty seven rooms. Wood panelled corridors. A different carpet pattern on every floor, so you always knew where you were.

The Queen had the presidential suite, and there was a plaque in the lobby to say so. Sharl de Goal stayed too, and as he was nearly two metres tall, a bed was made specially. Jimmy Carter, Ronald Reagan and Indira Gandhi all came through, and for decades deputies and senators simply lived here, because in the early capital there was nowhere else half as comfortable.

And here's the thing. That same lack of anywhere else is what made it, and what undid it. As embassies, flats and newer hotels arrived, the galas moved on. By the twenty tens the furniture was still the original, the air conditioning was old and the revolving doors no longer turned. It was sold at auction in twenty eighteen, and in June twenty twenty a court order emptied it and closed it.

So this is a stop to look at rather than check into. Works to restore it were announced in late twenty twenty five, with a small museum of the hotel's history planned inside. Walk a few minutes east and you reach the Konnick and the bus station, the centre Loosio Costa drew for this city, and you will see why the capital's first grand hotel was put exactly here.
