Centro Cultural Banco do Brasil

In late 2022, the government that was about to take power in Brazil chose to plan its first days from an arts centre. This one.

Stand in the gardens by the lake side, with the main building behind you and Lake Paranoá in front. The building is long and low, with walls of bare grey concrete and big openings cut into them, unusually for Brasília, where Niemeyer's buildings are nearly always painted white. Ahead, across the water, are the three steel arches of the JK Bridge.

Oscar Niemeyer designed it in the late nineteen eighties for the Banco do Brasil, the state bank, as offices for its staff and training. It carries the name of Tancredo Neves, the man elected president in 1985 who died before he could take office. In October 2000 the bank turned it into a cultural centre, with galleries, a theatre and a cinema, across more than six thousand square metres. Entry is free.

Have a look, when you go in, at the reception. The wall there is a large relief of white shapes on blue by Marianne Peretti, the only woman in Niemeyer's team, better known for the stained glass of the cathedral. The bank's own collection, the Museu BB, has works by Di Cavalcanti, Tomie Ohtake, Burle Marx and Athos Bulcão.

What's worth knowing, standing here, is that the gardens are half the point. They are planted with trees of the Cerrado, the savanna of central Brazil, and they are used for concerts, festivals and open air cinema, all looking out over the lake. In a city of formal lawns, this is one of the few places where people simply settle down on the grass for an evening.

[beat]

Check the programme before you come, as the exhibitions and concerts change. Then stay for the sunset, when the light goes down behind the city, and wait for the arches of the bridge to be lit up across the water.
