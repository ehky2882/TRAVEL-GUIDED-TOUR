Catetinho

Before there was a single concrete wall in Brasília, there was this: a wooden house, built in ten days.

Stand in the clearing in front of the house, under the trees, with the path behind you. It is a long, simple building of timber planks, raised up off the ground on stilts. It sits in a patch of Cerrado woodland, close to a spring, south of the city.

It was built between the twenty second and the thirty first of October 1956, and on the tenth of November President Juscelino Kubitschek came to open it. Oscar Niemeyer designed it, and the wood was cut on site, which is why it was first called the Palace of Planks. It was paid for by about ten of JK's friends, partly with a bank loan raised in Belo Horizonte.

The name came from the guitarist Dilermando Reis. In Rio, the president lived at the Catete Palace, so this little wooden version became the Catetinho, the little Catete. JK flew in from Rio almost every day and sometimes stayed the night. Inside you can still see his personal things, and Dilermando's guitar.

And here's the thing. Within three years, JK had it declared a national historic monument, in 1959, before the city it was built to oversee had even been opened. Somebody understood very early that the rough wooden start of all this was worth keeping as much as the marble at the end.

[beat]

It has been a museum since 1970, and it is free. Open from Tuesday to Sunday, so don't come on a Monday. Before you leave, walk a little way into the woodland round the house, towards the spring. This patch of Cerrado is protected, and it gives you a fair idea of what the whole plateau looked like before the city arrived.
