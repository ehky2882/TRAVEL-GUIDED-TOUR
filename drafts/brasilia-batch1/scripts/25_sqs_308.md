## Clean script

SQS 308 Sul

The word superquadra was coined for this city in 1956. This is where you see what it meant.

Stand in the garden in the middle of Superquadra 308 South, between the blocks of flats. All around you are long, six storey residential buildings, lifted on columns so that the ground floor is open air. You can see straight under them into the next garden. There are no fences, no front gardens and no through traffic.

It is known as the model quadra, because it followed Lúcio Costa's plan for the city more closely than any other. Costa wanted each block of flats set in a park, and four blocks grouped together into a neighbourhood unit sharing a kindergarten, schools, a church, a cinema and a row of shops. Here, nearly all of it was built. The city's first primary school opened in 1959, the Igrejinha is on the corner, and the Cine Brasília is a short walk away.

Now, a couple of myths. People will tell you the buildings are by Niemeyer. They are not; they are by Sérgio Rocha and Marcelo Campelo, and the tiles on the blocks are Campelo's own. And the tiles that everyone assumes are by Athos Bulcão are not on the blocks at all. His are on the kindergarten, designed by Stellio Seabra.

What is by a famous name is the landscape. Roberto Burle Marx did the gardens and squares, including a little plaza of benches and curving low walls known as the serpentário, the snake house, which has never held a snake. Find Block F and you will see one of his water gardens, still full of carp.

[beat]

This is a place where people live, so keep your voice down and stay on the paths. If you walk out to the north east corner, you reach the Igrejinha, the nun's hat church, and from there the little shopping street where the neighbourhood buys its bread, exactly as it was planned.
