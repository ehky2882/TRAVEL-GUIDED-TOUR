Eegrezheenya Nossa Senyora de Fatima

The first lady of Brazil made a promise. If her daughter recovered from her illness, she would build a church. The daughter recovered, and this is the church.

Stand on the grass beside the little church at Quadra three oh seven South, with the road behind you, facing its open front. It is tiny: one curved wall, open at the front, and over it a triangular roof slab that tilts upwards, held on three slim supports. Brazeelyans say it looks like a nun's hat, the starched, winged kind, and once you have heard that you can't unsee it.

The first lady was Sarah Koobitchek, the president's wife, and the daughter was Marseea. Oscar Neemyer designed it and Zhwakeen Cardozo engineered it. The first stone went down in October nineteen fifty seven, and it was consecrated in June nineteen fifty eight, the first church of brick and concrete in the new capital. The story usually told is that it took a hundred days.

Now, walk round to the outside of that curved wall. It is covered in Ahtoss Boolsowng's tiles, blue and white, with a star and a dove repeated across them: the Star of Bethlehem and the dove of the Holy Spirit. It is one of the rare panels in his work that shows actual things rather than abstract shapes.

And here's the thing. Inside there were once frescoes by the painter Alfredo Volpee, of angels and flags. In the nineteen sixties, during a renovation, they were simply painted over in blue. In two thousand and nine Franseesko Galeno, who had studied with Volpee, was asked to paint the walls afresh, and he filled them with kites, flags and cotton reels, the things of everyday Brazilian life.

This church was planned as the neighbourhood chapel for the super kwadras around it. Walk into the super kwadra next door, three oh eight South, the model super kwadra, and you can see the whole idea at once: flats on columns, gardens by Boorlee Marks, a school, and this church, all within a few minutes' walk.
