## Clean script

Congresso Nacional

Two towers, a bowl and a dome. If you have ever seen a single photograph of Brasília, it was probably this one.

Stand on the great lawn of the Esplanada of the Ministries, facing east towards the Congress at its far end. In front of you is a long, flat white platform. Rising from the middle, two thin towers, joined in the middle, about a hundred metres high. On the platform, one shape opens upwards like a bowl, and the other is turned upside down, like a dome.

The bigger one, the bowl, open to the sky, is the Chamber of Deputies. The smaller dome, closed and turned down, is the Senate. People love to read meanings into that: one house open to every idea, the other inward looking and careful. Niemeyer, rather wonderfully, said the arrangement was purely aesthetic.

It was inaugurated with the city, on the twenty first of April 1960, with Joaquim Cardozo as engineer. The towers hold offices, twenty eight floors each. Underneath the platform is a whole hidden building, of corridors, halls and committee rooms, so the shapes you see are really just its lid. Inside, the Senate's ceiling is covered with a hundred and thirty five thousand small metal plates by Athos Bulcão.

And here's the thing. You aren't looking at a monument on a hill. The top of that platform is a terrace, reached by a ramp, and it is usually closed now for security. But stand here and you can still see the idea: a parliament that sits low and flat, almost level with the people walking up to it, with only its offices allowed to rise.

[beat]

Both houses run free guided tours inside, and they are worth checking the day before. Afterwards, walk round the side of the building, past the reflecting pools, to the Square of the Three Powers behind it, where the presidential palace and the Supreme Court face each other across the stones.
