Foondassowng Ahtoss Boolsowng

If you have spent even an hour in Brazeelya, you have already seen Ahtoss Boolsowng's work. You probably just didn't know his name.

You should be on the double you three South, the long avenue of shops along the western edge of the south wing, at Quadra five ten, in front of Block B. The foundation's shop is a ground floor unit with his patterns in the window: blue and white tiles, bold geometric prints, simple shapes repeated in a way you will start to recognise everywhere.

Ahtoss Boolsowng was born in Reeoh de Zhanayro in nineteen eighteen. As a young man he worked as an assistant to the painter Kandeedo Porteenari on the church at Pampoolya, in Belo Oreezonchee, and in nineteen fifty eight his friend Oscar Neemyer invited him to Brazeelya to help build the city. He stayed for fifty years. The tiles on the Eegrezheenya, the panels in the Congress, the wall of concrete blocks on the National Theatre: those are all his.

The shop sells his patterns on things you can take home: actual tiles, mugs, umbrellas, necklaces, sandals, prints and books. The foundation was set up in nineteen ninety two to look after his work, and its collection holds more than seven hundred pieces.

And here's the thing. Ahtoss often handed the builders boxes of his tiles with a few rules and no fixed plan, and let them lay the pieces as they chose, so that no two walls came out quite the same. The bricklayer became a kind of co author. Buying a single one of his tiles here is the nearest you can get to taking Brazeelya home with you.

The foundation has moved more than once, and talk of a permanent home keeps coming and going, so check the address and hours before you visit. Then go and find the real thing. The Eegrezheenya, the little church at Quadra three oh seven South, is wrapped in his tiles, about a kilometre north east of here.
