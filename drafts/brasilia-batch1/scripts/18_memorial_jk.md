## Clean script

Memorial JK

The man who built this city is buried at the end of its main avenue, standing on a pillar, waving.

Stand at the foot of the Memorial at the western end of the Monumental Axis, looking up. On top of a tall, plain column is a bronze figure, arm raised, under a curved white shell that wraps over him like a hood. Below that, a low white building with very few windows.

The figure is Juscelino Kubitschek, known simply as JK, president from 1956 to 1961, and the man who decided Brazil's capital would move here, into what was then empty scrubland. The statue, four and a half metres tall, is by Honório Peçanha, and it stands on a pedestal twenty eight metres high.

After JK died in a car crash in 1976, his widow, Sarah, asked Oscar Niemeyer to design a memorial. It opened on the twelfth of September 1981, which would have been JK's seventy ninth birthday. And that shell over the statue caused a row. To the military government of the day, it looked rather like a hammer and sickle. Niemeyer, a lifelong communist, said it was nothing of the sort, and simplified the design, but it was still argued over before the statue was allowed up.

Inside, the mood changes. Upstairs is JK's tomb, in a dim room under a stained glass window by Marianne Peretti, where the colour of the light shifts with the sun. Around it, his library, his presidential sash, his medals, family photographs, and his last car, a Ford Galaxie.

[beat]

When you come back out, turn and look east. The whole Monumental Axis runs away from you in a straight line, past the TV Tower and the bus station to the Congress, exactly as Lúcio Costa first sketched it. JK was placed here so he could look down it for ever.
