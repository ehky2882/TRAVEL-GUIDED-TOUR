Ermeeda Dom Bosko

In eighteen eighty three an Italian priest dreamed that a great civilisation would rise between the fifteenth and twentieth parallels south. Brazeelya was built at fifteen degrees and forty seven minutes. This little chapel is where the city keeps that promise.

Stand on the slope beside the chapel, in the Dom Bosko Ecological Park, on the far shore of Lake Pahrah noh ah. The building is a small pyramid, white, on a raised platform faced in marble, with a metal cross on top. Turn round and the lake opens out in front of you, with the whole of the Plahno Peeloto stretched along the opposite shore.

Oscar Neemyer designed it, and it was finished in nineteen fifty seven, among the very first permanent structures of the new capital. Inside is a statue of Dom Bosko, carved in Carrara marble and signed by the Arreggeenee brothers, from Italy.

The priest was John Bosko, founder of the Salesians. He never visited South America, but the account of his dream speaks of a lake forming between those parallels, and of a promised land. When Brazil decided to build a capital on the central plateau, and then made a lake to go with it, the prophecy was too good to ignore. He became the city's patron saint.

And here's the thing. On almost any evening, people drive out here, sit on the grass with a bottle of wine or a flask of coffee, and wait. Nobody is here for the chapel, exactly. They come because this is the one spot where the sun sets over the whole city at once.

So time your visit for the last hour before dusk, and bring something to sit on. Watch the light go down behind the tee vee Tower and the Congress across the water. Then, if you want the other half of the story, the Sanctuary of Dom Bosko in the city, with its eighty columns of blue glass, is waiting on the far shore.
