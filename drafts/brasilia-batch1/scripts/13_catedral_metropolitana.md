## Clean script

Catedral Metropolitana Nossa Senhora Aparecida

Most of this cathedral is underground. What you can see from here is really just the roof.

Stand on the open plaza in front of the cathedral, on the Esplanada of the Ministries, facing the low ramp that leads down to the entrance. Rising ahead of you is a ring of curved concrete ribs, gathered at the waist and flaring open at the top, with glass between them. To one side stands a tall, thin bell tower. And lining your way to the door, four bronze figures, each about three metres high.

Those four are the evangelists, Matthew, Mark, Luke and John, made in 1970 by Alfredo Ceschiatti. The bells in the tower were given by Spanish residents of Brazil and came from Miranda de Ebro, in northern Spain. The oval building to the left of the entrance is the baptistery, its walls lined with tiles by Athos Bulcão.

Now the structure. There are sixteen ribs, each weighing around ninety tonnes. The first stone was laid in 1958, and the frame was finished by the day the city was inaugurated, the twenty first of April 1960. Then, for years, there was nothing between the ribs at all. The cathedral was consecrated in 1968 with no roof, and only completed in 1970.

And here's the thing. Oscar Niemeyer didn't want you to walk in through a grand door. He made you go down, through a short, dark tunnel, so that you come up into the light all at once. It is a very old idea, the dark passage before the revelation, built with a very new material.

[beat]

Once you are inside, look up. The stained glass sweeping across the roof is by Marianne Peretti, and three angels by Ceschiatti hang from steel cables above you, the largest more than four metres long. Then come back out, and walk a few minutes west along the Esplanada to the white dome of the National Museum.
