Santuário Dom Bosco

From the street this looks like a plain concrete box. Go inside and it turns out to be made almost entirely of blue.

You should be on the pavement of the W3 South, at Quadra 702, looking at a square building framed by a grid of tall, pointed concrete arches. Between them, from outside, the glass gives very little away. Find the door and go in.

Eighty columns, each sixteen metres high, hold up the roof, and every gap between them is filled with stained glass in twelve shades of blue, by the Belgian artist Hubert Van Doorne. The architect was Carlos Alberto Naves, and the pointed openings are his nod to Gothic cathedrals. Building began in 1963 and finished in 1970.

Above the middle of the nave hangs a chandelier, three and a half metres tall, weighing two tonnes, made from seven thousand four hundred pieces of Murano glass. It is only lit for ceremonies. And behind the altar, the crucifix was carved by Gotfredo Tralli from a single piece of cedar.

Now, the reason this church is dedicated to Dom Bosco. In 1883 the Italian priest John Bosco, who never set foot in South America, described a dream of a journey across the continent, and of a great civilisation that would rise where a lake formed between the fifteenth and twentieth parallels. Brasília sits just below the fifteenth, beside the lake it made for itself. So he became the city's patron saint, and this is his sanctuary.

The light changes completely through the day, but late afternoon is when it turns deepest blue, so come back then if you can. The glass has never been restored, and about a fifth of it is now damaged, which is a good reason to see it while you can. For the other end of the story, the Ermida Dom Bosco, a small chapel to the saint, stands across the lake on the far shore.
