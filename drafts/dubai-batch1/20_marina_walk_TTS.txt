At the turn of this century, there was no water here. No canal, no yachts, no reflections for the towers to admire themselves in — just a strip of flat desert between the coast road and the sea. Everything you are standing beside was dug.

You should be on Marina Walk, the waterfront promenade, with the canal directly in front of you and residential towers rising close behind. Moored yachts, cafe terraces at the railing, and across the water a second wall of towers facing this one, so the whole district reads as a canyon with a harbour at the bottom.

Ignore the towers for a moment — look at the water. It is the Gulf, invited inland. In the early two thousands, engineers cut a channel some three and a half kilometres long through open ground and then let the sea flow in to fill it. The designers borrowed the idea from a waterfront district in Vancouver, of all places — a cold-water model, rebuilt at desert temperature — and the promenade under your feet now loops the whole basin, roughly seven kilometres if you follow it all the way around.

Now find the tower that twists. On the far side of the marina, one skyscraper turns as it rises — a full ninety degrees between the ground and the roof, each floor rotated a fraction over the one below. That is kye ahn Tower, and the corkscrew is not a flourish: turning the building softens the wind that strikes it. Around it stand some of the tallest residential buildings ever constructed, most of them raced upward within a single decade.

Here is the part worth carrying home. The marina was excavated before there was a district to need it — the water came first, and the towers arrived afterwards, ring after ring, around a harbour that was briefly the emptiest address in the city. If you have stood downtown, you have met this habit already: building the thing before the need, then watching the need arrive. It is the city's oldest trick, run one more time in salt water.

Stay by the railing a while. In the evening the towers hand their light down to the water, and the whole dug canyon glows — and it becomes genuinely hard to remember that within living memory this was dry sand, and that somebody looked at that sand and saw this.
