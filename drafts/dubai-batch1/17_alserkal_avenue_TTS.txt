Your map has not made a mistake. This is an industrial estate — marble cutters, car workshops, tyre shops, the working back-of-house of a city that mostly shows you its front. And folded into the middle of it, behind gates that barely announce themselves, sits one of the most serious concentrations of contemporary art anywhere in the region.

You should be inside al sair kal Avenue, in al kohz, standing in the central courtyard they call The Yard — a wide open clearing between rows of corrugated warehouses. The gallery names are small, the doors are big, and nothing out here tells you what any building holds.

Pick a door and go through it. That is the entire method of this place. Behind one warehouse front is a white-walled gallery showing painters from three continents; behind the next, an independent cinema — cinema ah keel, one of the first arthouse cinemas anywhere in the Gulf; behind a third, a design studio or a chocolate maker or a climbing wall. The compound belonged to the al sair kal family's industrial businesses, and from two thousand and eight they began handing warehouses over to galleries, one lease at a time, until the district reached critical mass.

Look for the building that appears to be wearing curtains. That is Concrete — a warehouse rebuilt by oh em ay, the Dutch practice founded by rem kool hahs, as its first building in Dubai. Its facade panels slide apart like stage drapery, and the biggest exhibitions on the Avenue happen inside it.

Now the thing that makes this district the other half of a story. Downtown, in the financial centre, the art arrived where the money already was — sculpture lining a corridor between banks. Out here the order ran in reverse: the galleries came first, renting warehouses because warehouses were cheap, and the money now has to drive out from the towers to find them. Nothing here is distressed for effect or staged for your camera. The paint peels because it peels.

Take your time, and do not skip the unpromising doors — the reward structure of this place is inverted, and the plainest facades tend to hide the best rooms. The city out there sells you the view. This district asks you to open things.
