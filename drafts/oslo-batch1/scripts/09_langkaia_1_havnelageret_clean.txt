Langkaia 1 (Havnelageret)

When this warehouse was finished in 1920, it was described as the largest concrete building in Europe, and standing beneath its long, many-windowed front, that's not a hard claim to believe.

Your best vantage is the quayside along Langkaia, close to the water, so that the building known as Havnelageret, the Harbour Warehouse, fills your view, with its tower rising above the roofline and the Opera House a short walk away to the east.

It was designed by Bredo Henrik Berntsen and built between 1916 and 1920, when wartime shipping was booming and Oslo needed somewhere vast to store the grain and goods arriving by sea, and eleven floors on soft harbour ground called for serious foundations, so the building stands on a hundred and thirty pillars resting on some fifteen hundred and fifty piles.

During the Second World War, all that concrete made it useful for something else entirely, and the fourth floor served as a shelter for civilians, before a conversion in 1983 turned the warehouse into offices for shipowners, financiers and oil companies and painted it pink, which earned it the nickname the pink palace of Bjørvika.

The real story here is that the harbour moved but the building stayed, so the warehouse that once served ships now looks out over a waterfront given over to culture, with the opera, the library and the Munch museum lined up beside it, and in that sense it's the hinge between the working port Oslo was and the city it has become.

[beat]

Walk east along the water to the Opera House and climb its roof, then look back, since from up there the old warehouse and its tower sit squarely in view, and you can see just how much of the harbour this one building used to command.
