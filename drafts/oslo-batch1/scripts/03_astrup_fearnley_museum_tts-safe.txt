Astroop Fernlee Museum of Modern Art

That great curved glass roof is meant to look like a sail, with its supporting beams standing in for masts and rigging, and once someone has pointed it out, it's rather hard to see it as anything else.

Walk out onto the footbridge over the narrow canal that runs between the museum's two buildings and stop halfway across, so that one building faces the fjord, with the sculpture park and a small public beach beyond it, the other looks back towards the piazza, and the single sweep of glass ties the pair together over your head.

This is the Astroop Fernlee Museum of Modern Art, designed by Renzo Piano's studio with the Norwegian firm Naarood Stokkeh Veeg and opened in September twenty twelve, though the museum itself was founded back in nineteen ninety three, and its collection, with names such as Jeff Koons, Damien Hirst and Cindy Sherman, is counted among the most important for contemporary art in Norway.

Now look towards the water, where the Choovholmen sculpture park wraps around the fjord side building and runs down to the beach, so that art sits out on the lawns while, in the warmer months, people swim just a few steps away.

The cleverest move of all is that most of this asks nothing of you, since the canal, the bridge, the park and the beach are simply there to be used, which means a museum of demanding contemporary art spends much of its day as a place where people sit, chat and dangle their feet over the edge of the quay.

If you've got a few minutes, carry on to the very tip of the headland and then turn round, because from there the whole sail of the roof lines up against the city behind it, and that's the view where the idea finally clicks into place.
