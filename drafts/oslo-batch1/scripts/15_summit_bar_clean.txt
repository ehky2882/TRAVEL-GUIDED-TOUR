Summit Bar

On a clear evening, high above Holbergs gate, Oslo spreads out beneath you, from the fjord and its islands to the dark, wooded ridges that wrap round the city.

Twenty-one storeys up, right at the top of the Radisson Blu Scandinavia Hotel, the bar you've stepped into is arranged around its windows, with a raised floor running along the glass so that even from the seats further back you can see out over the rooftops.

It was designed by Snøhetta and opened in 2016, and the idea was to capture the feeling of standing on top of a mountain, with seating on two levels, linked by ramps so that everyone can reach both, and a natural palette drawn from the landscape outside.

Look for the two walls that do most of the storytelling: on one side a dark, sky-blue wall, and on the other a wall of warm brass that the architects likened to fire, so that you're sitting, in effect, between the evening sky and the hearth.

There's a third trick that's easy to miss, because the remaining wall is highly reflective, and after dark the bar's own warm light seems to dissolve into the reflections of the city, which makes the room feel bigger and the view wider still.

And here's the thing: in a country where walking up a mountain and then warming up by a fire is close to a national pastime, someone has managed to bottle that whole ritual into a hundred and forty square metres, without any of the hiking.

[beat]

Find a seat by the glass and, if the evening is clear, look north-west towards the hills, where you may be able to pick out the Holmenkollen ski jump on the skyline, and remember the bar opens from four in the afternoon, Monday to Saturday.
