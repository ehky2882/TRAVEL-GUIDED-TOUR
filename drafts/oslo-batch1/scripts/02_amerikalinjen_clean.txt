Amerikalinjen

It's 1919, and the shipping company that carries Norwegians across the Atlantic to New York has just moved into a grand new headquarters on the city's main station square, so that anyone leaving by train or by ship would walk right past its door.

Jernbanetorget, the busy square at the bottom of Karl Johans gate, is where you'll want to stand, with Oslo Central Station close by and the tall, pale building with the balustraded roofline in front of you, and over its entrance, look for a pair of tritons, the half-human, half-fish attendants of the sea, still keeping watch over the door.

The architects, Andreas Bjercke and Georg Eliassen, gave the Norwegian America Line exactly the Neo-Baroque confidence a young company wanted, and the line, founded in 1910, ran its passenger traffic between Oslo and America from here, its first steamer having sailed into the Oslofjord in 1913.

Since 2019 the building has been a hotel, and the conversion kept more of its past than you might expect: the old boardrooms are now suites under their original ornate ceilings, and the framed maps, photographs and ships' dining menus that turned up during the work are back on display. Even the basement jazz club, Gustav, shares its name with the company's first managing director, Gustav Henriksen.

The interesting bit is this: the building was designed around departure, a place where people bought tickets for journeys that many of them would never make in reverse, and it now earns its living from arrival, welcoming travellers who have only just stepped off the train.

If you step inside, keep an eye out for those maps and menus in the public rooms, and for the art chosen by the curator Sune Nordgren, with names such as Alex Katz and Julian Opie among them, then, back outside, look up once more at the tritons, who have watched an awful lot of comings and goings from that doorway.
