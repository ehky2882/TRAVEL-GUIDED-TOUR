Sommerroo

For most of the twentieth century, people came into this building to pay their electricity bills, beneath a mural celebrating the joys of being plugged into the modern world.

Sommerroo gahta, a few steps from Sollee plahss on the edge of Frongner, is where you'll find it, and the building facing you is the long, sober block with its rows and rows of windows, once the headquarters of the city's electricity company and now the Sommerroo hotel.

Andreas Byerkeh and Gayorg Elee assen won the design competition back in nineteen seventeen, though building didn't begin until nineteen twenty seven, and by the time it opened in the early nineteen thirties the company had renamed itself Oslo Leesvairker and spent generously on art, with twenty four reliefs by the sculptor Asbyurg Borgfelt and murals and a mosaic by Pair Kroog.

It also held something unexpected for a power company, Vestkahnt bahdeh, a public bath that opened in nineteen thirty two, which has been restored as part of the hotel, Kroog's mosaic wall and hand painted blue tiles included, with the old Roman style pool serving as a cold plunge.

The hotel opened in September twenty twenty two after a five year renovation, with the city's conservation office insisting that the stair, the bath, the foyer and the hall keep their original character, while a new disc shaped addition on the roof holds a pool, a sauna and a restaurant.

What strikes me most is the optimism of it all, since here was a utility company treating a trip to pay the bill as an occasion worth decorating, and nearly a century on, that same generosity is what makes the building feel like a gift to the city rather than simply a hotel.

Do step inside, if you can, and find Ekspedishoons hahllen, the old public hall that's now the all day dining room, where Pair Kroog's mural still looks down over the spot where the cashiers' tills once stood.
