Tim Vendelboo

Here's what I love about this little espresso bar: from the day it opened in the summer of two thousand and seven, it has sold coffee and nothing else, with milk and sugar about the only additions you'll be offered.

Grooners gahteh one, in Grooner lurka, has a small, plain shopfront that's easy to miss, so look for the name, then step inside, where the room is arranged around the bar and the coffees on offer change from one day to the next.

Tim Vendelboo himself won the World Barista Championship in two thousand and four and the World Cup Tasters Championship the following year, and before opening here he ran staff training at the Oslo coffee company Stockflets, so this place was always meant as much as a training centre and a showroom as a caffay.

The beans are now roasted in larger premises on Toyen gahta, where the roastery moved in twenty eighteen, and they're bought directly from farmers in Kenya, Ethiopia, Colombia, Honduras and El Salvador, whom the team visits every year, and since twenty nineteen they've even owned a farm of their own, Finka el Swaylo, in the Weela region of Colombia.

The radical part, though, isn't the coffee itself but the paperwork, because the company publishes what it pays for its green coffee, and in an industry where prices are often kept quiet, that kind of openness turns a cup of coffee into a small statement about how trade could work.

Ask what's on today and, if you're with someone, order two different coffees to compare, because tasting them together is the quickest way to understand what all that care at origin actually does to the cup.
