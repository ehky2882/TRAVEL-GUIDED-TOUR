Aesop Prinsens gate

Of all the places a skincare company might choose for its hundredth shop in the world, a small old room on a Kvadraturen side street isn't the obvious one, and yet that's where Aesop opened in 2014, its first in Norway.

The shopfront in front of you, at number twenty-one Prinsens gate, is easy to walk past, so look through the glass for whitewashed brick, rows of brown bottles and a glint of brass, and if the door's open, step inside and stand by the counter, where you can take in the whole room.

Now tilt your head back, because the ceiling is the real event: ten intersecting domes of different sizes, finished in matte plaster and borrowed from the vaults of Orthodox churches, with tiny spotlights tucked into the hollows so that the light gathers in pools.

Beneath them stands an outsized concrete sink with three brass taps over shallow basins, and it's there to be used, since washing your hands and trying things is the heart of the Aesop ritual, turned into furniture.

The walls carry a quieter story, because when Snøhetta's architects stripped the room back, they found surfaces hidden for decades, some dating from the 1800s, and rather than cover them again they whitewashed the old brick and gave the original floor tiles a pale, cloudy finish.

What I find rather lovely is how little has been added, since in a room of only sixty-six square metres the aim was to do a lot with little, and the result feels less like a shop fitted into an old building than an old building persuaded, very gently, into becoming one.

[beat]

Before you leave, look along the brick wall facing the counter for a long horizontal niche where a single course of bricks seems to be missing, a shelf made out of an absence, and if you'd like a companion piece, Aesop's other Oslo shop, in Homansbyen, is also by Snøhetta and dressed in faceted oak instead.
