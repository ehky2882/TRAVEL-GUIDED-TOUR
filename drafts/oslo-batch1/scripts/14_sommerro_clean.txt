Sommerro

For most of the twentieth century, people came into this building to pay their electricity bills, beneath a mural celebrating the joys of being plugged into the modern world.

Sommerrogata, a few steps from Solli plass on the edge of Frogner, is where you'll find it, and the building facing you is the long, sober block with its rows and rows of windows, once the headquarters of the city's electricity company and now the Sommerro hotel.

Andreas Bjercke and Georg Eliassen won the design competition back in 1917, though building didn't begin until 1927, and by the time it opened in the early 1930s the company had renamed itself Oslo Lysverker and spent generously on art, with twenty-four reliefs by the sculptor Asbjørg Borgfelt and murals and a mosaic by Per Krohg.

It also held something unexpected for a power company, Vestkantbadet, a public bath that opened in 1932, which has been restored as part of the hotel, Krohg's mosaic wall and hand-painted blue tiles included, with the old Roman-style pool serving as a cold plunge.

The hotel opened in September 2022 after a five-year renovation, with the city's conservation office insisting that the stair, the bath, the foyer and the hall keep their original character, while a new disc-shaped addition on the roof holds a pool, a sauna and a restaurant.

What strikes me most is the optimism of it all, since here was a utility company treating a trip to pay the bill as an occasion worth decorating, and nearly a century on, that same generosity is what makes the building feel like a gift to the city rather than simply a hotel.

Do step inside, if you can, and find Ekspedisjonshallen, the old public hall that's now the all-day dining room, where Per Krohg's mural still looks down over the spot where the cashiers' tills once stood.
