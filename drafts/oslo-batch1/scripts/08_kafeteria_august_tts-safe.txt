Kafeteria Owgoost

The name promises something plain and practical, a canteen with trays and a queue, and the gentle joke is that the place you're about to find is nothing of the sort.

Through the gateway at Ooniversitayts gahta nine lies a quiet courtyard, a few steps from the open square of Toolin lurka in the university quarter, and that's where to stand, with Kafeteria Owgoost's windows and, in good weather, its outdoor tables facing you across the yard.

It opened in twenty twenty two, started by the team behind Mah eh mo, one of Norway's most celebrated fine dining restaurants, as an all day place for coffee, lunch and a glass of wine, and it has since changed hands, so that today it's run by the crew behind Behtong, a lively restaurant over in Byurveeka.

One room lives three different lives here in a single day: it opens at eleven, Tuesday to Saturday, with coffee and lunch, slides into small plates such as tartare as the afternoon turns to evening, and on Fridays and Saturdays stays open until one in the morning, often with DJs, which it announces on Instagram.

The courtyard setting matters too, because tucked behind the street front you're sheltered from the traffic on Ooniversitayts gahta, and on a warm evening the yard can feel more like a private gathering than a restaurant.

The real charm, I think, is in the name, because a kafeteria is, at heart, somewhere people drop in without ceremony, and for all its pedigree this place has kept that promise, so you can come for a coffee, stay for wine, and nobody will make you feel you've ordered the wrong thing.

When you head off, wander back out towards Toolin lurka and, after dark, keep an eye on the ground, because light art has been set into the paving of the new Toolin quarter around you, and it's very easy to walk straight over it without noticing.
