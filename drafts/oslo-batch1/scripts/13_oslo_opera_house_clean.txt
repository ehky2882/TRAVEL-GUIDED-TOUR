Oslo Opera House

Most opera houses would much rather you kept off the roof, but this one was designed so that you'd walk straight up it.

That white ramp rising ahead of you is the roof itself, angled down to meet the waterfront so that anyone can stroll up, with the tall glass wall of the foyer set into its side and the stage tower, clad in white aluminium, standing above it all.

Snøhetta won the competition for the Norwegian National Opera and Ballet from some three hundred and fifty entries, and the building opened in April 2008, in the presence of King Harald and Queen Margrethe of Denmark, and rather unusually for a public building, it came in under budget. The surfaces under your feet are Carrara marble and white granite, which is why so many people, looking across the water, compare it to ice sliding into the fjord.

Look up at the stage tower and you'll see its aluminium panels are patterned, an echo of old weaving designs, and if you peer through the foyer glass, which rises some fifteen metres, you'll catch the warm oak inside, a deliberate contrast with the cool white shell.

If there's a single idea here, it's that a national opera house shouldn't belong only to people with tickets, and in that sense the roof is the building's real performance, running every day, free of charge, with tourists, families and office workers on their lunch break as the cast.

[beat]

Before you climb, look out on the water beside the building for She Lies, a sculpture of stainless steel and glass by Monica Bonvicini, which floats on the fjord and shifts with the tide and wind, then walk to the very top of the roof for a view right across the city.
