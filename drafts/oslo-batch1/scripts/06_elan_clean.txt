élan

Before you order anything, listen for a moment, because the sound in this room was composed for it, a bespoke soundscape by Jonas von der Fehr, playing through the turquoise ring that hangs from the ceiling and doubles as a speaker.

The stone building around you is the old Shipping Building at Kirkegata seven, in the grid of streets called Kvadraturen, and élan occupies the ground floor behind its Neo-Baroque front, so once inside, stand where you can see the round, dark oak counter in the middle of the room, with that ring floating above it.

The counter is the key to the whole design, since the architects at Snøhetta have explained that, rather than a conventional bar with baristas on one side and guests on the other, they wanted a new centre point for the conversation, so you can walk all the way round and watch every step of the brewing.

Beside it sits a carved volume of solid, heat-treated cork, soft and slightly rough against all that dark, precise wood, and the colours around the room come straight from coffee itself, with the red of the ripe cherry, the green of raw beans and the browns of the roast.

For about a decade this room was the showroom of Magnat Kaffehus, the coffee company behind élan, and the French name, suggesting flair, energy and style all at once, tells you something about the ambition.

To my mind, it treats coffee as theatre in the round, with no backstage and nowhere for the barista to hide, and in a city that takes its coffee very seriously, that kind of openness feels quietly confident.

[beat]

On your way out, run a hand over the cork, then peek into the room at the back, where a long, coffee-brown sofa waits for meetings, and since élan opens at eight on weekdays and ten on Saturdays, it makes a good first stop on a morning in Kvadraturen.
