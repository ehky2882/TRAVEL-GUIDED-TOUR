Moniker Oslo

This is Moniker, a fashion shop that decided to have five personalities instead of one, and borrowed each of them from a remarkable woman.

Valkyrie plahss, the little square in Mayor stooen where Valkyrie gahta meets Boogstad vayen, is the spot, with trams rattling through and the shop's entrance at Valkyrie gahta three ahead of you, so step through the door and pause just inside to get your bearings.

Moniker opened in the spring of twenty twenty, hardly the easiest moment to launch a shop, and its interior, designed by Snurhetta across some eight hundred square metres, is divided into departments that each take their mood from a different woman: Peggy Guggenheim for the eccentric and maximalist, Grace Jones for bright colour and bold geometry, Jane Birkin for the romantic and bohemian, Hedy Lamarr for glamour, and the Victorian traveller Isabella Bird for curiosity.

Even the logo joins in, appearing in five typographic variations drawn from one shared grid, so that the name shifts its character as you move through the rooms, much as the rails do.

Upstairs you'll find Moniker Man and Moniker Sport, added later, and the labels on the shop floor run from Ganni and Acne Studios to Totaym and Zimmermann.

Taken as a whole, it's a quiet argument against the identikit department store, because rather than one polished look repeated floor after floor, you get a cast of characters, and you're invited to work out which of them you'd most like to borrow from today.

When you come back out onto the square, spare a thought for what lies under your feet, since a metro station operated beneath Valkyrie plahss until nineteen eighty five, and although it has long been closed, trains still rumble through the tunnel below.
