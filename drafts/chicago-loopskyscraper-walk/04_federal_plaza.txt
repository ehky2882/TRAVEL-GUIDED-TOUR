ATLAS — CHICAGO
Walk 2: The Loop — Where the Skyscraper Was Born
Segment 04 — Federal Plaza (Dearborn & Jackson)
Clean version

---

Flat gray granite, three black buildings, and one enormous red thing that looks like it wandered in off a different block.

Mies van der Rohe designed the plaza and everything standing on it except the red thing. He took the commission in 1959, he died in 1969, and the last of it was finished in 1974, so he never saw it whole. Three buildings: the courthouse across Dearborn, the tall office block on this side, and the single-story post office parked in the middle of all that open space like a paperweight.

Worth noticing at your feet: the granite pavers are one size on one grid, and they run from out here straight in through the doors without a break — same stone inside as out, the only difference a polish. The red thing is a Calder, fifty-three feet, unveiled the year the complex was finished, and it is the only curve on the block.

Now look at the vertical black lines running the full height of the towers, evenly spaced, edge to edge, floor to roof. Those are I-beams, steel, standing a few inches proud of the glass. And in the towers Mies built to this pattern, they aren't holding anything up.

American fire codes required a building's structural steel to be buried in concrete or plaster, out of sight. Mies wanted the frame visible, because to him the frame was the building's one honest fact. So he buried the real steel where the code insisted and bolted more steel to the outside in a perfect rhythm, whether or not a column stood behind it.

*[beat]*

Eighty-nine years after Jenney, the frame has stopped being a solution to a problem and become the thing the building is about. Not hidden, not decorated. Advertised.

Cross Jackson. The last stop is the Monadnock, and it takes all of this back.
