ATLAS — CHICAGO
Walk 2: The Loop — Where the Skyscraper Was Born
Segment 00 — Intro (LaSalle & Adams)
Clean version

---

Between 1885 and 1893, inside the four blocks you're about to walk, a handful of architects and the landlords who paid them worked out how to hold a tall building up. Not how to decorate one. How to keep it standing.

You should be at the corner of LaSalle and Adams — stone on all four corners, not much sky, the middle of the financial district. This is the shortest walk in the set: five stops, about four blocks, all of it sidewalk, twenty minutes if nobody stops you.

Two of the five stops have interiors worth the detour, and both are conditional. One is a working office building that lets the public into its lobby on weekdays and Saturday mornings. The other is a federal complex with security at the door. Everything on this walk works from the outside, so treat the interiors as a bonus rather than a plan.

One question runs through all five stops, and it's simpler than it sounds: what holds it up. Walls, or a frame, or very nearly nothing. The answer changes as you walk, and at the last stop you'll watch it change inside a single building.

The tiered limestone tower on the northeast corner is where we start.
