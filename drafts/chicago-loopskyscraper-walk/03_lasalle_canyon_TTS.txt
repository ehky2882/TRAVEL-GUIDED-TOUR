ATLAS — CHICAGO
Walk 2: The Loop — Where the Skyscraper Was Born
Segment 03 — luh sal Street Canyon and the Board of Trade
TTS-SAFE version

---

Look south down luh sal and notice that the street ends. Not bends — ends, in a building, squarely, the way a corridor ends in a wall.

Follow the street south and watch it close in on you. luh sal is one of the few streets in the Loop that behaves like a room. The buildings come right out to the sidewalk on both sides and go straight up, thirty and forty floors of continuous stone and glass with no setbacks and no gaps between them, and the effect is less a street than a trench with sky for a ceiling.

This is what the frame bought, eventually. Once walls stopped carrying the load, nothing stopped an owner from filling the lot out to the property line and building until the money ran out. luh sal is what a street looks like when every owner on it makes that same decision, one after another, for eighty years.

The building that closes the corridor is the Board of Trade — nineteen thirty, six hundred and five feet, the tallest thing in Chicago for the next thirty-five years — and it sits at the foot of the street, plugging it. The aluminum figure on the roof is seer eez, Roman goddess of grain, thirty-one feet tall, and she has no face.

One more thing this street did, and it matters four blocks from here. An earlier Board of Trade stood on the same spot from the eighteen eighties, and it turned luh sal into the money end of downtown and pulled the land prices up after it. Expensive ground here sent a Boston family with Chicago holdings out looking for cheap ground somewhere else — southeast, at the edge of things, on a block nobody else wanted.

Turn left on Jackson. Next stop is Federal Plaza.
