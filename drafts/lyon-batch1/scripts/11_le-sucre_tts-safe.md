Luh Sookr

Lyon's best known club is on the roof of a sugar warehouse.

Put the Sone behind you on Kay Rom bo, on the Confluence. In front is La Soo kree air, a long concrete warehouse, and on its roof, behind the parapet, is Luh Sookr, a club and cultural venue for seven hundred and eighty people.

Have a look at the building first. The first warehouse here, a hundred metres long, was built in nineteen twenty five to store sugar for the river port. Two more floors were added in nineteen twenty seven, and it was extended again in nineteen thirty and nineteen sixty. By the early nineteen nineties the port trade had gone and the building was empty. Then, in the early two thousands, it was renovated and renamed La Soo kree air, the sugar refinery, and became one of the main venues for the Lyon Bee en nahl of contemporary art.

The roof came later. In October twenty eleven, during the Bee en nahl, a temporary bar opened up there. It was so popular that it was made permanent, and after a renovation costing around a million euros, Luh Sookr opened in the summer of twenty thirteen. It's run by the people behind Arty Farty, the organisation that created Nwee So nor, Lyon's festival of electronic music.

Here's the gift detail. Come at sunset and you'll understand the appeal straight away: the Sone below, Foor vee air on the hill, and the whole Confluence lit up around you. Club nights run on Friday and Saturday from eleven until five in the morning, and on Sunday from six in the evening until midnight. In between there are concerts, early evening sessions and talks.

And here's the thing. Old ports usually lose their buildings when they lose their trade. This one kept its warehouse and gave it a second life as a place to dance.

Before you leave, walk a few minutes north along the quay to the Kewb Oronzh, the bright orange building with a great hole cut out of its side. It's one of the boldest new buildings on the Confluence, and it's hard to miss.
