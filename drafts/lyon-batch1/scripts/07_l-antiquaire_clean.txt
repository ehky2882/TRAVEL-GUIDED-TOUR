L'Antiquaire

Lyon's cocktail revival has an address, and you're standing outside it.

Rue Hippolyte Flandrin is a short street in the first arrondissement, between the Saône and Place des Terreaux. Number twenty is easy to miss: a dark frontage, low light behind the glass, and nothing that shouts. That's L'Antiquaire, and the quiet is part of the act.

It has been here since 2010, and it was the first bar of Marc Bonneton, who has since become one of the best-known figures in French cocktail making. The style is what people call a neo-speakeasy: a bar that borrows the look of the American Prohibition years without pretending to be hidden. Inside, dark wood, bottle-green banquettes, dim light, and bartenders in shirtsleeves.

Have a look at the drinks list if you go in. The classics are done precisely, the Dry Martini, the Negroni, the White Russian. Then there are the house creations, which are where the fun is. The Pandan Killer is rum infused with pandan leaf, with house-made coconut cream, pineapple and bitters. And the Dirty Parmesan, which I'd call brave, is vodka infused with parmesan, vermouth flavoured with oregano and a dash of olive brine. A martini that tastes faintly of pizza. It is, I'm told, rather good.

And here's the thing. Lyon has always been a wine city first, a place of Beaujolais and Côtes du Rhône on the bouchon table. A bar like this one helped give it a second habit, and the counter seats now get booked like restaurant tables. Locals will tell you to arrive before eight on a weekend if you'd like to get in at all.

[beat]

If one bar isn't enough, walk across Place des Terreaux and up Rue Royale to number nineteen, Le Fantôme de l'Opéra. Two very different ideas of a cocktail bar, ten minutes apart.
