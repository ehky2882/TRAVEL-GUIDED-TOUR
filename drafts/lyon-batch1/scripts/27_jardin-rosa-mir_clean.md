# Jardin Rosa Mir

A mason fell ill and made a promise: if he lived, he'd build a garden for his mother. He lived, and here it is.

You should be on the Grande Rue de la Croix-Rousse, outside number eighty-seven. From the pavement there's not much to see. The garden is tucked inside the block, and you go in through the doorway when it's open, which is only on Saturdays, from spring to autumn.

The builder was Jules Senis, a mason, born in 1913 near Valencia, in Spain. He moved to France in 1947 and settled in Lyon in 1951. Then he developed cancer. He made a vow that, if he recovered, he'd build a garden in honour of his mother, Rosa Mir. He recovered, and in 1957 he began.

He worked on it for around twenty years, on his own, stone by stone. Have a look around you once you're inside. Pillars and arches, baskets and basins, all of stone, and every surface covered with shells and coloured pebbles set into the mortar in stars and geometric patterns. Many of the stones came from building sites he worked on. There's a small shrine with a statue of the Virgin, and everywhere, Mediterranean plants. It's a little piece of Spain, in a courtyard in Lyon.

Here's the gift detail. The whole garden is only a few hundred square metres, and only fifteen visitors can be inside at a time, so you may have to wait at the door. It's worth it.

Senis died in 1983. His friends looked after the garden, then the city took it over, and today it's a listed historic monument. It closed for some years when it became unsafe, and reopened in 2016 after restoration.

And here's the thing. Lyon is full of grand architecture paid for by merchants, bishops and kings. This is architecture paid for by one man's gratitude. It might be the most moving building in the city.
