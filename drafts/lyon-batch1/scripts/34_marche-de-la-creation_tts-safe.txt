Every Sunday morning since nineteen seventy nine, the quay along the Sone has become an open air gallery.

Kay Ro man Ro lon is on the Vyuh Lyon side of the Sone, with the river beside you and the hill of Foor vee air rising behind. On a Sunday morning the quay is lined with stalls, and behind every one of them is the person who made what's on it.

The market began in nineteen seventy nine. Today more than a hundred and fifty artists and makers set up here every Sunday, from eight in the morning until around two in the afternoon. Painters, sculptors, potters, jewellers, printmakers, photographers. And there's a rule that makes all the difference. Every piece must be sold by the person who made it. No dealers, no resellers, and no decorating of factory made objects either.

Here's the gift detail. You can't simply turn up and set out a table. Exhibitors have to be working professionals, registered as artists or craftspeople, and a committee chooses who gets a pitch. So the standard is higher than at most street markets, and it's a good place to buy something that will remind you of Lyon long after the Prah loo leen has gone.

Have a look along the stalls towards the bridges. Since nineteen ninety seven a craft market has run alongside it as well, so on a good morning the whole quay is lined with makers.

And here's the thing. Most of the art you'll see on this trip is behind glass, in museums. This is art you can talk to. Ask the person behind the stall how they made something, and they'll tell you, usually at length, and usually with pleasure.

When you've finished browsing, cross the footbridge over the Sone and look back at the stalls, the old town and the basilica above. Then come back across and climb up into San Zhon for lunch in a boo shon. That's a Lyon Sunday done properly.
