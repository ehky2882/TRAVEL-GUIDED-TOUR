La Com moon

For a hundred years this was a joiner's workshop. Now it's where Lyon's young chefs practise before they open restaurants of their own.

Roo Pray Go dree is in Zhair lon, the old industrial district south of the city centre. Look for a long, low workshop building with big doors and, on a fine evening, a crowd spilling out on to the terrace. That's La Com moon.

A family carpentry business worked here from nineteen oh seven. In twenty eighteen the building, about fifteen hundred square metres of it, became what Lyon's tourist office calls the city's first food court. Here's the clever bit. La Com moon isn't only a place to eat, it's an incubator. Chefs take a small equipped stall, about ten square metres each, for a residency. It's a chance to try out a restaurant idea for six months to a year in front of real customers, without the cost and risk of signing a lease. Some of them leave to open restaurants of their own. So the menu here changes as the chefs change, and in a sense you're tasting the next few years of Lyon's food scene.

Then there's the bar in the middle, and the terrace, which is why I've put it among the places to drink. On a summer evening, with a beer and three different kitchens to choose from, it's one of the most relaxed spots in the city.

And here's the thing. For a long time Zhair lon was a part of Lyon people drove through rather than visited. Places like this are what changed that. The idea has done well enough that the same team has since taken over the old market halls in San Tay tyen.
