Lay Al duh Lyon Paul Bo kooz

From the outside, the most celebrated food market in France looks rather like a multi storey car park.

Coor Lah fy et, at the corner of Roo Garibaldi. In front of you is a long, low, modern block of glass and concrete. No arches, no ironwork, no clock. If you're wondering whether you've come to the wrong place, you haven't. The point of this building is entirely on the inside.

Lyon has had a covered market since eighteen fifty nine, when the open air stalls of Plass day Cor duh lee ay were gathered under an iron hall down on the Presk eel. The market moved here, to the new business district of Par Dyuh, on the first of January nineteen seventy one. Thirteen and a half thousand square metres over three levels. In two thousand and four the city began a full renovation, and when it reopened two years later it carried a new name: Paul Bo kooz, the chef who did more than anyone to make Lyon's cooking famous abroad.

Have a look through the doors at the aisles. There are fifty six merchants inside: fishmongers, cheesemongers, butchers, bakers, shar kew tyay, wine merchants and a good many counters where you can simply sit down and eat. The ritual, if you'd like to do it the Lee on nay way, is a dozen oysters and a glass of white wine at a counter, standing up, before noon. On a weekend morning that's exactly what half the room is doing.

And here's the thing. People who come looking for a grand market hall are often a little disappointed by the outside. But that's rather the Lyon way. This is a working larder for the city's kitchens, the place where chefs buy before service, and it was never built to be admired. In twenty twenty six the American magazine Food and Wine ranked it the third best food market in the world, behind Borough Market in London and Chat oo chak in Bangkok. Not bad for a car park.

If you fancy following the name, Paul Bo kooz learned his trade from a woman called Oo zhay nee Brah zee ay, whose restaurant still stands on Roo Roy al, a short ride across the Rone. It's the next best way to understand why this market matters.
