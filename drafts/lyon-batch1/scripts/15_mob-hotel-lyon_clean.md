# MOB Hotel Lyon

This hotel would rather you thought of it as a neighbourhood than a place to sleep.

You should be on Quai Rambaud, on the Confluence, with the Saône on one side. In front of you is a building of chocolate-coloured metal cubes behind a perforated metal facade, with balconies stacked up its front. That's the MOB Hotel, which opened here in September 2017.

The project was led by Cyril Aouizerate, with the designer Philippe Starck among the partners behind the MOB idea. There are ninety-nine rooms over four floors, and nearly all of them have a balcony. They're not large. The Michelin guide describes them as thriftily decorated without feeling bare, which is a very polite way of putting it, and also quite true.

Here's the gift detail. The kitchen is ninety-five per cent organic, and the company is a certified B Corporation, which means it's been independently assessed for its social and environmental record. The hotel calls itself a place to live in common, open to everyone, and it means it. People who aren't staying come for the restaurant, the bar and the outdoor spaces.

And here's the thing. When the Confluence was being rebuilt, the risk was that it would become a district of offices and expensive flats, empty after six in the evening. Places like this, and Le Sucre on the roof of the old sugar warehouse two minutes away, are why it isn't. They give people a reason to stay after dark.

[beat]

From here, walk south along the quay. In about five minutes you'll reach the Musée des Confluences, standing right at the point where the Saône and the Rhône meet. Go at sunset, if you can.
