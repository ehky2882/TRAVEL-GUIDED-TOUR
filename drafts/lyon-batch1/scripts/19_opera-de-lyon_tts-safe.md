Oh pay rah duh Lyon

This opera house has eight muses on its roofline. There should be nine, and the ninth was never made.

You should be on Plass duh la Co may dee, with the back of the Oh tel duh Veel behind you. In front of you is the Oh pay rah duh Lyon: a classical stone front with arches at street level, and on top of it, far too big for it in the best possible way, a great black half cylinder of glass.

The stone building opened on the first of July eighteen thirty one, designed by On twahn Ma ree Shuh na var and Zhon Ma ree Po lay. By the nineteen eighties it was worn out. In nineteen eighty six the city held a competition, which was won by Zhon Noo vel, and the works ran from nineteen eighty nine to nineteen ninety three. Noo vel kept the four outer walls and the public foyer, and replaced almost everything else.

Have a look at the vault. The top of it is forty two metres above the street. Here's the clever bit: Noo vel went just as far down as up. Below your feet the building goes another twenty metres into the ground. Of its eighteen storeys, the first five are underground and the last five are inside that glass vault. In between, a black auditorium for eleven hundred people, hung inside the old shell like a box inside a box.

Now look along the top of the stone facade for the statues. They're the muses of Greek mythology. Eight were commissioned in eighteen sixty two and put up the following year. One fell off in eighteen ninety five, so in eighteen ninety six all of them were taken down, and the ones you see are cast iron copies put back in nineteen twelve. The ninth muse, Urania, the muse of astronomy, was never made at all.

And here's the thing. This building divided opinion when it opened, a modern glass hat on a classical head. Thirty years later it's simply part of the skyline, and one of the few places where you can see an old building and a new one share the same body without either losing face.

Come back after dark if you can. The vault glows red, and the bar Luh Fon tome duh lop ay rah, a few minutes up Roo Roy al behind you, is the obvious place to end the evening.
