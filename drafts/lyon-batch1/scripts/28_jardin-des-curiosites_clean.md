# Jardin des Curiosités

This viewpoint was a present from Montréal, and it came with six chairs.

This small walled garden sits on the hill of Saint-Just, high above the old town, at the end of Place de Montréal. In front of you, over the low wall, the whole of Lyon is spread out: the roofs of Saint-Jean below, the Saône, the Presqu'île, the Rhône beyond, and in the distance, on a clear day, the Alps.

The garden was a gift from the city of Montréal to Lyon, to mark twenty years of twinning between the two cities. It was inaugurated in 2000 and opened to the public in August 2001. It's about six thousand square metres, and it was designed with the help of the Canadian landscape firm Vlan Paysages. Lyon sent a garden back, and it's in the centre of Montréal.

Have a look at the chairs. There are six of them, by the Québec sculptor Michel Goulet. They're said to echo a set of chairs on a belvedere back in Montréal, and they give the garden its name. Each is a little curiosity of its own, and between them they tell you exactly what this place is for: sit down, and look.

Here's the gift detail. The garden has several names. Some people call it the Jardin de Montréal, some the Belvédère Abbé Larue. Most locals simply call it the best view in Lyon that the visitors haven't found yet.

And here's the thing. Most people see Lyon from the top of Fourvière, beside the basilica, with a crowd. This view is from lower down and closer in, and somehow you see the city better for it. You can pick out the streets and the roofs, not just the skyline.

[beat]

Before you go, find the chair with the best view, sit in it, and stay for a while. Then walk down the steps of the Montée du Gourguillon into Saint-Jean. It's one of the oldest streets in Lyon, and you'll come out right by the cathedral.
