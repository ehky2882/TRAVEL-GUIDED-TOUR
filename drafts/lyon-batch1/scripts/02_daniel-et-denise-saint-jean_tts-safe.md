Daniel ay Duh neez San Zhon

The best pah tay on kroot in the world is made a couple of streets back from the cathedral.

Roo Trah mah sack is one of the narrow streets of San Zhon, the old Renaissance quarter at the foot of Foor vee air hill. Look for a small boo shon with the name over the door. That's Daniel ay Duh neez, the San Zhon branch, which opened here in twenty twelve.

A boo shon, if you haven't met one yet, is Lyon's own kind of restaurant: small, crowded, checked tablecloths, and food that is rich, generous and entirely unbothered by fashion. This one belongs to Joseph Viola, who in two thousand and four was named May yur Oov ree ay duh Fronce, the title French craftsmen spend years working towards. In the same year he and his wife Fron swahz took over the original Daniel ay Duh neez on Roo duh Kray kee from its founder, Daniel Lay ron, who had won the same title himself back in nineteen seventy six.

Now, the gift detail. In two thousand and nine, Viola's pah tay on kroot, duck fwah grah and veal sweetbreads baked in pastry, won the world championship. Yes, there is a world championship for pah tay on kroot, and yes, it is held in Lyon. It's still on the menu, and it's the thing to order.

The rest of the menu reads like a list of Lee on nay classics. Kuh nell duh bro shay with sauce Non tew ah, a pike dumpling in a crayfish sauce. Omelette dew kew ray with crayfish. Bress chicken with morels and cream. And a tart tah tan made with pink praline, the bright pink sugared nuts this city puts into everything.

What I like about this place is that it takes a championship level skill and serves it on a paper napkin. All three Daniel ay Duh neez boo shons hold a Michelin Bib Goor mon, which is the guide's way of saying very good food at a fair price. That, if you ask me, is the whole boo shon idea in one line.

When you've eaten, walk a few minutes north to the cathedral of San Zhon, and then on into Roo dew Buff. The courtyards behind those doors are where Lyon's Renaissance merchants lived.
