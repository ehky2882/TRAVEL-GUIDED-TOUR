# Le Sucre

Lyon's best-known club is on the roof of a sugar warehouse.

Put the Saône behind you on Quai Rambaud, on the Confluence. In front is La Sucrière, a long concrete warehouse, and on its roof, behind the parapet, is Le Sucre, a club and cultural venue for seven hundred and eighty people.

Have a look at the building first. The first warehouse here, a hundred metres long, was built in 1925 to store sugar for the river port. Two more floors were added in 1927, and it was extended again in 1930 and 1960. By the early nineteen-nineties the port trade had gone and the building was empty. Then, in the early two thousands, it was renovated and renamed La Sucrière, the sugar refinery, and became one of the main venues for the Lyon Biennale of contemporary art.

The roof came later. In October 2011, during the Biennale, a temporary bar opened up there. It was so popular that it was made permanent, and after a renovation costing around a million euros, Le Sucre opened in the summer of 2013. It's run by the people behind Arty Farty, the organisation that created Nuits Sonores, Lyon's festival of electronic music.

Here's the gift detail. Come at sunset and you'll understand the appeal straight away: the Saône below, Fourvière on the hill, and the whole Confluence lit up around you. Club nights run on Friday and Saturday from eleven until five in the morning, and on Sunday from six in the evening until midnight. In between there are concerts, early-evening sessions and talks.

And here's the thing. Old ports usually lose their buildings when they lose their trade. This one kept its warehouse and gave it a second life as a place to dance.

Before you leave, walk a few minutes north along the quay to the Cube Orange, the bright orange building with a great hole cut out of its side. It's one of the boldest new buildings on the Confluence, and it's hard to miss.
