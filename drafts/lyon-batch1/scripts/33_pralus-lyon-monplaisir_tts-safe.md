In Lyon, pink is a flavour, and this is where you go to taste it.

You should be on Avenue day Frair Loo myair in Mon play zeer, a few minutes from the An stee tew Loo myair. Look for the Prah loo shop, and in the window, the thing everybody comes for: a round, golden brioche studded all through with bright pink pieces. That's the Prah loo leen.

The pink is praline, but not the praline you might know. In Lyon, pralines are almonds and hazelnuts coated in a crisp shell of sugar coloured a vivid pink. They turn up everywhere in the city: in tarts, in brioches, scattered on cakes.

The family business was started in nineteen forty eight by Oh goost Prah loo, a pastry chef, and his wife No ay lee. Their first shop was in Ro an, north west of Lyon. And here's the gift detail. The Prah loo leen was born, and it has made the family name ever since. In nineteen eighty eight his son, Fron swah, took over.

Have a look at how it's made, if you can see the counter. The pralines aren't just on top. They're folded right through the dough, so the sugar melts into the crumb as it bakes, and every slice is different.

And here's the thing. Most famous regional specialities are hundreds of years old and nobody knows who made them first. This one has an inventor, a date and an address, and it's still made by the same family.

Before you go, buy a Prah loo leen, or at least half of one, and take it with you. Then take it round the corner to the An stee tew Loo myair, and eat it on the street where cinema began.
