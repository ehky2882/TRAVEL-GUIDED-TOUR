# Ombellule

The chef in this kitchen came to Lyon from Brazil, and has now won a Michelin star in this city twice.

Cours Franklin Roosevelt is one of the broad, smart streets of the sixth arrondissement, a few minutes' walk from the Foch metro station. You should be outside number thirty-six, where a former luxury brasserie has become a restaurant called Ombellule.

The chefs are Tabata and Ludovic Mey, a married couple who cook together. Their first restaurant, Les Apothicaires, was round the corner on Rue de Sèze. They opened it in 2016 and won a Michelin star in 2020. In April 2023 they closed it, saying they wanted a new chapter, and in the autumn of 2024 they opened here. The Michelin inspectors came back within six months and gave Ombellule a star of its own.

Have a look at the menu if it's displayed. The longer tasting menus are called Floraison and Éclosion, flowering and blooming, and the name of the restaurant has the same botanical ring to it. That's a fair guide to the cooking. The Michelin guide says the kitchen gives vegetables the red-carpet treatment, and that's the point here. In a city famous for butter, cream and offal, the plants are the stars.

And here's what's interesting, standing on this pavement. Lyon has a reputation for looking backwards, for bouchons and quenelles and recipes that haven't changed since the nineteen-thirties. That's true, and it's lovely. But the city's cooking is also being written right now by people like the Meys, who arrived from elsewhere and cook something new in the old capital of French food.

From here, the Parc de la Tête d'Or is about fifteen minutes' walk north. If you'd like to see what Lyon grows, the great glasshouses there are a rather good place to finish the thought.
