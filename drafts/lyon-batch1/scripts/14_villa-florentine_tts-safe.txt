Villa Flor on teen

This hotel sits halfway up the hill that Lyon calls the hill that prays, and it began as a convent.

The Mon tay San Bar tay lay mee is the steep, winding street that climbs the slope of Foor vee air from the old quarter of San Paul. Look up the hill and you'll see the Villa Flor on teen, set into the slope with its terraces looking out over the city.

The building dates from the seventeenth century, a time when Italian artists and merchants were still arriving in Lyon and leaving their mark on it. It was a convent first, and later a place of education. Foor vee air is crowded with religious houses like it. The basilica at the top of the hill is the most famous, but the whole slope was built around convents, schools and chapels, which is why the Lee on nay call Foor vee air the hill that prays and the Krwah Roos, across the river, the hill that works.

Have a look at the view behind you. From the terraces of the villa you get the whole of Lyon laid out below: the Sone, the Presk eel, the Rone beyond, and on a clear day, the Alps. Here's the gift detail. The hotel has turned part of itself into a speakeasy bar called Seh gray toe, which is Italian for secret. It seems the right word for a hotel tucked into a hillside of hidden gardens.

The hotel belongs to the Bo vah lon collection and was recently renovated by the interior designer and curator Zho zay feen Fos say. Its restaurant, La Tahbl, is run by the chef On ree Car lyay.

And here's the thing. Most visitors take the funicular straight to the top of Foor vee air and back down again. The slope itself, with its stairways, terraces and walled gardens, is the real pleasure, and this is one of the few places where you can stay on it.

From here, keep climbing to the top of the hill and the Roman theatres. Then walk round to the Zhar dan day Kew ree oh zee tay in San Zhoost for the best view in the city, with six chairs placed there just for looking.
