# Tour Rose (Maison du Crible)

Behind a door in the old town is a tower so tall and so pink that it has become one of the best-loved sights of Vieux Lyon.

Find number sixteen, Rue du Bœuf, in Saint-Jean. Go through the doorway and along the passage into the courtyard. Look up. Rising out of the courtyard is a stair tower, washed in a warm pink, much taller than the houses around it. This is the Tour Rose, in the house once known as the Maison du Crible.

The tower holds the staircase that served the house, and it's the height that's remarkable. In Renaissance Lyon, it's often said, a tall stair tower was a status symbol, a way for a merchant to show his wealth from the courtyard, where his neighbours would see it, rather than from the street. Some historians have dated the house to the sixteenth century, others to the seventeenth, and the arguments go on.

Have a look at the doorway as you come in. Its design has been linked to the Italian architect Sebastiano Serlio, whose books of architecture travelled across Europe in the Renaissance, and whose ideas are all over this part of Lyon. The influence of Italy is everywhere in this street, from the merchants who lived here to the shapes of the doors.

Here's the gift detail. The pink is the colour the tower came to be known by, after its restoration, and the house took its present name from it. You'll hear people say it wasn't always pink at all.

And here's the thing. The tower can no longer be climbed. It's a thing to stand under and look up at, which is rather what its builder had in mind. A staircase built to impress, still doing the job four or five centuries on.

[beat]

When you come back out on to Rue du Bœuf, walk a few doors along to number six, the Cour des Loges. Its courtyard, under a modern glass roof, is the grandest of the old town's hidden spaces.
