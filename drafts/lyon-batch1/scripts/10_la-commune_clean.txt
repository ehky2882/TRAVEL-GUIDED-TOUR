La Commune

For a hundred years this was a joiner's workshop. Now it's where Lyon's young chefs practise before they open restaurants of their own.

Rue Pré-Gaudry is in Gerland, the old industrial district south of the city centre. Look for a long, low workshop building with big doors and, on a fine evening, a crowd spilling out on to the terrace. That's La Commune.

A family carpentry business worked here from 1907. In 2018 the building, about fifteen hundred square metres of it, became what Lyon's tourist office calls the city's first food court. The project was led by the architect Déborah Hirigoyen with Damien Doublet and Damien Beaufils, and the industrial shell was kept rather than tidied away. You can still see the workshop in the bones of the place.

Here's the clever bit. La Commune isn't only a place to eat, it's an incubator. Chefs take a small equipped stall, about ten square metres each, for a residency. It's a chance to try out a restaurant idea for six months to a year in front of real customers, without the cost and risk of signing a lease. Some of them leave to open restaurants of their own. So the menu here changes as the chefs change, and in a sense you're tasting the next few years of Lyon's food scene.

Then there's the bar in the middle, and the terrace, which is why I've put it among the places to drink. On a summer evening, with a beer and three different kitchens to choose from, it's one of the most relaxed spots in the city.

And here's the thing. For a long time Gerland was a part of Lyon people drove through rather than visited. Places like this are what changed that. The idea has done well enough that the same team has since taken over the old market halls in Saint-Étienne.
