# Musée des Confluences

Lyon built a museum at the exact point where its two rivers meet, and then made it look like a cloud that had landed there.

Stand on the walkway at the southern end of the museum, at the very tip of the Presqu'île. The Rhône is on one side, the Saône on the other, and they join just ahead of you. Behind you, rising up on its legs, is a shape of glass and stainless steel that the architects described as a crystal and a cloud.

The architects were Coop Himmelb(l)au, from Vienna, and the museum opened on the twentieth of December 2014. The glass part at the front, the crystal, is the entrance hall, a sort of covered public square. The cloud behind it is the steel-clad block that holds the galleries. The whole thing is forty-four metres high and a hundred and fifty metres long, and much of it seems to float above the ground.

Have a look underneath. The building stands on piers so that you can walk beneath it, all the way to the point. The point is the reason for the whole site. For a long time this tip of the Presqu'île was industrial land beside the motorway, not somewhere you'd come for a walk. This building, and the walk under it, gave it back to the city.

Here's the gift detail. The collection inside comes largely from Lyon's old natural history museum, the Guimet, and runs to about two point two million objects, from fossils and meteorites to masks and religious objects from all over the world. The permanent exhibitions are arranged in four parts: Origins, Species, Societies and Eternities.

And here's the thing. The building cost far more than planned. The budget was a hundred and fifty-three million euros, and the final bill was forecast at close to three hundred. People argued about it for years. But on a summer evening, sitting at the point with the two rivers meeting in front of you, it's quite hard to stay cross.
