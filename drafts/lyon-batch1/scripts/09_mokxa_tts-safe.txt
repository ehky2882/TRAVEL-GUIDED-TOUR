In twenty eleven, the idea of paying extra for a good coffee in Lyon was so unfamiliar that the people who brought it here had to teach their customers how to drink it.

Up here on the plateau of the Krwah Roos, a few minutes' walk from the Plass duh la Krwah Roos, is Roo Victor For. Look for a small cafe with the name Mock sah over the door. This is a cafe run by a roaster, rather than a cafe that simply buys its beans in.

The story starts on the other side of the world. Roz Morris James is a New Zealander who worked as a barista at Matakana Coffee Roasters in her home town. Sad ree Ah bee dee is from Lyon. They met in Barcelona in two thousand and six, spent six months in New Zealand in twenty ten, and came back convinced that French coffee could be much better than it was. In April twenty eleven they opened a cafe of just twenty nine square metres in a former halal butcher's shop on the slopes below here. It was the first specialty coffee shop in Lyon.

Here's the gift detail. It took about six months for anyone to understand what they were doing. Roz said they had to train their customers, explaining filter coffee and single origins to people who had grown up on a quick, bitter espresso at the bar. That first little shop has since passed to new owners, but Mock sah carried on roasting, and this cafe on the plateau is where you can drink it now.

Have a look at the counter if you step in. The brewing is the point here: syphon, vee sixty filter and other slow methods, alongside the espresso. It's coffee made the way you might make tea, carefully and with time.

From here, walk north up the Grond Roo duh la Krwah Roos. At number eighty seven, on Saturday afternoons from spring to autumn, there's a garden built entirely of shells and stones by a single mason. It's one of the strangest and most touching places in the city.
