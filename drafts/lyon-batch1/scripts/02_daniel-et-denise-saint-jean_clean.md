# Daniel et Denise Saint-Jean

The best pâté en croûte in the world is made a couple of streets back from the cathedral.

Rue Tramassac is one of the narrow streets of Saint-Jean, the old Renaissance quarter at the foot of Fourvière hill. Look for a small bouchon with the name over the door. That's Daniel et Denise, the Saint-Jean branch, which opened here in 2012.

A bouchon, if you haven't met one yet, is Lyon's own kind of restaurant: small, crowded, checked tablecloths, and food that is rich, generous and entirely unbothered by fashion. This one belongs to Joseph Viola, who in 2004 was named Meilleur Ouvrier de France, the title French craftsmen spend years working towards. In the same year he and his wife Françoise took over the original Daniel et Denise on Rue de Créqui from its founder, Daniel Léron, who had won the same title himself back in 1976.

Now, the gift detail. In 2009, Viola's pâté en croûte, duck foie gras and veal sweetbreads baked in pastry, won the world championship. Yes, there is a world championship for pâté en croûte, and yes, it is held in Lyon. It's still on the menu, and it's the thing to order.

The rest of the menu reads like a list of Lyonnais classics. Quenelle de brochet with sauce Nantua, a pike dumpling in a crayfish sauce. Omelette du curé with crayfish. Bresse chicken with morels and cream. And a tarte tatin made with pink praline, the bright pink sugared nuts this city puts into everything.

What I like about this place is that it takes a championship-level skill and serves it on a paper napkin. All three Daniel et Denise bouchons hold a Michelin Bib Gourmand, which is the guide's way of saying very good food at a fair price. That, if you ask me, is the whole bouchon idea in one line.

[beat]

When you've eaten, walk a few minutes north to the cathedral of Saint-Jean, and then on into Rue du Bœuf. The courtyards behind those doors are where Lyon's Renaissance merchants lived.
