Cour des Loges

Italian merchants built the first of these houses in the fourteenth century. Behind this door, you can still sleep in them.

You're on Rue du Bœuf, the long, narrow street that runs through the old quarter of Saint-Jean, parallel to the Saône. Number six is the entrance to the Cour des Loges. From the street you see an old stone doorway, and very little else. Lyon's old quarter likes to keep its best rooms hidden.

The hotel is made up of four houses built between the fourteenth and sixteenth centuries, joined by six inner courtyards. The oldest part dates from 1341, when it was built as a home for Italian merchants, part of the community of Italian traders who did so much to make medieval and Renaissance Lyon rich. Later the buildings became a Jesuit college. Then, until the nineteen-sixties, they were simply flats for families of modest means. In 1987 they were turned into a luxury hotel.

Have a look through the entrance if you can. The main courtyard, in the Florentine style, is covered by a contemporary glass roof seventeen metres high, so you can stand among Renaissance galleries and spiral staircases and look up at the sky. Here's the gift detail. The hotel bar, rather neatly, is called 1341.

The hotel reopened in May 2025 after a thirty-month renovation, which was planned to take nine. That's old buildings for you. It now has sixty-one rooms and suites, and two restaurants under the chef Anthony Bonnet, including Les Loges, with one Michelin star.

And here's the thing. In most cities the old quarter is a set of facades. In Vieux Lyon the interesting part is always through the door: the courtyards, the galleries, the passages the Lyonnais call traboules. This hotel is simply the grandest example of that.

When you step back out, walk a few doors along Rue du Bœuf to number sixteen. Through that doorway is the Tour Rose, a pink stair tower in a courtyard. It's one of the most beautiful corners in the old town, and it's free to look at.
