# Washington National Cathedral

---

Eighty-three years. That is how long this building took to finish, and the men who set the first stone knew they would not be there for the last one.

You should be on the east sidewalk of Wisconsin Avenue, across from Woodley Road, looking across the lawn at the west front: two towers, and a taller central tower rising behind and between them. Keep an eye on the tower to your left, the northwest one. It comes up again.

The cornerstone went in in 1907, with a president swinging the mallet, and the last stone went on in 1990 with another president watching. In between, the stonemasons built a Gothic cathedral the medieval way: Indiana limestone, carved by hand, the vaults carrying their own weight, almost no steel in the structure. It is among the largest churches in the country. The carvings include a few hundred gargoyles and grotesques, and in the nineteen eighties the cathedral asked children to design new ones. One of the winners was a Darth Vader. He is on the northwest tower, too high and too small to see from the street, which is where he was always going to be.

You will hear that nothing in Washington may stand taller than the Capitol. That was never the law. The law, from 1910, measures the width of the street and adds twenty feet, and the same law has an exception written into it for spires, towers and domes, which is why this one is allowed to stand three hundred feet. The hill does the rest. By elevation above sea level the top of that central tower is the highest point in the city, though the highest natural ground is a park a couple of miles north.

Woodrow Wilson is buried inside, the only president buried in the city. So is Helen Keller. The state funerals that are not held at the Capitol are held here. Going in costs something on most days unless you have come to pray, and if you do go in, the window on the south side with the dark circle in it holds a piece of the moon. If there is scaffolding on the central tower, that is the earthquake of August 2011, which cracked the pinnacles, and the repairs have run for more than a decade since.

[beat]

A cathedral is a building that outlives everyone who works on it. The people who laid the first course here knew that and built anyway, and whoever is up on the scaffolding is working to the same understanding.
