# Ford's Theatre and the Petersen House — Tenth Street

---

Two buildings on this street matter, and they face each other.

You should be standing on Tenth Street Northwest, between E and F, in the middle of the block. On the east side, a three-story brick front with five arched doorways at street level: Ford's Theatre. Directly across from it, on the west side, a narrow brick rowhouse with a flight of steps up to a door and a plaque beside it: the Petersen House. The street between them is about forty feet wide. Stand so you can see both without turning your head.

The theatre began as a Baptist church in 1833. John Ford bought the building and opened it as a playhouse in 1863, in the middle of the war, and for two years it was one of the busiest stages in the city. On the night of April fourteenth, 1865, Good Friday, five days after the surrender at Appomattox, Abraham Lincoln came here with his wife to see a comedy. During the third act he was fatally wounded in his box by John Wilkes Booth, an actor who knew the building well. He was carried out through the doors in front of you, across this street, and up those steps.

The house belonged to William Petersen, a tailor, who rented rooms to boarders. Lincoln was laid in a back bedroom on the first floor, in a bed too short for him, and he died there at twenty-two minutes past seven the next morning, with the room full of people who had stood all night. The street outside was full too.

The theatre did not reopen as a theatre for a century. The government bought it, stripped the inside, and used it for offices and storage. It was restored and reopened in 1968, and it has been a working playhouse since — the box kept as it was, the stage in use. The theatre and museum close from time to time for works and rehearsals; the house across the street is the steadier visit, and both are ticketed when open. You can see what matters from here.

[beat]

What this block holds is not the event. It is the distance — forty feet, from a theatre box to a boarding-house bed, crossed in a few minutes on an April night. Every other place in this city that carries his name was built afterward, on purpose, to be large. These two were just here. Stand between them and look from one door to the other. That is the whole of it, and it is enough.
