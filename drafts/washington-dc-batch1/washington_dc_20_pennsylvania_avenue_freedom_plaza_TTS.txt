You are standing on a map of the place you are standing in.

Freedom Plaza sits on the north side of Pennsylvania Avenue between Thirteenth and Fourteenth Streets, raised a few steps above the sidewalk. Find the middle of the paving. Under your feet, cut into the stone in dark and light bands, is the central section of the seventeen ninety one plan of Washington: the long rectangle of the Mall, the Capitol at one end of it, the President's House off to the north, and the avenue beside you running between the two. Facing east along the avenue you have the Capitol dome at the far end; to your left, across the plaza, is a square stone building with a tower and a flag, the Wilson Building, the District's own city hall.

Listen for a second. That clatter is skateboards. The granite here is smooth and the plaza is flat, and skaters found a use for it before anyone else did.

Look at the map, then at the avenue it shows. On the floor, Pennsylvania Avenue is one straight line from the Capitol to the President's House, drawn so that each building could see the other. Now look west, toward where the White House should be. The avenue runs into a wall of columns and stops. That is the Treasury, put down across the line in the eighteen thirties, and the avenue has had to bend around it ever since. The sightline the plan was built on lasted forty five years. The bend has lasted nearly two hundred.

The building across the plaza is the other half of this. Every four years this avenue is the parade route from the swearing in to the house, and has been since the early eighteen hundreds. The plaza you are on is federal ground, kept by the National Park Service. The one building facing it that answers to the city, not the country, is the Wilson Building, where the District's government has worked since nineteen oh eight. The parade passes the local government's front door on its way to the national one, and the people inside watch it go by like everyone else.

There is a plan on the table to rebuild this plaza and the blocks of avenue around it. If it goes ahead, the floor you are standing on may be redrawn.

Everything on the floor here is as drawn. Everything standing around it is what happened instead, and you don't have to move an inch to see both.
