# Old Stone House

---

A clock was made inside this house, more than two hundred years ago, by the man who kept a shop here. It left, and it is back inside, standing in the house that built it.

You are on M Street in Georgetown, between Thirtieth and Thirty-first, on the south sidewalk, looking across at a small gray stone house set back behind a low fence: two stories, a steep roof, a chimney at each end, and a gate to one side that leads to a garden. Everything else on the block is brick and touching. This one stands alone.

Start with the stone. It is rough fieldstone, not cut blocks, laid up by a cabinetmaker in the 1760s, when this street was Bridge Street in a tobacco port in the British colony of Maryland and the capital of the United States was a quarter of a century from being thought of. He built one room. His family added the rest in two more rounds, which is why the house steps and changes as you look along it. The walls are thick enough to sit inside the window openings, and people did.

It is, by the plainest measure, the oldest building in Washington still standing on the ground it was built on. Not the grandest. Nothing about it is grand, and that is the reason it is here. It was a clock shop, a paint and glazing shop, a corner store; families lived upstairs; and in its last private years it was the office of a used-car dealer who parked his stock where the roses are. Along the way the house collected a story about the city's founding, and the story is part of what saved it. The federal government bought the parcel in the early 1950s, cleared the lot, and opened the house in 1960.

[beat]

The clock, then, is the thing to find. It was made here by the clockmaker who kept the shop, and after a long absence the park service bought it and carried it home. One object stands for the place: a thing made here, kept, and returned to the room that made it. The garden is open in daylight; the house keeps shorter hours. Stand at the fence a moment. The street has been renamed and the town has been absorbed and the whole capital has grown up downstream, and the stone has not moved.
