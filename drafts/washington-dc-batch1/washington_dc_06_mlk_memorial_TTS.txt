He is thirty feet tall and he has not finished coming out of the rock. Look at the back of the figure as you walk around it: the stone is still rough there, and the arms are folded, and the face is set toward the water as if the statue has somewhere to be.

You should be on the plaza by the Tidal Basin, between two tall granite blocks that stand apart like a mountain split in half, with the single block holding the figure pushed forward out of the gap toward the water. Beyond it, across the Basin, is the Jefferson Memorial; behind you and to your right, over the trees, the Lincoln Memorial, which matters here.

Take the three stones together.

They are the memorial's argument, and it comes from a line in the speech he gave on the Lincoln Memorial steps in nineteen sixty three, a mountain, and a stone taken out of it. The designers made the image literal. You walk through the split mountain and find the stone on the far side, closer to the water, carved by a sculptor named lay yee shin, which caused an argument of its own when the memorial opened in twenty eleven: an American civil rights figure cut in Chinese granite by a Chinese artist. It took the criticism and kept the statue.

Now walk the long curved wall behind the plaza. Fourteen passages from his sermons, speeches and letters are cut into it, chosen from thirty years of his life and deliberately not including the famous one from nineteen sixty three, the designers wanted the words you don't know. One inscription that used to sit on the side of the statue was removed in twenty thirteen, after critics said it compressed a longer sentence into something he hadn't quite said; the stone was ground smooth, and the lines on it now are simply the marks of the tool.

Stand at the water's edge, then turn and look back over the trees toward the Lincoln Memorial. From those steps, in nineteen sixty three, he looked out over a crowd. From here, he looks out over the Basin toward Jefferson, and the two memorials look back at each other across the water with nearly two centuries between their subjects. This spot was chosen for that sightline. The man who spoke from the stage was given a place on it, a few hundred yards along the shore, facing the other author.
