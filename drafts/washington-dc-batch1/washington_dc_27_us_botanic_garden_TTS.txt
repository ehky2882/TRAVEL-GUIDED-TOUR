You should be standing on Maryland Avenue Southwest, at the foot of the Hill, looking at a long, low limestone building with a great curved glass roof rising behind it, the Conservatory. To your left, the Capitol climbs its terraces. Behind you, across Independence Avenue, a small park holds a tall cast iron fountain. That is the bar tol dee Fountain, the other half of this stop.

Right.

Congress chartered the garden in eighteen twenty, which puts it ahead of nearly everything else on the Mall still doing what it was set up to do. For decades it was mostly an idea. Then in eighteen forty two a Navy expedition came home from four years circling the globe with crates of living plants, and the idea got a greenhouse. A cycad and a giant fern from that voyage are still alive inside, older than the dome next door.

The Conservatory went up in nineteen thirty three, and it is on this side of Maryland Avenue because it was evicted. The garden had spent eighty years at the center of the Mall, directly below the Capitol, where the Reflecting Pool is now. The plan to clear the Mall into one long lawn, the plan that moved the railroad, planted the elms, and made the view, had no room for a greenhouse in the middle, so the garden was picked up and set down off the axis. What you see is a very good building that lost an argument about geometry.

The ground it lost was not dry, either. This end of the Mall was a canal until the eighteen seventies, fed by a creek from the north, and the garden sat on the fill. The city grew by making ground where there had been water, and then deciding what the ground was for.

Now turn to the fountain. Frédéric Auguste bar tol dee made it for the Centennial Exhibition of eighteen seventy six, and Congress bought it afterward. Three sea nymphs hold the basin over their heads, and it was built to be lit by gas, so the water glowed at night. He had a much larger figure on his desk at the time, also holding something up, which went to New York harbor a decade later.

A garden older than the Mall, a fountain by the man who made the Statue of Liberty, and a greenhouse moved out of the way of a view. The plan for this city has always decided what stands where. This corner is what got set aside so the rest could line up, and it has done very well on the margin.
