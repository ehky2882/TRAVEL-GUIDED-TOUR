# Old Post Office & Clock Tower

---

This building has had four jobs and one address.

You should be on the south side of Pennsylvania Avenue, between Eleventh and Twelfth Streets, looking north across the traffic. The building in front of you is gray granite, rough-cut, with round arches at the street and a square tower rising above the avenue front. The clock is at the top of the tower, one face on each side. To your right the avenue runs east toward Capitol Hill; to your left it runs west toward the Treasury.

Two things to look at: the stone, and the tower.

It was finished in 1899 as the headquarters of the United States Post Office, and the department had outgrown it within fifteen years; the rest of the avenue was later rebuilt in pale limestone while this stayed dark and heavy. Twice the government decided to tear it down. The first time, in 1934, it ran out of money. The second time, in the early 1970s, a woman named Nancy Hanks, who ran the national arts agency, led a campaign to keep it, and the campaign won. The building was redone in 1983 with shops and a food hall in the atrium, and since 2016 it has been a hotel. Post office, offices, food hall, hotel.

The tower is a little over three hundred feet to the top, which makes it one of the tallest things in the city, and it is allowed to be only because it was here first — it went up eleven years before the law that keeps everything around it low. There is an observation level just below the clock. For most of the building's life the National Park Service ran it, and when the building changed hands in 2026 the public's right to go up was written into the deed — so you can still climb it, most days of the week, through the hotel. Check before you go in.

That sale is the quiet fact here. For more than a century this was federal property on a federal avenue, and now it is not. The government kept the clock tower for the public and let the rest of the building go.

[beat]

The clock has kept time through all four jobs and two demolition orders, and it is still running. Whatever the building is doing this decade, look up on your way past. The time is right.
