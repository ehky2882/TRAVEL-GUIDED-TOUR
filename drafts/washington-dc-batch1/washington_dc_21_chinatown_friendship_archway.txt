# Chinatown — Friendship Archway

---

Two cities built this arch, and only one of them is on this continent.

Stand at the corner of Seventh and H Streets Northwest. The arch spans H Street itself, just east of the corner: seven tiled roofs stacked in gold and green, red columns at the curbs, dragons painted along every beam. The whole street passes under it. The subway entrance is at the corner; the arena is a block south, on your right.

Not the arch yet. The signs.

Look along H Street in either direction. Pharmacy, coffee chain, phone store, bank — every one of them has its name in Chinese characters above the English. The city asks for that. It is a design rule for these blocks, and it is the main reason the street still reads as Chinatown at a glance. The Chinese population here was once counted in the thousands. It has been counted in the hundreds since the arena opened in 1997 and the rents followed.

The arch itself was dedicated in 1986, designed by Alfred Liu, an architect from this city, and built as a joint project between Washington and Beijing when the two became sister cities. The style is Beijing's — the tile color, the stacked roofs, the bracket work under the eaves — and the location is pure Washington: a traffic intersection over a subway station. Liu had argued, in print, that a neighborhood this small needed something you could see from blocks away or it would stop being noticed at all. The arch was that something. It has been restored twice since, in 2009 and 2020, each time by the city.

This is not where Chinatown started. The first one was a few blocks south, on Pennsylvania Avenue, and in 1931 the federal government cleared it to build the Federal Triangle — the long row of limestone offices between the avenue and the Mall. The residents moved up here, into a German neighborhood, and started again. So the arch marks a second site, and the gold roofs are, among other things, a way of saying so out loud.

[beat]

Whether a street of required lettering is a neighborhood kept or a neighborhood remembered is debated here, as it is in every city with a Chinatown, and both readings are available from this corner. The arch was built to be seen from far off, and it still is — over a street that has changed under it twice.
