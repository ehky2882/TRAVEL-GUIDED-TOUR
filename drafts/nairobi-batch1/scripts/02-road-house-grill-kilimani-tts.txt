Road House Grill, Kilimahnee

Smell it before you see it. Woodsmoke, charcoal, and goat.

Dennis Pritt Road, Kilimahnee. Road House Grill sits inside a private compound, so you may be through a gate before you realise you've arrived. What you'll find is gloriously unglamorous: plastic chairs and tables, a few of them branded with local beer, a television showing whatever football is on, and somewhere round the back, the grill. If the scene in front of you looks more like a family's back garden than a restaurant, you're in the right place.

This is a nyahma chohma joint. Nyahma chohma is Swahili for roasted meat, and in Nairobi it's less a dish than an institution — the thing friends do on a Friday after work. Goat is the classic. You'll usually see it ordered by weight, grilled slowly, then chopped on a board and brought to the table for everyone to share. Alongside come oogahlee, the stiff maize porridge you tear and use as a spoon, and kachoombahree, a sharp little salad of tomato, onion and chilli.

The regulars' tip is worth passing on: ring ahead and order your meat, or be prepared to wait the best part of an hour. Good chohma isn't hurried, and nobody here pretends otherwise.

Then there's the mootoora. Have a look for it if you're feeling brave. It's a Kenyan sausage, made from finely minced meat with onion, chilli, garlic and ginger, packed into a casing and grilled — the nearest thing Nairobi has to black pudding. One local reviewer insists you simply have to try it, and I'd be inclined to agree.

Lonely Planet says locals widely regard this place as the top nyahma chohma spot in town. Food critics have said much the same. And that's the interesting bit. The reputation hasn't been bought with décor or design — there isn't any. It's been built entirely out of smoke, patience and meat done right.

It's worth noticing, too, that the architects who recommended this place spend their working lives thinking about space. And they sent you to a compound full of plastic chairs.

If you've a free evening, come back on a Friday, when the after work crowd arrives and the place finds its rhythm. Order the goat, order the mootoora, and let the grill set the pace.
