Kosehweh Rahnahlo Foods

It all started with five kilos of kebabs.

Keemahthee Street, in the heart of the city centre, a short walk from Nation Centre. You're looking for Kosehweh, which you'll also see written as Rahnahlo Foods — two names for one restaurant, and Nairobi uses them interchangeably. Politicians, office workers and tourists have been squeezing through its doors on this street for years.

Here's the story. In the early nineteen seventies, a young man called William Osehweh sold a five kilogramme batch of mshikahkee — skewered meat, Kenya's answer to the shish kebab — to his neighbours in Kahlohlehnee, in Nairobi's Eastlands. The hawking grew into an eatery under a tree in Kahlohlehnee. That became Rahnahlo Foods at the Kenya Railways central station. Then a move to the old Cameo Cinema on Kenyatta Avenue. And finally, here, on Keemahthee Street. From a tree to a cinema to one of the best known restaurant names in the capital. Rather a good journey.

What you're here for is Loo oh cooking, from the shores of Lake Victoria in western Kenya. The classic order is a whole tilapia, fried, served with brown oogahlee — the maize staple, made with a darker flour — and greens such as sookooma weekee, which is kale, more or less. Fish from the lake, eaten in the middle of the city. Use your hands; everybody else does.

Now, it hasn't all been plain sailing. In the last few years the Osehweh business has had a rough time in the courts and with the banks, and one branch, in Upper Hill, was auctioned off. It's worth knowing, because it makes the place feel less like an institution carved in stone and more like what it actually is: a family business, built from nothing, still trying to keep its lights on.

And that's why the architects at cave bureau put it on their list. Not for the room — for the route. A street hawker's kebabs, followed all the way to the city's main shopping street. That's Nairobi's story in a single menu.

When you've eaten, walk to the corner of Keemahthee Street and Mahma Ngeena Street and find the statue of Dehdan Keemahthee — the Mau Mau leader this road is named after, rifle in hand. The man who fought from the forests, standing in the middle of the city that came after.
