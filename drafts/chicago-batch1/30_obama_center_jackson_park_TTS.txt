This is the Obama Presidential Center. It opened in June of twenty twenty six, eleven years after it was announced.

You should be at the western edge of Jackson Park, near where the Midway runs out, with the tower ahead of you: stone clad, square, and wider at the top than at the bottom. Around its base, low buildings pressed down into the landscape. A forum, an athletic building, a branch of the Chicago Public Library. And between them, John Lewis Plaza. The Griffin Museum is a few blocks north of here.

The surface of the tower is the part worth standing still for. Text from a speech Obama gave at Selma in twenty fifteen is carved into the stone, and the letters are cut clean through, so daylight enters the galleries as words. Near the top there's a room where the South Side is framed through the sentences.

Eleven years. Announced in twenty fifteen, supposed to open in twenty twenty one, ground broken instead that year. And what sits in the gap is federal review and litigation, because this is a public park on the National Register of Historic Places. Preservationists argued that parkland is not a site to be given away, whoever the recipient. Others in the neighborhood argued the South Side had waited long enough for investment of this size, and pressed instead for guarantees on housing costs. The courts allowed construction. The arguments have not entirely stopped, and standing here you are standing in the middle of them.

Presidential libraries usually go up on a donated parcel or a university campus, at a polite distance from ordinary life. This one went into the park at the end of the Midway, a few streets from where the family lived. Whatever you make of that, it is the more difficult choice, and it is legible from where you're standing: no plaza of separation, no perimeter, the lawn simply running up to the building. Give the lettering a minute. The light moves through it all day, so the wall you're looking at is not the wall that was here this morning.
