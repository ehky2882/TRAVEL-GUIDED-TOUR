ATLAS — CHICAGO
Stop 16: LaSalle Street Canyon & Board of Trade — Ceres at the vanishing point
T2 Popular | Clean version

---

Look south down LaSalle and notice how little sky there is.

You should be on the LaSalle Street sidewalk somewhere between Monroe and Jackson, tall stone buildings shouldering in on both sides, the street running straight ahead into a single Art Deco tower that closes off the end of it. That's the Chicago Board of Trade. The figure standing on the very top, small from down here, is the one you want.

Okay.

She's Ceres, Roman goddess of grain, thirty-one feet of aluminum, and she has no face. The version you'll usually hear is that the sculptor assumed nothing taller would ever be built nearby, so why bother. What John Storrs actually said at the time was simpler: from the street she would read as a silhouette, so he suggested the features rather than carving them. Either way, she has spent almost a century looking down at the money without expression.

The building under her opened in 1930 — six hundred and five feet, the tallest in Chicago until 1965. Grain and livestock futures were traded here by shouting, in tiered pits, by people in colored jackets. A market in wheat that didn't exist yet, grown on land most of the traders had never seen.

Now turn around and look north to the corner of LaSalle and Adams. On the northeast side of that intersection stood the Home Insurance Building, finished in 1885 and widely called the first skyscraper — though historians have argued the point for a hundred years and haven't stopped. It came down in 1931 to make room for the taller building standing there now.

[beat]

That's the thing about this street. It has been one of the great financial corridors in the country, it invented or nearly invented the office tower, and several of these buildings right now — including the one that replaced the first skyscraper — are being converted into apartments, because the offices emptied out and the city would rather have residents than vacancy.

A canyon built for money, quietly turning into somewhere people sleep.
