Stop 13: Chicago Cultural Center — Tiffany Dome

This building exists partly because a lot of strangers in England felt sorry for Chicago.

You should be at the corner of Michigan Avenue and Washington Street, facing a gray classical block with arched windows and a heavy stone base, the park at your back. Go in — admission is free, and always has been, which turns out to be the whole point. Take the mosaic staircase up from the Washington Street lobby and keep climbing until you reach a large domed hall. That's Preston Bradley Hall.

After the fire of eighteen seventy-one burned something like two million books out of the city, a Londoner named Burgess proposed that England should simply give Chicago a free library. A member of parliament — Thomas Hughes, who had written Tom Brown's School Days and visited Chicago the year before — took up the collection. Around eight thousand volumes crossed the Atlantic. Queen Victoria sent one. So did diz ray lee, Tennyson, Darwin, Ruskin, John Stuart Mill. Chicago had no public library to put them in, so it started one — in a round iron water tank that had survived the fire.

Twenty-five years later, the city built this instead.

Look up. The dome above you is about thirty-eight feet across and holds roughly thirty thousand pieces of glass set in an iron frame. It's Tiffany work, restored in two thousand eight, and it's generally called the largest Tiffany dome anywhere. Stand near the middle and give your eyes a moment; the color shifts with whatever the weather is doing outside.

There's a second dome at the far end of the building, on the Randolph Street side. That half was built for the Grand Army of the Republic — Union veterans of the Civil War — who kept a hall and a memorial room here. Two institutions, one roof, one address.

The library moved out in the nineteen seventies. The city could have sold the building. It kept it open and free instead, and Chicagoans have called it the People's Palace ever since.

Eight thousand donated books. This is the room they turned into.
