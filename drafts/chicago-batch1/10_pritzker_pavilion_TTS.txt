Stop 10: Millennium Park / Pritzker Pavilion — Gehry trellis, Great Lawn

Officially, the thing in front of you is not a building. It's a sculpture.

You're at the north end of Millennium Park, facing the bandshell — that burst of curling stainless steel ribbons around the stage opening, with a lattice of steel pipes arching back over the lawn above your head. Walk out onto the grass rather than into the seats. The grass is where the design is aimed.

Frank gair ee took the commission in nineteen ninety-nine, and the pavilion opened in July two thousand four, four years after the millennium it's part of. The ribbons are brushed stainless steel and the stage opening stands about a hundred and twenty feet. People have read the shape as sails, as flames, as a dropped ribbon, as an exploded can. gair ee has been reliably unhelpful about settling it.

The trellis is the part worth understanding. Those crossing pipes overhead — some of the arches run more than four hundred feet — carry dozens of speakers positioned directly above the audience. Sound arrives down on the grass at the same instant and the same volume as it reaches the front row, so someone lying four hundred feet back hears roughly what someone in seat one hears. It's an attempt to build the acoustics of an indoor concert hall outdoors, with no walls, and Randolph Street running past.

The lawn is also a roof. Three stories of parking garage sit directly beneath the grass, and the whole park is a deck built over a working railyard — which makes this one of the largest green roofs in the country. Nothing here is resting on the ground.

Now, the sculpture business.

Grant Park has been legally restricted against buildings since a notation on an eighteen thirty-six map, upheld again and again by the Illinois courts. A hundred-and-twenty-foot bandshell is, by any plain reading, a building. So the city classified it as a work of art instead — and the trellis, which the sound genuinely requires, conveniently helps the whole assembly read as sculpture rather than architecture.

Free concerts have run in this park since nineteen thirty-one, when the city started them during the Depression and then never stopped.

If something's playing while you're here, don't go hunting for a seat. Sit down where you're standing. The lawn is the point.
