## The question that started it

The owner asked whether a whole creator account could be scraped and sorted — some posts are
place-based and belong on the map, some cover several places, some do not fit at all. The answer
splits in two, and the halves behave completely differently.

### Classifying is easy; enumerating is the wall

Captions are free on all three platforms, via code this repo already had (`oembed()` in
`make-link-pin.py`, which routes Instagram through its embed page). The caption is the whole
triage signal — it names the venue, the city, and usually says outright whether the post is about
one place or five.

Listing an account's posts is the hard part. **Measured 2026-09-11, not assumed:**

| | |
|---|---|
| `tiktok.com/@handle` | 200, 371 KB, **zero** video ids, 25 mentions of captcha |
| `tiktok.com/embed/@handle` | 200, **14 video ids**, no captcha — what we use |
| `instagram.com/<h>/` | 302 to login |
| `instagram.com/<h>/embed/` | 200, a Facebook shell, zero post links |
| `instagram.com/api/v1/users/web_profile_info` | **401 `require_login`** |
| `youtube.com/@handle` | 200, carries `channelId` → RSS — what we use |

A bare handle therefore yields **~14 recent posts on TikTok, ~15 on YouTube, and none at all on
Instagram**. ⚠️ A `cursor` or `count` parameter does **not** deepen the TikTok embed — tested on
two creators, both capped at 14. Do not "fix" that by adding one.

🔴 **For Instagram there is no route, ours or anyone's.** Basic Display died December 2024, the
Graph API only reaches accounts that authorised you, and Meta's oEmbed is per-post and lists
nothing. This is a platform decision, not a gap in our effort — say so plainly rather than
implying we could try harder.

Web search was tried as a second channel and is a **lucky dip, not an enumerator**: one query
returned 8 real TikTok URLs spanning 2021–2026 (genuinely the back catalogue), while topical
follow-ups returned TikTok *discover* pages and other creators. It surfaced two real Instagram
post URLs, so IG posts are indexed — just sparsely.

### Why this is still worth having, stated honestly

The owner pushed back that 14 posts sounds thin, and was right about enumeration. The catalogue
answers the value question better than any argument: **252 of 343 creators had exactly one pin**
(median 1), and **101 of those are TikTok/YouTube** — reachable by handle alone. So it is weak
for mining a back catalogue and strong for two jobs actually being done: expanding
already-vetted creators from 1 pin, and (as the pin catalogue ages) re-checking every creator for
new posts. The triage itself works on any list however obtained, which is the real deliverable.

## `scripts/triage-account.py`

Takes `--handle` or `--urls`, reads every caption, flags each post **LIVE** (already pinned) /
**SINGLE** / **MULTI** (several places, or a route) / **THIN** / **DEAD**, writes a report, and
**mints nothing**. Already-pinned posts are caught by re-deriving the catalog's own uuid5 id, so a
re-run never proposes the same post twice. A run that reaches nothing exits 2 rather than
reporting an empty feed as a clean one, and the report never prints raw JSON.

### 🔴 The lesson, and it is the important part of this handoff

**The flags are a pre-sort. They cannot replace reading the captions.** Both failure directions
were measured on `@jamiepeva` in one run:

- **It over-accepts.** 9 of 11 posts came back SINGLE; **3** were actually pinnable. The rest
  were a reply-to-commenters video, a press-mention post, a property listing, and a post about a
  Metro station that was never built.
- **It over-rejects.** It binned a post captioned **`📍 Mount Vernon, Virginia`** as THIN, because
  "anniversary" had been added that morning to catch *channel* anniversaries and the caption read
  "the United States' 250th anniversary". It was the best post in the batch.

Fixes: an explicit location marker (📍, a street address, an `Address:` line) now clears THIN
outright, and "anniversary" must name the channel's own. Over-accepting is the safer failure
**only because a human reads every candidate before minting** — skip the reading and that safety
is gone. Both documents now carry this warning in the same words.

Three further patterns came from real runs rather than imagination: "Two of the things" read as
SINGLE; two channel-anniversary posts read as SINGLE; and "🙌 Stay curious, my friends!" read as
naming a place called **Stay** (a leading emoji shifted which word looked sentence-initial). A
bare `both` was tried as a multi-place signal and removed the same day — it fires on idiom ("the
boat does both jobs"). What replaced it flags **routes**, which are neither a pin nor a roundup
and are the commonest reason a good post does not fit a point-geofenced app.

22 self-tests, including that the id scheme reproduces a live pin exactly.

## The six pins

Eleven posts pulled, every caption read, six survived; the owner decided the three that needed a
call.

| Pin | Location | How the coordinate was settled |
|---|---|---|
| The Opportunity Shop | 1427 Wisconsin Ave NW | OSM matched the shop by name |
| The Exorcist Stairs | 36th & Prospect St NW | OSM has it as a named feature |
| The Harriman House on N Street | 3038 N St NW | OSM *and* an independent source agree |
| The Georgetown Metro Stop That Never Was | Wisconsin Ave & M St NW | see below |
| George Washington's Mount Vernon | Mount Vernon, Virginia | OSM named attraction |
| Stonington Food Company | 68 Main St, Stonington ME | address found, then geocoded |

**The Metro pin is the odd one.** The video is about a station that was **never built** — Georgetown
has no Metro — so there is no place to put it. The owner supplied the corner the creator names.
⚠️ **Intersections do not geocode**; it was confirmed instead by two reverse-geocodes five metres
apart returning "M Street Northwest" and "Wisconsin Avenue Northwest".

**Five posts were rejected on the reading, not by a flag**, and one of them set policy: the creator
is a **Georgetown estate agent** as well as a historian, so some posts are property listings.
**Owner decision 2026-09-11: listing videos are judged case by case** — not banned, not
auto-included. Recorded in the runbook and the skill.
