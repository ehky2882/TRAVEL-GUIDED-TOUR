## The headline finding

**Of twelve queries a real traveller might type, ELEVEN return literally zero
results today.** Not bad results — none. `SearchView.filteredTours` (L510) never
scans stops, so 1,774 transcripts are invisible to it.

With the model, seven of the twelve are genuinely good and their top hits are
often the world's most famous example: "old bookshops" → **Livraria Bertrand**,
the oldest on earth; "brutalist concrete" → **The Barbican**; "art deco lobby" →
**Radio City Music Hall**. None of those words appear in any of those titles.

## Three things worth not re-learning

1. **`tokenizer.json` pads and truncates by default, and it does not error.**
   Left on, it discarded everything past the first ~128 tokens of every tour —
   the whole transcript — and handed back `[PAD]` zeros that the attention mask
   counted as real words. Result: vectors so alike that two tours topped every
   unrelated query. **The tell was that mean- and max-pooling produced
   byte-identical rankings**, which is only possible at one chunk per tour;
   the scores alone just looked "mediocre". Now `no_padding()` +
   `no_truncation()`, explicitly, with a comment. Same class as
   `AudioTranscriber` refusing an English model for Spanish narration.
2. **Store chunks, not one averaged vector per tour.** The mean buries whatever
   is distinctive (the Chrysler's lobby is one paragraph in six). Best-chunk
   alone over-fires on broad queries. `0.6 × best + 0.4 × mean`, mean derived on
   device from the chunks at no extra bytes. `tour_scores()` is the function the
   Swift side must mirror exactly.
3. **A weak result set is data, not noise.** The failures are all one shape:
   abstract or subjective queries ("somewhere to take my kids", "a building that
   was never finished") fail because **the transcripts rarely discuss who a place
   suits or what did not happen there**. That is a content fact, not a model
   defect, and no amount of model-swapping fixes it.
