## What the row is

```
≡ All  ·  Format ⌄  ·  Price ⌄  ·  Dozents ⌄  ·  Tags ⌄
```

`All` is a **door, not a filter** — it never fills brass, and its badge counts the values switched
on behind it, which is the only thing in the row that reports state once the chips scroll past the
edge. A set chip becomes its own answer (`Museum`, or `Museum +2`), because a group's name tells
you nothing you did not already know once you have chosen.

The split follows a real seam rather than taste: three **structural fields** on `Tour` — `kind`,
`priceTier`, `makerId` — get a chip each because each asks a different kind of question, and the
**entire controlled vocabulary** gets one chip because it is all the same question.

## 🔴 The mistake worth carrying forward: Price was excluded on false evidence

I wrote "every tour is free today" after reading `Tours.json`, and left Price out of the design on
that basis. It was wrong, and the file will **never** say otherwise: `seed_from_toursjson.py`
**omits `price_tier` deliberately** — price is maker-set and lives in the database, so a content
re-seed cannot reset it — which means the bundled catalogue reads `priceTier: nil` for every tour
however many paid tours exist.

The live database has **66 paid tours**, all `multiStop`, all $0.99. Read with the 47-byte count
query (`Range: 0-0` + `Prefer: count=exact`), never the 3.4 MB RPC. The chip exists because the
owner said *"Price should be a chip also"* — not because the check caught it.

The rule is now in `docs/filter-chips-design.md` with the one-line curl, and in
`TourFilter.swift`'s own doc comment beside `PriceOption`, which is where the next person to
wonder will actually be looking.

## Decisions the owner made

1. **B** over A for the row shape (a door plus grouped chips).
2. **`Dozents`** — the branding for all creators, studios and pinned alike; plural on the chip.
   No "studio" / "pinned" distinction anywhere in the panel.
3. **One `Tags` chip** for the whole vocabulary instead of five facet chips. 🔴 This was free:
   `Tag.matches` derives each tag's facet from a flat `Set<String>`, so the chip count is
   presentation and the combine rule never moved. I initially claimed merging would turn AND into
   OR — wrong, and corrected.
4. **`Nearby` cut** — pin density made it either everything or nothing depending on the city.
5. **`All` restored**, per the AllTrails reference: some people navigate by opening everything.
6. **32 pt panel chips in a 48 pt frame**, after testing 22 pt. A thumb feels the vertical
   **pitch** — the chip plus the gap it absorbs — not the drawn height, so the capsule is drawn at
   32 inside a frame that owns the 16 pt row gap, with `.contentShape(Rectangle())` over the whole
   thing. The row itself stays **44 pt**: Apple's minimum, and this is the
   one-handed-while-walking surface.
7. **Format splits seven ways** — audio stop · audio walk · Instagram posts · Instagram Reels ·
   TikTok · YouTube Shorts · YouTube videos — in each platform's own words: **Reels** and
   **Shorts** are product names and take a capital, **videos** and **posts** are generic and do
   not. ⚠️ Shorts is **3 pins** and Instagram posts **9**, which cuts against the rule that an
   option finding a handful reads as broken. Kept anyway, on reachability: picking `instagramReels`
   excludes those 9 posts and nothing else would select them.
8. Polish, all from artifact comments: flat lists over grouped ones, one `Clear` label, bigger
   spacing, centred titles (titles only — *"i did not ask to center these tags"*), `Format` stacked
   one-per-row (*"i only meant for the format sheet only"*), alphabetical ordering.

## Two rules that came out of the polish round

**Counts are contextual, never global.** `Contemporary` matches 401 tours alone and **zero**
alongside `Museum + Market + Food + Hidden Gem`. A global count beside an option is a lie the
moment anything else is on, and it invites the one tap that empties the map. Options that would
yield 0 are **dimmed, not hidden** — a chip that vanishes reads as a bug, and "impossible here" is
information. This is where the tests are weighted, because it is the part most likely to ship
subtly wrong and look fine.

**A floating pill needs run-out.** `Format` hid YouTube behind the commit pill, and the owner saw
it in the preview twice (*"still quite tight, further up pls"*). The root cause was general rather
than that one panel's height, so `panelRunOut = 96` went into **every** panel — without it the
last option of any scrolling panel cannot be tapped. "Slide it up so everything fits" was also
impossible on its own: the Tags panel was 829 px against a 733 px budget, so the promoted sets were
trimmed to Place 4 / Subject 4 / Why go 3 / Era 3 (637 px, ~30 px spare). And the "fits on one
screen" claim was 3 px optimistic — recorded in the doc as such rather than rounded in my favour.

**Ordering:** counts decide *which* values are promoted; the alphabet decides the *order* they
appear in. `Dozents` (pin count, then alphabetically) is the documented exception.
