Barcelona (session 96) shipped **ten** wrong coordinates and **every one was displaced north**.
That was recorded as "a systematic upstream error — check it before the next city." It recurred.

| Tour | Supplied point resolved to | Off by | Corrected to |
|---|---|---:|---|
| **Basilica of Sant'Ambrogio** | `MaintSoft`, an office on Via Terraggio | **262 m N** | `45.4623754, 9.1758455` |
| **Abbazia di Mirasole** | **Tangenziale Ovest di Milano — a motorway** | **653 m N** | `45.3878869, 9.2017844` |

Both are also very slightly *west*, exactly as Barcelona's ten were. **At the catalog's 30 m
geofence neither tour would ever have fired** — no error, no dead link, just a tour that does
nothing while you stand in front of the building. This class is invisible to
`validate-tours.swift` (the coordinates are valid), to `check-image-duplicates.py`, and to CI.

**Do the coordinate sweep on every city, and specifically test the northward hypothesis first.**

### ⚠️ FOUR FALSE ALARMS — correct as supplied, do not "fix" them

A distance alone proves nothing. All four of these looked wrong and were not:

- **Triennale di Milano — "3,227 m out."** Nominatim's forward geocode matched the **QT8
  *Triennale* district**, a neighbourhood that shares the name. The supplied point
  reverse-geocodes to *"Triennale Design Museum, Viale Emilio Alemagna, Parco Sempione"* — exactly
  what the script names. **Reverse-geocoding the supplied point is the check that settles it.**
- **Officina Antiquaria — "9,383 m out."** Matched a **Via Piero Maroncelli in Cinisello Balsamo**,
  a different comune. Milan's is Via *Pietro* Maroncelli in Isola, where the point sits.
- **balay (326 m) and Fortela (256 m).** Street-centroid noise. Both reverse-geocode onto real
  doorways — Via Maiocchi 27 and Via Malpighi 7. (My Fortela street guess was wrong, not the
  coordinate — the script names a *district*, not a street.)
- **Alzaia Naviglio Grande (218 m).** It is a **towpath** — a linear feature. A distance from a
  street centroid is meaningless for one.

**Method that worked:** reverse-geocode the supplied point at **zoom 18**, then forward-geocode
the venue *and* the address the script itself states, and only treat a disagreement as real when
the reverse lookup lands on something that is plainly not the venue (an office, a motorway).

## 🐛 One wrong hero, fixed from the tour's own folder

**Castello Sforzesco** shipped image `01` — an **inner-courtyard** tower — as its hero, while the
script opens *"You should be standing in front of the main facade, facing the tall brick tower
directly ahead — the Torre del Filarete."* Image `02` in the same folder is exactly that shot,
across the fountain, clock and crenellations included. Promoted to hero; the courtyard image moved
into the gallery.

**This is the Chicago DuSable Bridge case: the correct picture was already in the folder.** When a
hero contradicts its script, check the tour's own gallery before sourcing anything.

**All 54 heroes were opened and read against their scripts.** Look-alike risks checked
deliberately: three churches (Sant'Ambrogio's atrium vs Santa Maria delle Grazie's Bramante
tribuna vs San Cristoforo's twin naves under separate rooflines), three towers (Velasca's mushroom
overhang vs Bosco Verticale vs Pirelli), and the Galleria cluster (the arch vs Camparino vs
Libreria Bocca, all within metres). Many are confirmed by **signage in frame** — "Libreria Bocca
dal 1775", the Bitter Campari Cappiello poster, "Pirelli HangarBicocca", San Siro's "INGRESSO 11",
"ADI Design Museum", the Brellin sign at Vicolo dei Lavandai.

**Judgement calls left as supplied** (the Cape Town Gigi Rooftop precedent — honour the owner's
`01` = hero pick order unless the script is actually contradicted): Palazzo Citterio's hero is a
temporary timber pavilion *inside* the palazzo rather than its facade, and Piazza del Duomo's is
the Arengario side rather than the cathedral — which also usefully differentiates it from the
Duomo tour's own hero. Both are one-line swaps if the owner disagrees.

### ⚠️ `check-image-duplicates.py` reported OK and had checked NOTHING

Every fetch failed with `SSL: CERTIFICATE_VERIFY_FAILED` — it uses `urllib`, which does not work
on this Mac (already recorded in memory as `reference-local-mac-url-checking`) — and the script
still printed **"OK — no suspicious duplicates"** at the end. A tool that cannot reach the network
should not be able to return a pass. **Its OK is not evidence.** The duplicate question was
answered instead by hashing the 236 source files locally and hash-verifying all 236 live URLs with
`curl`, which is strictly stronger. Worth fixing the script to exit non-zero when every fetch fails.
