## 🔴 The finding that outlasts the places: NINE wrong coordinates

The sweep was asked for as a way to group entries. It turned out to be a way to
**find entries standing somewhere they are not** — the one defect class nothing
else in the pipeline can see. The validator passes, CI compiles, every URL 200s,
and the tour simply never fires where it should.

## 🔴 A place propagates a coordinate error

`Habitat 67` is the case worth carrying forward, because it is different in kind.

The error was in the **place**, not an entry. A place's coordinate *is* its
identity, so every member is pinned to it — which means one wrong coordinate had
silently relocated **two** entries, and **neither could disagree**: sitting
exactly on the place is what membership *requires*. The check that members sit
on their place **passes by construction**. The validator was satisfied. The
sweep was satisfied.

The only reason it was visible is that a **third pin, outside the place**, sat
10.9 m from the real building. Without it, the place would have stayed wrong
indefinitely with nothing capable of noticing. That pin is now a member of the
place it corrected.

**Check a place's own coordinate against the source, not only its members
against the place.**

## 🔴 The pattern behind the defects

In the 26–49 m band, **fifteen of seventeen groups had the link pin on the OSM
feature — usually to 0.0 m — and the Atlas tour off.**

That is not chance. **Pins are geocoded per link at import; tours carry
coordinates typed once and never checked.** A 4-decimal coordinate is the
signature: it appeared in every one of the nine.

---

## ⚠️ Where the anchoring method breaks, and what to do

"Anchor on the member OSM agrees with" has two failure modes, both real:

- **Both members off** (`The Ansonia`, 44 m and 74 m). Anchor on the one that
  keeps the other inside its own geofence; neither position is authoritative.
- **Neither member on a feature** (`The Barbican`). **Not a defect** — it is a
  forty-acre estate, not a building. Anchor on the polygon centroid. "Neither
  matches" is evidence about the **site**, not the entries.

## ⚠️ Five traps paid for today

1. **A "false positive" needs the same check as a real one.** `Lloyd's /
   Leadenhall at 7 m` was called an obvious two-buildings-across-a-street false
   positive and recommended for decline. It was a defect in disguise, and
   **declining it would have buried the bug under a decision** where nothing
   would look again.
2. **The seed could never DELETE a place** (#814). Upsert-only, so a place
   removed from `Tours.json` survived as an empty row — the live count read 267
   against a catalogue of 266. Caught only because the Jordaan split changed the
   total. **Any derived table seeded by upsert needs a matching delete.**
3. **OSM's first result is often the wrong feature** — Port Authority returned
   the *subway* station, Marmorkirken the *metro stop*, Villa Charlotte Bronte a
   **beauty salon 17 km away**.
4. **Title is not identity, and neither is title+position.** Two pins shared both
   at `UIC Skyspace`; `sourceAuthor` separated them.
5. **A 6-dp literal of a full-precision float reads as a move** — 2.8 cm at
   Gamla stan, 5.5 cm at UIC. Take anchors **from the entry**, and assert exact
   coordinate equality rather than a distance under a threshold.

⚠️ And two process ones: **a `| head` pipe killed a geocoding run before it wrote
its output** (five of seventeen groups silently missing, exit status 0), and **the
seed can hold the old count for fourteen minutes** — a verification run fired too
early reads exactly like a failed publish.

## ⚠️ Checked and deliberately NOT changed

The `Gamble House` tour carries `city: Los Angeles` while its pin says `Pasadena`,
and the building is in Pasadena. **That maker files all 42 of its tours as Los
Angeles** — a bureau convention, not a typo. Changing this one would make it the
sole exception. The *place* is filed under Pasadena, which is a fact about the
building; the two fields are separate.

**Before "fixing" an inconsistency, check whether it is a convention.**
