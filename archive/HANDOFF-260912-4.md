## What the owner asked

*"i want the 'breathing' icon to come back on the splash screen. i never wanted it to go away"*

## What had happened

- From the first build until #559 (2026-08-22), the splash's brass circle faded **1.0 ↔ 0.2
  opacity every 0.8s**. The website still draws exactly that (`site/atlas.css`, `splash-pulse`).
- #559 replaced it with a **10% size pulse** (`scaleEffect` 1.0 → 1.10). Its reason was real: a
  SwiftUI animation's in-flight opacity was still on screen at the first frame of the hand-off,
  so the disc started growing half-transparent. But a 10% pulse on a 44pt dot is ~2pt either
  side — it read as no animation at all.

## What changed

- `SplashView.swift` — the fade is back, and it runs as a **Core Animation opacity animation on
  the disc's own layer** (`SplashBreathingDisc`, a `UIViewRepresentable`), not a SwiftUI
  animation. The macOS build (no UIKit) reads the same curve off a `TimelineView`.
- The breath is **presentation-only** (the layer's model opacity stays 1). When the hand-off
  starts, a 50ms settle carries it from wherever the breath was up to solid, on the render
  server, before the disc grows. That answers #559's objection directly.
- The disc's growth and dissolve still read `handOff` exactly as before, so they still move on
  the same number as the ground. Nothing else in the launch choreography was touched.
- `LaunchGateTests` — two tests pin the curve and the layer animation's values (1.0 → 0.2,
  0.8s, autoreverses, infinite).

## 🔴 The reason for Core Animation, measured rather than assumed

Recorded three launches in the Simulator (splash held open by temporarily raising
`LaunchGate.floor`, reverted before commit — `grep TEMP-SLOWMO` is clean) and sampled the
disc's centre pixel frame by frame:

| Build | What the breath did |
|---|---|
| SwiftUI-driven fade, Debug | visible, but held each value **0.4–0.6s** at a time |
| SwiftUI-driven fade, Release | the same stepping, holds up to **0.5s** |
| **Core Animation, Release** | a new value every **1–2 frames**, a clean glide to ~0.2 and back |

The resting splash is exactly when the main thread is busiest — the map, the first clustering
pass and the drawer are all built behind it — so **anything SwiftUI animates there stutters**.
This very likely explains why the old size pulse was never visible either. **Any future splash
animation belongs on a layer, not in SwiftUI.**

## 🔴 Build 147 showed NO breathing on device — and my simulator check had hidden why

Owner on TestFlight 1.1.2 (147): *"i really dont see any breathing."* They were right, and the
cause was in the launch gate, not the animation.

- **The gate's floor was timed from launch; the splash isn't drawn until the app behind it is
  built.** Measured with temporary timestamps (removed) and a frame-by-frame video probe in a
  Release simulator launch with the real timings: `onAppear` fired **27ms** after the gate
  started, the gate handed off at **5.66s** (its 3s ceiling long passed, photos never ready),
  and the splash was actually **on screen for ~0.77s** — one dip — before hand-off.
- 🔴 **My earlier verification was invalid because of `TEMP-SLOWMO`.** Stretching the floor to
  6s to film the breath is exactly what made it visible; it also hid that ~4.9s of any floor is
  spent behind iOS's static launch picture. **A timing change made to observe something can
  remove the thing you are looking for.** Verify at the real timings before telling the owner
  what to expect.
- ⚠️ **SwiftUI `onAppear` is not "on screen".** It fires when the view is built.

**Fix (owner decision: one full breath, 1.6s):** `SplashDiscView` reports its first frame with a
`CADisplayLink` tick → `LaunchState.markSplashShown()`; `LaunchGate.isReady` takes `shownFor` and
requires `shownFor >= floor` (1.6s = 2 × `breathHalfPeriod`) before loading/ceiling are consulted.
Backstop: if no frame is ever reported, hand off at `neverShownCeiling` (8s). **Re-measured:** the
splash drew at 13.83s and handed off at 16.50s — **~2.7s visible, one full breath plus most of a
second** (the display-link report lands a little after the frame, so it errs toward more).
`test_sim` **607 passed, 0 failed** (+4 gate tests).

## Build 149: the breathing was visible — but it arrived late

Owner on TestFlight 1.1.2 (149): *"the breathing is visibly there but i had to wait a long time
for it"*, clarified as the wait **before** it started breathing (the static launch picture).

- **Cause:** `ContentView` was mounted from the first frame (#559's design) and the splash is an
  overlay on it, so the map, first clustering pass, drawer and rails all had to be built before the
  splash could draw at all — every bit of it behind iOS's static launch picture.
- **Fix:** `Components/LaunchDeferredContent.swift` renders only the ground (with the splash overlay)
  until the mark reports its first drawn frame, then mounts `ContentView` underneath it. The heavy
  build now happens *while the mark breathes*. 🔴 **This only works because the breath is on Core
  Animation** — the build blocks the main thread for seconds, and a SwiftUI breath would freeze for
  exactly that stretch. Fallback: if no frame is reported, the app mounts after 1s.
- **Measured (Release simulator, real timings):** static launch picture **~1s** (was ~10s in the same
  simulator); breathing starts immediately and runs smoothly — several breaths, because the sim takes
  seconds to build the map — until hand-off; launch-picture-to-app **~8.4s** against ~11–13s in the
  earlier runs. A screenshot after launch confirmed map, pins, drawer, mini-player and tab bar all
  present. `test_sim` **608/608**.
- ⚠️ **The breathing now lasts as long as the build does.** On a phone that should be well under the
  simulator's seconds, but if the owner finds the breathing itself too long, the lever is the work
  done behind it, not the floor.

## 🔴 The zoom had lost its solid brass — caught by the owner

Owner, on the draw-first fix: *"did this get rid of my cool zoom effect?"* Partly, and it had since
build 147.

- **What was wrong:** the breathing layer also drew the zoom, and the "settle to solid in 50ms"
  never took. A recorded Release launch caught the circle **filling the screen at ~25% brass**.
  That is the half-transparent growth #559 was built to avoid. The centre-pixel probes used all
  session could never show it, because the disc covers the centre whatever its opacity; **only
  off-centre sampling plus looking at the frame found it.**
- **Fix, part 1:** `SplashBreathingDisc` only breathes. At hand-off `SplashView` swaps to
  `solidDisc`, the original solid SwiftUI `Circle` the owner approved, for the zoom.
- **Fix, part 2 (owner decision: wait for a bright moment):**
  - The breath is anchored to an explicit `beginTime`, reported to `LaunchState.breathStartedAt`.
  - The gate and a last-moment loop before `playHandOff` both require
    `SplashView.breathStaysBright`: ≥80% now **and** ≥80% `handOffLatency` (0.35s) ahead.
  - The last-moment loop is capped at 1.7s; the 8s backstop still overrides everything.
- ⚠️ **The first version checked brightness only "now", and that was wrong.** A recording caught
  the zoom starting at **~37%**. Temporary logging (removed) showed the phase maths was exact
  (predicted 0.737 vs the layer's actual 0.749). The fault was that a decision at the *end* of the
  bright stretch draws a few hundred ms later, after the breath has faded. Hence the look-ahead.
- **Measured with the look-ahead:** on two launches the solid disc took over at **0.90 and 0.88**
  brightness, a near-invisible step. Expected extra wait ~0.7s on average, ≤~1.4s. That is a
  touch more than the "~0.5s, ≤1s" given when the owner chose; say so if it's felt.
- `test_sim` **612/612**.
- ⚠️ **The simulator recorder drops almost every frame of the 0.37s hand-off.** One recording
  caught none. Two nearly got stopped before the hand-off, because the launch outlasted a single
  screenshot. **Screenshot until the home map is visible before stopping a recording.**
