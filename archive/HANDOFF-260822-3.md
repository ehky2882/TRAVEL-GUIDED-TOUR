## 1. The splash follows the colour scheme now

`SplashView` painted `Rectangle().fill(.black)` and drew the wordmark
`.white`. In light mode that is simply the wrong screen — and worse than
wrong on its own terms:

- **`UILaunchScreen` is an empty dict in `Info.plist`**, so iOS paints its
  own launch screen in `systemBackground` — **white in light mode**. Every
  light-mode launch therefore went **white → black → white**, a flash
  nobody had named.

The ground is now `AtlasColors.background` and the wordmark
`AtlasColors.primaryText`. `background` on iOS is `systemBackground`, so
**dark mode draws exactly the pure black it always did** and light mode
continues the system launch screen with no seam.

⚠️ The brass disc and the hand-off choreography are untouched — the
sequence, the ramps and the two owner-settled numbers from session 102–103
(`~0.2s` growth, `settleLatency 0.06`) are exactly as merged in #559. Only
what the disc sits *on* changed.

⚠️ To look at the splash in the Simulator you must hold it open: raise
`LaunchGate.floor`/`ceiling` temporarily and **grep for `TEMP-SLOWMO`
before committing** (this session used 12s/15s and reverted them).

## 2. Light-mode surfaces: the two tokens swapped

**The owner picked "Raised white" from five candidates**, shown as a design
canvas of the same two screens under each palette:
https://claude.ai/code/artifact/6dad0497-6324-47fd-bfec-886abe70dcd2
(Now · Raised white · Deeper grey · Warm paper · White + hairline). Verdict
on the built version: *"i like it as shown."*

| token | light before | light after | dark |
|---|---|---|---|
| `background` (deep surface) | `#FFFFFF` | **`#F2F2F7`** | `#000000` (unchanged) |
| `secondaryBackground` (chrome) | `#F2F2F7` | **`#FFFFFF`** | `#1C1C1E` (unchanged) |

**🔴 THE POINT IS THE RELATIONSHIP, NOT THE VALUES.** `background` is the
*ground* and `secondaryBackground` is the surface *raised on top of it*.
Dark mode had that already — `#1C1C1E` sits on `#000000`. Light mode had it
**backwards**: the raised surface was the darker of the two, so every piece
of chrome sat in a haze a few percent off the page and nothing read as
raised. Swapping the light pair puts both schemes on one rule and **costs no
call site its meaning** — a form field is still recessed against its page,
and text drawn on brass is still the page colour.

`background` is now a literal `UIColor(dynamicProvider:)` pair like
`secondaryBackgroundUIColor` beside it, for the same reason: a semantic
colour resolves differently by elevation, and these two must hold their
exact relationship across the app's **two windows**.

### What it fixes, and what it cannot

- **Library was the worst case and is the biggest win** — it had been one
  flat grey wash from the tab strip to the tab bar, because the page *is*
  the chrome token. It is white now, and the system-fill thumbnail on the
  Liked row finally reads as a box.
- **Tour detail**: the photograph and the brass play bar carry the page.
- **Home**: the drawer and bars separate from the map — but this is the
  weakest of the three, because Apple's muted light map is itself a
  near-white warm grey. ⚠️ **No fill value fixes the Home boundary**; that
  needs a hairline, which is the fifth palette on the canvas and is a
  two-line addition on top of this if the owner wants it.
- **⚠️ Light mode CANNOT separate the bars from a page they sit on**, and
  must not try: one token paints both, deliberately, and that is what
  killed the dark-mode chrome seam in PR #91. Any hairline goes on the
  chrome's OUTER edges (drawer top, island sides, search field, chips) and
  never on that inner boundary.
