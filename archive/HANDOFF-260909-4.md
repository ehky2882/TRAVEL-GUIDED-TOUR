## 🔴 The find: a short Plus Code recovers against its reference, and the reference was wrong

Two links arrived labelled **`Lower Town, Greece`**. Read as **Monemvasia** — a real, well-known
Peloponnesian lower town — both codes recovered to **37.074, 23.367: eighty-eight kilometres out,
in the Myrtoan Sea.**

Both captions say **Mystras** outright, the Byzantine city beside Sparta. Recovered against a
Sparta reference, both land on the site, 1 km apart, exactly where the captions describe.

**Nothing downstream would have caught this.** `validate-tours-mirror.py` passes, CI compiles,
every URL 200s, and the pin sits in the sea and never fires. It was caught only by reading each
recovered coordinate back against what its own post was about — which is now the habit, and is
written into `docs/lessons.md` § 4.

The general shape: **a vague locality (`Lower Town`, `Old Town`, `Centro`) is not a place name,
it is an unresolved reference**, and a short Plus Code has no absolute position to fall back on.

All 43 supplied-coordinate pins and the 18 I geocoded from captions were read back the same way.
Every other one agrees to within metres of its named subject — the Cyclone, Tom's at 112th &
Broadway, the Hamilton–Burr duel site, the Dimitrios wreck at Valtaki, the Real Mary King's Close,
the Choragic Monument of Lysicrates.

## [#780](https://github.com/ehky2882/TRAVEL-GUIDED-TOUR/pull/780) — the place

That left **three Parthenon pins inside ~10 m drawing two markers**, because the map merges only
within `MapClustering.coincidentEpsilon = 1e-7` — about a centimetre — so `The Parthenon: The
Missing Roof`, 10 m away, stayed separate. Raised with the owner as a presentation question rather
than a defect; they asked for a place. **Places 140 → 141.**

### 🔴 A place is not a proximity grouping — it required moving a pin

`validate-tours.swift` **hard-errors** with `place … : member not on the place coordinate` when a
member's **`stops[0]`** is more than `1e-9` degrees off the place. The first attempt failed exactly
there, and the mirror refused a verdict rather than passing something unchecked:

```
selftest 32/32 faults caught; control DIRTY
SELFTEST FAILED — verdict not trustworthy      (exit 2)
```

So the place required **moving `The Parthenon: The Missing Roof` 9.7 m** onto the shared
coordinate — centroid and stop both. That is the established move ([#746](https://github.com/ehky2882/TRAVEL-GUIDED-TOUR/pull/746)
shifted a pin 3.8 m onto the Gilder Center for the same reason), and `Place.swift`'s standing rule
is that grouping looser than exact coordinate equality **must be approved by a human**. It was.

### The id was derived, not invented

Nothing in `scripts/` mints a place and the scheme was undocumented. Rather than generate a fresh
uuid — indistinguishable from a derived one, which is how two link-pin ids came to be invented on
the spot — it was recovered by reproduction:

**`uuid5(NAMESPACE_URL, "atlas-place:<city-slug>:<name-slug>")`, lowercase — reproduces 131 of the
140 live place ids.** The misses are accented names whose transliteration differs, plus six with
uppercase ids. So `atlas-place:athens:the-parthenon` → `c3a4e974-cfb2-59ce-a86c-6a67502cafde`.

That scheme, the members-on-coordinate rule, and why a place's `heroImageURL` is best left null are
now in `docs/lessons.md` § 3.

### Two smaller things worth not re-paying for

- **`heroImageURL` left null on purpose.** The place page falls back to its top-ranked member's
  hero. Pointing a place at a file a pin already claims is the "two entries, one file" shape
  `check-image-duplicates.py` exists to catch.
- **Append to `places`, never sort it.** Sorting the array to insert one entry produced a
  **2,800-line diff** for a 16-line addition. Reverted and appended: 20 insertions, 4 deletions.

The Erechtheion (89 m) and the Theatre of Dionysus (147 m) keep their own pins — different
monuments, correctly separate. No member is claimed by another place.
