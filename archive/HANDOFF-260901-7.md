**⚠️ THE CONTROL IS WHAT MAKES THIS TRUSTWORTHY, AND MY FIRST VERSION OF IT WAS WRONG.** I fetched
two Instagram pins **already live in the shipped catalogue** as a control — and got the same failure,
which I reported to the owner as "decisive" proof that Instagram was blocking this container. **That
was wrong, and my own diagnostic caused it: I had added a browser User-Agent, which trips Instagram's
challenge page** (~625 KB, `challenge` ×9, a bare `<title>Instagram</title>`, and **no embed markers
at all** — no `WatchOnInstagram`, no `contextJSON`). Re-run with the tool's real
`Dozent/1.0 (link-pin tool; +https://dozent.world)` UA, **the control returns a healthy 260 KB with
the owner blob** while posts 1 and 2 still return 217 KB with none.

**Durable rules from that hour:**
- **Do not send a browser User-Agent to Instagram's embed endpoint from a datacenter IP.** curl's
  default UA and the tool's own UA both work; a Chrome UA gets challenged. Counter-intuitive, and it
  cost a wrong conclusion delivered to the owner.
- **The control must use the SAME transport as the thing it is controlling for.** A control that
  differs in one header is not a control.
- The proxy reported `recentRelayFailures: []` throughout — so the requests reached Instagram and
  Instagram answered. **The proxy is not the suspect when its own status is clean.**

**🔴 CORRECTION: THOSE SIX ARE ALIVE. The owner opened them on their phone.** Calling them dead was an
over-read: a bimodal split and a passing control prove only that **our reader cannot read them**, and I
collapsed that to "deleted".

**The real cause, from the embed page's own state: `contextJSON` is `null` on all six**, where a live
post carries the full `gql_data.shortcode_media` payload. Instagram serves the page to a logged-out
reader and withholds the media context; the owner sees the posts because they are **signed in**.

**⚠️ They still cannot ship, for a worse reason than "we cannot author them" — they would be BLANK in
the app.** `derivable_embed` (mirroring `LinkSource.embedURL`) builds **exactly**
`instagram.com/reel/{code}/embed`, the same URL, and the app's `WKWebView` is logged out. Both the dead
and live pages render **empty server-side**; the player is drawn client-side *from* `contextJSON`, so a
null one draws nothing. There is no `display_url` either, so **no hero exists**. A hand-forced pin would
be a dead card for every Atlas user.

**✅ NOT WIRED** (owner instruction "drop those", given while they were believed dead). Nothing was
removed — they were never added. ⚠️ **Do not re-wire on "the links work" alone; verify `contextJSON` is

⚠️ **WHY the context is withheld is not determinable from outside** — a private account, embedding
disabled, or an age/region restriction all look identical. The shells carry no `not available` /

## ⚠️ THE TOOL'S RETRY LOOP MAKES A DEAD-POST BATCH UNAFFORDABLY SLOW

`make-link-pin.py`'s 4 attempts × (45 s curl + 2/4/6/8 s backoff) is ~200 s **per dead post**. With
6 dead in 31 the first driver was on track for 40+ minutes. **Rewritten as a single-pass grab that
caches the raw embed HTML to disk** (~8 s/post, 31 posts in ~4 minutes), then analyses offline and
re-verifies only the failures. **Reuse that shape for any batch over ~20 links.**

**⚠️ `except Exception` DOES NOT CATCH THE TOOL'S FAILURES.** `instagram_meta` raises `SystemExit`,
which is a `BaseException` — so a driver's `try/except Exception` lets it kill the whole run at post
1. Catch `BaseException`.
