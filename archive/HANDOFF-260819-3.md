## 🔴 A slide-up layer must switch the bars to edge-to-edge

Reported on 1.1 (73): the place page's tour rows were visible under the
tab bar. The owner's own read was right — *"in this view the control bar
isn't extended to the edges."*

The island form leaves 8 pt side gaps and a transparent strip below, and
**those gaps are deliberate**: on a bare Home tab the map shows through
them. So anything covering the map must take the edge-to-edge form, or
its own content shows through instead. The rule read:

```swift
selectedTab != .home || navState.isShowingDetail || tourPresenter.presentedTour != nil
```

Open a place from Home and all three are false. `PlaceView` never calls
`navState.push()`, and the place presenter was never consulted.

**The reserved height was never the problem.** `AtlasBottomModule.height()`
is constant across both forms and `PlaceView` already reserved it exactly
as `TourDetailView` does. Only what is *painted* differs — worth checking
before reaching for the inset.

### The structural part

**Two lists had to name every layer** — this one and `tabSelection`'s
tab-tap teardown — and the place layer shipped missing from the teardown
list in 1.1 (69) and from this one in 1.1 (73). Third instance of the
class overall, after the session-93 tab bar and the batch-D Follow button.

They are now one property, **`isAnyLayerPresented`**, so a new layer is
added once. `tabSelection` still needs its own line, because each
presenter must be dismissed individually — the comment says so.

The decision is extracted as a pure static, `BottomModuleRoot
.extendsToScreenEdges(isHomeTab:isShowingPushedDetail:isAnyLayerPresented:)`,
pinned by `BottomModuleGeometryTests`. ⚠️ **Be honest about what that test
buys:** it would NOT have caught this bug — the logic was correct and an
*input* was missing. The single property is the safeguard; the test only
stops the rule being "simplified" later.

[#543](https://github.com/ehky2882/TRAVEL-GUIDED-TOUR/pull/543), squash `8f89d7d`.

## AMNH is one place now, because its coordinate was wrong

Reported from a screenshot: two gold pins on the museum that should have
been one place pin. They were **107 m apart**, so exact-equality identity
could never have grouped them.

**The single tour's coordinate was the actual fault.** Its script opens
*"at the foot of the grand steps on Central Park West"* and its whole
subject is the Roosevelt Memorial entrance and the empty pedestal — but
its pin sat mid-block at four-decimal precision. Same class as the
Chandler House and Camp Cove corrections. Moved onto the entrance, where
the walk's intro already sits; trigger mode is `manual`, so no geofence
behaviour changed. Both markers are now coincident, so the pair satisfies
the existing rule and **nothing was loosened**.

### The survey is the reusable part

Rather than fix the screenshot and wait for the next one, every marker
the maps draw (a single's stop, a walk's stop 0) was checked for
un-grouped pairs within 200 m. **There are 398**, and the top of that
list is exactly why a distance rule was rejected: LACMA and the Academy
Museum are 22 m apart, Casa Amatller and Casa Batlló 17 m.

Filtering on **title** — one title being a prefix of the other, the
`X` / `X: Four Facades` shape — leaves **two** catalog-wide:

| Pair | Distance | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| AMNH / AMNH: Four Facades | 107 m | fixed |
| Tibidabo / Tibidabo Amusement Park | 48 m | **left alone** |

Tibidabo is genuinely two subjects — the mountain, and a funfair whose
aeroplane ride has circled the same track since 1928. Different scripts,
different photographs. Grouping them would be the LACMA mistake.

⚠️ **This fix worked because the coordinate was wrong, which is a lucky
shape.** A pair whose coordinates are both correct and metres apart
cannot be solved this way — it needs a human-approved place, which the
exact rule deliberately does not allow automatically.
