## The three exclusions — the owner's judgement, recorded so nobody "completes" them

- **🔴 The Beauchamp Tower stays its own pin, NOT inside The Tower of London.** Only `The White
  Tower` pin joined the place. The Beauchamp Tower is a specific building within the fortress with
  its own story (the prisoners' carved graffiti), and the owner's call is that it reads as its own
  subject rather than a member of the site. **Do not add it.**
- **🔴 Only the two general Westminster Abbey entries are members.** The batch put **six**
  `@history_alice` posts at the abbey — nave, sanctuary, the Confessor's shrine, the Lady Chapel,
  the Weston Tower and the abbey node — each deliberately placed on a distinct, reverse-geocoded
  interior point so the stack cap could never hide one. The place takes the Atlas tour plus the one
  general abbey pin; **the four interior-specific pins stay where they are.** That is what those
  distinct coordinates were for.
- **🔴 Houses of Parliament groups the Atlas tour with the House of Commons pin only.** The Big Ben
  pin is excluded — the tour is titled *Houses of Parliament and Big Ben*, so the tower is already
  covered by a member, and the owner wanted the place kept to the building rather than the
  landmark cluster.

⚠️ **All three are the same shape: a famous site has more posts than it has subjects, and the owner
decides which of them are the same place.** A future audit will see these as unresolved EXACT/NEAR
pairs. They are settled — honour the decision (the Ministry of Enterprise / Casa Lleó Morera

## Heroes — nine third photographs, one deliberate null

**Every hero is a third photograph promoted from a member tour's own gallery** — already uploaded,
already verified, **nothing sourced** — and the build asserts it: `hero_url not in member_heroes`
is a hard check, so a place cannot print one picture three times (the fault found across 13 of the
first 24 places). All nine live URLs return **200**.

- **⚠️ The Charles Dickens Museum ships `heroImageURL: null`, and that is correct rather than
  lazy.** `Place.heroImageURL` is optional by design — *"when absent the place page falls back to
  the hero of its top-ranked tour"* — and the tour's only spare gallery image is a **19th-century
  engraving**, not a photograph of the house. A borrowed member hero would have been the
  Waterlooplein case; a fallback to the same image with no claim attached is honest. One sourced
  photograph fixes it whenever one exists.
- **⚠️ Natural History Museum took `_5` rather than the obvious `_2`, and the reason is a
  pre-existing bug — see below.**

## 🔴 FOUND, FLAGGED, NOT FIXED: the two Natural History Museums share a gallery

The London **Natural History Museum** tour and the Los Angeles **Natural History Museum of LA
County** tour reference the **identical seven image URLs**. It is a bare-slug collision from before
the handle-suffix convention: `natural-history-museum_2.webp` is **the LA County museum** and
`natural-history-museum_4.webp` is **Los Angeles Union Station**. So both tours' galleries mix
London and LA photographs today, and have for some time.

- **This is why the place hero is `_5`** — it was the highest-numbered image verified to be the
  London building.
- **The fix is not a byte swap.** Per the standing rule (#567), correcting an image means a **new
  filename**, never overwriting bytes at a live URL — a phone that has downloaded either tour reads
  its photographs off its own disk and would keep the wrong ones forever. So this needs new files
  under handle- or city-suffixed names plus a `Tours.json` repoint on both tours.
- **Pre-existing and out of scope for a place-card batch.** Flagged to the owner, not touched.
