Swift counterpart in a comment. Nothing enforces the link — **if a value changes
in Swift, change it here in the same session.**

- **Everything is caption**: SF Mono 13px, **one size, one weight, one colour**.
  Hierarchy is carried by case, tracking and spacing alone.
- **The wordmark is the one exemption**, in face and colour: New York serif,
  15px, tracked 2, Title Case — identical to the splash's, brass on the prose
  pages and white on the splash.
- **Dark only, for now.** `prefers-color-scheme` is not answered at all; a
  visitor on a light system still gets black. The file says how to put the
  adaptive palette back — every rule reads tokens, so it is a `:root` change.

  `rgb(139,117,53)`, `splash-pulse 0.8s ease-in-out alternate`, wordmark
  `ui-serif 15px / 2px / white`. Pulse suppressed under Reduce Motion.

## Five things that cost real debugging — worth not rediscovering

1. **🔴 A heading does NOT inherit `font-size` from `body`.** Removing `h2`'s
   explicit size on the assumption it would inherit left it at the browser
   default `1.5em` — **19.5px against everything else's 13px**, which is what
   the owner spotted as "not all the same size". Fixed at the root with a
   `body, body *` reset declaring size, weight and colour, so no browser default
   can leak in when a new tag appears. **That reset is load-bearing; leave it.**
2. **🔴 The footer jumped between pages for TWO reasons, and the second is
   invisible.** Each page omitted its own link, so the row's labels and width
   differed everywhere — fixed by making the footer markup **byte-identical on
   all five pages**. But it still moved **7px**, because the splash fits its
   window and has no scrollbar while every policy page does, shifting the
   centred column. `html { overflow-y: scroll; scrollbar-gutter: stable }` is
   what holds it still. **Undoing either brings the jump back.**
3. **⚠️ `position: sticky` cannot make a footer sit at the window bottom on a
   page shorter than the window** — it only ever shifts an element *up* to keep
   it visible, never down. `/about/` above ~1060px tall proved it. The footer is
   therefore `fixed`, and its height has to be reserved by hand: that is
   `--footer-h`, with a larger value under 500px where the link row wraps.
   **Add or rename a footer link and BOTH the token and the breakpoint need
   re-measuring** — shortening two labels moved the wrap point 570px → 500px and
   left the phone reserve 12px too small, which would have hidden the last line
   of every policy behind the bar at 375px.
4. **⚠️ `bottom: 0` does not reliably reach the window's edge.** Fractional
   device pixel ratios and browser zoom round it and expose a sliver of
   scrolling text. The footer paints an opaque copy of itself 32px lower via
   `box-shadow` so there is nothing for a rounding error to reveal.
5. **⚠️ The browser pane cached `atlas.css` across edits and screenshots came
   back stale, off-centre, and eventually all black.** A measurement that "did
   not move when it should have" was the only tell. **Verify layout by reading
   the DOM (`getBoundingClientRect`, `getComputedStyle`), not by looking at the
   pane**, and append a cache-buster to the stylesheet href when measuring in an
   iframe. Every claim in the PR was measured this way — 30 checks across 5
   pages × 6 window sizes.

- **The owner's full name is still in the BODY of `/terms/` and `/privacy/`**
  (*"operated by Edward Ho Kiu Yung, a sole proprietor"*). Deliberately left:
  those are the operator-identification sentences a policy is expected to carry,
  and they are the documents Apple and Stripe read. The real fix is the LLC
  decision, where the operator becomes a company name everywhere. **Owner has
  not yet answered whether to change them anyway.**
- The policy pages are long legal prose at 13px monospace. The owner has not
  said whether that reads acceptably; exempting prose on those two pages only,
  keeping every heading and label in caption, is a few lines if it does not.
